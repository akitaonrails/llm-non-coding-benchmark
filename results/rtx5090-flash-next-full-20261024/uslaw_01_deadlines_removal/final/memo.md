## 2. Service date and method — the clock starts Sep 11, not Sep 27

- Service was **personal delivery to the LLC's registered agent** in Delaware (declaration). The
  library's CR 4(a)(2) excerpt expressly notes the 60-day periods (out-of-state service by
  publication or under RCW 4.28.185) are "**not applicable to personal service on a registered
  agent**" — so the ordinary 20-day period applies. Wash. Super. Ct. Civ. R. 4(a)(2)
  (`wa_cr_4_6_12.md`).
- For **removal**, 28 U.S.C. §1446(b)(1) runs 30 days from "receipt by the defendant, **through
  service or otherwise**, of a copy of the initial pleading." The *defendant* — the LLC — received
  the pleading by service on its registered agent on **Fri Sep 11, 2026**. That the agent's email
  went to an old address and Noor saw it only on Sep 27 does **not** restart the clock.
  (28 U.S.C. §1446(b)(1), `28usc_1332_1441_1446_1447.md`.)
- Caveat (flagged, not cited): the library's only reference to the statute governing out-of-state
  service on a registered agent is a parenthetical inside the CR 4(a)(2) excerpt ("under
  RCW 4.28.185"); the text of that statute is **not in the library**, so the *validity* of serving
  a Washington-registered agent in Delaware cannot be verified from it (any statement of which
  rule governs that service would be from memory — unverified, and none is made here). I assume
  service was valid: the declaration recites delivery to Corporation Service Company, "its
  registered agent," left with a person "authorized to accept service," and the federal analogue,
  Fed. R. Civ. P. 4(h) (library), likewise authorizes serving "any other agent authorized by
  appointment or by law to receive service of process." Attacking service would not be a smart
  play anyway — it only buys time against a plaintiff who will simply re-serve.

## 5. Removal analysis — why the answer is **no, finally**

### 5.1 Citizenship of every party

- **Plaintiff Sound Cascade Foods, Inc.** — "a Washington corporation" with PPB in Kent, WA.
  Under 28 U.S.C. §1332(c)(1) a corporation is a citizen of its state(s) of incorporation *and* of
  its PPB. Either way: **citizen of Washington**.
- **Defendant Harbor Street Bakery LLC** — a **limited liability company**, not a corporation.
  The §1332(c)(1) incorporation/PPB test does **not** apply to it. An LLC is a citizen of **every
  state of which its owners/members are citizens** — "like a partnership, an LLC is a citizen of
  every state of which its owners/members are citizens." *Johnson v. Columbia Properties Anchorage,
  LP*, 437 F.3d 894, 899 (9th Cir. 2006); *Carden v. Arkoma Associates*, 494 U.S. 185 (1990)
  (`llc_citizenship_cases.md`).
  - Complaint ¶2's recitation that the LLC has its PPB in Oregon is **legally irrelevant** to
    diversity; it does not make Harbor Street a citizen of Oregon (or of Delaware).
  - **Confirmed membership (client response, Sep 28):** exactly two members —
    - **Noor Haddad — 65%, lives in Portland, Oregon** (Oregon citizen assumed; see §8 on
      domicile-vs-residence);
    - **Caleb Sorensen — 35%, lives in Seattle (Ballard), Washington** — the "partner [who] runs
      the Seattle shop and signed for deliveries." On these facts a Washington citizen.
  - Therefore Harbor Street Bakery LLC is a citizen of **Washington and Oregon**. Registering to
    do business in WA is not what makes it so — Caleb's membership does.

### 5.2 Amount in controversy — satisfied

$110,916.40 (contract balance) + $18,240.00 (negligence) = **$129,156.40**, which is the amount of
the prayer "not less than" figure, and exceeds the §1332(a) $75,000 threshold **exclusive of
interest and costs** (the 12% contract interest and the Agreement §14 / RCW 4.84.330 fee claim
recited in the complaint are excluded from the computation). Whether separate counts may be
aggregated toward the amount requirement is doctrine the library does not address — and it need
not be answered here: the contract count alone ("not less than $110,916.40") clears $75,000 by
itself.
Arithmetic check: $31,400.00 paid + $110,916.40 unpaid = $142,316.40 invoiced ✓ (complaint ¶5).

### 5.3 Forum-defendant rule — the second, independent bar

Even if the parties were otherwise diverse, removal "solely on the basis of §1332(a) ... may not
[be removed] if any of the parties in interest properly joined and served as defendants is a
citizen of the State in which such action is brought." 28 U.S.C. §1441(b)(2). The action was
brought **in Washington**, and the sole served defendant, Harbor Street, is a **Washington
citizen** through its 35% member Caleb Sorensen. The rule bars removal.

### 5.4 Consequences of removing on the wrong record

§1447(c): a remand for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction can be sought "at any time," the court
"**shall**" remand, and "**an order remanding the case may require payment of just costs and any
actual expenses, including attorney fees, incurred as a result of the removal**"
(`28usc_1332_1441_1446_1447.md`). A guess at membership is not worth a fee award. The friend's
"Delaware company vs. Washington company = diversity" theory is exactly the mistake *Johnson*
forecloses: Delaware incorporation means nothing for an LLC.

**Conclusion (final): `removal_available = false`.** Diversity is destroyed — Washington plaintiff
vs. a defendant LLC that includes a Washington member — and §1441(b)(2) independently bars removal.
Removing anyway would draw a mandatory §1447(c) remand at any time, **with a risk of an order to
pay Sound Cascade's costs and attorney fees incurred because of the removal.** The case stays in
King County Superior Court.
