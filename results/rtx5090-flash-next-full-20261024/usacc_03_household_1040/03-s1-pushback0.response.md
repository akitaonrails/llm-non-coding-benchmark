Fair question — here's the plain answer, with the arithmetic.

## The $16,500 isn't a judgment call — it's arithmetic on your own basis note

The library rule is explicit: *"A shareholder's share of losses and deductions is allowed only to the extent of the shareholder's adjusted stock basis plus debt basis... A guarantee of corporate debt does not create debt basis; only a direct loan from the shareholder to the corporation does."* (`form7203_instructions_excerpt.md`)

Your basis from your own note, in order each year (income first, then distributions, then losses):

| Year | Put in / income | Out (distributions) | Basis end |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2021 | 10,000 in; −2,100 loss | 0 | 7,900 |
| 2022 | +6,400 | 0 | 14,300 |
| 2023 | +9,800 | **−12,000** | 12,100 |
| 2024 | +4,600 | **−5,200** | **11,500** |

2025: loss 28,000 vs. basis 11,500 → allowed 11,500, suspended 16,500. You confirmed you've put nothing in since 2021 and never lent the company money. **The $12,000 + $5,200 distributions you took in 2023–24 are what drained the basis** — that's $17,200 of cash in your pocket that the IRS treats as already-recovered basis, and it's exactly why there isn't enough left to absorb 2025's loss.

## Pike's guarantee gives you zero basis

A guarantee only gives the bank someone to chase if Reyes & Pike defaults. Until money actually leaves your pocket, you've risked nothing that the rules count. (And if you *did* ever pay on it, the library doesn't cover how that would work — that would need research beyond our reference set, so I won't promise anything.) What *would* work is a **direct loan from you to the S-corp** or a **capital contribution** — but that means putting real new money in, which is your business decision, not a paperwork trick.

## Why the buddy's CPA "let him take the whole loss"

Most likely his buddy simply has bigger basis — he never distributed as much as he earned, or he *has* personally lent the company money (a direct note counts). We can't see his return, and the library doesn't cover partnership/LLC rules which genuinely do count entity debt differently *[not in our library — unverified]*. It's also possible his CPA is wrong; that shows up later, not never.

## Two more things worth knowing

1. **The $16,500 is not lost.** It carries forward indefinitely and comes back the moment basis is restored — future S-corp profits, a contribution, or a direct loan.
2. **Even if we could take the full $28,000, you wouldn't get all of it back.** Every $1 of extra S-corp loss lowers your §469(i) MAGI, which shrinks your rental special allowance by 50¢ — that's the mechanics already shown in `judgment_calls.md` #2 and `return.md` §7. (For reference: full $28,000 allowance would put taxable income around $49,100 — *outside the tax-table excerpt in our library, so we could not compute the exact tax from it*.)

Bottom line: the suspension is what your own numbers force, it's documented on Form 7203, and the $16,500 is waiting for you — not gone. If you want it usable in 2026, the lever is contribution or a documented direct loan to the S-corp, and we can model that before year-end.
