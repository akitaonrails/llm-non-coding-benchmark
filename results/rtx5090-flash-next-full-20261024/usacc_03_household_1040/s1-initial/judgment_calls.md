# Judgment calls

Every point where the file required a choice, with the alternative considered and rejected.

## 1. S-corp loss allowed only to basis — Form 7203 basis reconstruction
**Decided:** Beginning 2025 stock basis = $11,500, reconstructed from Marcus's note (contributions,
income, distributions 2021–2024), applying the library's ordering (basis increased by contributions
and income; decreased by distributions and losses; distributions applied before losses each year).
Loss allowed 11,500; 16,500 suspended. Pike's guarantee of the bank line creates **no** debt basis
(only a direct shareholder loan does — Marcus made none). **Client confirmed (round 2):** nothing put
into the S-corp since 2021, never lent it money, and Pike personally guarantees the bank line — so no
post-2021 contributions and no debt basis; the 7203 trace is final (K-1 shows $0 2025 distributions).
**Rejected alternative:** taking Marcus's expectation that the full $28,000 "comes straight off"
income. Also rejected: treating the bank-line guarantee as debt basis — the library expressly says a
guarantee is not debt basis.
**Residual doubt:** the 2021 trace assumes the $10,000 contribution was in basis before that year's
loss (same year, company formed 2021 — the note's "put in $10,000 when we formed" supports it). If
ordering within 2021 were loss-then-contribution, 2021 basis would still end at 7,900 (income items
increase basis before losses under §1366 mechanics *[ordering detail not spelled out in library —
minor, unverified]*; no 2025 effect: no suspended loss exists from 2021 either way as long as the
contribution preceded or accompanied formation).

## 2. S-corp loss character — nonpassive, and it reduces §469(i) MAGI
**Decided:** K-1 Part II says "shareholder materially participates: Yes" → the allowed $11,500 is a
nonpassive Schedule E loss, fully usable against wages, and per the 8582 library excerpt it **reduces**
MAGI for the rental special allowance.
**Rejected alternative:** treating it as passive (which would have stranded it behind the rental
activity and *raised* MAGI to $122,400, cutting the allowance to $13,800). Rejected because the K-1
itself states material participation.

## 3. Residency period — resident through June 30, nonresident from July 1
**Decided:** 181 resident days (Jan 1–Jun 30): Brooklyn lease ended 6/30 and they were out; domicile
surrendered then. Nonresident Jul 1–Dec 31 even though the Tampa closing/move-in was Jul 3.
**Rejected alternative:** ending the resident period on July 2 (until a new abode was established in
FL) → 183 days. Rejected because NY residency turns on having a NY abode/domicile, not on when the
new state's abode began; the gap days (Jul 1–2, in transit) have no NY abode. Immaterial to wages
(both are NY-source all year either way; see #6), material only to the un-computed NY tax.
Also rejected: treating the June 28–30 Queens nights at Elena's mother's as anything — they fall
inside the resident period.

## 4. Below-market Brooklyn rent — rental treatment kept
**Decided:** full rental activity; $21,018 loss computed; §280A(d)(2)(C) not triggered. The library
says renting below market to an *unrelated* tenant "because rent was never raised" does not by itself
make the days personal use "if the rent is a fair rental for that lease" — a 6-year standing lease at
$1,500 vs ~$2,600 market is the library's paradigm for that safe sentence. **Client confirmed
(round 2):** the tenant is a stranger — a nurse found through a broker in 2019 — and the rent was
simply never raised. Squarely within the library's safe sentence.
**Rejected alternative:** treating all 365 days as personal use → **no loss at all** (library: "the
days are treated as personal-use days… a loss is then not allowed"). If the Service re-characterized
$1,500 as below fair rental even for that lease, AGI would jump to 110,900, taxable income to 79,400
(outside the table excerpt — exact tax *[not computable from library]*; reference-only schedule gives
≈ 9,051) and the $2,322 refund would flip to a balance due of ≈ $1,270 *[unverified — 8962 repayment
would also rise to the 300–400% FPL cap of $3,150]*. This is the single largest swing item in the return;
**remaining unknown:** the client has no signed lease or rent documentation, so the "fair rental for
that lease" condition rests on the rental summary's own comparables — flagged as an open item.

## 5. Leo — not claimed; but kept in 8962 family size (assumption)
**Decided (dependents):** father's year. 2025 is odd; the custody order alternates and the library
says the agreement controls; **Elena signed Form 8332 for 2025 — client confirmed ("it's his
year")**; no CTC for Leo. The client has no information on child-support delinquency; some states let
the custodial parent reclaim the child in that case, but that rule is not in the library
*[unverified]* and is not applied.
**Rejected alternative:** claiming Leo because he is the custodial parent's child and lived with them
all year (including Florida). Rejected — the library is explicit that the alternating decree controls
and a released child is not a CTC qualifying child.
**Decided (8962 family size = 4):** household income is 292.79% of the family-of-4 FPL ($31,200).
**Rejected alternative:** family size 3 (Leo not a dependent on this return). Rejected only because
the library supplies **no** family-of-3 FPL — computing it would require inventing a figure not in
the library. The 1095-A enrolled exactly these four individuals, supporting 4. If family size is
really 3, FPL (2025, family of 3) is not verifiable here *[unverified]* and the credit/repayment would
change materially. Flagged as an assumption; the client has no Marketplace-application records, so the
assumption is now confirmed unresolvable (see `QUESTIONS.md`).

## 6. NY wages — convenience of the employer applies to BOTH spouses (client-confirmed)
**Decided:** Marcus $68,000 and Elena $54,000 fully NY-source. **Client confirmed (round 2)** both
remote arrangements were for the employees' own convenience: Marcus asked to move and the firm "didn't
need me out of state" (he is a 40% owner); Elena's remote work was her own request, approved by
Halvorsen. Both employers are NYC-based per the W-2s. No workday log exists (client has no further
information) and none is needed for this conclusion.
**Rejected alternative:** Elena's remoteness as employer necessity/job-location accommodation → her
nonresident-period wages not NY source; her NY wages would be only the resident-period portion
≈ $26,690 (129/261 workdays) or $26,778 (181/365 days). **Resolved out on the client's own
statements** — remote was requested by the employees and not employer-required. Marcus has no such
alternative — he owns 40% of the NY employer.
**Allocation-ratio sub-call:** resident-period wage split by workdays (129/261) rather than days
(181/365) — the library sanctions either ratio; shown both ways above.
