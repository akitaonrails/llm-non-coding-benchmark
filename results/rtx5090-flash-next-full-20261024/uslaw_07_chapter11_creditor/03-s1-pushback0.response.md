## Frank's objection, answered

**He's mixing up two different numbers and two different directions of money.**

1. **Review ≠ clawback.** Preference law freezes the last 90 days of payments (Dec 10 → Mar 10) and asks "did one creditor get cash while others got letters?" Mesa got $180,000 in that window, so it gets *reviewed* — Redwood's insolvency in that window is presumed (§547(f)). Nobody is accused of wrongdoing; it's about equal treatment of creditors, and clawback only survives if our defenses fail.

2. **Most of the money is plainly safe.** The Dec 18 ($51,000) and Jan 22 ($44,000) payments came 45 and 43 days after invoice by ACH — identical to the eight payments before them (43–47 days, all ACH). Textbook ordinary course (§547(c)(2)(A)): **$95,000 stays put.**

3. **The $85,000 at risk is the money *our own credit dept* forced early** — Feb 10 at 29 days and Feb 27 at 25 days *by wire we demanded*, right after we unilaterally cut terms to NET-15 on Jan 20. That's Mesa's own doing, not Redwood's.

4. **The knockout: we kept shipping after both payments.** $48,200 of unpaid deliveries sit behind the Feb 10 payment, $51,200 behind Feb 27 — unpaid new value offsets dollar-for-dollar (§547(c)(4)). **Realistic clawback ≈ $0** (worst realistic ~$6,900; the scary $128,800 requires every defense to fail, which the records contradict). And even in the worst case, an avoided payment swaps cash for a revived unsecured claim — not confiscation [labeled: reinstatement doctrine not spelled out in our library].

5. **On "$210,000":** invoices move the number, assertions don't. Mesa's ledger proves exactly $125,000 across five open invoices. If accounting produces the missing invoices/aging detail before the **June 11 bar date**, the claim is **amendable upward** and the 503(b)(9)/GUC split gets recomputed from delivery dates — and note the arithmetic cuts Frank's way: avoided payments make the claim go *up*, so a documented $210,000 is worth more to Mesa as a claim than the $85,000 the estate might chase.
