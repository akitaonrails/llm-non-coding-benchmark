## Who's right

The advisor's line — "the gain lands in the LLC" — is *literally* true and *legally* irrelevant:

- **An LLC taxed as a partnership is a conduit, not a vault.** It doesn't park or shelter gain; the gain it realizes is reported and taxed on **your** return anyway. Moving the shares in changes who *reports* (still you) — not whether it's *taxed*.
- **Worse, putting the shares in is the single move that destroys your exclusion.** The library's §1202 excerpt is blunt: a contribution of stock to a partnership is **not** a qualifying transfer, the partnership never acquired the stock at original issue, and **"the stock ceases to qualify in its hands."** So:
  - Sale **by Holdings** → $8,988,000–$9,988,000 of gain, **zero exclusion**, all reportable at your rates.
  - Sale **by you personally** → same gain, **entirely excluded** under §1202 across your whole $9–10M range.
- Same gain, same year, opposite outcome — the only variable is whether the shares were in the LLC on the closing date. That difference is worth up to ~$9.99M of excluded gain.

One warning: §1202(h) *does* let stock tacked back via **distributions** from a partnership, so a clever-sounding fix — "contribute now, distribute the shares back before closing" — may be proposed. **Treat that round trip as off the table**: the excerpt says the stock "ceases to qualify" while the partnership holds it, revival rules live in Treas. Reg. §1.1202-2 which our library references but doesn't reproduce [unverified], and a pre-planned in-and-out is precisely what the step-transaction doctrine collapses. If anyone floats it, it requires a written tax-attorney opinion first (flagged as open item #9 in `QUESTIONS.md`).

## Can Holdings own it after the sale?

After the sale there's no Helio stock left to own — you'll hold **$9–10M of cash**, and Holdings can absolutely hold that:

1. **Close personally** → gain fully excluded (§1202(a)).
2. **Contribute the proceeds to Holdings** as a documented money capital contribution — §721(a): no gain, no loss.
3. **Holdings reinvests.** For *new* startup stock Holdings buys **at original issue**, §1202(g) passes the §1202 exclusion through to you — provided you held the Holdings interest when it bought the stock and continuously after — and post-July-4-2025 issuances even get the bigger **$15M cap** with 50/75/100% tiering. (One open question for your CPA: whether your LLC-interest holding period feeds the 5-year clock for passthrough QSBS — [unverified], ask before Holdings buys intended QSBS.)

That's the advisor's promise done correctly: **the tax-free event comes first, personally; the LLC is the reinvestment vehicle after.** The only thing the LLC can never do is shelter ongoing investment income — every future gain, dividend, or interest inside it still lands on your return the year it's earned.
