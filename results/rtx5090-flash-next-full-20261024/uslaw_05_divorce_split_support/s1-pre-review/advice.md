The schedule divides **face values** equally: $770,000 each. But a dollar inside a pre-tax 401(k), a dollar inside a house with a low tax basis, and a dollar in the bank are not worth the same. Revaluing each side for the tax that will eventually be paid (full arithmetic in §2):

|---|---:|---:|
| Wife | $770,000 | **$613,008** (48.2%) |
| Husband | $770,000 | **$658,960** (51.8%) |

Columbia law expressly makes "the tax consequences of the division to each party" a factor in an equitable division — *Col. Rev. Stat. §14-10-113(1)(d)* (`library/columbia_equitable_distribution.md`). So the proposal is a **starting point, not a fair endpoint**.

On top of that, Husband understated his income (§8). That is a separate lever: a party who fails to disclose may have the judgment reopened and may be sanctioned, **including an unequal division in his disfavor** — *Col. R. Fam. P. 16.2(e)* (`library/columbia_equitable_distribution.md`).

### Answering "it's exactly 50/50 and you're getting the biggest asset"

Both sentences are technically true and both mislead:

- **"Exactly 50/50" is true only of face values.** A $770,000/$770,000 split becomes **$613,008/$658,960** after tax (§2). Stat. §14-10-113(1)(d)*) — his lawyer's "exactly 50/50" line is the part of the math the statute says *not* to ignore.
- **"The biggest asset" is the most discounted one.** On paper: house $700,000 equity; his 401(k) $400,000; brokerage $300,000. A $401(k) divided by QDRO is portable and penalty-safe (§5); a house you cannot finance or sell cheaply is neither.
- **Nobody is trying to take the house.** Both recommended structures (B and C) start from your housing goal; Option C *is* the stay-in-the-house deal, with him indemnified on the loan he stays on. And Option C is not a raid on his side: at the engagement's 24% convention it puts **both** sides at **$661,707** — his slice does not shrink versus his own proposal; the ≈ **$51,446** gain comes from keeping both §121 exclusions alive (the IRS's money, not his), plus he keeps a half interest in the home and an indemnity package.

## 5. Instruments you need (and what a QDRO is)

- **QDRO ("qualified domestic relations order") — you need one if any part of his 401(k) comes to you.** A 401(k) division *must* be done by a QDRO; without one, a payout to you is **taxed to him** and, if he's under 59½, hit with a **10% penalty** — and you get nothing taxed-to-you basis. *26 U.S.C. §414(p); ERISA §206(d)(3)* (`library/qdro_72t.md`). Under a QDRO, your share is taxed to **you**, the 10% early-withdrawal penalty does **not** apply to QDRO distributions to you (*§72(t)(2)(C)* — a useful safety valve if you need cash before 59½), and you can roll it into an IRA. Get the QDRO entered with (not after) the decree.
- **Deed / title language for the house** that preserves the joint §121 exclusion in the deferred-sale option (he remains an owner; you have exclusive possession and indemnify the mortgage; sale or refinance triggered on a fixed date — e.g., 2032 or your youngest finishing middle school).
- **Corrected maintenance clause** (see §6) and **income-trust/notice language** so his February bonus installment ($22,500) is reported.
- If any IRA is divided later, no QDRO is needed (transfer incident to divorce, §408(d)(6), per `library/qdro_72t.md`).

## 6. Maintenance: his own clause is legally wrong — a free win for you

His proposal says $2,000/month for 36 months, *"deductible to Husband and taxable to Wife."* For decrees **executed after Dec 31, 2018**, alimony is **neither deductible by the payor nor taxable to the recipient** — *TCJA §11051 (P.L. 115-97)* (`library/irc_1041_121_71.md`). Practical effect:

- You keep **all $72,000** (36 × $2,000) at the federal level. Under his mislabeled clause you would have owed ≈ **$17,280** (36 × $24,000 × 24%) — delete that language.
- To him the $72,000 costs a full $72,000 (he keeps no ~$17,280 deduction). Expect him to try to cut the amount on that basis; that trade is his to argue, not a reason for you to accept the bad wording.
- Child support is **never** deductible or includible (same file).
- For the **child-support worksheet only**, maintenance still shifts income: −$24,000 to him, +$24,000 to you (Columbia guidelines — see §7).
