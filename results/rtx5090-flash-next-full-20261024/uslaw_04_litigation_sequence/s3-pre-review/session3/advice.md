## 1. Does the arbitration clause matter? It decides where and how this case is fought

Marcus means **Exhibit C — Dispute Resolution**. It is not a boilerplate afterthought; it controls everything about the mechanics of this dispute:

- **C.1:** "Any dispute, claim or controversy arising out of or relating to this Agreement, **including** … **the arbitrability of any claim**, shall be resolved **exclusively by binding arbitration** administered by the American Arbitration Association under its Commercial Arbitration Rules before a **single arbitrator** seated in Capital City, State of Columbia."
- **C.3:** "The **prevailing party in any arbitration or in any action to compel arbitration** shall be entitled to recover its **reasonable attorneys' fees and costs**."
- **C.4:** Either party may seek **provisional injunctive relief in court in aid of arbitration**.
- **C.5:** Exhibit C **survives termination** — so even if Pinecrest serves 60-day notice under §4, the arbitration/fee machinery continues.
- **C.6:** The only court door for money claims: "claims **solely for collection of undisputed invoices of less than $25,000**" in small-claims court.

Two preliminary client questions are answered directly:

1. **"We never looked at the exhibits."** That does not help. The FAA makes a written arbitration provision in a contract involving commerce "valid, **irrevocable**, and enforceable, save upon such grounds as exist at law or in equity for the revocation of **any contract**" (9 U.S.C. §2 — library `faa_2_4.md`). The savings clause covers contract-wide defenses (fraud, duress, incapacity, unconscionability). "Our old lawyer drafted it off a template" and "we never read it" are not among those grounds; §7 of the Agreement states the parties' entire agreement is "This Agreement, Schedule 1 and Exhibits A–C," and both principals signed. (The general proposition that a signed contract binds a party who did not read it is standard contract law but is **not in our library**; we label it unverified and rest on §2, which is in the library.)
2. **"Hanna's lawyer hinted at it."** Correct hint — their Sept. 29 letter, ¶4, was telling us they know the forum is arbitration. Our Oct. 26 reply letter already says Juniper proceeds in AAA. That is the correct posture and we will not contradict it.

## 2. What happens if we "hit them hard" in Superior Court anyway

If Juniper files a Superior Court complaint on these claims, the sequence is mechanical:

1. **Pinecrest moves to compel arbitration and to stay (FAA §§3–4).** A court "shall stay" referable issues on application (§3) and can compel a party who refuses to arbitrate (§4). Our escape arguments are foreclosed by the clause's own text: C.1 sends "**the arbitrability of any claim**" to the arbitrator — a delegation clause, which the FAA enforces "if clear and unmistakable" (*Rent-A-Center v. Jackson*, 561 U.S. 63 (2010), as synthesized in `faa_2_4.md`). Even an argument that "this claim isn't covered by Exhibit C" is itself decided by the arbitrator, not the judge.
2. **We then pay for the mistake, in cash.** C.3 covers "**any action to compel arbitration**," and Columbia enforces prevailing-party clauses "as written, including in arbitration and in motions to compel" (*Zuniga v. Mercer*, 198 Col. 2d 77 (2017) — `fee_shifting_and_candor.md`). The library's FAA file says the same thing specifically: "A party that files suit on arbitrable claims may be compelled to arbitrate and, **under a fee clause, may owe the fees of the motion to compel**." So a court filing converts us from the party holding a near-unlosable documentary claim into the party funding Pinecrest's motion practice before a merits hearing ever happens.
3. **We would also be contradicting our own file and letters.** Our reply letter states Juniper's forum is AAA; a court complaint would contradict it (Rule 3.1's ban on claims without a non-frivolous basis, and Rule 3.3 if the old demand letter's "never consented to any direct hire" language migrated into a complaint — see `session2/advice.md` §§1, 3 and the candor file).

**The one legitimate courthouse:** C.4 — provisional injunctive relief **in aid of arbitration**. That is where we would go if Pinecrest takes further Juniper personnel in breach of §3 (no-hire during the term + 12 months). That is the version of "hit them hard in court" that the contract itself allows, and it pairs with the outstanding question of any post-June hires (QUESTIONS.md #13).

**Small claims is also closed:** C.6 applies only to "undisputed invoices of less than $25,000." Every deemed-accepted invoice exceeds that — J-4411 $68,400.00, J-4452 $71,250.00, J-4490 $66,100.00, J-4571 $59,450.00 — and J-4533 ($71,700.00 net) is disputed. Nothing qualifies.

## 3. Is arbitration good or bad for Juniper? Mostly good — say so plainly

**Good:**
- Our core claims are **documentary**: four invoices not disputed within the §2 15-day window are "deemed accepted," and an invoice retained without timely objection is prima facie evidence of the debt (account stated, CJI 14.02; course-of-dealing rules, CJI 16.10 / UCC §1-303 by analogy — `columbia_account_stated_waiver.md`). A single commercial arbitrator on paper is the best forum for that claim. Pinecrest's own letter concedes those invoices are "undisputed" while withholding them — that is a partial award waiting to be written.
- **C.3 runs against Pinecrest on the holdback.** If they insist on holding admitted-undisputed debt pending the fee fight, and we win that issue, we ask the arbitrator for **our** fees and costs under C.3, enforced as written (*Zuniga*). That is the real answer to "can we make them pay our legal fees" — yes, as prevailing party, in the arbitration.
- Speed and finality: one arbitrator, Commercial Rules, no jury. (Practical observation, not a library citation.)

**Bad (candor):**
- The **$48,000 fact fight survives** wherever we file: Bell's acquiescence texts ("ha, we'll talk," "😬 call me") and the Feb. 11 email's "keep the RN orders coming" line support an implied-waiver/estoppel argument on Priya/Luis/Keisha — "a fact question" per `columbia_account_stated_waiver.md`. An arbitrator decides facts with less formality; it cuts both ways.
- **We pay into the system too**: AAA filing/administrative fees and the arbitrator's compensation are real costs, and we lose if we lose. (No fee-schedule authority in the library — figures below are labeled planning assumptions, not verified numbers.)
- There is **no automatic appeal** of an adverse award. (General practice point, not in the library; treated as unverified.)
- C.2 waives class/consolidated proceedings — irrelevant here, but it means one dispute, one award.

## 4. "Can we at least make them pay our legal fees?"

**Yes — through Exhibit C.3, and only through it.** The mechanics:

- C.3 pays the **prevailing party** its **reasonable** attorneys' fees and costs in (a) any arbitration, or (b) any action to compel arbitration. Columbia enforces such clauses as written (*Zuniga*; `fee_shifting_and_candor.md`).
- Section 6 does **not** block it: it bars consequential damages but expressly provides "Client's liability for unpaid fees and conversion fees is **not limited**," and C.3 is a separate fee-shifting covenant in Exhibit C, which §7 makes part of the entire agreement.
- There is **no statute or rule in our library that awards fees for anything else** (no fee-shifting consumer/employment statute applies to this commercial contract dispute; and whether fees absent a clause are recoverable at all — the default American rule — is not in the library and is labeled unverified). So the answer to the CEO is simple: **the fee clause is our fee statute.** Use AAA arbitration (where it helps us), never a court complaint (where it helps them).

**Planning arithmetic for the fee question** (illustrative assumptions, clearly labeled — no cost authority in the library; to be replaced with actual AAA figures at filing, and refined in session 5):

- Our path to a fee award: prevail on the deemed-accepted-invoice issue + substantially prevail overall. Assume our fees/costs through a documentary arbitration at, say, 250–400 hours × $400/hr = **$100,000.00–$160,000.00**, plus AAA filing/administration + arbitrator compensation assumed at **$15,000.00–$35,000.00** ⇒ our all-in arbitration cost roughly **$115,000.00–$195,000.00** (placeholder range; recoverable if we prevail, subject to "reasonable" reduction).
- The court-mistake cost, if we filed in Superior Court: their fees of the motion to compel — C.3/Zuniga — plausibly **$15,000.00–$40,000.00** (placeholder), plus our own wasted pleadings, before any merits hearing.
- Against a recovery corridor of **$377,747.50–$425,747.50** plus $2,652.00/month interest, an arbitrated win with a C.3 fee award means the **economic burden of the dispute sits on Pinecrest**, not Juniper. That is the only lawful version of "make them pay our legal fees," and it is worth more than any courtroom spectacle.
