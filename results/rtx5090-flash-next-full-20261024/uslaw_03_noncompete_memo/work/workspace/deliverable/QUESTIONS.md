## A. Facts bearing on the Texas-law fallback (the only branch where §8 could survive at all)

**Q1. Does the full agreement (beyond the excerpt) contain a Meridian promise to provide confidential information or specialized training, and did Meridian in fact deliver it to Zhao?** — **[partial]**

- **[answered]** The agreement is Meridian's **standard form for all engineers**; engineers **sign the same day** it is issued; **nobody negotiates** it; no engineer, including Zhao, was represented by counsel. The HR file note for Zhao's own signing reads: sent 9:12 a.m., DocuSign signature 11:05 a.m., "No negotiation; no attorney review requested or offered. Standard form for all engineers."
- **[flagged]** The **complete agreement was not produced** — we have the §§8, 9, 10, 14, 15 excerpt only — and the client had no further information about an employer-side promise elsewhere in the document.

Why it matters: this is **the one fact that could move our numbers**. *Sheshunoff*, 209 S.W.3d 644 (Tex. 2006), made an at-will covenant enforceable where the employer's promise (e.g., to provide confidential information) was **performed**. The excerpt contains no employer-side promise — §10 is Zhao's obligation, not Meridian's — so on the record we have, Meridian probably cannot carry its §15.51(b) burden and P(§8 ancillary) is **~25–40%** (memo §5.4(a)). If a performed Meridian promise exists, ancillarity becomes a live fight instead of a probable loss. We did **not** adjust the estimate for a fact we do not have.

**Q2. Is the agreement at-will, and did Zhao receive anything at signing beyond continued at-will employment (bonus, equity, training reimbursement)?** — **[partial]**

- **[answered]** Nothing was negotiated and the form is uniform, so there is no bespoke, bargained-for exchange for §8.
- **[flagged]** Formal at-will status and any standard equity/bonus component of Meridian's engineer package. We **assume** no equity or ownership interest (memo §10).

Why it matters: *Marsh USA Inc. v. Cook*, 354 S.W.3d 764 (Tex. 2011), is Meridian's best Texas authority precisely because there **was** consideration tied to a protectable interest (stock options). If Meridian's engineer package includes equity, *Marsh* gets materially stronger on the ancillarity branch — but it still does not help against §16600 in a California forum, which is where this case lands.

**Q3. What did Zhao actually work on — named accounts, roadmap programs, pricing?** — **[flagged]**

Bears only on how far a Travis County court could reform §8 under §15.51(c). On our reading of the text that section reaches unreasonable time, geographic-area and scope limits and cannot supply a missing ancillary basis (our reading, not a library holding), and §15.51(c) bars damages for breach before reformation, so the upside is limited no matter the answer.

**Q4. Does Meridian have any evidence (access logs, downloads, device transfer) that Zhao took confidential material?** — **[answered]**

- **No.** Meridian has **no evidence** Zhao took anything, and its stated objection is "**the competing itself**."

Consequences we act on: there is no covenant-independent claim (trade secret / breach of §10) to bring today; a 24-month, nationwide bar on being *employed by* a competitor with no misappropriation evidence is the definition of a naked restraint, which is the worst posture under §15.50(a)'s requirement that the restraint "do not impose a greater restraint than is necessary"; and Zhao's written assurance ("I will honor my confidentiality obligations and won't take anything with me") stands unrebutted in the file.

## C. Strategy questions

**Q11. Is Meridian's real objective (a) stopping Zhao, (b) deterring other engineers from going to Kestrel, or (c) protecting specific technology or accounts?** — **[answered]**

- "**It's the competing itself we object to.**"

That answer is decisive and it is not favorable. Objective (a)/(b) — suppressing competition as such — is what §16600 forbids in California and what §15.50(a) does not protect in Texas ("do not impose a greater restraint than is necessary to protect the goodwill or other business interest of the promisee"). Objective (c) is the only one the law assists, and Meridian has no evidence supporting it (Q4). It also means option (b) is self-defeating: a public Texas suit advertises the notice failure to the exact population with a §16600.5 claim.

- **[flagged]** Whether other departures to California competitors are planned.
