# Proposed Form 8594 allocation — Lone Star (Buyer A) asset purchase, $3,100,000.00

Counter to Buyer A's Exhibit A. Under IRC §1060 and Treas. Reg. §1.1060-1 (`irc_1060_form_8594.md`), consideration in an applicable asset acquisition is allocated by the **residual method in class order I–VII, each class up to fair market value before the next class receives anything**. Both parties must file Form 8594 and report consistently with any written allocation agreement; and the written allocation binds the parties (*Danielson* rule, per `irc_197_and_noncompete.md`). So the only safe place to fix Buyer A's draft is **in the LOI/PSA before signing**.

## Proposed allocation

| Class | Asset | Buyer A draft | **Proposed** | Δ |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| III | Accounts receivable | $200,000.00 | **$200,000.00** | — |
| IV | Inventory (materials) | $150,000.00 | **$150,000.00** | — |
| V | Equipment & vehicles | $900,000.00 | **$650,000.00** | −$250,000.00 |
| VI | Covenant not to compete (5 yr) | $400,000.00 | **$0.00** | −$400,000.00 |
| VII | Goodwill & going concern (incl. trade name) | $1,450,000.00 | **$2,100,000.00** | +$650,000.00 |
| | **Total** | $3,100,000.00 | **$3,100,000.00** | — |

Residual check: $3,100,000.00 − $200,000.00 − $150,000.00 − $650,000.00 − $0.00 = **$2,100,000.00** to Class VII.

## Justification by class

- **Class III — receivables, $200,000.00.** Face value, no reserve per the tax-history file. FMV = face; allocated at face.
- **Class IV — inventory, $150,000.00.** Carrying value $140,000; Buyer A's own draft ascribes $150,000 of value — we accept it (a small $10,000 premium, ordinary either way).
- **Class V — equipment & vehicles, $650,000.00 (FMV cap).** The company's fixed-asset register values all equipment and vehicles at FMV **$650,000** (CNC $230,000; edgebander $95,000; spray booth $90,000; dust collection $30,000; trucks $135,000; misc $70,000). §1060's residual method permits allocation to a class only **up to FMV** before the next class takes the residual (Treas. Reg. §1.1060-1, per `irc_1060_form_8594.md`). Buyer A's $900,000 exceeds FMV by $250,000 with no identified support; because these are all depreciable personalty with adjusted basis $240,000 and $860,000 of depreciation taken (recomputed basis $1,100,000 > any realistic allocation), every allocated dollar above basis is §1245 **ordinary income** to the seller (`irc_1245.md`). At the engagement rates (37% ordinary vs 20% LTCG, `capital_gain_rates.md`) the $250,000 over-allocation costs Ray $250,000 × (37% − 20%) = **$42,500.00** and merely accelerates the buyer's depreciation. If Lone Star produces a credible appraisal above $650,000, we will move to it — and price the character shift.
- **Class VI — covenant not to compete, $0.00.** A covenant in connection with the sale of a business is a §197 intangible **amortizable by the buyer over 15 years** (`irc_197_and_noncompete.md`) — and so is Class VII goodwill (`irc_1060_form_8594.md`); Lone Star's amortization benefit is **identical** whether the $400,000 sits in VI or VII, while for the seller a covenant allocation is **ordinary income** versus capital for goodwill (Rev. Rul. 69-643; *Muskat*, per `irc_197_and_noncompete.md`). Under the economic-reality principle in the same authority, an allocation must reflect what the parties are actually buying: Lone Star is buying the business; the 5-year covenant is the standard protection that accompanies buying it (Hensley obtains a customary 3-year covenant at **no separate consideration** — proof such covenants are not separately priced here). Ray now confirms he is **retiring** and will sign the 5-year covenant for the same total price (`documents/email_ray.txt`): a retiring seller poses little competitive threat, so his covenant has weak stand-alone value, the economic reality of the $3,100,000 is payment for the going concern, and the $0 Class VI allocation is the allocation that matches what is actually being sold — which also lowers the risk that the IRS re-cuts the deal under the same authorities. Reallocation: $400,000 × (37% − 20%) = **$68,000.00** saved, $0 cost to buyer. If Ray genuinely provides post-closing consulting, that is services compensation for a **separate services agreement priced at market outside the purchase price**, not purchase-price consideration (services compensation is ordinary whatever Exhibit A calls it).
- **Class VII — goodwill, $2,100,000.00 (residual).** The going-concern value is where the residual lands once each lower class is capped at FMV. Zero basis in self-created goodwill **[unverified in the closed library — treated as settled practice]**; long-term capital gain.

Seller tax effect of the counter vs. Exhibit A as drafted: total gain is unchanged at $2,520,000 ($3,100,000 − $580,000 aggregate bases); the mix moves from $1,070,000 ordinary / $1,450,000 capital to **$420,000 ordinary / $2,100,000 capital**, saving $250,000 × 17% + $400,000 × 17% = **$110,500.00**. §1374 exposure is unchanged ($237,300 — both allocations leave equipment gain above the $230,000 conversion-BIG cap and goodwill gain above the $900,000 cap; `irc_1374.md`).

## Fallback positions (conceding in this order, never past this)

1. Class VI $100,000 (max) only if Lone Star adds ≥ $17,000 to price to cover the 17-point character cost.
2. Class V above $650,000 only supported by an appraisal and only with price increased ≈ $17 per $100 shifted (each shifted dollar moves from 20% capital to 37% ordinary; §1245 applies "notwithstanding any other provision," `irc_1245.md`).
3. Non-negotiable: no allocation signed above FMV support, and both parties initial the final Exhibit A and file consistent Forms 8594 (`irc_1060_form_8594.md`).
