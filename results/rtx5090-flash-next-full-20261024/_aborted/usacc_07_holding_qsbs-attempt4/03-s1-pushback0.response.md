## You're asking the right question — and the advisor's sentence is wrong, twice over

The proposal does say it in exactly those words (`documents/advisor_proposal.pdf`): *"when Helio exits, the gain lands in the holding so you can reinvest without tax."* On the five authorities I'm allowed to cite, that fails for two independent reasons. Either one alone is fatal.

**1. The contribution destroys the exclusion years before the sale.**
§1202(c)(1) requires that the taxpayer acquired the stock "**at its original issue**." Holdings would get the shares from *you*, not from Helio. §1202(h) then lists the only transfers that preserve the character — "**by gift, at death, or in a distribution from a partnership to a partner**" — and the library states expressly that a contribution of stock to a partnership is **not** on that list and "the stock ceases to qualify in its hands." So the shares stop being QSBS on the day you contribute them. On your own $9–10M estimate, that one signature gives back **$8,988,000–$9,988,000**.

**2. Even good QSBS inside an LLC doesn't stay there.** "Gain lands in the entity, reinvest without tax" describes a **corporation**. The library's own wording is the opposite: §1202(g) talks of gain from QSBS held by a partnership "**flowing to a partner**" — you pay in the year the entity sells. And §1202(g) adds a condition your plan is built to fail: gain flows to a partner "only if the partner held its partnership interest **when the partnership acquired the stock** and at all times thereafter."

His §721 line is a true statement about the wrong event: §721(a) covers the *contribution*, not the later *sale*.

## Can the holding own it after the sale?

- **The stock — no.** After closing there's no Helio stock left; and in a merger, moving shares into Holdings before the exchange is again a transfer not listed in §1202(h).
- **The cash proceeds — yes, and only that.** The exclusion is fixed when *you* sell stock *you* own. After the money reaches you, contributing cash to Holdings is a §721(a) contribution — no gain. Four conditions: **(1)** only after closing, never a pre-arranged route through the entity (`step_transaction.md` — get this wrong and the whole $9–10M is at risk); **(2)** it buys no further shelter — as drafted (you 99% / your own revocable trust 1%) Holdings most likely has one owner and is disregarded anyway, so it's bookkeeping and liability structure, not tax; **(3)** watch non-cash consideration — **rollover equity in the acquirer** is the one item that can destroy the exclusion on the rolled part; **(4)** don't bundle in the Properties contribution — that's the §752(b)/§731(a) trap ($1.9M mortgage, negative tax capital, ~$380,000 of gain at a 99% transfer on the pessimistic basis reading).

If the real goal is getting shares to family or a trust, §1202(h)'s "**by gift, at death**" route does work with a tacked holding period — but the donee-cap question goes to the §1202 specialist before anything is signed.
