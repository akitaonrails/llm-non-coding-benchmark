**1. Redwood does not owe $210,000. It owes $125,000 — and the $210,000 came from our own books error.**
The March 12 letter's figure is exactly the *gross* of the six ledger invoices:
48,200 + 62,400 + 41,300 + 6,900 + 36,800 + 14,400 = **210,000**
with no credit for the **$85,000 Redwood already paid** in February (MS-7701 paid in full Feb 10: 48,200; MS-7740 partial Feb 27: 36,800). 210,000 − 85,000 = **125,000**. So the letter — the one that violated the stay — demanded money that had *already been paid*. That's in §6 of the CEO memo.

**2. Nobody is taking back "money for steel we delivered." Two separate buckets:**

- **The unpaid steel (MS-7740 balance, MS-7788, MS-7815, freight, services = $125,000)** — that stays a claim *in Redwood's favor of us*: a proof of claim, with **$78,100 of it upgraded to priority** as §503(b)(9) goods received in the 20 days pre-filing. No one claws back a claim.
- **The preference bucket** is the money Redwood *already paid us* during the 90 days before it filed (window opens Dec 10): we received **$180,000**. Bankruptcy law lets the estate ask for back payments received in that window while the debtor was insolvent if the payments were *atypical* — the theory is equal treatment of unsecured creditors, not fairness of the deal (library: `bc_547.md`). Yes, the steel was delivered. That isn't the issue; timing and normality are.
