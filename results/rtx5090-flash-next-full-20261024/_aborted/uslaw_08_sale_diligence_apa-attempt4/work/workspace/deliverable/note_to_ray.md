## 1. The 1099 installers — the biggest number with your name on it

You told us: "The installers have been 1099 forever, everyone in the industry does it," and "they
like it." We hear you. But two things are separate:

- **The legal question.** Your own data room (§2.2) describes the nine installers as "working
  exclusively for Brightline, using Brightline trucks and tools, scheduled daily by the shop
  foreman, paid hourly ($34–41/hr), for an average of 3.4 years." The reference library says that
  exact fact pattern makes people **employees under the common-law control test regardless of the
  label** (`library/worker_classification_tx_irs.md`: IRS Rev. Rul. 87-41 20 factors; Treas. Reg.
  §31.3121(d)-1; IRC §3509; Tex. Lab. Code §201.041; 40 TAC §815.134). "Everyone in the industry
  does it" and "the workers like it" do **not** appear in the library as defenses —
  **[unverified — not in library]** — so do not offer either to the buyer as a defense.
- **The drafting question.** As drafted, the APA routes that problem to you personally after
  closing:
  - **§4.12** makes you *represent* that everyone is properly classified and all taxes were paid.
    On your own data room's facts, that rep is not true as written.
  - **§7.4(b)** caps §4.12 breaches at the **full purchase price — $3,100,000**, not the $310,000
    general cap. Money beyond the $310,000 escrow (3,100,000 − 310,000 = **$2,790,000**) would
    come from you directly, not just out of escrow.
  - **§7.4(c)** says the buyer's **knowledge changes nothing** — even though we will have told
    them about this in diligence, they can still claim against you. The library is blunt: a seller
    who discloses the classification issue and then gives a clean rep under this kind of clause
    "has effectively agreed to indemnify for the disclosed issue" (`library/sandbagging.md`).
  - **§7.4(d)** keeps §4.12 alive to the **statute of limitations plus 60 days** — likely years —
    while the escrow releases at **18 months** (§9.3). The claim outlives the escrow; it comes
    after you personally. (The limitations period is not in the library — **[unverified — not in
    library]** — Q10 asks your CPA to confirm it.)

**What the problem probably actually costs (arithmetic shown, all flagged):** employer FICA
7.65% × $612,000 of 1099 pay = $46,818/yr × 3 = $140,454; FUTA 9 × $7,000 × 0.6% = $378/yr × 3 =
$1,134; overtime premium for 5–10 OT hrs/wk at $34–41/hr over 12/20/26 busy-season weeks =
$3,060–$15,990. Three-year scenarios: **$144,648–$157,578**; adding a 50% allowance for the items
the library lists but doesn't quantify (SUTA, §3509 gross-up, penalties) gives ≈ **$236,400**.
The 7.65%/0.6% rates, the 3-year window, and the busy-week count are assumptions your CPA must
confirm (Q10). Your confirmed 45–50 busy-season hours fit the pay data: $68,000 average annual
pay ÷ $37.50/hr midpoint = 1,813 hrs/yr = 33–50 hrs/wk over a 40–50-week year — busy-season-heavy,
consistent with what you told us. Nothing here was forced.

**The point:** the real bill is roughly $150,000–$240,000. The draft lets the buyer claim up to
$3,100,000 for it — about **13×–21×** the realistic number (3,100,000 ÷ 236,367 ≈ 13.1; ÷ 144,648
≈ 21.4). The library calls a full-price cap on a worker-classification rep **"off-market"** and
says to replace it with a **sized special indemnity** (`library/baskets_caps.md`). That is exactly
our ask: an exception on Schedule 4.12, or a special indemnity capped at **$[250,000], escrow-only**
(the bracket is our proposed number, not the buyer's).

## 2. Hensley Homes — 38% of your revenue, and the buyer wrote "consents: None."

The Hensley MSA (§18) says neither side may assign it "whether by operation of law, merger, sale
of assets, change of control or otherwise" without prior written consent, "which may be withheld
in its sole discretion," and that any attempted assignment "is void." The library says clauses like
that are enforced as written, and "sole discretion" hands Hensley leverage to renegotiate
(`library/anti_assignment_change_of_control.md`). Buyer's Schedule 3.3 currently reads **"None."**
That is false on the face of your own data room, and we will not sign it.

The money: 0.38 × $4,600,000 = **$1,748,000** of revenue; on two independent valuation bases the
Hensley relationship is worth ≈ **$1,178,000** of the $3,100,000 price (both bases converge — math
in `apa_redline.md` §3.3). You told us the owner is a long-time personal friend, **has not been
told about the sale**, and "won't love a corporate buyer." So:

- **You call the owner first, person to person.** Our redline (§3.3(d)) blocks the buyer from
  contacting Hensley on its own before that happens. Do not let anyone from Lone Star call ahead
  of you.
- If Hensley refuses and the deal closes anyway without the MSA, expect the buyer to demand a
  **price reduction — our fallback range $[500,000]–$1,178,000** — or to walk, and either way your
  **earn-out goes to zero** on the math in item 3.
- We will also schedule Cascade (consent-free, notice-only — data room §1.2), the 22 residential
  contracts (data room §1.3), and the shop lease (§3.1; landlord consent "not unreasonably
  withheld") on Schedule 3.3. Small items, but they belong there.

## 3. The non-compete — the heaviest thing *you personally* are signing

§8.2 as drafted, verbatim: **"Seller and Ray Brightline shall not compete in the custom cabinetry
business within 500 miles of Austin for 7 years."**

You said: "I'll sign whatever non-compete, I'm retiring." Retirement is your plan; the signature
is still yours, and the library says 7 years / 500 miles for a local custom-cabinet shop is
**likely excessive in both dimensions**, with 3–5 years within the trade area typical
(`library/tx_noncompete_sale_of_business.md`). Our counter: **5 years** (fallback 3), scoped to
Travis County, contiguous counties, and counties where Brightline actually worked in the last 24
months; it should bar **owning or operating** a competing shop, not hiring you as an employee; and
it needs a **consulting carve-out** in case "retiring" turns into consulting. The library also
warns that an over-broad covenant invites IRS scrutiny of any consideration allocated to it — and
the file contains **no allocation schedule at all** (Q6), so we cannot even say what that covenant
is paying you.

And the earn-out you may be counting on is mostly an illusion: §2.4 pays 40% of EBITDA **above
$700,000 for the 12 months following Closing** (not calendar 2026), max $400,000. The maximum
requires $700,000 + $400,000 ÷ 0.40 = **$1,700,000** of EBITDA — 1,700,000 ÷ 740,000 = **2.30×**
your adjusted 2025 figure. Flat at $740,000 the payout is 0.40 × ($740,000 − $700,000) =
**$16,000**; on the unadjusted books ($518,000) it is **$0** — and §2.4 lets the **buyer** compute
EBITDA "in accordance with Buyer's accounting policies." Realistic value of the earn-out:
**$0–$16,000**, and if Hensley is lost there is no path to the floor: even keeping every add-back,
$740,000 − $281,200 (Hensley's margin-consistent EBITDA share) = $458,800, below $700,000. The
redline rewrites §2.4; that rewrite matters more than the headline percentage.

## So — can we just sign it?

**Not as drafted.** Signing today's draft means: (1) you *represent* your installers are properly
classified when your own data room reads the other way; (2) you agree that breach of that rep is
capped at the **full $3,100,000**, not the $310,000 escrow; (3) you agree the buyer's **knowledge
of the problem protects you zero** (§7.4(c)); and (4) that claim can arrive after the escrow is
gone. That is how a roughly $150,000–$240,000 known issue becomes a $2,790,000-beyond-escrow
problem. Separately, the Hensley consent has **never been sought** and it is the critical path of
the deal; the spray-booth permit (data room §4.1) is non-transferable and takes 30–60 days.

**Signing in two weeks is realistic** if the §3.3 / §4.12 / §7.4 fixes in our redline are agreed
now, with you opening the Hensley conversation immediately. **Closing** in two weeks is not
realistic — consents and the permit gate that, and the timetable should be built around them
(Outside Date in the redline). Nothing in the redline is a deal-breaker for the buyer; every ask
is the library's own prescription for exactly these problems.
