## 1. Schedule 3.3 — it cannot be "None"

You wrote that you left Schedule 3.3 blank because "we understand there are no consent issues."
The data room does not support that. Schedule 3.3 must be completed before signature, with:

**Item 1 — Hensley Homes Master Supply Agreement §18 (data room §1.1). Consent required; we treat
it as a closing item, not an efforts-only line.**

- Hensley is the largest customer at **38% of revenue** (`0.38 × $4,600,000 = $1,748,000`), term
  2024–2028.
- §18 bars assignment "by operation of law, merger, sale of assets, change of control or otherwise"
  without prior written consent, "which may be withheld in its sole discretion," and makes any
  purported assignment **"void."**
- The library: such a clause "is enforced according to its terms; an asset sale transferring the
  contract without consent is a breach and the transfer is void where the clause so provides," and
  a "sole discretion" consent "gives the counterparty leverage to renegotiate price"
  (`library/anti_assignment_change_of_control.md`: *SQL Solutions v. Oracle*, 1991 WL 626458
  (N.D. Cal.); Restatement (Second) of Contracts §317, §322).
- Our redline therefore: (a) Schedule 3.3 lists Hensley; (b) Hensley's written consent (or an
  agreed carve-out with a conforming price adjustment) is a **condition to both parties' obligation
  to close**; (c) consent outreach is **seller-led** — the seller has the relationship and manages
  the approach, and our §3.3(d) draft bars unilateral buyer contact with Hensley before the seller
  has made the introduction; (d) an Outside Date governs the consent window. We ask Buyer to
  confirm in writing that it will take the MSA **on its existing commercial terms through 2028** —
  that confirmation is what makes the consent obtainable.
- Status: consent has **not yet been requested**. The seller will initiate that outreach directly,
  in a sequence the seller controls, and we will report each milestone to you. Consent is not
  guaranteed — which is precisely why the closing-condition structure, the Outside Date and the
  fallback carve-out mechanism must be in the document before signature, not improvised after.

**Item 2 — Cascade Builders framework agreement (data room §1.2; 11% of revenue ≈ $506,000).**
Assignment "permitted to a successor to substantially all assets upon notice." List it on
Schedule 3.3 with the notice as a pre-closing deliverable. This one is easy; we list it so nobody
pretends it doesn't exist.

**Item 3 — the 22 residential contracts (data room §1.3), "silent on assignment."** The library:

**Item 4 — shop lease, 7700 Burleson Rd (data room §3.1; lease §22).** Landlord consent required

A draft that assigns "all assets … including the Assigned Contracts" (§3.1) while Schedule 3.3

## 2. WARN — confirmed, no notice required

Per the library (`library/warn_act.md`: 29 U.S.C. §2101(a)(1); 20 C.F.R. §639.3): WARN applies to
employers of **100 or more employees** and to closures/mass layoffs of **50+ employees at a single
site**. Brightline has **14 W-2 employees** (data room §2.1: 7 shop, 4 installers, 3 office). Even
counting the nine 1099 installers as employees — we take no position on classification for this
answer — the total is **23**, far below both thresholds. **No WARN notice is required**, and the
library confirms **Texas has no mini-WARN statute**. Buyer's post-closing hiring decisions are
Buyer's own WARN question going forward; as to this transaction, you are under threshold.

## 3. §7.4(c) and the classification package — our answer to "it's market"

You wrote: *"7.4(c) is market, every deal has it. And 4.12 is a plain-vanilla compliance rep — if
Ray's contractors are fine, what's the concern?"* Three responses:

**(a) We do not dispute the label; we dispute the combination.** Our redline **keeps §7.4(c)**. The
problem is that §7.4(c) sits between a **clean §4.12 rep** and the **§7.4(b) carve-out** that caps
§4.12 at the full Purchase Price. The library states the consequence precisely: "Sellers who
disclose a known issue (e.g., contractor classification) in diligence and then give a clean
representation subject to a pro-sandbagging clause **have effectively agreed to indemnify for the
disclosed issue**. The seller's protection is either (i) an anti-sandbagging clause, (ii) a
disclosure schedule exception to the representation, or (iii) a special indemnity with a negotiated
cap — **not a clean rep**" (`library/sandbagging.md`). We are offering exactly (ii) and (iii). If
7.4(c) is "market," then so is the library's market summary on caps: ~10% for general reps, and a
specific operational rep like worker classification carved out to a full-price cap is
**"off-market and should be resisted or replaced with a sized special indemnity"**
(`library/baskets_caps.md`). Your draft pairs an "every deal" clause with a clause the library
calls off-market. Pick one.

**(b) §4.12 is not untrue because we say so; it is untrue on Buyer's own data room.** The concern
is not hypothetical. Data room §2.2 — compiled by your deal team's diligence list — describes nine
installers "working exclusively for Brightline, using Brightline trucks and tools, scheduled daily
by the shop foreman, paid hourly ($34–41/hr), for an average of 3.4 years," with "no written
contractor agreements." The library's classification memo says individuals who work "exclusively
for one business, on its schedule, with its tools and vehicles, paid hourly, for years" are
employees under the common-law control test **regardless of the label**, and flags successor
exposure and IRC §3509 consequences (`library/worker_classification_tx_irs.md`: Rev. Rul. 87-41;
Treas. Reg. §31.3121(d)-1; IRC §3509; Tex. Lab. Code §201.041; 40 TAC §815.134). A "plain-vanilla"
rep cannot be squared with a data room that reads like the library's fact pattern; asking the
seller to rep it clean while carving it to full price and keeping pro-sandbagging converts a
known, disclosable issue into an uncapped bet against the seller.

**(c) The numbers, so the ask is calibrated.** Our scenario table (`apa_redline.md` §4.12):
employer FICA 7.65% × $612,000/yr = $46,818/yr, × 3 years = $140,454; FUTA 9 × $7,000 × 0.6% =
$1,134; FLSA overtime premium on 5–10 OT hrs/wk × $34–41/hr × 0.5 over 12/20/26 busy-season weeks
= $3,060–$15,990. Three-year totals: **$144,648–$157,578**; with a 50% allowance for the items the
library cites but does not quantify (SUTA, §3509 gross-up, penalties, interest) ≈ **$236,400**.
(Rates and the 3-year window are flagged assumptions — the library cites IRC §3509 and the TWC
provisions but not the rates or the limitations period **[unverified]**.) Against that, §7.4(b)
sets the cap at $3,100,000 — **$2,790,000 beyond the escrow**, roughly **13×–21×** the realistic
exposure (3,100,000 ÷ 236,367 ≈ 13.1; ÷ 144,648 ≈ 21.4). And §7.4(d) keeps the rep alive to the
limitations period + 60 days while the escrow releases at 18 months (§9.3).

**Our position, concretely:**
1. Preferred: **Schedule 4.12 exception** disclosing the data room §2.2 facts; §4.12 then subject
   to the general $310,000 Cap; §7.4(c) stays as you drafted it.
2. Fallback: §4.12 stays clean, but (i) a **new §7.5 special indemnity for classification/tax
   exposure capped at $[250,000], escrow-only**, survival tied to escrow release; (ii) §4.12 back
   inside the general Cap for everything else; (iii) if Buyer keeps pro-sandbagging, the special
   indemnity is the *only* route for classification Losses.
3. Either way, "Fundamental Representations" must be **defined** — §7.4(b) caps them at the
   Purchase Price but the circulated sections never define the term; an undefined exception to the
   Cap is worse than a defined one.
