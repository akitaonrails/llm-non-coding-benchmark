**Biggest open items: Q1 (the price), Q2 (installer hours), Q3 (Fundamental Representations),
Q4 (your post-closing work plans), Q5 (the personal guarantee). We assumed rather than stalled —
every assumption is flagged prominently in the deliverables — but items 1–5 change dollar figures
or positions materially.**

---

**Q1. Purchase price and the definition of "Purchase Price."** No dollar amount appears anywhere in
   the data room or the drafted APA sections. We derived **$3,100,000** from the buyer's own §9.3
   caption — "$310,000 (10%)" escrow → $310,000 ÷ 0.10 = $3,100,000 — and used it for every cap and
   multiple. (a) Is $3.1M right? (b) Does the full, ungroomed APA define "Purchase Price" to
   **include** the earn-out and the §8.2 non-compete payment? If those are inside "Purchase Price,"
   the §7.4(c) cap is above $3.1M and the §7.4(b) cap math moves too. If the escrow percentage is
   not exactly 10%, restate the price and we re-run every number.

**Q2. Installer hours (data room §2.2).** Actual average weekly hours per 1099 installer, and the
   hourly vs. per-job split of the $34–41/hr rates. This is the single fact that decides how big
   the misclassification exposure is — and it is also the one number in the file that does not
   reconcile ($68,000 ÷ $34–41/hr implies 1,659–2,000 hrs/yr ≈ 33–40 hrs/wk; a 40-hr week at the
   midpoint already pays $75,000 > $68,000). If hours were ≥40/week, the numbers are inconsistent
   and we need the real ones. If hours were ≤40/week, the overtime layer of our exposure estimate
   drops toward $0.

**Q3. The definition of "Fundamental Representations."** §7.4(c) caps fundamental-rep breaches at
   $310,000 — *less* than the general cap, which makes no commercial sense and is probably a
   drafting error. We are asking the buyer to fix it (redline §7.4(c)) and we need your position.
   (b) Also: does Schedule 3.1 actually except the fundamental reps from the survival-period
   brackets, and is §3.1 a full rep schedule we never received?

**Q4. Your post-closing work plans.** The §8.2 non-compete binds you personally for two years,
   everywhere Brightline does business, and sweeps in "Affiliates" including your spouse's and
   children's LLCs. (a) Do you intend to keep working in cabinetry in any capacity? (b) Is anyone
   in the family planning to continue the trade? If the answer is yes, the covenant as written
   stops them too — tell us before we agree to its scope.

**Q5. The leased shop — personal guarantee (data room §4.1).** Is the Lone Star Lease personally
   guaranteed by Ray or anyone else? We asked the buyer to flag whether it terminates on assignment
   (reply item 7), but we need your own answer too: if the lease is personally guaranteed, the
   45-day Lessor notice and any Hensley-related friction are personal exposure, not just deal
   exposure.

**Q6. Purchase price allocation.** Does an allocation schedule exist (goodwill, equipment,
   non-compete, inventory)? Without it we cannot say what the §8.2 payment is worth relative to
   market or whether it shifts tax character between you and the buyer.

**Q7. Austin permit — interim operation (data room §4.2).** Does the City of Austin let the buyer
   operate the showroom before the change-of-use permit is issued? The file does not say. If not,
   the permit is a practical closing condition and the two-week signing target becomes a two-week
   signing, later closing.

**Q8. Residential contracts naming Brightline personnel (data room §4.3).** Do any of the ~200
   Schedule 3.3 contracts name Ray or specific employees for performance? If yes, the buyer will
   want those names waived or re-papered.

**Q9. Data room inconsistency — §2.3 vs. §§5.1/5.2.** §2.3 says "No pending claims or disputes."
   §5.1 is the Hardaway payment dispute/lien notice ($9,800) and §5.2 logs 11 open warranty repair
   quotes (~$23,000). Which description is current? We scheduled all of them defensively.

**Q10. How far back does the payroll-tax exposure go?** Three years was our illustrative window for
    the misclassification estimate ($268,150) because the limitations period for the relevant
    employment taxes is not in the reference library **[unverified — not in library]**. Confirm the
    real lookback period (and ask your CPA) so we can size the special-indemnity fallback properly.
