## 1. Schedule 3.3 — it cannot be "None"

You wrote that you left Schedule 3.3 blank because "we understand there are no consent issues." The data
room does not support that. Schedule 3.3 must be completed before signature with the following:

**Item 1 — Hensley Homes Master Supply Agreement §18 (data room §1.1). Consent required. This is a
closing condition, not an efforts item.**

- Hensley is the largest customer at **38% of revenue** (`0.38 × $4,600,000 = $1,748,000`), contract term
  2024–2028.
- §18 bars assignment "by operation of law, merger, sale of assets, change of control or otherwise" without
  prior written consent, "which may be withheld in its sole discretion," and provides that "any purported
  assignment in violation of this Section is void."
- That is the clause form that is enforced according to its terms; an asset sale transferring the contract
  without consent is a breach and the transfer is void where the clause so provides, and a consent
  withholdable in sole discretion gives the counterparty leverage to renegotiate price
  (`library/anti_assignment_change_of_control.md`: *SQL Solutions v. Oracle*, 1991 WL 626458 (N.D. Cal.);
  Restatement (Second) of Contracts §317, §322).
- Our draft therefore: (a) Schedule 3.3 lists the Hensley Consent; (b) obtaining it is a condition to both
  sides' obligation to close, with an outside date; (c) if **you** elect to close without it, you waive all
  Article VII claims arising from its absence. We are not prepared to give a representation that no consents
  are required, and we do not agree that an indemnity is a substitute for the consent — the general cap of
  $310,000 (10% of the $3,100,000 price implied by §9.3) does not come near the value at stake.
- Because this is a sole-discretion consent, the two-week signing target is not achievable if the consent is
  a closing item. We propose starting the Hensley approach this week, with your confirmation to Hensley that
  you will assume existing commercial terms through 2028.

**Item 2 — Shop lease, 7700 Burleson Rd, §22 (data room §3.1). Landlord consent required; standard is "not
unreasonably withheld."** Term runs through 2029, so you need the premises. List it, with the reasonableness
standard noted, and tell us whether you want a condition here too. If the lease is personally guaranteed by
Ray Brightline, we additionally need a landlord release — we do not have the lease in the extract, so we
cannot confirm.

**Item 3 — Cascade Builders framework agreement (data room §1.2). No consent; notice only.** Assignment is
permitted to a successor to substantially all assets upon notice. Please list it on Schedule 3.3 as a
notice item and confirm that Schedule 3.1(c), which has not been provided, in fact transfers substantially
all assets of the Business so the notice condition is satisfied. `0.11 × $4,600,000 = $506,000`.

**Item 4 — City of Austin spray-booth permit (data room §4.1). Not a consent — a reapplication.** The permit
is issued to "Brightline Cabinetry LLC," is non-transferable, and a new owner must reapply (30–60 days per
the data room). This needs its own covenant on your side: Buyer files, Seller cooperates. It also puts the
closing date at issue; whether operations may continue while the application is pending is **[unverified —
not in the library]** and we will confirm with the city.

**Item 5 — Twenty-two residential contracts under $40,000 each (data room §1.3). No consent expected.**
They are silent on assignment; absent anti-assignment language assignment of rights is generally permitted,
and obligations need consent only if performance is personal (`library/anti_assignment_change_of_control.md`:
Restatement §317, §322). Aggregate ceiling `22 × $40,000 = $880,000`. Schedule them in Schedule 3.1(c) and
we will assign at Closing. Flag to us now if any of them names Brightline personnel for install or finish
work.

We also note §2.2 and §5.1–5.2 of the data room do not reconcile ("No pending claims" against the Hardaway
Builders dispute and the warranty log). That is being corrected on our side, and the corrected version will

## 2. WARN — confirmed, no notice is required, and it is not the exposure

You asked us to confirm headcount is under threshold, and said you are not planning notices.

**Confirmed.** Census: **14 W-2 employees** (7 shop, 4 installers, 3 office) and **9 individuals paid on
Form 1099** as installers (data room §2.1, §2.2). Even if every one of the nine were counted as an employee,
that is **14 + 9 = 23**.

- WARN applies to employers with **100 or more employees** (excluding part-time) and to plant closings or
  mass layoffs affecting **50 or more employees at a single site**; the library states that a 14-employee
  business is **not** a covered employer and that no WARN notice is required even if the buyer does not hire
  everyone, and that **Texas has no mini-WARN statute**
  (`library/warn_act.md`: 29 U.S.C. §2101(a)(1); 20 C.F.R. §639.3).
- Reclassifying the nine installers would not change the answer: 23 is under 100, and 23 is under the
  50-employee single-site trigger.
- So: no seller WARN notice is required, whether or not you hire the full workforce, and we are content to
  give a factual representation to that effect (headcount; not a covered employer). We are not giving an
  open-ended "compliance with all applicable law" warranty.

**Two cautions, so nobody is surprised later.** First, the WARN answer is clean precisely because of the
headcount; it says nothing about the classification question in data room §2.2, which is a payroll-tax and
wage-and-hour issue, not a WARN issue. Second, post-Closing WARN obligations are yours as the employer; we
will ask you to indemnify for pre-Closing employment matters as scoped in §7.5 of our markup, and nothing in
our WARN answer transfers any of that.

## 3. §7.4(c) — we cannot accept it as drafted

You described §7.4(c) as "the market-standard 'knowledge' language." As drafted it is a **pro-sandbagging**
clause: your right to indemnification is unaffected by knowledge acquired "at any time, whether before or
after" signing, "or capable of being acquired." That is the opposite of a knowledge qualifier: it preserves
recovery *despite* knowledge. Nothing in our library supports the "market standard" characterisation either
way, and the library does not state the default rule where the agreement is silent — **[unverified]** — so
we have to settle it in the text rather than argue about defaults.

Why we are refusing it in combination with your §§4.12 and 7.4(b):

1. You already have the facts. Data room §2.2 discloses nine installers paid on 1099, working exclusively
   for Brightline, on Brightline's trucks and tools, scheduled daily by the shop foreman, hourly $34–41,
   average 3.4 years, no written agreements — $612,000 in 2025 payments.
2. §4.12 as drafted asks Ray to represent that everyone is properly classified and all required taxes were
   paid. On your own reading of the data room, that representation is not accurate.
3. §7.4(b) carves §4.12 out of the general cap and caps it at the **entire purchase price** —
   `$310,000 ÷ 0.10 = $3,100,000`, i.e. **10.0× the escrow** — while §7.4(d) keeps it alive to the statute of
   limitations plus 60 days, long after the escrow is released at 18 months
   (`$3,100,000 − $310,000 = $2,790,000` outside escrow).
4. Result as drafted: Ray indemnifies you, without a real cap, for a problem you identified in diligence
   yourself. That is the specific combination the library warns sellers about — a seller who discloses a
   known issue such as contractor classification in diligence and then gives a clean representation subject
   to a pro-sandbagging clause "have effectively agreed to indemnify for the disclosed issue," and the
   seller's protections are an anti-sandbagging clause, a disclosure-schedule exception, or a special
   indemnity with a negotiated cap — **not a clean representation** (`library/sandbagging.md`:
   Delaware/Texas practice; *Akorn v. Fresenius* (Del. Ch. 2018) (dicta); ABA Private Target Deal Points
   Study). Separately, a specific operational rep such as worker classification carved out to a full-price
   cap is described as off-market and to be replaced with a sized special indemnity
   (`library/baskets_caps.md`).

**Our position, in order of preference:**

- **Preferred:** §4.12 given subject to a Schedule 4.12 exception for the nine installers, §7.4(c) replaced
  with anti-sandbagging (no Claim for facts disclosed in the data room or on a Schedule, and no recovery to
  the extent Buyer had actual pre-signing knowledge of the facts), and §4.12 back inside the 10% cap.
- **Acceptable:** keep §4.12 clean but add §7.5 — a **Worker Classification Special Indemnity** limited to
  pre-Closing classification of those nine individuals, subject to the $75,000 deductible basket in §7.4(a),
  with a negotiated cap sized to the exposure, no double-counting against the general cap, and recovery only
  from the existing escrow. If you want that indemnity, tell us the number you are underwriting and we will
  look at it: our own illustrative three-year figure, using rates our library does not state and hours the
  data room does not disclose, is roughly **$268,000** (employer FICA $612,000 × 7.65% × 3 = $140,454;
  FUTA $378 × 3 = $1,134; overtime premium at 45 hrs/wk = $42,188 × 3 = $126,562) — before penalties,
  interest, Texas unemployment contributions and any §3509 gross-up.
- **Not acceptable:** a clean §4.12, plus §7.4(b) full-price cap, plus §7.4(c) as drafted, with no schedule
  and no cap. That is not a representation; it is an unpriced guarantee of a disclosed condition.

One further point on §2.4: if you intend to measure the earn-out on the same EBITDA basis you are pricing
off, put the accounts in a schedule. EBITDA "as determined by Buyer under Buyer's accounting policies"
permits overhead, management-fee, integration and purchase-accounting charges to zero the earn-out
(`library/earnout_definitions.md`; *Lazard Technology Partners v. Qinetiq*, 114 A.3d 193 (Del. 2015)).
Arithmetically the clause as written pays **$16,000** on flat 2025 performance and needs EBITDA of
`700,000 + 400,000 ÷ 0.40 = $1,700,000` — 2.30× 2025 — to pay the $400,000 maximum. Neither of us should
pretend that number is consideration.
