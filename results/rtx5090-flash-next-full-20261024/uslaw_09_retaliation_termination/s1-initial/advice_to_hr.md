## Your answers, and what they mean

1. **"Tom wrote the PIP and Tom wants her out. I'm just processing it."**
   The manager Dana accused is the decision-maker seeking her termination. That is the
   causation link a retaliation case needs (`title_vii_704a.md`: statements and role of
   decision-makers). "Just processing" does not insulate you or the company — you are now a
   key witness and a document custodian.
2. **"We haven't approved or denied it... Tom said to wait."** (re: FMLA)
   Deliberately sitting on an FMLA request at the direction of the man who wants her out
   before leave *restrains her attempt to exercise an FMLA right* — unlawful on its own,
   separate from any termination (`fmla_2615.md`: interference includes restraining "the
   exercise of or the attempt to exercise" a right).
3. **"Just her. No RIF."**
   The OWBPA consideration period for any release is **21 days** (the 45-day group-program
   rule is not triggered — `adea_owbpa_626f.md`).
4. **"Tom's been traveling. I was going to get to it."** (re: the complaint)
   That is 24 days (09-12 → 10-06) with no investigation. The duty is to investigate
   promptly and impartially, via someone outside the accused's chain of command
   (`harassment_investigation_duty.md`).

## Why "at-will" doesn't save this

Employment is at will in Columbia, **but not terminable for an unlawful reason** —
discrimination, retaliation, or because someone exercised a statutory right
(`columbia_at_will_exceptions.md`). Two protected acts sit immediately before the proposed
termination:

1. **She filed a written harassment complaint on 2026-09-12** against the Regional VP,
   Tom Hale. An internal complaint is legally protected activity (`title_vii_704a.md`).
2. **She requested FMLA leave on 2026-09-30** (to care for her mother) and sent the medical
   certification on 2026-10-03 (`fmla_2615.md`).

Very close timing (days to a few weeks) can itself prove causation in a retaliation case
(`title_vii_704a.md`). Four and six days are about as close as it gets.

## The email is the case

Hale's 10-02 email is direct evidence: a decision-maker stating in writing that the
termination is timed around her FMLA leave. Under the library, terminating an employee
**to prevent her from taking requested leave is FMLA interference**, the leave cannot be
a "negative factor" in employment actions, and she doesn't even have to have started the
leave yet (`fmla_2615.md`). Your confirmation that "Tom said to wait" on the leave decision
adds a second, ongoing interference act. If the email is deleted or lost, spoliation
sanctions and an adverse-inference jury instruction are on the table (`litigation_hold.md`).

## What she could win

  (`fmla_2615.md`).
- **Title VII/state law:** Columbia state claims mirror federal law but have a **longer
  filing window and uncapped compensatory damages** (`columbia_at_will_exceptions.md`).
- **The release won't protect you:** with this confirmed as an individual termination, the
  ADEA requires **at least 21 days to consider** (form gives 7 — short by 14), **at least 7

  the severance and sue anyway** (`adea_owbpa_626f.md`; details in
