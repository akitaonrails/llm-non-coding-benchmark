## Why "at-will" doesn't save this

Employment is at will in Columbia, **but not terminable for an unlawful reason** —
discrimination, retaliation, or because someone exercised a statutory right
(`columbia_at_will_exceptions.md`). Two protected acts sit immediately before the proposed
termination:

1. **She filed a written harassment complaint on 2026-09-12** against the Regional VP,
   Tom Hale. An internal complaint is legally protected activity (`title_vii_704a.md`).
2. **She requested FMLA leave on 2026-09-30** (to care for her mother) and sent the medical
   certification on 2026-10-03 (`fmla_2615.md`).

## Addendum — answering Lena's follow-up questions (same day)

**Tom says "she's at-will and we have a PIP, so we're covered." He is wrong on both halves.**
- At-will in Columbia means no unlawful reason — "termination may not be for an unlawful
  reason (discrimination, retaliation, exercise of statutory rights)"
  (`columbia_at_will_exceptions.md`). The reason for this one is written down by the
  decision-maker himself: *"before she's out on leave."* At-will does nothing for a termination
  whose stated motive is a statutory right.
- The PIP is not a shield; on these facts it is **her best evidence**: written by the man she
  accused, 4 days after her complaint, the first written performance document in 7 years,
  targeting 100% quota while peers at 79%, 84% and 86% were never PIP'd. Thin justifications
  applied selectively are read as **pretext** (`title_vii_704a.md`). Also note the arithmetic:
  a 60-day PIP from 09-16 ends ~11-15, but the plan is to terminate ~11-10 days into it,
  "before she's out on leave." A PIP used as a countdown clock rather than a cure program
  looks like a prop, not a process.

**"He wants it done before she goes on leave."** That sentence, if repeated in any email,
text, or meeting notes, **is** the FMLA interference case. Terminating an employee to prevent
her from taking requested leave is interference; leave cannot be a "negative factor"; she
doesn't even have to have started the leave (`fmla_2615.md`). The 10-02 email already does
this in writing.

**"If we wait, aren't we stuck with her forever?"** No. Two things at once:
1. FMLA is not tenure. At-will employment continues; the statute prohibits using the taking
   or requesting of leave as a negative factor (29 C.F.R. § 825.220(c), via `fmla_2615.md`) —
   it does not immunize an employee from a genuinely performance-based discharge, even during
   leave.
2. But a defensible future termination requires everything the current one lacks: a decision
   maker with no connection to Hale; a performance record built prospectively with documented
   coaching (not the quarantined PIP); the same standard actually applied to Brennan (79%),
   Osei (84%) and Park (86%); and completion of the harassment investigation so the record
   isn't "complainant out the door." On the current numbers, no such case may exist — she
   outperforms three of four peers. **Do not backfill paper to justify a decision already
   made; shifting justifications are pretext evidence** (`title_vii_704a.md`).
   (Her reinstatement entitlement on returning from leave is not addressed by this library —
   UNVERIFIED.)

**"Can we at least do it right after her leave?"** Not on this record, and not "right after."
- A discharge timed immediately after protected leave is the classic retaliation timeline;
  closeness of days-to-a-few-weeks can itself prove causation (`title_vii_704a.md`), and a
  decision consciously held until leave ends makes leave the operative factor
  (`fmla_2615.md`).
- The taint here is not only timing — it's the **decision-maker**. Tom Hale is the accused,
  the PIP author, and the author of the 10-02 email. No amount of waiting cures a decision
  made by him. If Tom is still choosing, November is as bad as October.
- If after the investigation and leave decision you still have a real, independent
  performance case, it must be made by someone else, on fresh documentation, applied
  consistently, and with enough distance from the leave and the complaint that a jury isn't
  handed a proximity shortcut. The library gives no safe waiting period — UNVERIFIED;
  proximity is judged case by case (`title_vii_704a.md`). Any agreement used then must
  follow the corrected terms (21 days, 7-day revocation, written attorney advice).
- Meanwhile: approve/deny the FMLA request on its merits, immediately and without Hale.
  Deciding her leave correctly is also the strongest evidence that leave was never the factor.
