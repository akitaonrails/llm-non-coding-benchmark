## 2. What survives the operating agreement, and what does not

### 2.1 Does NOT survive: "breach of fiduciary duty"

OA §7.3 eliminates all fiduciary duties (care and loyalty) "to the fullest extent permitted by the Delaware Limited Liability Company Act." 6 Del. C. §18-1101(c) authorizes exactly that: duties "may be expanded or restricted or eliminated by provisions in the limited liability company agreement," subject only to the implied covenant. A straight loyalty/self-dealing claim will be dismissed. Say this to the client plainly: the case must be re-labeled, not abandoned.

### 2.2 Survives: express contract duties the Manager wrote for himself

- **§5.4 affiliate-transaction conditions.** Affiliate deals are permitted *only if* (a) terms "no less favorable … than … arm's-length," and (b) transactions paying over $50,000/yr are **disclosed in writing to all Members within 30 days**. The Castellan fees ($180k–$240k/yr asset management + 6% of gross rents; `bank_affiliate_fees.csv`) blew through (b) — the Manager's own produced minutes show **no written disclosure** — and (a) is contestable once market comparables are obtained. These are contract terms; §7.3's fiduciary waiver does not touch them.
- **§6.3 call mechanics.** The call must be for "a bona fide Company purpose set forth in the call notice," delivered **"by certified mail and email at least 30 days before the due date."** The 2026-08-20 call went **by text message**, with **no certified mail**, and the email **bounced** to an address on the Company's records that has been dead since 2019 — the Company itself kept the stale address on file. Client confirms: texted a PDF on 2026-08-20, no letter ever arrived. (Timing was fine: 2026-08-20 → 2026-09-20 is 31 days.) Under *Zimmerman v. Crothall*, dilution provisions are enforced as written, but **the caller must strictly comply with notice mechanics and stated purpose — a non-compliant call does not trigger dilution.** This is the single most powerful point in the case: it is objective, documentary, and independent of any valuation fight. **No Dilution Event ⇒ she is still an 18% member ⇒ the §9.2 buyout election has no trigger.** Counter-argument to expect: she *read* the text (actual notice), so the Company will plead substantial compliance. The answer is that §6.3 specifies the delivery method expressly — certified mail **and** email — and the bounced email to the Company's own file address cannot be "delivered … by email" as the section requires; strict compliance is precisely what *Zimmerman* demands.

### 2.3 Survives: the implied covenant (the only duty §7.3 cannot kill)

§18-1101(c)–(e): the agreement **may not eliminate the implied covenant**, and may not exculpate a **bad-faith violation** of it; §7.3's own proviso concedes this. Where the contract vests discretion — "fair value … as determined by the Manager **in good faith**," capital calls "**for a bona fide Company purpose**" — the covenant requires honest, non-arbitrary exercise (*Nemec*; *Gerber*; *Dieckman* — misleading process violated the covenant despite exculpation). *Zimmerman* expressly makes a manager's "good faith" fair-value call reviewable where the value is far below a contemporaneous independent indication.

Constraint to state plainly (*Nemec*): the covenant is a gap-filler and **cannot override express terms**. If the Dilution Event somehow stands, the "Book Value" buyout price is an express term and the covenant will not simply re-price it. The covenant attack therefore rides **through the trigger** (was there a valid Dilution Event? was the discretion exercised honestly?), not around the price formula.

### 2.4 Direct vs. derivative and demand (*Tooley*; §18-1001–1003)

- **Direct** (her own pocket): invalid dilution; bad-faith valuation; §5.4 disclosure breach (a right running personally "to all Members"). These she controls personally; recovery/damages go to her.
- **Derivative** (the Company's pocket): the excessive affiliate fees — the classic example in the library. Recovery would flow to the Company (82% of the benefit back to Bram himself). Demand futility is readily pleaded: the sole Manager *is* the interested counterparty (*Zuckerberg* context; §18-1001).
- Strategic sequencing matters: the derivative fee claims **lift Company value** but only help her personally if her **percentage interest and exit price are resolved first** (18% vs. 6.1% of any recovery).

### 2.5 Limitations (10 Del. C. §8106 — 3 years; Chancery applies it by analogy via laches)

Fee payments (asset management) and the corresponding §5.4 disclosure defaults accrue each Sept 1 / Oct 1:

| Payment | Amount | Status if suit filed ~Nov 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| 2022-09-01 | $180,000 | time-barred absent tolling |
| 2023-09-01 | $195,000 | time-barred absent tolling (accrual 2023-09/10, just outside) |
| 2024-09-01 | $210,000 | timely |
| 2025-09-01 | $225,000 | timely |
| 2026-09-01 | $240,000 | timely |
| **Total** | **$1,050,000** | **$375,000 (35.7%) at risk without tolling** |

**Tolling (updated on client's answers):** the expected barred portion is now **$0**. The fees were masked: the Company sent Imani **one-page summaries labeling the payments as undifferentiated "management expenses,"** and she saw the true payee and amounts **only in 2026, after obtaining bank statements through a books-and-records demand**. That is the concealment-based tolling the library expressly supports for the undisclosed §5.4 fees (and "inherently unknowable" injury), with the labeling of affiliate payments as generic "management expenses" as the affirmative-concealment hook. Accrual should therefore run from the 2026 discovery. Fallback if a court finds earlier constructive knowledge: $375,000 (2022–2023) outside 3 years if suit is filed ~Nov 2026. Caveat from the library: *equitable tolling on the fiduciary rationale is weaker because §7.3 eliminated fiduciary duties* — argue concealment/breach-of-disclosure-duty, not fiduciary reliance. The dilution/buyout claims (Sept–Oct 2026) are fresh; no limitations issue.

## 3. The numbers (shown; nothing forced to balance)

**The offer:** 6.1% × $3,100,000 (2025 year-end members' equity) = **$189,100**.

**Independent indication (broker, 2026-10-05):** EV $14.5M → equity ≈ $9,800,000.
- 18% × $9.8M = **$1,764,000**
- 6.1% × $9.8M = **$597,800**
- Offer ÷ $1,764,000 = **10.7%**; Offer ÷ $597,800 = **31.6%**

**The Manager's own valuation:** $7.2M vs. broker $9.8M → broker is **1.36×** the Manager's figure; $2.6M gap.

**The 6.1% does not reconcile.** Using the Manager's own inputs (FV $7.2M; $1.5M funded — Bram's $1.23M + Imani's $270k share):
- Value-weighted pro rata: 18% × 7.2 ÷ (7.2 + 1.5) = **14.9%**
- Counting only Bram's $1.23M: 18% × 7.2 ÷ (7.2 + 1.23) = **15.4%**
- Capital-accounts on book: (0.18 × 3.1) ÷ (3.1 + 1.5) = **12.1%**
- To reach 6.1% at FV $7.2M, new capital ≈ **$14.05M**; or at $1.5M new capital, FV ≈ **$0.77M**.

**Leave open, do not force:** the file contains no calculation that yields 6.1%. Demand the Manager's math (and the full OA — we hold only excerpts). The irreproducibility is itself evidence of arbitrariness.

**Unquantified:** the "6% of gross rents" stream (no rent figures produced) and the treatment of Bram's $270,000 payment of her share (loan to the Company vs. additional contribution to Bram). Both go on the document list.

## 4. Recommended path

### Litigate (in Delaware Chancery, per §11.2 forum clause)

File a verified complaint now (or a standstill/tolling agreement immediately) seeking:

1. **Declaratory judgment** — the 2026-08-20 call failed §6.3's certified-mail-and-email requirement and bona-fide-purpose requirement; no Dilution Event occurred; Imani remains an **18%** member (*Zimmerman*).
2. **Injunction** — bar the §9.2 buyout at Book Value / rescind the election as trigger-less; alternatively bar consummation pending the valuation dispute.
3. **Implied covenant** — arbitrary/bad-faith exercise of §6.3 valuation discretion (the irreproducible 6.1%; the below-market value; the scheme sequencing: undisclosed self-dealing fees → sudden call she cannot fund → buyout at book) (*Dieckman*; *Zimmerman*; §18-1101(e)).
4. **Breach of §5.4 disclosure covenant** (direct) and **§5.4 arm's-length proviso** (derivative, demand futile) — disgorgement/surcharge of the above-market fees to the Company, up to $1.05M plus the 6% rent stream, with concealment tolling for 2022–2023.
5. **Plead fiduciary duty only as a fallback count**, expecting dismissal under §7.3/§18-1101(c). Do not tell the client "we win by proving he's looting" — under this OA the wins come from **notice mechanics, express covenants, and the implied covenant**.

Speed matters: (i) the offer lapses ~**2026-10-31**; (ii) every month of delay erodes the tolling story and, past the payment anniversaries, nothing new is lost but nothing is recovered either — the 2022/2023 fees live or die on concealment tolling.

### Negotiate (in parallel — this is where the case actually lands)

- **Reject** $189,100. Counter at **$1,764,000** (18% × broker equity), anchored on the notice defect: "the dilution is void; price my 18%."
- Realistic settlement corridor on current facts: **~$600k** (6.1% at broker value — her number if she loses the dilution fight but wins a re-valuation) to **~$1.76M** (dilution void, broker value credited). A 25–40% minority discount and litigation risk are the pressure pushing toward the middle; Chancery remedies (invalidation, disgorgement, bad-faith damages) push toward the top.
- Non-price terms worth taking: company buyback at fair value **with** fee-disgorgement and clean books, or a structured/instalment payout (she is cash-constrained; lump-sum discount is expensive).
- If she wants out cleanly and quickly, an acceptable floor given the strengths here should not be below **~$900k–$1.1M** without something close to certainty on the dilution point — but that is a judgment, not a rule; see risks.

### Funding (client cannot fund $270,000; realistically ~$60,000 for legal fees)

~$60k does not fund a full contested Chancery case with dueling valuation experts — assume such a case runs well beyond that without contingency participation. So spend it where it wins:

- **Phase 1 (weeks, low five figures):** (i) written rejection of the $189,100 offer **with full articulation of the §6.3 notice defect**, before 2026-10-31; (ii) demand for the Manager's dilution arithmetic, the full OA, the Castellan management agreement, rent rolls, and 2022–2026 financials — **she already holds the bank statements from her 2026 books-and-records demand; produce and tabulate the "6% of gross rents" payments from them, they quantify the second fee stream**; (iii) a standstill/tolling agreement so the buyout cannot close while talks run.
- **Phase 2:** a **lean verified complaint** confined to the documentary claims — declaratory judgment (no Dilution Event, §6.3 strict compliance) + injunction against the Book-Value buyout + implied-covenant count on the irreproducible 6.1%. This is largely a documents-and-construction case: no experts needed to kill the dilution. Filing (or even service of the draft complaint) converts the fight from "will a cash-poor member sue?" to "is the 6.1% real?"
- **Phase 3 (only if settlement fails):** the derivative fee-overcharge claims (up to $1.05M + the rents stream) — pursue with contingency/hybrid counsel or as leverage in the exit negotiation; **no fee-shifting provision appears in the OA excerpts and no fee-recovery authority is in the library — [unverified]; do not budget on getting fees from the other side.** A books-and-records statutory inspection right is likewise **[unverified] in this library** — note as fact that Imani already obtained bank statements via a books-and-records demand in 2026 (the basis/procedure of that demand is not in our file), and rely from here on pre-suit demands and, post-filing, normal Chancery discovery.

If Bram's counsel is rational, Phase 1 alone may collapse the buyout: the notice point is objective, the 6.1% is irreproducible, and the fees were masked in her summaries — a bad-faith narrative with documents. That is the leverage that makes the counter credible.

## 5. Risks, stated plainly

1. **The dilution point is strong but not magic.** She read the text (her words: "assumed it wasn't real notice"), and the 31-day timing was met, so the Company will plead actual notice/substantial compliance. If the court accepts that, dilution stands and she is arguing only about the 6.1% math and the $7.2M value — upside collapses toward the $597,800 zone.
2. **"Bona fide purpose" cuts partly against the sham-call theory:** Bram actually funded the full $1.5M, so "working capital and opportunities" is not obviously pretextual. The clean point is delivery mechanics, not purpose.
3. **Nemec limit:** if the Dilution Event stands, Book Value pricing is an express term; the implied covenant cannot rewrite a bargained formula. Her leverage must run through invalidating the trigger or proving bad-faith exercise.
4. **Tolling is now strong but still contested work.** The "management expenses" summaries and the 2026 books-and-records production give a real concealment case, so the 2022–2023 fees ($375,000 of $1.05M) should survive; residual exposure is a constructive-knowledge ruling (summaries *did* show "management expenses" yearly — the Company will argue she should have asked). The library flags that fiduciary-rationale tolling is weakened by §7.3 — plead concealment and breach of the §5.4 disclosure duty, not reliance on fiduciary duty.
5. **Fee claims need market evidence we do not yet have.** Whether 6% of gross rents + $180k→$240k exceeds arm's-length terms is unproven on this file; without comparables the §5.4 pricing claim is speculative.
6. **Derivative recovery is partly illusory:** money paid back into the Company flows 82% back to Bram; it matters mainly as leverage and for the exit price.
7. **Costs and time.** She is cash-constrained; a contested Chancery case with valuation experts can outlast her. The strategy is built so the cheapest claim (notice defect) does most of the work.
8. **The broker opinion is one data point** (informal opinion, not a formal appraisal — ask for its workpapers); a court-appointed or dueling appraisal could land anywhere between the $7.2M and $9.8M equity figures, or outside them.
