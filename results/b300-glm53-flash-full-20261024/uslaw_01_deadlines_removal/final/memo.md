## 4. Removal analysis — why "no" (FINAL, on your confirmed roster)

### 4.1 The only ground on the table is diversity

The complaint pleads breach of contract and negligence (F4) — state-law claims all the way down; there is no federal-question removal path. §1441(a) therefore rides entirely on §1332(a)(1) (complete diversity + amount), and — because the ground is "solely on the basis of §1332(a)" — §1441(b)(2) applies. (28usc_1332_1441_1446_1447.md)

### 4.2 Amount in controversy — passes; no aggregation question even arises

$110,916.40 (contract) + $18,240.00 (negligence) = **$129,156.40**, exactly the prayer figure (F4). Two independent reasons AIC clears §1332(a)'s $75,000:
1. The complaint demands a **single judgment of not less than $129,156.40** for one plaintiff against one defendant.
2. Even without any aggregation, the contract count alone ($110,916.40) exceeds $75,000.
Prejudgment **interest and costs are excluded** by §1332(a)'s own terms; attorney fees would only add. ($142,316.40 invoiced − $31,400.00 paid = $110,916.40 — see §7 reconciliation.) **AIC is not the problem. Citizenship is.**

### 4.3 Plaintiff — a Washington citizen, once

§1332(c)(1): a corporation is a citizen of **every state of incorporation** and of its **principal place of business**. Sound Cascade: Washington + Kent, Washington → **Washington only** (complaint ¶1). It is not a dual-state citizen.

### 4.4 Defendant — the LLC membership decides (currently fatal)

*Johnson*, 437 F.3d at 899: "like a partnership, an **LLC is a citizen of every state of which its owners/members are citizens**" (llc_citizenship_cases.md); *Carden* same, and one **non-diverse member destroys complete diversity**. So:
- Delaware (state of formation) — **irrelevant** to an LLC's citizenship;
- Portland (principal place of business) — **irrelevant**;
- "registered to do business in Washington" — relevant to being **sued/served** (and F6 is exactly that), but **not to citizenship**;
- operational footprint (Seattle bakery) — also not itself citizenship.

The members decide. Your Sept 28 reply (F10) answers this directly: **two members, no entities — Caleb Sorensen (35%), who lives in Seattle, Washington, and Noor Haddad (65%), who lives in Portland** (Oregon). Sorensen's Seattle residence is a Washington affiliation on the defendant side. The narrow technical wrinkle: citizenship turns on **domicile** (residence *plus* intent to remain), which no library authority defines **[UNVERIFIED D]**, and you have told us (in substance) that you know of no other home for him. Treat him as a Washington citizen: not only is Seattle living powerful evidence of Washington domicile, but we have **no affirmative fact** supporting any other domicile, and a removal premised on "he might really be an Oregon/Texas domiciliary" would be filed on hope — exactly what §1447(c) fee-shifting punishes if it fails. So Harbor Street is a **Washington citizen** — the same citizenship as the plaintiff — and complete diversity fails: one non-diverse member is fatal. **→ No §1332 jurisdiction → no removal.** (You, by contrast, take Oregon citizenship — but a diverse co-member cannot rescue a case with a non-diverse member.)

### 4.5 Forum-defendant rule — the same fact bars removal a second time

§1441(b)(2): a case "removable **solely** on the basis of §1332(a)" may **not** be removed "if any of the parties in interest properly joined and served as defendants is a citizen of the State in which such action is brought." Forum state = Washington (the action is pending there). There is one defendant (Harbor Street) and it was properly joined and served (F6). With **any** member a Washington citizen, Harbor Street is a Washington citizen → the forum-defendant rule **independently bars removal** even if some diversity somehow existed. Your roster (F10) shows exactly that (Sorensen). The same single fact trips both wires; a roster showing **no Washington-domiciled member at all** would have cleared both bars at once and opened a narrow removal window (by Oct 13) — but that is not the roster you gave us.

### 4.6 Timing facts

- Clock started **Sept 11** (agent receipt, through service); window closed on rolls to **Oct 13** (§3.3).
- The Sept 27 late sighting does not restart it on any in-library theory (A5). The registered agent's forwarding to an old address is a business-process failure on your side, not a jurisdictional reset.
- If removal were sought and later challenged, the answering burden shifts to us; on a remand §1447(c) allows the court to impose "just costs and any actual expenses, including attorney fees" (28usc_1332_1441_1446_1447.md). Removing on an assumed roster that later shows a Washington member = paying the plaintiff's removal bill.
- Confirmed (F11): **nothing has been filed or served for you in the King County case**, so no procedural complication exists on the removal side (and none is needed — removal is off).

### 4.7 Conclusion

**`removal_available = false` — final, not provisional.** Your Sept 28 roster (F10) confirms the bar twice over: Sorensen (35%, Seattle) is a Washington citizen on the defendant side, defeating complete diversity (§1332(a)(1); *Johnson*/*Carden*) and tripping the forum-defendant rule (§1441(b)(2)) in the same fact. The October 13 deadline is moot — it never had to be beaten, because nothing can be honestly filed by then. The compressed timeline the paperwork created (agent receipt Sept 11 → your Sept 27 sighting) does not change that; it only compressed the removal window we can now comfortably ignore.

### 9.1 What the friend is quoting

"Different states = diversity" is right as far as it goes (§1332(a)(1)). But citizenship for an entity comes from a test that depends on **what kind of entity it is**:

- **Corporations** get the two-prong test: "a corporation shall be deemed to be a citizen of every State by which it has been incorporated **and of the State where it has its principal place of business**" — §1332(c)(1). The library adds: "*Section 1332(c)(1) applies to corporations; see the case law entry on unincorporated associations*" (28usc_1332_1441_1446_1447.md). For a corporation, the friend's instinct would be correct: the person who lives in Seattle **is not** relevant — only the entity's two prongs are.
- **Unincorporated entities — expressly including your LLC** — use a *different* test: "An unincorporated association — **including a limited liability company** — is a citizen of **every state of which its owners/members are citizens**; the §1332(c)(1) corporate test (state of incorporation and principal place of business) **does not apply**" (*Johnson v. Columbia Properties Anchorage, LP*, 437 F.3d 894, 899 (9th Cir. 2006); *Carden v. Arkoma Associates*, 494 U.S. 185 (1990)). *Johnson*'s own words: "**like a partnership, an LLC is a citizen of every state of which its owners/members are citizens.**"

The complaint fixes the entity type, not you or me: defendant "**Harbor Street Bakery LLC** is a **Delaware LLC**" (complaint ¶2; fact F3). So the corporate test — the only version of the analysis in which Delaware would count and Caleb would not — **never reaches us**.

### 9.2 "The company isn't him" — that is the corporate intuition, and it is exactly what *Carden* rejected for LLCs

For a *corporation*, the company truly isn't its people — §1332(c)(1) looks only at the charter and the principal place of business. For an *LLC*, the Supreme Court and the Ninth Circuit say the opposite: the entity has **no separate citizenship of its own to look at**; its citizenship **is** the collection of its members' citizenships, one by one. That is why your own **65% Oregon membership cannot help you** (a diverse co-member cannot cure a non-diverse one: "a single non-diverse member destroys complete diversity"), and why Caleb's membership is decisive even at **35%** — the membership test is not weighted; one member in the wrong state is enough.
