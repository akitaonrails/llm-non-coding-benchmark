## Answer to Imani's question

**Your articles are right — and that's exactly the problem for this claim.** Everything she read is true as the *default* rule: managers owe duties of care and loyalty. The entire point of [§18-1101(c)](workspace/library/implied_covenant_cases.md), however — the statute in our library — is that Delaware lets the *parties turn that default off in the contract*. Granite Hollow's operating agreement does precisely that, in §7.3 (quoting the exact text from his own document):

> "To the fullest extent permitted by the Delaware Limited Liability Company Act, the Manager shall not owe any fiduciary duties (including the duties of care and loyalty) to the Company or any Member, and the Members agree that the Manager's duties are solely those expressly set forth in this Agreement; **provided that nothing herein eliminates the implied contractual covenant of good faith and fair dealing.**"

Two things follow, and both run in her favor with honest caveats:

1. **A count titled "breach of fiduciary duty" is dead the moment it's filed.** Under §18-1101(c) the waiver is *enforceable* — the statute's express purpose, per the library file, is that elimination of fiduciary duties is not contrary to policy. His lawyer files one motion, it's granted, and the count that was supposed to "scare" him becomes the exhibit his counsel uses to tell him *she exaggerates*. A knowingly-eliminated claim also invites fee-shifting/sanctions argument **[unverified — not in this library]** and it burns contingent-fee dollars from a case where every dollar must be spent on the counts that can actually pay her.

2. **The loyalty charge does not disappear — it changes clothes and stays in the case, wearing forms his own contract preserved.** Read the proviso again: *"nothing herein eliminates the implied covenant."* He drafted a contract that keeps the one enforceable analog of loyalty alive, and [§18-1101(e)](workspace/library/implied_covenant_cases.md) makes that liability non-eliminable. So the complaint pleads every act of "self-dealing" she cares about — but under labels that survive his motions:
   - **§5.4 express count (fees):** Castellan Asset Services — his wholly-owned affiliate — took $675,000 within the limitations window (up to $1,050,000 with tolling), plus the monthly 6% of rents, with **no written disclosure**, in breach of his own contract's condition. The word *self-dealing* appears in those fact allegations, filed under a count he cannot knock out with a statute.
   - **§6.3 express count + implied covenant (*Dieckman*):** the call, the dilution, and the "fair value" determination are exactly the *discretionary* acts the library says the covenant polices — exercised honestly, not arbitrarily, not to strip her of the bargain's fruits. His $189,100 against $1,764,000 is the arithmetic of a bad-faith exercise, and it survives his waiver.
   - **Derivative disgorgement count:** this is the count that genuinely frightens a manager personally — his own production of bank statements, discovery into every fee, and money clawed back *to the company* whose value she still owns 18% of.

So the honest answer to "are you sure?" is: **yes as to form, no as to substance.** Yes — the fiduciary *label* is gone, and pleading it would mark the complaint rather than scare him. No — she is not giving up the *substance* of loyalty: every grievance she has is pleaded, under enforceable theories, against the same conduct. The complaint can lead with self-dealing facts on page one.
