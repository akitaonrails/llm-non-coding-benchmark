## 1. Bottom line

**Reject the $189,100 offer. Do not accept, and do not counter at any number below ≈$1.4M.** Counter in writing at a rescission-based price — restoration of her 18% at fair value, i.e. ±$1,764,000 on the broker opinion — with full reservation of rights, and file in Chancery if no acceptable response arrives within ~2–3 weeks. ## 3. Why the Dilution Event is invalid (the case should be built here)

1. **The recomputed 6.1% is arithmetically indefensible — even on the Manager's own $7.2M. (Lead ground after the client's 2026-10 answers.)** Proper pro-rata dilution at the Manager's asserted fair value: **0.18 × 7.2/8.7 = 14.90%** (new money at pre-money value) or **0.18 × 5.7/7.2 = 14.25%** (if $7.2M is post-money). To produce 6.1% (Bram 82% → 93.9%), the $1.5M raise must buy 66.1% of the post-money company, i.e. a post-money value of **$2,268,877** (implied pre-money ≈ **$768,877**) — one-third of the $7.2M the Manager himself asserted. Under §6.3 the fair-value determination is "in good faith"; under *Dieckman*, discretion must not be exercised arbitrarily or in bad faith to strip the counterparty of the bargain's fruits. A number that is internally inconsistent by a factor of ~3 is arbitrary on its face.
2. **Notice mechanics — objectively failed on the certified-mail prong, but weighed honestly.** §6.3 requires delivery "by certified mail **and** email at least 30 days before the due date." The call was a **texted PDF**: no certified mail was ever attempted (undisputed — the Manager's own production shows none), and the only email address on file has been dead since 2019. The library states dilution requires **strict compliance** with the specified delivery method; a call that fails it **does not trigger dilution**. **Candid risk:** the client received actual notice (she saw the texted PDF with the full 30-day window), and defendants will argue the defect caused no prejudice — she could not have funded $270,000 in any event. Preserve this ground, but do not stake the case on delivery mechanics alone.
3. **The $7.2M itself is low.** Independent broker (2026-10-05): enterprise value **$14.5M**, equity ≈ **$9.8M** — 36% above the Manager's $7.2M on the same figure (equity basis). Even at $7.2M, her stake is worth ≈**$439,200–$456,700** as diluted (6.1% is worth $597,800 at broker equity) — versus an offer of $189,100.
4. **The sequencing is a squeeze-out pattern** (call 30 days before a cash-constrained member → she cannot fund → dilution → book-value offer at 3.16× below broker equity). *Dieckman* squarely covers discretionary processes used to mislead/deprive the other side of the fruits of the bargain. This ground is medium-strength and needs discovery (what the $1.5M was actually spent on — "working capital **and opportunities**" is an indefinite purpose; §6.3 requires the bona fide purpose be set forth in the notice; use of proceeds is still unknown).
5. **Affiliate fees (separate track, derivative).** Castellan Asset Services (wholly Bram-owned) took $180k/$195k/$210k/$225k/$240k in asset-management fees 2022–2026 plus 6% of gross rents monthly, under a self-executed 2022-08-15 resolution **with no written disclosure to Members** (per the Manager's own production). §5.4 conditions affiliate transactions > $50,000/yr on written disclosure within 30 days. Liability on the disclosure prong is high on this record; quantum is the open fight (no evidence in the file whether the fee is above market — an arm's-length benchmark is needed). Recovery is **derivative** (fees were company money; demand is futile because the sole Manager is the interested party — *Zuckerberg* futility per the library).

## 5. Recommended path

**Step 1 — Demand letter (this week).** From contingent-fee counsel, to Bram/the Company: (a) reject the $189,100 offer with full reservation of rights; (b) demand rescission of the Dilution Event and reinstatement to 18%; (c) offer to negotiate a buyout at fair value (18% × independently appraised value); (d) 10-business-day response window. Resolution before the offer lapses (2026-10-31) preserves all leverage and avoids the cost of a contested opening posture.

**Step 2 — Litigate (Chancery, if the demand stalls).** File by the offer lapse window:
- **Count 1 (direct):** declaration that the Dilution Event is void, rescission, and restoration of the 18% interest — **two grounds pleaded together, honesty-ordered**: (a) **LEAD — the fair-value determination is arbitrary on its face** (§6.3 requires it be made "in good faith"; 6.1% back-solves to a $2,268,877 post-money value against the Manager's own asserted $7.2M — *Dieckman*); (b) **ALTERNATIVE — delivery noncompliance** (no certified mail ever attempted; §6.3 requires certified mail AND email; *Zimmerman* strict compliance), with the client's conceded actual notice (she saw the texted PDF) acknowledged in the pleading rather than left for cross-examination. In the alternative, recomputation at any honest fair-value figure: 14.25% at the Manager's own $7.2M; 18% if the $7.2M is itself displaced.
- **Count 2 (direct):** implied covenant — bad-faith/arbitrary fair-value determination and dilution arithmetic (*Dieckman*; §18-1101(e) preserves this liability notwithstanding §7.3).
- **Count 3 (direct, alternative):** implied covenant — squeeze-out orchestration.
- **Count 4 (derivative, no demand — futility vs. the sole interested Manager):** disgorgement/recovery of the undisclosed §5.4 affiliate fees: $675,000 in asset-management fees within limitations; up to $1,050,000 if concealment tolling holds; plus the unidentified 6%-of-gross-rents amounts. State plainly: this money goes to the Company; if Imani remains a member, she benefits ~18% of it ($121,500–$189,000) — worth pleading for leverage and for any ultimate fair-value buyout that internalizes Company assets, but **not** the lead claim.
- **Do not plead** fiduciary-duty counts (§7.3 waived them) and do **not** attack the §9.2 book-value price head-on through the implied covenant (*Nemec* forecloses overriding express terms).

**Step 3 — Funding.** Imani cannot fund the $270,000 call and is cash-constrained. She also has **no obligation to fund it** — §6.3's stated consequence of non-funding is dilution only, not liability (nothing in the excerpts or the library imposes more; the standard default until disproved). Litigation funding: retain contingent-fee counsel. **[unverified]** Delaware Chancery offers attorney-fee shifting only per common-fund/exceptional-cases doctrines, which are not in this library — do not bank on fee recovery. Note the cost/value skew: Count 1 is mostly a declaratory/dilution-legal issue needing little expert work; the fight is over the appraisal once the dilution falls.

**Step 4 — Negotiate what, from where.** Once the dilution is in doubt, Bram negotiates against himself: his leverage dies because the offer rests on a mechanism he cannot paper. Counter = restoration at 18% × real value. Realistic settlement zone is between **$597,800** (the absolute floor in the least favorable litigation outcome — diluted stake at real value) and **$1,764,000** (restored stake at broker value), with the "honest arithmetic at the Manager's own $7.2M" corridor of ≈$1.40M–$1.46M as the principled fallback. **Recommended floor before conceding the 18% restoration: ≈$1,400,000.** Any cash deal must be on a **true-fair-sale basis** (independent appraisal, not book value) and must settle the fee claims with credit to the Company.

## 8. Risks, stated plainly

1. **Actual notice / no-prejudice argument (former risks 1–2 merged; facts updated 2026-10).** The client saw the 2026-08-20 texted PDF with the full 30-day window and did not act; her email on file has been dead since 2019 and she never updated it. Defendants will argue (a) actual notice cured any delivery defect — no prejudice, since she could not have funded $270,000 in any event — and (b) her own stale address contributed to the email prong's failure. What we keep: the certified-mail prong failed **objectively and undisputedly** (never attempted, on the Manager's own production); the library states the rule is **strict compliance**; §6.3's "certified mail and email" is mandatory on its face. What we do not do: stake the case on delivery mechanics — the arithmetic ground leads and Count 1 is pleaded accordingly. Open fact: whether the full OA (the excerpts are partial) imposes any member duty to keep contact information current.
2. **Tolling on the fee claims — strengthened by the client's 2026-10 answers; residual risks stated.** Pre-2023-10-08 fees ($375,000: 2022 + 2023 payments, both made before the 3-year cutoff) are presumptively barred; recovery depends on concealment tolling per the library. **Strengthened:** until 2026 the client received only one-page summaries carrying a generic "management expenses" line — the payee (the Manager's wholly-owned affiliate) was masked in the documents she was given; the affiliate identity and payment stream surfaced only in 2026 when her books-and-records demand produced bank statements. Masked counterparty + formal-demand diligence support inherent-unknowability, and the breached §5.4 30-day-disclosure duty supplies the affirmative basis for tolling. **Residual risks:** (i) the exact 2026 demand/response dates are unknown and set the limitations start point for the older payments; (ii) a court may still ask why she never inquired earlier into the "management expenses" line — the masked counterparty is the answer, but acknowledge the question; (iii) **QUANTUM remains unproven**: nothing in the file shows whether 6% of rents + $180–240k/yr is above or below market, no market benchmark exists anywhere in the library or the file, and the CSV shows the fees tracked the self-set schedule (180→195→210→225→240, i.e. +8.33%, +7.69%, +7.14%, +6.67% per year — no visible mischarging under the schedule itself). The fee claim's leverage is real; its dollar value is unproven.
3. **Valuation risk cuts both ways.** A formal appraisal could come in below the broker's $9.8M (or above it). The broker's $14.5M/$9.8M is an opinion, not an appraisal; book ($3.1M) vs. market ($9.8M) is a 3.16× gap that needs to be explained by appreciated real estate, and confirmation of any company debt is needed (a remaining unknown — the client offers no further information; see QUESTIONS.md).
4. **Cash burn.** Contingent-fee representation is the only realistic structure given her cash constraints. **[unverified]** Contingency percentages and Chancery cost expectations are market facts, not library facts — confirm with counsel.
5. **Bram-objective risk.** He may use the Company's funds to defend and to buy more value-laden assets; if the dilution is rescinded, his $270,000 "payment" for her share returns to him (treat as loan to the Company or refund; structure in the prayer for relief).
6. **Where the fee recovery lands.** Derivative recovery goes to the Company. If she is a member at settlement/close, her pro-rata benefit is ~18%. If she is bought out before that recovery materializes, she loses it — so any settlement should be struck **after** (or simultaneously with) resolution of the fee claim, or price it in.
