**1. Where the $16,500 comes from** (`form7203_instructions_excerpt.md`)

Your stock basis entering 2025, from your own note:

| Step | Running basis |
|---|---:|
| 2021 contribution | 10,000 |

The rule: losses are "allowed only to the extent of the shareholder's adjusted stock basis plus debt basis." The 2025 K-1 loss is 28,000, so allowed = the lesser of 28,000 and 11,500 → **11,500 allowed, 16,500 suspended** (carried forward indefinitely "until basis is restored"). Notice the real cause: the **17,200 of distributions you took in 2023–2024** consumed most of the income basis you'd built. Your friend's company may simply never have paid distributions — that alone explains a different result.

**2. Pike's guarantee counts for nothing — twice over**

Same excerpt: "**A guarantee of corporate debt does not create debt basis; only a direct loan from the shareholder to the corporation does.**" So:
- Even if *you* guaranteed the bank line, it wouldn't count;
- Here it's *Pike's* guarantee anyway, which adds nothing for your basis.

No direct loan from you to Reyes & Pike exists (your note: "I never lent the company money"), so debt basis = $0.

**3. The suspended loss is deferred, not lost**

It carries forward until basis is restored — by your share of future income or new capital contributions. Illustration (hypothetical, per the same excerpt): a 2026 K-1 income allocation of $16,500 with no distributions would rebuild basis to 16,500, and the carryover would then be deductible against it. So "suspended" means later-year deduction, not a permanent disallowance.

The library's language is mandatory ("allowed *only* to the extent of…"), not a conservative option, and `marcus_basis_note.md` leaves no basis I could add. Delivered figures stand: Form 7203 = 11,500 allowed / 16,500 suspended, and the $2,322 federal refund is unchanged.
