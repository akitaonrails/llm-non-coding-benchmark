## Why: the three problems, in calendar math

### 1. The email is the smoking gun (FMLA interference is *direct evidence*)

timeline.md records that Tom Hale emailed you on **2026-10-02**: *"Let's get this done before she's out on leave. Once she's on FMLA it's a nightmare to move on her."* That email came **2 days after** her leave request (2026-09-30) and **4 days before** this termination request.

The library file [library/fmla_2615.md] states three things that together do the legal work:

- Terminating an employee **to prevent her from taking approved or requested leave is interference** (§2615(a)(1)); an employer may not use "the taking of FMLA leave as a negative factor" (§825.220(c)).
- "An employee who requested leave **need not have begun it**" — her request alone triggered protection.
- It is unlawful to retaliate (a)(2) for exercising or attempting to exercise FMLA rights.

Hale's email says the quiet part in writing: the objective is to move on her **before** the leave starts. That is the definition of interference in the library. Compare the dates:

## What to do about the harassment complaint

1. **Open an investigation immediately.** The library [library/harassment_investigation_duty.md] requires a prompt, impartial investigation with corrective action once a complaint lands. Your inbox shows it landed **2026-09-12** and got "Tom is on the road; will circle back." Twenty-four days later, nothing — and the 10-06 follow-up confirmed **not one investigative step** has been taken.
2. **Staff it outside Tom Hale's chain of command.** The library says so explicitly — and here the conflict is worse than proximity: Hale is the accused *and* the PIP author *and* appears to be advocating termination. HR cannot run a credible investigation of the same person deciding Dana's fate.
3. **Preserve everything now.** Once litigation is reasonably anticipated — an internal discrimination complaint followed by a contemplated termination qualifies — documents, ESI, and executives' emails and texts must be preserved; non-preservation risks adverse-inference instructions or sanctions [library/litigation_hold.md]. That includes Hale's 10-02 email, texts, and the acknowledgment trail from 9/12.

## What to do about the FMLA request

1. **Answer it, in writing, now — and do not wait for Tom Hale to release you.** She requested leave 2026-09-30 and its certification was submitted 10-03. Per the 10-06 follow-up, the request remains neither approved nor denied, and the hold exists because *"Tom said to wait"* — the accused's instruction, not a merits-based hold. Silence reads as denial and hands her an interference claim on top of the email.
2. **Process the eligibility and certification determination on the merits** and, if she qualifies, approve the leave. Using "the taking of FMLA leave as a negative factor," or terminating to beat the leave to the door, is interference per §825.220(c), and she need only have requested leave — beginning it is not required [library/fmla_2615.md]. *(Note: the library file states the §2615(a) prohibitions and the §825.220 regulation, but does not set out FMLA eligibility criteria (e.g., service or hours thresholds); the FMLA-status question is answered — see the 2026-10-06 follow-up above — and HR's eligibility determination itself remains to be made on the merits.)*
3. Whatever the ultimate performance merits, **separate the time streams**: decide performance after the leave request is resolved and the investigation is done — not before, not overlapping, not "before she's out."
