# Corrected Separation Agreement Terms (for any future agreement)

**Client:** Whitcomb matter (Employee #1187) — company-form Separation Agreement and General Release
**Prepared:** 2026-10-06 · **Authority relied on:** only files in `library/INDEX.md`; anything beyond the library is labeled *unverified*.

> **Threshold point before any term-by-term fix:** the company form as provided fails 29 U.S.C. §626(f)(1) in at least four independent ways. An ADEA release that fails these requirements is unenforceable against ADEA claims, and the employee may keep the severance and sue anyway (*Oubre v. Entergy*) [library/adea_owbpa_626f.md]. The form was reviewed from an excerpt containing only paras. 3, 4, 7, 9, and 11; the full text must be re-checked by counsel before any use (open item QUESTIONS-1). **Timing confirmed:** the client stated on 2026-10-06 that the contemplated action is a single-employee termination ("Just her. No RIF"), so this memo's timeline uses the **21-day** consideration minimum (the 45-day group-program provision does not apply).

## Timeline consequence of the corrected terms (arithmetic)

With compliant terms, even an employee who signs on day one cannot produce an effective, irrevocable release sooner than the statutory minimum:

- 21 days to consider + 7 days to revoke = **at least 28 calendar days** from offer to an effective release. Worked example: an offer made **2026-10-08** runs its 21-day consideration window through **2026-10-29**; even if she signs that day, the 7-day revocation period expires **2026-11-05** at the earliest.
- Therefore "terminate today and paper it by this week" cannot work with a lawful ADEA waiver under [library/adea_owbpa_626f.md].

## What the current form would buy if actually used today

Nothing as to ADEA claims: age-42 employee (OWBPA applies), waiver fails (F), (G), (C), (E) as shown above — so she could accept the 4 weeks, keep it, and still sue (*Oubre v. Entergy* [library/adea_owbpa_626f.md]).

- Retaliation/interference mechanics could not be waived retroactively: the claims arise in part **after** any execution date (e.g., accrued post-termination exposure, including the FMLA's liquidated (double) damages) [library/fmla_2615.md].
- State-law exposure with uncapped compensatory damages and a longer limitations period remains unless the state release independently satisfies Columbia requirements — unverified in the library [library/columbia_at_will_exceptions.md].
