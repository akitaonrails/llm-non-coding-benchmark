Marcus, I get why it feels arbitrary — here's the walk, and your buddy's deal almost certainly isn't the same shape as yours.

**Your basis at January 1, 2025 = $11,500.** Form 7203 makes you add up every dollar you left in the company and take out every dollar it paid you (Form 7203 instructions, library §3 — income first, then distributions):

| Year | Started | K-1 income/(loss) | Distributions | Ended |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2021 | 10,000 contribution | (2,100) | 0 | 7,900 |
| 2022 | 7,900 | 6,400 | 0 | 14,300 |
| 2023 | 14,300 | 9,800 | (12,000) | 12,100 |
| 2024 | 12,100 | 4,600 | (5,200) | 11,500 |

The 2025 loss is **$28,000**, and a shareholder's loss is allowed "only to the extent of adjusted stock basis plus debt basis" — you had $11,500. So $11,500 is deducted now; **$16,500 suspends and carries forward indefinitely** until basis is restored. It is not lost — 2026 income, a fresh capital contribution, or an actual loan *from you* would start releasing it.

**Why not Pike's guarantee.** The Form 7203 instructions in our library are explicit: "A guarantee of corporate debt does **not** create debt basis; only a direct loan **from the shareholder** to the corporation does." Pike pledged his own credit to the bank; no money of yours went into the company, so there's nothing to hang a basis on. Your own note says you never lent the company money.

**Why your buddy could take his whole loss.** I can't see his file, but the likely differences: he had more basis entering the loss year — he never took distributions (you took out $17,200 in 2023–24, precisely what eroded your cushion), he has shareholder loans on the books, or his loss was smaller than his basis. One of those facts will be true for him and is false for you; the same $16,500-of-loss-over-$11,500-of-basis math applies to every shareholder, or his preparer simply carried basis wrong.

**One consolation already priced in:** the $11,500 allowed is doing maximum work — it cuts AGI dollar-for-dollar, and because it's a nonpassive loss, it also pulled your MAGI down $11,500, which *raised* your rental special allowance by $5,750 on Form 8582.
