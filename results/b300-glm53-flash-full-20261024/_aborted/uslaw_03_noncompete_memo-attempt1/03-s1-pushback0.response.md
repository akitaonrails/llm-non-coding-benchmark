## 2. "The FTC rule is dead, so what's the problem?"

**The FTC rule was never the problem — nor part of the solution.** It was vacated nationwide (*Ryan LLC v. FTC*), never took effect, and we relied on it for **nothing**; our memo cites it only to record that it's dead. Dana's observation is correct and completely costless.

Everything that creates your exposure is **California statute, in force, untouched by the FTC outcome**:

- **§16600** — voids §8, read broadly per *Edwards* ("no matter how narrowly tailored");
- **§16600.1 (AB 1076)** — makes *including* §8 in a California engineer's contract itself unlawful, and your February 14, 2024 notice deadline was missed company-wide (LO-233);
- **§16600.5 (SB 699)** — makes an enforcement *attempt* (filing or even a demand letter) a civil violation with **mandatory** fee-shifting to a prevailing employee, regardless of where or when the contract was signed;
- **Lab. Code §925** — voids the Travis County venue and Texas choice-of-law clauses at Zhao's election.

So the honest framing is the reverse of Dana's: the *dead* federal rule is the least of the problems — the *live* ones are all California's own. No filing strategy that ignores them improves Meridian's position, which is why the recommendation stays: don't file, don't send a §8 demand, hold Zhao to §10, and remediate the AB 1076 failure through counsel before it finds you.
