## The income story (why line 1 is not "the sum of the 1099s")

Dana asked to "just add up the 1099s" ($41,350 + $18,500 + $12,200 + $9,400 = $81,450). The bank does not support that number. The checking export (the only account, per Dana's email) contains exactly these credits: PayHub payouts **$40,150.85**, Tessaro ACH **$9,400.00** (2025), Tessaro ACH **$3,000.00** (12/27/2024, prior year), and Larkspur payroll **$19,502.80** ($40,150.85 + $9,400 + $3,000 + $19,502.80 = the statement's total credits of $72,053.65 — the export reconciles to the penny, so **no deposit is missing**).

- **PayHub 1099-K:** payouts equal 1099-K box 1a × 97.1% exactly ($41,350.00 − 2.9% × $41,350.00 = $41,350.00 − $1,199.15 = $40,150.85). So all $41,350 was processed and received in 2025; only the fees were retained. No overlap with any 1099-NEC (include-once rule in the Schedule C instructions excerpt is not triggered by this evidence).
- **Tessaro 1099-NEC $9,400** = the four 2025 ACH receipts (2,400 + 3,100 + 2,150 + 1,750). The separate 12/27/2024 receipt of $3,000 (INV 1041) predates the year — prior-year return.
- **OPEN ITEM — Beacon Fintech 1099-NEC $18,500 and Orchard Health 1099-NEC $12,200:** not one deposit from either payer appears in the account, and the export reconciles exactly, so none are missing from *this* account. Under the cash-method rule in the library these cannot be counted as received in 2025 on the current evidence. **Excluded from line 1.** If Dana confirms they were received (cash, another account, or constructive receipt), gross receipts become $81,450 and every figure below must be recomputed — Question 1 in QUESTIONS.md.
