## 7. Your three follow-up questions, answered (2026-10-25 follow-up call)

**"Tom says she's at-will and we have a PIP, so we're covered."** No — that's backwards on both counts.
- *At-will* never covered unlawful reasons: at-will means you may terminate without cause — but per library/columbia_at_will_exceptions.md, "termination may not be for an unlawful reason (discrimination, retaliation, exercise of statutory rights)." Tom's own 10/2 email states the *reason*: act "before she's out on leave" because FMLA makes it "a nightmare." An unlawful reason is not cured by at-will status; it is aggravated by a decision-maker's own written statement of it.
- *The PIP* is not your shield today — it is her Exhibit A: written by the accused manager, four days after her complaint, as her first discipline ever, while her attainment ranked 2nd of 5 and three lower-scoring peers (all non-complainers) were untouched. Adverse actions can reach beyond termination (library/title_vii_704a.md — the standard covers actions that would dissuade a reasonable worker), so the PIP itself is part of what needs defending, not part of the defense.

**"If we wait, aren't we just stuck with her forever?"** No. At-will runs both ways — the ability to terminate later does not erode with time; only the *risk* does if the file improves. What the law removes is the ability to use her leave, her complaint, or her request as the reason — ever, on any timetable. So the question is never "can we get out of this employment relationship" (you always can, for lawful grounds) but "what grounds, decided by whom, on what record." Waiting does the following:
- It lets the harassment investigation get decided on the merits instead of being replaced by a termination;
- It lets the PIP close on its own terms (it runs 60 days, opened 9/16, expires 11/15 — as of today 39 days elapsed, 21 remaining);
- It lets the performance case be built with the measurement it currently lacks (quantified hygiene data for all five managers, comparator-consistent application).

**"Can we at least do it right after her leave?"** No — that is the same violation wearing the opposite calendar. The rule in library/fmla_2615.md (§825.220(c)) is that an employer "...is prohibited from using the taking of FMLA leave as a negative factor in employment actions" — it does not matter which side of the leave the negative factor is scheduled on. A termination dated "the day she returns" is dated by reference to her leave, which is precisely what that rule forbids; it simply adds "and we knew it, which is why we scheduled it" to Hale's email. The retaliation clock works the other way too: she has protected activity from 9/12, and any adverse action on a fixed post-leave date keeps the temporal-proximity story alive months later (library/title_vii_704a.md).

**The lawful sequence instead** — and note the law does not require waiting for her leave to end before *any* action; it requires the action to be decided on grounds and by a decision-maker independent of the leave, the complaint, and Hale:
1. Respond to the FMLA request now (it has sat 25 days — 9/30 → 10/25 — on Hale's "wait").
2. Investigation with an independent investigator; Hale isolated from her PIP, evaluations, leave administration, and any decision.
3. Litigation hold; stop ordering anyone to wait on anything Hale-directed.
4. Let the PIP conclude (11/15) with an honest, independently-run evaluation; quantify hygiene data for all five and apply one standard.
5. If the merits then support a termination, act when that decision is actually made — its date is set by the record, not by the leave calendar — and use only a corrected, OWBPA-compliant agreement (see `corrected_separation_agreement_terms.md`).
