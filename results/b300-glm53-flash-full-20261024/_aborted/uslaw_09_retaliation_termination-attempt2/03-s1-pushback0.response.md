## 2. "If we wait, aren't we just stuck with her forever?"

**No.** At-will runs both ways — the ability to terminate later doesn't erode with time; only the *risk* does, and it goes down if the file improves. What the law removes is using her leave, complaint, or request as the reason — on any timetable. Waiting lets the harassment investigation get decided on the merits, lets the PIP close on its own terms (**day 39 of 60 as of today; 21 days left, expires 11/15**), and lets a real performance case get built with the measurement it currently lacks.

**No — that's the same violation wearing the opposite calendar.** The FMLA rule is that an employer is "prohibited from using the taking of FMLA leave as a negative factor in employment actions" — it doesn't matter which *side* of the leave the factor is scheduled on. A termination dated "the day she returns" is dated by reference to her leave, and adds "we knew it, which is why we scheduled it" to Tom's email.
