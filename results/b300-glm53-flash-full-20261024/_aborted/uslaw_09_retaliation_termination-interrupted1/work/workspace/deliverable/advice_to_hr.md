# Advice to HR — termination of Dana Whitcomb (Employee #1187)

**Bottom line: do not terminate today.** The answer to "please confirm we're fine" is that you are not fine, and the file itself shows why. Below is what the documents show, what the law in our reference library requires, and what to do instead.

**Assumption stated up front:** this advice is as of the latest document date, **2026-10-06**. If anything has happened since then (a reply to her FMLA request, further PIP steps, any contact with Dana), tell us so we can refresh the analysis. One question set is open in `QUESTIONS.md`.

## 1. Why "not today"

Three things collide, and all three are in your own file:

**The PIP came from the person she accused, four days after she accused him.** Dana filed a written complaint about Tom Hale's comments on 9/12. Hale opened her first-ever PIP on 9/16 — a 4-day gap — despite a rating history going back to 2022 with no discipline and ratings of "Meets" or better (`documents/personnel_file_summary.md`, `documents/timeline.md`). Our library states that mere "days to a few weeks" between a protected complaint and an adverse action supports a retaliation finding (`library/title_vii_704a.md`). A jury will be asked a simple question: what changed? Nothing in the file changed except the complaint.

**There is direct evidence tying this to her leave.** Tom Hale's 10/2 email — *"Let's get this done before she's out on leave. Once she's on FMLA it's a nightmare to move on her"* — is less than a week old and lands in this file. Library authority: terminating an employee to prevent her from taking requested leave is interference; an employer may not use FMLA leave as a negative factor; she need not have even begun the leave (`library/fmla_2615.md`, §2615(a)(1), §825.220(c)). This email reads like text lifted from a plaintiff's exhibit list. Please treat it accordingly: preserve it, do not explain it away in writing, and make sure no one involved acts on it.

**The comparator record undercuts the performance story.** Every Account Manager is below quota except one — yet Dana (88%) is the only one on a PIP, while Brennan (79%), Osei (84%), and Park (86%) were left alone. Her number is 9 points *better* than Brennan's, 4 better than Osei's, 2 better than Park's — all three of whom are younger men who never complained. Our library names "disparate treatment of comparators who did not complain" as a classic pretext indicator (`library/title_vii_704a.md`). If HR's reaction is "but her trend line is declining" — the company rated 92% and even 96% as "Meets" with no discipline for years (`documents/personnel_file_summary.md`). The enforcement history doesn't match the sudden urgency.

The stated business reason for speed ("the VP wants this done this week") is itself a liability fact: urgency is signature evidence of retaliation. HR's "at-will state" premise does not save it — under the Columbia statute mirrored in our library, at-will employment may still not be terminated for an unlawful reason such as retaliation or exercise of statutory rights (`library/columbia_at_will_exceptions.md`).

## 2. What to do about the harassment complaint

**Open a prompt, impartial investigation — now.** The complaint has been sitting unanswered for 24 days while the accused harasser manages the complainant and writes her performance reviews. That is precisely the failure our library warns about: the employer must promptly and impartially investigate and take corrective action, the failure undermines the *Faragher/Ellerth* defense, and it is independent evidence in a retaliation case (`library/harassment_investigation_duty.md`).

Concretely:
- Assign the investigation to someone **outside Hale's chain of command** — the library is explicit on this point (`library/harassment_investigation_duty.md`). That cannot be Lena Ortiz while she is the recipient of Hale's termination-advocacy email or anyone reporting to Hale. If your only internal option is compromised, use an outside investigator.
- Remember the trigger date: the clock on "reasonably anticipated litigation" started at the 9/12 complaint, not today.
- Interim measures: Dana keeps working without penalty while it proceeds; Hale's hands come off her evaluation cycle in the meantime (which also cleans up the PIP problem, below).
- If the investigation supports her complaint, take corrective action with respect to Hale — that is what the law contemplates, and it's what makes the defense real.

## 3. What to do about the FMLA request

Simple rule: **answer it, and get it out of every decision.**

- HR has not responded to a leave request filed 9/30 with certification submitted 10/3. Respond now: determine her status and communicate it (`library/fmla_2615.md`). Silence while simultaneously drafting a termination is exactly the pattern the interference provision prohibits (§2615(a)(1); `library/fmla_2615.md`).
- Instruction to everyone on this decision (especially whoever may have inherited Hale's urgency): no decisions "based on" her leave, no phrases like "before she's out on leave," nothing that treats the request as a problem to solve. The library says an employer may not use taking of FMLA leave as a negative factor, and she does not need to have started the leave for the protections to apply (§825.220, §2615(a)(1); `library/fmla_2615.md`).
- If a termination nonetheless later happens on documented, comparator-consistent performance grounds after a real FMLA determination, it must be evaluated the same as any other employee's — no interference, no timing games.
- Note what's at stake: our library lists remedies including lost wages, **liquidated (double) damages**, fees, and reinstatement (`library/fmla_2615.md`), and the Columbia-law mirror adds a longer limitations period and **uncapped compensatory damages** (`library/columbia_at_will_exceptions.md`). We cannot put a dollar figure on exposure yet because base salary/comp details aren't in the file — see `QUESTIONS.md` #7. No number here balancing; it's an open item.
