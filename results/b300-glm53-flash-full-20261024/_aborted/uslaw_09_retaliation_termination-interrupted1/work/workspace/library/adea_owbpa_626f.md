# ADEA/OWBPA — knowing and voluntary waiver

*Citation:* 29 U.S.C. §626(f)(1)  
*Status in this library:* in force

A waiver of ADEA claims is not knowing and voluntary unless, at a minimum: (A) written in a manner calculated to be understood; (B) specifically refers to rights or claims arising under the ADEA; (C) does not waive future claims; (D) is in exchange for consideration in addition to anything of value to which the individual is already entitled; (E) advises the individual **in writing to consult with an attorney**; **(F) the individual is given a period of at least 21 days** within which to consider the agreement (**45 days** if requested in connection with an exit incentive or other employment termination program offered to a group); **(G) the agreement provides that for a period of at least 7 days following execution, the individual may revoke** the agreement, and the agreement shall not become effective until the revocation period has expired. An ADEA release that fails these requirements is unenforceable as to ADEA claims; the employee may keep the severance and sue (*Oubre v. Entergy*, 522 U.S. 422 (1998)).
