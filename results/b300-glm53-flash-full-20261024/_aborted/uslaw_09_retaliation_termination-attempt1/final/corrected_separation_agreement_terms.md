## 1. Changes required by statute (ADEA/OWBPA, 29 U.S.C. §626(f)(1))

| # | Current draft term | Required correction | Authority |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | §7: "**seven (7) days to consider**" | **At least 21 days** to consider for an individual termination; **45 days** if the agreement is offered in connection with an exit-incentive or group termination program. Arithmetic: 7 offered vs. 21 required = **14 days short**. | §626(f)(1)(F) |
| 2 | §7: "may revoke within **three (3) days** after signing" | **At least 7 days** following execution to revoke, and the agreement **is not effective until the revocation period expires**. Arithmetic: 3 offered vs. 7 required = **4 days short** (and the effectiveness rule is missing entirely). | §626(f)(1)(G) |
| 3 | Not present in the excerpt HR provided (only §§3, 4, 7, 9, 11 were reviewed) | Add a written **advisement to consult an attorney** before signing. If an advisement exists elsewhere in the full form, verify it satisfies §626(f)(1)(E). | §626(f)(1)(E) |
| 4 | §4: "Release of **all claims**" — no carve-out appears in the excerpt | Add an express paragraph that the waiver **does not cover future claims** (claims arising after execution). Confirm no carve-out exists elsewhere in the full form; if absent, add it — "all claims" without the carve-out fails the statute. | §626(f)(1)(C) |
| 5 | §4: ADEA named only in a listing of statutes | §626(f)(1)(B) requires the operative waiver to **specifically refer to rights or claims arising under the ADEA**. Restate the ADEA waiver in its own sentence ("Employee expressly waives any rights or claims Employee may have under the Age Discrimination in Employment Act…"). The library does not state whether a list reference alone is insufficient — this change removes the doubt. | §626(f)(1)(B) |
| 6 | Whole-document tone | Rewrite in plain, understandable language "calculated to be understood" by the employee, with the §626(f)(1) items visibly separated. | §626(f)(1)(A) |
| 7 | §3: severance of **4 weeks' base pay** | Confirm in writing that this **exceeds anything of value Dana is already entitled to** (check severance policy, bonus accrual). If policy already grants 4 weeks, the consideration must be **additional**. Arithmetic of exposure if not cured: employee may keep the severance *and* sue (Oubre v. Entergy). | §626(f)(1)(D); Oubre v. Entergy, 522 U.S. 422 (1998) |

**Consequence of leaving items 1–7 unfixed:** the ADEA release is unenforceable as to ADEA claims — the employee may keep the severance and still sue (Oubre, per `adea_owbpa_626f.md`).

## 2. Changes required because of this employee's situation (form must reflect them)

8. **Carve-out of non-waivable/pending rights:** the draft releases "all claims, including under Title VII, the ADEA, the FMLA, and state law." As applied to Dana today, that clause would purport to wipe out (a) the uninvestigated 9/12 harassment complaint and (b) the pending 9/30 FMLA request. A release does not cure the company's failure to investigate (Faragher/Ellerth duty, `harassment_investigation_duty.md`) and cannot launder interference with a pending leave request (FMLA §2615(a), `fmla_2615.md`). Future use of the form must not be presented as an alternative to investigating or to deciding the leave request.
9. **State-law claims:** add an express reference to Col. Rev. Stat. §24-34-402 in the waiver (state claims carry the longer limitations period and uncapped compensatory damages, `columbia_at_will_exceptions.md`). *Note:* the library states no state-specific waiver formalities; whether and how state claims can be waived is **unverified** — confirm against a source outside this library before relying on the state release.
10. **FMLA waiver language (unverified):** the library documents FMLA interference/retaliation liability and remedies but states nothing about whether FMLA claims can be waived by agreement. Do not assume the FMLA clause of the release is enforceable; verify from a source outside this library.
11. **Timing/sequencing term (practice, not statute):** the agreement should never be presented within the window in which a litigation hold is in force and the employee is under an active complaint or leave request without a written record showing the release is not being used to suppress the complaint or reaction to it — otherwise the release invites a §2000e-3(a) retaliation challenge (employee's post-signing suit remains possible under Oubre regardless).

## 3. Non-disparagement (§9) and governing law (§11) — keep, unchanged
- §9 mutual non-disparagement and §11 governing law (State of Columbia) raise no defect identified in this library. Keep them, provided §9 does not purport to gag the employee's protected complaints to agencies (the library does not address this point — **unverified**; flag for counsel review outside the library).
