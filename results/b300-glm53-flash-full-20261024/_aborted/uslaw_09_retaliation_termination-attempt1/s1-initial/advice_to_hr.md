## What to do about the harassment complaint

1. **Open a real investigation now.** The library is blunt: once a harassment complaint arrives, the employer must **promptly and impartially investigate and take corrective action** (*Faragher v. City of Boca Raton*; *Burlington Industries v. Ellerth*; EEOC Enforcement Guidance (2024)). "Tom is on the road" is not an investigation — and your confirmation that no work has been done, 24 days later, makes the breach undeniable.
2. **Appoint the investigator in writing, today.** Per your answer, no one has been assigned yet. The library requires an investigator **outside the accused's chain of command**. Use senior HR, legal, or an external investigator from another division.
3. **You and anyone who received Hale's 10/2 email — or his 'wait' instruction on the FMLA request — are witnesses now.** You cannot be the neutral decision-maker on Dana's termination or the sole investigator on Hale's conduct. Recuse yourself from that decision and route it above you.
4. **Suspend Hale's authority over Dana immediately** (decisions on her PIP, pay, schedule, headcount) while he is under investigation: he is both the accused and, per your own answer, the sole mover of the termination. Alternatively, if a lawful basis for separation genuinely exists, it must be decided by someone with no role in the 10/2 email chain or the 'wait' instruction. Consider interim protective measures for Hale (e.g., paid administrative leave pending investigation) — common 'corrective action' practice, but the library does not address it specifically (**unverified**; confirm with counsel).
5. **Preserve everything before anyone deletes anything.** Litigation is reasonably anticipated (complaint + contemplated termination), so a **litigation hold** is mandatory: Hale's and your emails and texts, the 9/12 complaint, the PIP file, comparator personnel files, CRM/quota records, the FMLA request and certification, **and any record of Hale's 'wait' instruction** (Fed. R. Civ. P. 37(e); *Zubulake*). Per your confirmation, no formal files exist for the investigation or the FMLA decision — so the sweep must reach drafts, texts, calendars, and message metadata as well. Sanctions and adverse-inference instructions are what failure looks like.

## What to do about the FMLA request

1. **Decide it now, in writing.** Certification was submitted 10/03 and nothing has been approved or denied — which a plaintiff will characterize as HR deliberately waiting out the clock while planning termination. Respond promptly and in writing, based **solely on whether the certification supports eligibility**. (Any specific regulatory response deadline or cure procedure is outside this library — **unverified**.)
2. **The 'wait' instruction must stop and be documented.** Per your answer, Hale told HR to wait on the request. Complying puts HR inside the interference: the leave may never be used, even indirectly, as a factor in employment actions (29 U.S.C. §2615(a); 29 C.F.R. §825.220), and a decision deferred at the direction of a manager who wants her out before she is out on leave is interference in slow motion. Write a dated note to file memorializing exactly when and in what words the instruction was given, and place it under the litigation hold.
3. **Decide eligibility on the medical certification alone** — eligibility is a benefits-administration question, not a headcount one; the identity of the person who would prefer her absent must not reach that decision.
4. **Never treat the request as a factor in the termination.** The penalty for interference includes **liquidated (double) damages**, fees, and reinstatement — a 4-week severance conversation versus a two-times-wages conversation plus attorney's fees. The fact that leave hasn't started doesn't matter: interfering with the *attempt* to exercise FMLA rights is unlawful, and the employee need not have begun her leave.
5. Note the arithmetic of the timing HR was asked to produce: an eligible employee would have been terminated **6 days after her request**, on **day 4 after the certification arrived and while the request was still stalled at the accused's instruction**, and **4 days after his written 'do it before leave' email**.
