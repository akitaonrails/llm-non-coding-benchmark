# Response to the CEO — Q4 2025 revenue and what I can sign

**To:** Rafael Castellanos (CEO) — re your email of 12/30/2025 ("Q4 numbers for the bridge — need $1.5M+")
**From:** Controller · **Date:** December 31, 2025

Rafael,

I owe you a straight answer, because the bridge depends on it: **the P&L you asked me to send — $1.54M of revenue and no going-concern paragraph — is not one I can give you or sign.** The corrected Q4 revenue is **$850,000**, not $1.54M, and the going-concern disclosure is required. Here is each point, and then what I can and cannot do.

## 1. Halvorsen — $480k cannot be booked

The LOI says on its face that, except for confidentiality, "this letter creates no legal obligation on either party," is subject to a definitive MSA, Procurement Committee approval, and a security review, and either party may walk away. ASC 606-10-25-1 (`library/asc606_contract_and_bill_hold.md`) is explicit that an **expressly non-binding LOI subject to a definitive agreement is not a contract for revenue purposes** and creates no enforceable rights. "They're committed, procurement is just slow" describes a deal in progress — which is exactly what 25-1 says we cannot book. **Reversing $480,000.** If you and Halvorsen close the MSA in January, this becomes real, recognized Q1 revenue — legitimate and sizable.

## 2. Saltline — $210k does not qualify for bill-and-hold in Q4

This one is closest to what we did with Trestle in 2024, but the facts that made Trestle acceptable are precisely the ones missing now. ASC 606-10-55-83 requires **all** four conditions (`library/asc606_contract_and_bill_hold.md`):

- the customer asked us to hold the kits ✓ *(that one we have)*;
- the kits must be **identified as Saltline's** — ops pulls them from **general, untagged stock** ("don't bother tagging anything") ✗;
- they must be **ready for transfer** — no pick, no segregation, February ✗;
- **we must be unable to ship them to anyone else** — untagged common SKU in our stock, fully fungible ✗.

Saltline's own 12/23 email confirms they are "not ready to take delivery." An invoice dated 12/28 does not transfer control; billing is not revenue. In addition, the side letter gives Saltline **a full refund within 180 days** if the Tampa rollout is cancelled, which under 606-10-55-22 makes the consideration highly uncertain and constrains any revenue even at delivery. **Reversing $210,000.** The right way is simple and fast: in February, tag the kits for Saltline and deliver, then book revenue applying the refund constraint. Bill-and-hold only works for Q4 if we actually meet all four criteria now — segregated, tagged, ready — which ops says we deliberately did not.

Your auditors' 2024 management letter is unambiguous on the precedent point: they accepted the Trestle transaction because the units **were physically segregated and labeled** and there was **no right of return**, and they wrote they "would not expect to reach the same conclusion absent those facts." Citing last year to hold a materially different fact pattern is not a basis I can rely on; the Code forbids subordinating my judgment to it (ET §2.130.020, `library/aicpa_code_subordination.md`).

## 3. Ironvale hosting — it is a lease, and that is good news for the P&L

The contract identified three years of servers **by serial number**, for our **exclusive use**, that Ironvale **cannot substitute without our consent**, while **we control the workloads, configuration and utilization**. That is the textbook definition of an embedded lease under ASC 842-10-15-3 to 15-20 (`library/asc842_identification.md`) — identical to the example in the criterion. On a net-income basis nothing much changes: under my stated straight-line assumption Q4 lease expense stays **$54,000**, so the P&L is unchanged; the change is **balance-sheet** recognition of a **$574,413 ROU asset and $574,413 lease liability** at commencement (calculated at an assumed 8% rate — I need our borrowing rate to finalize; full math in the memo). This is required whether or not it is "weird."

**Net effect of 1 and 2 — corrected Q4 revenue $850,000 (1,540,000 − 480,000 − 210,000), net loss $(234,000).**

## 4. The going-concern paragraph — I cannot leave it out, however much I understand your concern

ASC 205-40 (`library/asc205_40_going_concern.md`) requires management to assess whether conditions raise **substantial doubt about our ability to continue within one year after the statements are issued**, and to evaluate plans only if it is **probable they will be implemented and mitigate** the trouble. Our own 13-week cash flow says what the risk is: **cash runs out during the week ending 2/20/2026** and shows a **$509,000 cumulative shortfall by 3/27/2026** absent new money. That is substantial doubt, inside the assessment window, on management's own numbers.

The bridge is the plan, and I wish I could call it probable, but the library criterion is unambiguous: **a non-binding term sheet with conditions precedent is not a probable plan.** And note the circularity that we would be endorsing: the term sheet pays out only if audited/reviewed Q4 revenue is ≥ $1.5M and there is no going-concern qualification — conditions we cannot meet with $850k of supportable revenue. I cannot sign statements whose purpose is to make a lender believe the conditions it insisted on are met. Under ET §2.130.020 I must not knowingly misrepresent facts; presenting the drafted P&L to Crestline to secure financing would do exactly that.

So the disclosure paragraph stays in. I am sorry — I know the timing lands hard on 1/31, but omitting it would put a materially false document in front of a lender, our auditors, and the board, and it would not survive review anyway: the auditors just spent 2024 watching our bill-and-hold issues, and the side letter is sitting in the file.

## What I will and will not sign

**Will sign:** the Q4 P&L showing **revenue $850,000 and net loss $(234,000)**, the Ironvale lease presentation (ROU asset/lease liability; expense unchanged under my stated assumption), the **going-concern disclosure** drafted per 205-40, and the revenue memo supporting the two reversals. I will also send you, by Friday, the cash flow and covenant math so you can walk into January talks with the true picture.

**Will not sign:** the drafted $1.54M P&L, the inclusion of the Halvorsen LOI or Saltline kits as Q4 revenue, and any version that omits the going-concern disclosure. If we disagree on any of this after today, I am obliged under ET §2.130.020 to take it to the next level — the board — and to document the discussion.

**What I would do instead, this month:** (1) close Halvorsen's definitive MSA — that is $480k of real Q1 2026 revenue and it shows the platform is sellable; (2) execute the Saltline delivery properly in February; (3) take the corrected numbers to Crestline with a candid liquidity presentation and, if needed, renegotiate the covenant or the bridge amount; (4) resolve the open items itemized in `deliverable/QUESTIONS.md` — after follow-up, the Saltline question is answered (kits remain untagged on the shelf, confirming the $0 conclusion) and the rest stand as explicitly flagged unknowns, with the borrowing rate the one that moves real numbers.

The business does not become a going-concern problem by my saying so; cash runs out in February whether the paragraph exists or not. What the bridge actually needs is a lender's confidence in a plan, and that starts with numbers our own auditors can stand behind.
