# RouteWise MSA — COO one-pager

**Deal:** RouteWise Technologies, Inc. provides its routing / fleet-optimization platform (SaaS) to Cobalt Logistics Holdings LLC for up to 350 vehicles. $18,500/month billed annually in advance ($18,500 × 12 = $222,000/year) plus a one-time $25,000 implementation fee — $247,000 in year one. 36-month initial term, Delaware law. The playbook applies (over $100k/year). Cobalt will run EU driver data (Rotterdam/Hamburg) through the platform.

## What must change before signature (in order of money at risk)

1. **Restore the uncapped IP indemnity (MSA §§9.1/10.1).** The contract *says* RouteWise's infringement indemnity is "uncapped," but the liability clause opens with "notwithstanding anything to the contrary" and caps *all* liability at 12 months' fees ≈ **$222,000**. As written, that cap wins and the indemnity is worth ~$222k — a patent or copyright claim over the platform would be Cobalt's cost above that line. Fixes are two short sentences in §9.3 and §10.1 (see redlines.md).
2. **Sign the data processing addendum (DPA) at signing (§5.3, Exhibit C).** Exhibit C is currently a placeholder — "[To be attached]." Client has confirmed the real scope: driver names, locations, hours and EU customer addresses from the Rotterdam/Hamburg operations. Signing without an executed DPA puts Cobalt in breach of the GDPR processing-contract requirement from the first day of processing (Regulation (EU) 2016/679, Art. 28(3), per the reference library). This is a signature blocker, not a cleanup item.
3. **Kill the price-increase clause in the Order Form (Exhibit A ¶4).** RouteWise can raise fees **once mid-term on 60 days' notice by at least 8%** (greater of 8% or CPI): 8% × $222,000 = **$17,760** (to $239,760/yr). Playbook allows zero mid-term increases and renewal increases of the *lesser* of 5% or CPI — at CPI 3% that's a $6,660 maximum, a **$11,100/year gap** in that example (formulas shown in issues.json; future CPI unknown). The clause lives only in the Order Form, which controls over the MSA, so it must be redlined there.
4. **Put the affiliates on the paper (§2.2, §1.6, signature page).** Client confirmed both affiliates will use the platform — Europe B.V. for Rotterdam/Hamburg routing, Brokerage LLC for carrier routing — and that Holdings signs on their behalf. But the delivered document has **no signature blocks**, §2.2 still limits the license to "Customer only" with affiliates "at additional fees," and "Users" covers only Customer's own staff. The execution version must: sign as Holdings on behalf of both named affiliates, add one sentence to §2.2 (affiliates as parties covered at no additional fee), and extend "Users" to affiliate staff. Without these edits, the affiliates' use either breaches the license or triggers an upsell.
5. **Cut auto-renewal to 12 months / 30-day notice (§3.3).** As drafted, missing a 90-day notice window locks Cobalt into a **24-month** extension (3× the notice lead time; 2× the extension). Simple fix; large avoided commitment.

## OK as-is (checked against the playbook — won't spend goodwill)
Liability cap level and the gross-negligence/fraud carve-outs (B1: accepted); 30-day data-export window (B2: acceptable); Delaware law, Wilmington venue, jury waiver (B3: acceptable); 15-day suspension notice with cure (B4: acceptable); 99.5% SLA with credits (B5: acceptable).

## One negotiable non-hard item
SLA credits are the sole remedy with no exit for chronic failure (missed 3 months in any 6 = still no termination right; best case is credits capped at 25% of monthly fee = $18,500 × 25% = **$4,625/month**). Recommended but concede-able.

**Bottom line:** five changes, all standard, none deal-threatening: two contract sentences on the indemnity, an executed DPA exhibit, one Order Form paragraph on pricing, the signing structure and two definitional edits for the affiliates (per the client's choice), and the renewal sentence. Then the COO can sign.
