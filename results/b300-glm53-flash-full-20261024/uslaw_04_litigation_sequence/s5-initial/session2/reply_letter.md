## 1. There is no interference claim for your draft motion to dismiss

Juniper asserts no tortious-interference count — in the demand, in Session 1 analysis, or in any contemplated filing — and will file none. The theory was evaluated and rejected before the demand, on exactly the grounds your enclosed draft motion develops: a party to a contract cannot tortiously interfere with it; the two February hires were consented to in writing; the remainder of the theory would face the justification and means defenses your authorities recite. **A Rule 12(b)(6) motion has to have a pleading to move against; this one does not.** Any action Juniper files will plead contract claims only.

Our October 25 letter did not state that Juniper "never consented to any direct hire." It stated the opposite as to Rosa M. and Tay W., and in terms: *"on February 11, 2026, our client confirmed in writing that the conversion fee was waived for Rosa M. and Tay W. as a one-off. Juniper makes no claim for those two hires."* If a Juniper communication containing the sentence you quoted exists, it was inaccurate as to Rosa M. and Tay W. and has been superseded by this office's correspondence. Identify it, and we will address it directly.

## 2. Conversion fees — a contract dispute, as your letter itself frames it — and the contract requires a writing

We agree with your Point 2 that the scope of what Pinecrest received is "a contract dispute, not a tort." That is precisely the claim: breach of Section 3 for the five hires made without payment of the conversion fee.

- Section 3 permits waiver **"in writing."** The only writing either side has identified is the February 11 email, limited by its own terms ("for Rosa and Tay **as a one-off**").
- Our October 25 letter states the position on the other five, and it has not changed: *"Priya S., Luis F., and Keisha B. transitions were discussed with our account manager, but no fee was ever quoted as waived, in writing or otherwise, and Anne O. and Victor H. were hired with no discussion at all. The February 11 waiver cannot be extended by usage or implication."* Marcus Bell, the account manager who communicated the February 11 waiver, has confirmed to our client's CEO in writing (July 20, 2026: "I only ever waived for Rosa and Tay back in Feb") and to this office that no other fee was waived.
- **If Pinecrest contends a writing broader than the February 11 email exists, please identify it by November 10, 2026.** Otherwise, the position that it covers only the two February hires is a merits question for the arbitrator, as your letter concedes.
- Section 3 fixes the fee at 25% of each hire's first-year annualized compensation. Until actual figures are produced, we claim **$80,000*** (5 hires × 25% × $64,000 average), which will adjust up or down against documentation. Our request for offer letters or payroll data for the five hires — first made October 25 — stands.
