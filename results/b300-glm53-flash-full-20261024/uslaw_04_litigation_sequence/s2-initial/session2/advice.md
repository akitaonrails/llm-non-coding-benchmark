Worst case on that line stays: five hires → two clean hires (Anne O., Victor H.) ≈ $32,000 on the unverified base.

## 4. Point-by-point response to the September 29 letter

**Point 1 — "Interference"; consent; draft 12(b)(6) motion.**
Three counters, one constructive:
- *No such claim exists.* Juniper asserts no tort count; the interference theory was rejected in the Session 1 assessment for the identical reasons their draft motion gives — party-to-contract bar (*Ashby v. Caldera Partners*, via `library/columbia_tortious_interference.md`), consent (the Feb 11 email), justification (Restatement §§766, 767, 773). A draft motion therefore has nothing to move against. We owe them one sentence confirming this, not a fight.
- *The quoted statement is not ours.* "Your letter states that Juniper 'never consented to any direct hire'" — that sentence appears nowhere in our October 25 letter, which instead expressly acknowledged and declined to challenge the Feb 11 waiver and claimed a fee only for five hires. Setting this straight matters: their Exhibit-A paragraph is built on a quotation we cannot find.
- *Constructive:* if their quotation comes from some other Juniper communication, we ask them to identify it (also asked of the client as Question 9). If it exists, it overclaims as to Rosa and Tay; our letters already state the accurate, limited position.

**Point 2 — "Conversion fees. Section 3 permits Juniper to waive the fee; it did so."**
- "It did so" must mean *in writing* (§3), covering *the five hires at issue*. The only written waiver produced (by either side) is the 2/11 email — expressly "for Rosa and Tay as a one-off." Bell's 7/20/26 email from our file confirms: no other waiver ever given. We reaffirm the limited waiver without embarrassment and rest our position on the waiver's own limiting language ("as a one-off").
- They are right that the scope of the waiver is "a contract dispute, not a tort" — their framing mirrors ours: our claim is a §3 breach for the five hires, at $80,000* (5 × 25% × $64,000), which will adjust to actual compensation data (request renewed, Nov 10). The asterisk stays: the $64,000 base remains unverified; the claim does not go to an arbitrator until evidence-based (Rule 3.1 via `library/fee_shifting_and_candor.md`).
- Residual risk stays candid and internal: implied-waiver/estoppel exposure on Priya/Luis/Keisha from the texts and the 6-minute call (`library/columbia_account_stated_waiver.md`). It is a fact question; our sentence in any pleading will never assert more than Bell's written statements support.

**Point 3 — invoices: J-4533 disputed; the rest "undisputed" but conditioned.**
- *The condition dies first.* Their letter says Pinecrest "will pay the undisputed balance of the remaining invoices **upon resolution of the conversion-fee issue**." You cannot both concede invoices are undisputed and suspend payment for them pending resolution of an unrelated dispute — particularly one whose resolution requires only the compensation documents Pinecrest alone holds (§2 deems an invoice undisputed at day 15). The reply demands the $276,417.47 unconditionally by November 10 and states that interest continues to accrue meanwhile (currently $88.40/day on the four invoices; $112.30/day across all interest-bearing principal).
- *J-4533.* Two threads: (i) their "notice given within 15 days" is a new written commitment on paper (our file could never verify or refute it — invoice receipt dates are a standing unknown, and whatever the pinning, our position never relied on deemed-acceptance against J-4533, only against the four); (ii) on the merits, we move first — the corrected invoice J-4533R **$71,700.00** already carried the $3,100 double-entry credit our own client identified; interest at 1%/month on the corrected principal from the original due date: $71,700 × 1% × (86/30) = **$2,055.40**, total **$73,755.40**. Their audit may produce more; our offer stands: documented findings by Nov 10, same standard applied both directions. Absent documents, "padded hours" is an assertion without a file behind it (nothing further has been supplied since the 7/9 email).
- *Net effect of the thread:* the four invoices are now a §2 deemed-accepted, account-stated receivable that Pinecrest has conceded in print; J-4533R is the one genuinely contested invoice. That is a materially cleaner board than at the end of Session 1.

**Point 4 — "…your letter threatens suit in the Superior Court… We trust you have reviewed the parties' agreement in full."**
- Our letter threatened and contemplated the opposite — Exhibit C AAA arbitration — and says so in its forum paragraph. Correcting the record here doubles as the answer: Juniper's position is the one the Agreement points to.
- To the "trust / review in full" jab: our reply will simply note we have reviewed the Agreement in full — including C.1 (arbitration, arbitrability to the arbitrator), C.3 (mutual prevailing-party fees, including in actions to compel), and C.4 (provisional relief in aid of arbitration) — and will hold Pinecrest to all of it. Both sides' readings run to the same forum; there is nothing to compel.
- There is no longer any dispute about forum in principle — their point 4's own premise (they assume court filing is what needs guarding against) concedes the Agreement's arbitration machinery governs. Session 3 will dimension the consequences (FAA §§2–4 via `library/faa_2_4.md`; motion-to-compel fee exposure under C.3/*Zuniga* via `library/fee_shifting_and_candor.md`), which this memo flags but does not re-derive.
