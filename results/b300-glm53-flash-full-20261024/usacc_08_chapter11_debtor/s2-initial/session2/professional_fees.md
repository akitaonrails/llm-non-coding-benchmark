# Professional fees — April 2026: what may be paid to each professional, and when

**Authority applied.** Dkt. 77 (Order Establishing Procedures for Interim Compensation, entered
4/14/2026) and 11 U.S.C. §§330(a), 331 (*library: bc_330_331.md* — professionals are paid only
upon application and court award, or under interim compensation procedures ordered by the Court;
**payments outside those procedures are subject to disgorgement**). Form 11-MOR Part 5 reports
approved / paid / held-back amounts (*library: ust_mor_instructions.md*).

## The four Dkt. 77 rules (restated as I apply them)

1. Retained professionals may serve monthly fee statements.
2. Absent objection within 14 days, the Debtor may pay **80% of fees and 100% of expenses**.
3. The **20% holdback on fees** is paid only upon Court approval of an interim or final fee
   application — it is not a discount and not optional; it must be withheld from every fee payment.
4. **No payment may be made for any period before the professional's retention date**
   (Holland & Pierce retained nunc pro tunc to 3/10; Corbin retained effective 3/20).

- **Holdback: $28,000** (20% of fees) — withheld until the Court approves H&P's interim or final

- Timing: payment may be made on or after 4/29. The April activity file records **no professional
  disbursement in April**, so this payment is due to go out immediately upon confirmation that it
  was not already disbursed at the end of April (QUESTIONS.md Q8). Pay it promptly — the
  procedures are designed to keep retained professionals current at 80%/100%, and a missed
  payment is the kind of avoidable friction a case under C-11 pressure does not need.

1. **Pre-retention period.** The statement spans 22 days, of which the first 10 (3/10–3/19) predate

Operating discipline going forward (what keeps MOR Part 5 clean and auditable):

- Every professional payment in the check register is tagged "per Dkt. 77" with the statement
  served, window close date, and holdback ledger. No advances, no paying an unserved statement, no
  expense reimbursements outside a served statement.
- Holdbacks accrue only when fee payments are made — track per professional per month: once H&P's
  $116,200 is paid, its holdback balance is $28,000 for March fees, plus 20% holdbacks on each
  subsequent month's fees paid.
- Interim fee applications are how holdbacks get released: flag to counsel that H&P's first interim
  application should go up promptly so the $28,000 (and later holdbacks) is not stranded for the
  life of the case.

- **Approved $0** — Dkt. 77 established *procedures*; no §330 fee application was filed, let alone
  approved, in April. "Approved" on the form means court-awarded amounts.
- **Paid $0** — the April activity file records no disbursements to professionals. (If H&P's
  $116,200 was in fact sent at month end, Part 5 becomes paid $116,200 / holdback $28,000 and the
  ending cash rolls down by the same amount — QUESTIONS.md Q8.)
- **Holdback $0** — a holdback exists only where a fee payment was made and 20% was withheld; with
  no payments, nothing was withheld. The *prospective* $28,000 holdback on any H&P payment is
  disclosed here and in `april_mor_notes.md`, not booked as a payment.

Note: the served March statements ($144,200 H&P; $45,000 Corbin) are carried in Part 2
post-petition payables in full — a served fee statement is an accrued liability even where the
procedures defer its payability; the deferral is disclosed, not netted out.
