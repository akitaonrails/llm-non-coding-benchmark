## 1. Schedule 3.3 / consents

We do not share the premise that "there are no consent issues," and we cannot accept Schedule 3.3 in its drafted form ("None"). On this record:

- **Hensley Homes Master Supply Agreement** — Brightline's largest customer (38% of revenue, i.e., $1,748,000 of the $4.6M 2025 figure shown in the data room). Its §18 bars assignment "by operation of law, merger, sale of assets, change of control or otherwise" without prior written consent, consent may be withheld **in Hensley's sole discretion**, and a non-consensual assignment is void. In an asset sale delivering these contracts, a clause of that breadth is enforced according to its terms, and the transfer without consent is a breach and void where the clause so provides (*SQL Solutions v. Oracle*; Restatement (Second) of Contracts §§317, 322, per our referenced authority, `library/anti_assignment_change_of_control.md`).
- **Shop lease, 7700 Burleson Rd (through 2029), §22** — landlord consent required, "not unreasonably withheld."
- **City of Austin spray-booth permit** — issued to the LLC, non-transferable; the new owner must reapply, and the city's processing runs **30–60 days**. This one is a closing-calendar item as well as a consent schedule item.
- **Cascade Builders framework agreement (11%)** — assignment to a successor to substantially all assets is permitted on notice; included for completeness as a notice item, not a consent.
- **Twenty-two residential contracts (<$40k each)** — silent on assignment; no consent changes needed on the authority we rely on (contract rights assignable absent language to the contrary; obligations require consent only if personal performance has been made a condition — see `library/anti_assignment_change_of_control.md`). Flagged for completeness; buyer assuming the obligations at closing addresses interpersonal concerns.

## 2. WARN

Confirmed **no WARN exposure** on this headcount, and the reason is structural rather than a close call:

- WARN reaches employers with **100 or more employees** (excluding part-time) and covers plant closings/mass layoffs affecting 50+ employees at a single site (29 U.S.C. §2101(a)(1) as summarized in `library/warn_act.md`; primary/affiliates exclusion in 20 C.F.R. §639.3).
- The Brightline census shows **14 W-2 employees** (7 shop, 4 installers, 3 office), no union, single site.
- Even if your client elects to treat the 9 "independent installers" as employees for WARN counting (readers of our correspondence will see that classification is separately addressed on the disclosure side), the count is 14 + 9 = **23 — well under 100**. A business this size is not a covered employer, and there is no WARN duty even if Buyer ultimately hires fewer than all of them.
- Texas has no mini-WARN statute; the county-based state analog is likewise not a factor at this headcount.

So your client may proceed on its planned staffing decisions free of WARN notice obligations for this target. (We note for completeness only: nothing here should be confused with the *other* heads of exposure around the installers — decoupled from WARN, those are addressed in ¶3 and the indemnity scheduling.)

## 3. §7.4(c) — our position

We cannot accept §7.4(c) as drafted ("knowledge acquired (or capable of being acquired) at any time, whether before or after the execution of this Agreement"). That is the broadest pro-sandbagging formulation, and it is not a reasonable simplification **in this transaction**: it operates together with other provisions of your draft to make Seller the insurer of any issue Buyer learns about anywhere, any time — including on the §4.12 classification facts we are scheduling. Our authority on practice confirms the alternative approaches; a seller who discloses a known item and then signs a clean rep under a pro-sandbagging clause has simply pre-paid an indemnity for that item, which is not anyone's intent here (`library/sandbagging.md`, citing Delaware/Texas practice and *Akorn v. Fresenius* (Del. Ch. 2018) (dicta)).

Our counter is attached as a redline; in substance:

- **§7.4(c)** becomes an anti-sandbagging provision: Buyer's recovery is not affected by Buyer's knowledge, but is precluded (outside Fundamental Representations and Buyer's knowing fraud, and outside scheduled matters, where recovery runs through the newly-added §7.1A Special Indemnity) where Buyer had actual knowledge of the breach at or prior to Closing.
- **§4.12** is qualified by a new **Schedule 4.12** (the nine installers) and paired with a **§7.1A Special Indemnity capped at $275,000** — sized to the arithmetic your client will want: indicative exposure brackets $228,031–$302,619 (employer FICA at an assumed 7.65% on $612k of annual installer pay plus overtime premiums at 3.4-year average tenure; we will share the calculation schedule on request, with the flagged assumptions visible). A capped special indemnity is the market-typical answer where a buyer wants certainty on a specific known item; a full-price-cap rep with statute-of-limitations-plus survival for an operational matter like this is not the market answer (`library/baskets_caps.md`).
- **§7.4(b)**: we pull §4.12 back under the general 10% cap, and cap the classification exposure specifically through §7.1A. Buyer keeps Fundamental Reps at full price cap. Nothing above touches Buyer's fraud or Buyer's right to be told the truth through schedules.
- Closing mechanics note (not a numbered section): the spray-booth reapplication window (30–60 days) is on everyone's critical path. Signing in two weeks works if Buyer is flexible on the closing date; we'd propose a target closing of approximately 60 days post-signing.

The other redlined provisions (§§2.4, 3.3, 8.2) are self-executing on their face; happy to walk through them. In particular, on §2.4: "EBITDA ... determined by Buyer under Buyer's accounting policies" gives your client both the yardstick and the scoreboard — we've replaced it with a defined-Earn-Out structure with ordinary-course covenant, audit and independent-accountant resolution, and acceleration, the protections conventionally found in these provisions (`library/earnout_definitions.md`).
