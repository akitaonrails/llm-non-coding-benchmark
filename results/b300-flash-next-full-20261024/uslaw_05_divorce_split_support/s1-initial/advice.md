**But here is the point his counsel is counting on you to miss: you do NOT need to refinance to stay.** The "refinance or sell" framing is a false choice. Alternative C leaves **both spouses on the existing joint loan and on title** — no refinance, no closing costs, you keep the house 3–6 years, and the later sale uses **two** §121 exclusions. His price for that is knowing his liability is managed: you pay all carrying costs, indemnify him, he's secured by a deed of trust, and a sale trigger is fixed in the decree. If he refuses to stay on the loan at all, then selling is genuinely forced — which is why Alt C (and the QDRO/liquidity equalization in it) should be tabled *with* the disclosure leverage from §3, not after it.

**The tax trap of owning it outright (IRC §121, library `irc_1041_121_71.md`).** You take his basis by §1041: gain is ~$734,000 today (more if price rises over the next 3–6 years) and your single exclusion shelters only $250,000 — about a third of it. If instead:
- **you both sell now**, while still married, the joint return exclusion is **$500,000** — saving ~$47,000 of tax; or
- **he stays on title** while the decree grants you exclusive use, §121(d)(3)(A) treats *him* as using the home as a principal residence during your occupancy, and his ownership period tacks on under (d)(3)(B) — so a sale in ~2031 can use **two $250k exclusions** (each of you sells your interest), same tax saving without selling now.

**Recommendation (Alternative C in `division.json`, updated):** stay in the house **with the loan left joint** (refinance only if your finances improve later — it is an option, never a gate); keep both names on the deed; decree grants you exclusive use with a fixed sale trigger (youngest completes 6th grade, or you vacate); you pay carrying costs and indemnify him; split later net proceeds 50/50 post-tax; and take liquid equalization **now** (half the brokerage, $50k cash, Volvo, and a $200,000 QDRO share of the 401(k)). Fallback if he won't stay on title/loan: Alt B, sell now while the joint $500k exclusion is available.

## 6. Instruments you need (and what a QDRO is)

1. **QDRO (Qualified Domestic Relations Order)** — the *only* way to split a 401(k) in a divorce (26 U.S.C. §414(p); ERISA §206(d)(3), library `qdro_72t.md`). Without one, a payout to you is **taxed to him** and, if he's under 59½, hit with the extra 10% penalty — so his plan money cannot simply be "handed over," and a cash settlement backed by the 401(k) burns ~24%+ of its face. **With** a QDRO, your share is taxed to *you*, never the 10% penalty (§72(t)(2)(C)), and you can **roll it into your own IRA and leave it invested** — do not cash it out; an IRA split needs no QDRO (§408(d)(6)). Get the plan-administrator's QDRO procedures now and attach the order early; it's a separate court order with separate fees.
2. **Deed + loan mechanics**: quitclaim/warranty deed per whichever house alternative you choose; refinance or a written hold-harmless on the joint mortgage.
3. **In-kind brokerage split** as a §1041 transfer (carryover basis, no gain on transfer) rather than a taxable sell-down.
4. **Deferred-sale / co-tenancy agreement** (Alt C): trigger date (e.g., youngest completes 6th grade or wife vacates), who pays carrying costs, sale-agent selection, and the net-proceeds formula **after 6% costs and each party's own tax**.
5. **Child support & maintenance orders**, with the maintenance clause stripped of the false "deductible/taxable" recital and health-insurance/child-care add-ons addressed (not covered by the library excerpt — unverified, raise with counsel).
6. **Form 8332 clause** — see next section.

## 7. Children as "dependents" and filing status

The children are with you **255 nights/year = 69.9%** (confirmed by client) — you are the custodial/residential parent (≥65% under the worksheet; Husband's 110 nights = 30.1% is below the 35% shared-parenting threshold, so no adjustment — both thresholds confirmed on real counts, not estimates). Now to the proposal's "Husband claims both children in alternating years": that is backwards from your default rights. **IRC §152(e): the custodial parent (more overnights — you) claims the children unless she signs a Form 8332 release.** A release moves *only the child tax credit* for that year — **head-of-household status, EITC and the dependent-care credit stay with you no matter what** (library `filing_status_dependents.md`). So: refuse the alternating-years term, or sell it year-by-year with the credit's value (credit amounts aren't in the library — I won't guess). You should file **head of household** once the decree is final, if a child lives with you more than half the year (255 nights satisfies this) and you pay more than half the household costs — the cost half is still unverified; track your contributions from the decree forward. Status is set **on December 31**: a decree final by 2026-12-31 means you file HoH for 2026 rather than married — worth confirming timeline, and it also interacts with whether you can/should file jointly in a house-sale year (Alt B).
