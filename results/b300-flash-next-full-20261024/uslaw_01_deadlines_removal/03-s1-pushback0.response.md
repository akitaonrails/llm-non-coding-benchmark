

Your friend isn't inventing a rule — he's applying the right rule to the wrong kind of entity. Let me answer both halves, then I'll put it in the memo for the record.

**"Delaware LLC vs. Washington corporation is textbook diversity."**
That slogan is the **corporation** rule — 28 U.S.C. § 1332(c)(1) says a *corporation* is a citizen of its state of incorporation and PPB. It's real, it's textbook, and it's exactly why Sound Cascade is a Washington citizen. But the section says "a corporation shall be deemed a citizen…" — and the library's controlling authority says the flip side: *"like a partnership, an LLC is a citizen of every state of which its owners/members are citizens"* (*Johnson*, 437 F.3d at 899; *Carden*). Delaware is only where your articles were filed. Test it: if you'd formed the identical company in **Wyoming** instead, would it become a Wyoming citizen? No — Delaware never enters the analysis at all, not even in your friend's favor.

**"Caleb isn't the company."**
For a corporation, correct — and that instinct is exactly what fails for an LLC. For unincorporated entities the law looks **through** the entity to its owners. The rule isn't "the company isn't him"; it's **the company *is* its owners**. If Caleb were your *employee* or a *manager* with no membership interest, you'd be right and his Seattle address would be irrelevant. But you told me he's a **35% member** — that's precisely why membership was the first question I asked. His Seattle domicile is imputed to Harbor Street whole.

**And it cuts both ways.** The same sentence that gives Harbor Street your Oregon citizenship also gives it Caleb's Washington citizenship. You can't take the Oregon half and decline the Washington half — it applies identically to diversity (§ 1332(a)) *and* to the forum-defendant rule (§ 1441(b)(2)). Two separate doors, both shut.

**The risk is lopsided, which is the practical answer to "are you sure."**
- If he's right and we don't remove: **nothing is lost.** The case sits in state court, exactly where it is now. There's no deadline lost, no right forfeited.
- If I'm right and we remove: the remand is **automatic and can happen at any time** (§ 1447(c)), and the court "may require payment of just costs and any actual expenses, **including attorney fees**" Sound Cascade incurred because of the removal. You'd pay your opponent for filing a void document — and the prize you'd win by removing is… nothing; plaintiff picked King County, you keep a shop there.

So: not a judgment call, a rule — and even the gamble has no upside.
