## 3. Calendar A — state court (what applies if we stay in King County)

### 3.1 Which rules
* Answer time: **CR 12(a)(1)** — answer within **20 days** after service of the summons and complaint; **CR 4(a)(2)** says the same and states that the 60-day extensions (out-of-state service **by publication** or under RCW 4.28.185) do **not** apply to **personal service on a registered agent**. `library/wa_cr_4_6_12.md`
* Counting: **CR 6(a)** — exclude the day of the act that starts the period; count every intervening day; include the last day **unless it is a Saturday, Sunday or RCW 1.16.050 legal holiday**, in which case the period runs to the next day that is none of those. `library/wa_cr_4_6_12.md`
* Holidays: **RCW 1.16.050** for 2026 (MLK 1/19, Presidents' 2/16, Memorial 5/25, Juneteenth 6/19, Independence observed 7/3, Labor 9/7, Veterans 11/11, Thanksgiving 11/26, Native American Heritage 11/27, Christmas 12/25). No state holiday in September or the first week of October. `library/rcw_1_16_050_wa_holidays.md`

### 4.3 Subject-matter jurisdiction — the element that kills it
**§ 1332(a)** requires the matter in controversy to exceed **$75,000, exclusive of interest and costs**, and be "between … citizens of different States." `library/28usc_1332_1441_1446_1447.md`

**Plaintiff** — Sound Cascade Foods, Inc.: a corporation is a citizen of its state of incorporation *and* its principal place of business, § 1332(c)(1). Incorporated in Washington, PPB Kent, Washington → **citizen of Washington**, and nothing else.

**Defendant** — Harbor Street Bakery LLC: **§ 1332(c)(1) does not apply to an LLC.** "An unincorporated association — including a limited liability company — is a citizen of **every state of which its owners/members are citizens**"; "*like a partnership, an LLC is a citizen of every state of which its owners/members are citizens*" (*Johnson v. Columbia Properties Anchorage, LP*, 437 F.3d 894, 899 (9th Cir. 2006)); see also *Carden v. Arkoma Associates*, 494 U.S. 185 (1990). **A single non-diverse member destroys complete diversity.** An individual is a citizen of the state of his or her **domicile**. `library/llc_citizenship_cases.md`

So the complaint's own allegations — "a Delaware limited liability company with its principal place of business in Portland, Oregon" — establish **nothing** about Harbor Street's citizenship. Delaware is a red herring: an LLC is not "a citizen of Delaware" merely because Delaware formed it, and Portland (its PPB) is equally irrelevant under *Johnson*. To remove, we would have to affirmatively plead the citizenship of **every member** (and, if any member is itself an entity, the citizenship of *its* owners, all the way down to human beings). As of today we can write that sentence — and writing it is what kills the removal theory.

**Confirmed as of 9/28 (client reply).** Harbor Street's membership is exactly two people: **Noor Haddad — 65% — lives Portland, Oregon → Oregon citizen**; **Caleb Sorensen — 35% — lives Seattle (Ballard) → Washington citizen.** Under *Johnson*, Harbor Street is therefore "a citizen of every state of which its owners/members are citizens": **Washington and Oregon.** Plaintiff Sound Cascade is a **Washington** citizen. A Washington citizen suing a defendant who is also a Washington citizen means **complete diversity fails — there is no § 1332(a) jurisdiction at all.** That is dispositive: no federal court can hear this case on diversity, and under § 1447(c) a jurisdictional defect can be raised — and remand ordered — "at any time before final judgment."

One caveat, stated rather than assumed: the library defines an individual's citizenship as the state of **domicile** (residence plus intent to remain), and Sorensen's intent is nowhere documented; the client has no further information. The gap changes nothing. The removing party must *affirmatively establish* jurisdiction, and the only reported fact about the Washington member places him living in the forum state; no notice of removal could be signed in good faith. (If — contrary to everything reported — his domicile were Oregon, diversity would technically be complete and § 1441(b)(2) inapplicable; I record that hypothetical for completeness only. It is not a basis to remove, and even if it were true, spending client money litigating jurisdiction in plaintiff's home county, where you operate a shop, would serve no purpose.)

Domicile ≠ where the business is, and that symmetry now cuts against us twice: your Oregon residence stands even though the company runs a Seattle store and holds a WA license — but Sorensen's Seattle residence makes him a Washington citizen even though the LLC's PPB is Portland.

#### 4.3.1 Your friend's theory, on the record — why "Delaware LLC vs. Washington corporation" is the wrong comparison

You asked again whether your Texas lawyer friend is right that this is "a diversity case — Delaware company vs. Washington company." Respectfully, his advice applies the **corporate** rule to an **LLC**, and each step of the comparison fails:

1. **"State of incorporation + principal place of business" is the rule for corporations only.** § 1332(c)(1) deems a *corporation* a citizen of its incorporating state and its PPB state; the library note says the provision "applies to corporations; see the case law entry on unincorporated associations." An LLC is not a corporation. That Delaware formed Harbor Street is legally **irrelevant** to its citizenship.
2. **For diversity purposes the LLC *is* its members.** "*Like a partnership, an LLC is a citizen of every state of which its owners/members are citizens*" (*Johnson*, 437 F.3d at 899; see *Carden*). So "Caleb isn't the company" is exactly backwards for this rule: as to § 1332, Caleb **and** Noor *are* the company. Harbor Street's citizenship is Oregon (Haddad) **and** Washington (Sorensen) — not Delaware, and not "Oregon only" via Portland either.
3. **The overlap cannot be split or averaged away.** The LLC is a citizen of *each* of its members' states at once; a single overlap with a plaintiff's citizenship destroys "citizens of different States" for the whole case — "A single non-diverse member destroys complete diversity" (*Johnson*). Harbor Street is not "mostly an Oregon company with a Washington partner"; it is **fully** a Washington company *and* fully an Oregon company, and Sound Cascade is fully Washington. One shared state ends the analysis.

His second premise ("federal court is better for defendants") may be a fair general instinct — but it can't open a locked door. A removal here would be void: the case comes back under § 1447(c) (the court "shall" remand for lack of jurisdiction), and Harbor Street could be ordered to pay Sound Cascade's costs and attorney fees caused by the filing (§ 4.7). The complaint pleads "Delaware LLC" with a Portland PPB only because the corporate frame is what lawyers habitually plead — for an LLC it proves nothing about citizenship.

### 4.5 Forum-defendant rule — § 1441(b)(2)
> "A civil action otherwise removable solely on the basis of § 1332(a) may **not** be removed if any of the parties in interest properly joined and served as defendants is a **citizen of the State in which such action is brought**."
