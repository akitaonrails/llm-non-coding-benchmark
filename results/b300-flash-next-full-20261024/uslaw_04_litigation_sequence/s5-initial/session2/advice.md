## 6. ADDENDUM (Nov. 3, 2026) — the CEO says: "We don't fold because of one email. Hanna poached SEVEN. Keep the interference claim — I want them scared."

Dolapo — you are right that we are not folding. But the interference claim is not the muscle you think it is, and this is not about one email. Plain words, point by point.

### 6.1 Kill the Feb 11 email entirely and the interference claim still dies — four independent reasons

1. **They can't interfere with their own contract.** "A party to a contract cannot tortiously interfere with it" (*Ashby v. Caldera Partners*, 212 Col. App. 3d 440 (2019); *columbia_tortious_interference.md*). Pinecrest is the counterparty to the Agreement. The remedy for Pinecrest taking staff is **breach of §3 — which is exactly what we are claiming**. Marcus's email has nothing to do with this wall.
2. **Hiring at-will employees requires "improper means."** Interference with at-will staff is actionable "only if the means are improper" (Restatement (Second) of Torts §§766, 767, 773; *columbia_tortious_interference.md*). The file shows Pinecrest **emailed and texted openly** before and during the hires and invoked a corporate cost mandate. No fraud, no threats, no misrepresentation anywhere in our own documents. "Hanna poached seven" is exactly what the Restatement treats as *fair competition* unless means are improper — and the means here were a memo and a phone call.
3. **Justification is a complete defense.** The May 2 "corporate mandate" email is documented business justification (§773; *Ashby*, id.). Their draft motion already pleads it as Ex. 2.
4. **The $410,000 number is barred by OUR contract.** Lost profits on the account are consequential damages; §6 excludes consequential damages both ways and rescues only "unpaid fees and conversion fees." Even in a world where we won the tort, **§6 caps its value at $0**.

A claim with four independent killers is not a threat. It is $410,000 × ~0% probability = **$0.00 of leverage**, plus a price tag (§ 6.3).

### 6.2 "Seven people" — the contract already gives you the seven-hire remedy, without the tort

Section 3 does **not** require proving poaching, persuasion, or bad faith. Pinecrest hiring anyone from our roster during the term + 12 months triggers the 25% fee, period. That is why our §3 case is stronger than any tort case could be. The math:

| | Hires | Fee (25% × $64,000 = $16,000 each) | Status |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Anne O. + Victor H. | 2 | $32,000 | **Clean** — zero Juniper-side contact in the file |

| Rosa M. + Tay W. | 2 | $32,000 → $0 | Written waiver — conceding costs us $32,000, **not** $112,000 |

So Marcus's email costs $32,000. It does not cost the seven. **We are actively claiming five of them — $80,000 — plus interest, plus C.3 fees.** That is the poaching remedy the contract designed.

### 6.3 What "keeping the interference claim" actually costs — it scares them the wrong way

- **We are ethically barred from asserting it.** No non-frivolous basis exists on our own documents; Columbia R. Prof'l Conduct 3.1 forbids it, and the same sentence repeated in an arbitration filing or pleading runs into Rule 3.3's prohibition on false statements of fact to a tribunal (Columbia R. Prof'l Conduct 1.4, 3.1, 3.3; *fee_shifting_and_candor.md*). If you directed us to file it, we would have to decline — this is not a judgment call we can trade.
- **It hands them their exhibit.** A claim "fails where the complaint and incorporated documents show consent or justification" (Rule 12(b)(6); *Ashcroft v. Iqbal*, 556 U.S. 662 (2009); *frcp_12b6.md*). To plead interference we must attach the Agreement and the email traffic — the Feb 11 email and the "corporate mandate" are **in our own file**. We would be pleading our loss and mailing them the exhibit. Halvorsen has the motion drafted already (it arrived with their letter).
- **Fee-shifting cuts on their side of that claim.** Prevailing-party clauses are "enforced as written, including in arbitration and in motions to compel" (*Zuniga v. Mercer*, 198 Col. 2d 77 (2017); *fee_shifting_and_candor.md*; Exhibit C.3). Whether Pinecrest could recover C.3 fees for defeating a single dismissed count (as opposed to prevailing overall, or on a motion to compel) is **[not in library — unverified]** — but betting real dollars on it is pointless, because the count is worthless anyway (§ 6.1).
- **Credibility is money in this case.** Our strongest asset is that we conceded the $3,100 double-enters *before* they found them, conceded Rosa/Tay, and corrected the "never consented" line. That is why the $428,399.50 lands as a serious document. One file-contradicted tort count rebuilds their "Juniper cries wolf" narrative and devalues every real dollar we're claiming.
