**1. The correspondence you answered; two corrections.**
The demand letter your September 29 response addresses was transmitted before this firm was engaged [date/sender to be confirmed]. Two characterizations in it were inaccurate, and Juniper's position — stated here once, definitively — is as follows:

- *Consent.* Juniper does not contend that it "never consented to any direct hire." Marcus Bell's e-mail of February 11, 2026 waived the Section 3 conversion fee for Rosa M. and Tay W. "as a one-off." Juniper has never claimed those two fees and does not claim them now. By its own terms that waiver extends to no one else; Section 3 requires any waiver to be **in writing**, and no writing exists as to any other individual.
- *Interference.* Juniper is not asserting, and will not assert, a tortious interference claim. Your draft Rule 12(b)(6) motion is directed at a count that will never exist, in the one forum — a court — where it would have had any role. We expect you will agree the draft is moot.

You are correct that, beyond the two waived fees, any dispute over the scope of the February 11 waiver is a contract dispute, not a tort. We agree — and under Exhibit C.1 it is a contract dispute to be resolved in **binding arbitration before the American Arbitration Association**, which is precisely where Juniper will bring it.

For the avoidance of doubt: Juniper does not intend to commence, and will not commence, a court action in breach of Exhibit C. Your point 4 is noted, and met: we have reviewed the Agreement in full, including Exhibit C, and we invoke it.

**2. The undisputed invoices must be paid now — $276,699.50.**
Your letter states that Pinecrest "will pay the undisputed balance of the remaining invoices upon resolution of the conversion-fee issue." Section 2 requires payment within 30 days of invoice, and interest at 1% per month on late amounts. Pinecrest's unilateral condition is not a term of the contract.
