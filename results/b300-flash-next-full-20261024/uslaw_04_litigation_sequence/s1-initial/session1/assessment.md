## 3. What the contract requires / gives us

1. **Deemed acceptance (§2).** The four undisputed invoices were never disputed within 15 days of receipt → deemed accepted; retained without objection, they are prima facie evidence of the debt (account stated, CJI 14.02; *columbia_account_stated_waiver.md*). This is our strongest claim.
2. **Contractual interest (§2).** 1%/month — computed in § 4.
3. **Conversion fee (§3).** The contract supplies its own remedy: 25% of **each hire's** first-year annualized compensation — **not** a 7 × $64,000 average. Actual comp data does not exist in the file and the client has none (**open risk**); the $16,000/hire figure is the CEO's estimate and is used as a placeholder subject to true-up.
4. **The waiver is real but narrow.** A written waiver limited by its terms ("for Rosa and Tay as a one-off") does **not** extend to later hires (*columbia_account_stated_waiver.md*) — Priya, Luis, Keisha, Anne, Victor are still claimable, and Bell's confirmation ("Just those two… never agreed to the other five") aligns the client's testimony with the email's own text. But a **course of dealing** of "acquiescence in later hires without objection ('ha, we'll talk', 'call me')" may support implied waiver/estoppel as to Priya, Luis and Keisha — "a fact question" (id.). Pinecrest will press this; the fact-finder sees our pattern-manager texts. Anne and Victor face no such defense.
5. **§6 cuts both ways.** It expressly preserves full recovery of unpaid fees and conversion fees, but eliminates the $410,000 consequential-type account claim however we label it.
6. **Exhibit C controls forum** — see § 6.

## 5. Claims, strengths, weaknesses

### 5.1 Claim 1 — Breach of §2 (unpaid invoices): **STRONG**
- Four deemed-accepted invoices, $265,200 + $8,847.50 interest. §2's deemed-acceptance clause plus retention without objection makes this near-summary (account stated, CJI 14.02; *columbia_account_stated_waiver.md*).
- **J-4533 (adjusted to $71,700):** Pinecrest disputed July 9. If the invoice was received ~July 1 (due date implies invoice ≈ July 1), the 15-day window was met, so it is *not* deemed accepted; the receipt date is **unknown and unobtainable from the client**, so we no longer rely on deemed-acceptance for this invoice. The credit-and-substantiate posture in the letter supersedes the point: we conceded the real $3,100 error ourselves, put the burden of specific findings on Pinecrest by Nov. 9, and carry the "rest is legit" position with shift-level documentation (which we have not yet inspected — see open items).
- Candor check: claiming the full $74,800 after our own review found $3,100 of double-enters would be a file-contradicted assertion (Rules 1.4, 3.1; repeating it in a pleading implicates 3.3 — *fee_shifting_and_candor.md*). We credit it.

### 5.2 Claim 2 — Breach of §3 (conversion fees): **MODERATE**
- Clean as to **Anne O. and Victor H.** (June hires; no Juniper-side contact).
- **Priya, Luis, Keisha:** no waiver by its terms (the waiver is "a one-off"), but our texts ("ha, we'll talk"; "😬 call me" + the content-unknown 6-minute call) let Pinecrest argue implied waiver/estoppel by course of dealing — a fact question (CJI 16.10 and *columbia_account_stated_waiver.md*; UCC §1-303 by analogy). Litigation risk, not a bar. §3's requirement that a waiver be **in writing** is our best answer ("ha, we'll talk" is no written waiver); the call's content is unknown and is the loose thread.
- **Rosa, Tay: do not assert.** Bell's Feb 11 email is a written waiver of exactly those two fees, and Bell now confirms it ("Just those two. I didn't think it was a big deal."). We *could* argue an account manager lacked authority to waive a contract right, but §3 attaches no signature formality, Lindqvist asked and got a same-day yes, and agency/apparent-authority doctrine is **[not in library — unverified]** — we cannot support it with any cited authority, and the CEO's own manager has now admitted the waiver on interview. Asserting these fees would contradict our file (Rule 3.1) and hand Pinecrest its credibility-winning exhibit. Posture: **we acknowledge the waiver in the demand letter** — costs $32,000 of paper, inoculates against the "smoking gun" narrative.
- Damages measure needs actual first-year compensation per hire; the client has none — the $80,000 is a stated placeholder (letter ¶3 true-ups it).

### 5.3 Claim 3 — Tortious interference / $410,000 lost profits: **DO NOT ASSERT**
Every path is blocked, on our own documents:
1. **Interference with the staffing contract:** "a party to the contract cannot tortiously interfere with it" — Pinecrest is the counterparty; the remedy is breach of contract (*Ashby v. Caldera Partners*, 212 Col. App. 3d 440 (2019); *columbia_tortious_interference.md*).
2. **Interference with Juniper's at-will staff:** actionable "only if the means are improper." Pinecrest emailed/texted before and while hiring, invoked a corporate cost mandate, and partly obtained consent — nothing in the file shows fraud, threats, or other improper means (Restatement §§766, 767, 773; *Ashby*, id.).
3. **Complete defenses on our papers:** **consent** (the Feb 11 waiver; the acquiescence texts) and **legitimate business justification** — the documented "corporate mandate" to cut agency spend (§773; *Ashby*, id.).
4. **Pleading risk:** documents referenced in the complaint may be considered, and an interference claim "fails where the complaint and incorporated documents show consent or justification" (Rule 12(b)(6); *Ashcroft v. Iqbal*, 556 U.S. 662 (2009); *frcp_12b6.md*). We would attach the very contract and emails that defeat us.
5. **Contract route also barred:** lost profits on the account are consequential damages; §6 excludes consequential damages and rescues only "unpaid fees and conversion fees." §6 is why the conversion-fee *formula* (not the account) is our damages theory for the hires.
Asserting it anyway risks Rule 3.1; repeating file-contradicted statements in pleadings risks Rule 3.3 (*fee_shifting_and_candor.md*).

## 6. Forum — the CEO's "lawsuit" is off the table; the unread Exhibit C does not save it

The CEO asks whether the never-read arbitration exhibit "kills us." **No — but it changes the venue, and that is the whole answer:**

- Exhibit C.1 is a broad, FAA-covered arbitration clause covering "any dispute… including its formation, breach, termination or the arbitrability of any claim." FAA §2 makes such a clause "valid, irrevocable, and enforceable, save upon such grounds as exist at law or in equity for the revocation of *any* contract" (9 U.S.C. §2; *faa_2_4.md*). Nothing in the file shows a revocation-type ground (fraud, duress, incapacity) — a negotiated B2B contract signed by both principals, whose §7 entire-agreement clause expressly incorporates Exhibits A–C. **Not having read a signed contract is not such a ground; no library authority addresses an unread-boilerplate defense and I cannot cite one — [not in library — unverified].** If Juniper tried to disclaim Exhibit C, Pinecrest could equally enforce it against us — including C.3.
- Practical consequence: a court **shall stay** referable issues (§3) and can **compel** arbitration (§4); the arbitrability delegation is "clear and unmistakable," so the arbitrator decides arbitrability (*Rent-A-Center v. Jackson*, 561 U.S. 63 (2010), as summarized in *faa_2_4.md*). Even a tort claim "relating to" the Agreement is arbitrable.
- **C.3 + fee-shifting:** prevailing-party clauses are "enforced as written, including in arbitration and in motions to compel" (*Zuniga v. Mercer*, 198 Col. 2d 77 (2017); *fee_shifting_and_candor.md*). **If we file the lawsuit the CEO wants, Pinecrest wins a motion to compel and Juniper owes its fees and costs for that motion before the case starts.** Filing suit is the one move that turns the fee clause against us.
- **Small claims (C.6) unavailable:** covers only *undisputed invoices under $25,000*; every invoice exceeds $25,000.
- Correct forum: **AAA Commercial arbitration, single arbitrator, Capital City**, where C.3 lets *us* recover fees and costs if we prevail. C.4 interim injunctive relief in court is available in aid of arbitration but unnecessary now (the hires already happened; it is live leverage against future hires).
- **[Not in library — unverified]** AAA filing/administrative fees and arbitrator compensation; pull the schedule at filing and budget.
