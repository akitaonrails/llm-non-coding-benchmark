## 4. Candor about the February 11 email — the whole point of this session

**What the email is.** On Feb. 11, 2026 Lindqvist asked to hire Rosa and Tay directly, acknowledged "there's a fee thing," and Marcus Bell answered the same day: "consider the fee waived for Rosa and Tay **as a one-off**." §3 lets Juniper waive conversion fees "in writing." An email is writing. This is a valid, effective waiver **for exactly two fees** — Bell confirms it, the words confirm it, and Halvorsen has a copy.

**What it is not.** It is not a consent to the other five hires. The library is explicit: "a written waiver limited by its terms ('for Rosa and Tay as a one-off') does not extend to later hires" (*columbia_account_stated_waiver.md*). Their broader "Juniper **consented** to the hires" argument (bolded in their letter) has no supporting writing for Priya, Luis, Keisha, Anne, or Victor — §3 requires the waiver to be in writing, and none exists.

**The honest risks we will not paper over.**
- For Priya, Luis and Keisha, the texts ("ha, we'll talk"; "😬 call me" + an unexplained 6-minute call) let Pinecrest argue **implied waiver/estoppel by course of dealing — the library calls it "a fact question"** (CJI 16.10; UCC §1-303 by analogy; *columbia_account_stated_waiver.md*). An arbitrator could find for Pinecrest on some or all of those three ($48,000 at placeholder comp). We plan around that risk, not past it.
- **The pre-engagement letter's statement that Juniper "never consented to any direct hire" is contradicted by our own records.** Rules 1.4 and 3.1 (*fee_shifting_and_candor.md*) require us to say that plainly to you, and Rule 3.3's logic explains why it is dangerous: a demand letter is not a tribunal filing, **but the same sentence in a complaint becomes a false statement of fact to a tribunal**, with the Feb. 11 email as Pinecrest's exhibit. It will not appear in anything we send or file. The reply letter affirmatively corrects the record: we *do* acknowledge the Rosa/Tay waiver, and we are *not* asserting interference.
- To the CEO, in plain words: **the email costs us $32,000 of the spreadsheet and it kills the interference count outright. We conceded both in Session 1. The reply letter doubles down, and that is what makes the remaining $428,399.50 credible.**
