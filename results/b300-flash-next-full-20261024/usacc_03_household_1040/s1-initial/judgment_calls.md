# Judgment calls — Reyes 2025 engagement

Each entry: the decision, the authority, and the alternative I rejected (and what it would change).

## 1. S-corp loss: basis-limited to $11,500 (Marcus's expectation rejected)

Marcus expects the $28,000 K-1 loss to "come straight off" income. Form 7203 instructions (§1366(d))
allow the loss only to the extent of stock + debt basis; his rollforward gives $11,500 beginning basis,
so $11,500 allowed, $16,500 suspended. **Rejected alternative:** deducting the full $28,000 — would lower
AGI to $74,850, taxable income to $43,350, and change the tax table lookup, MAGI, and the 8962 band;
unsupported by the library and wrong on its face. **Rejected alternative 2:** counting the bank line as
debt basis because Pike guarantees it — the library is explicit that a guarantee creates no debt basis;
only a direct shareholder loan would, and Marcus confirms none exists ("I never lent it money").

## 2. S-corp loss treated as nonpassive (reduces §469(i) MAGI)

K-1 Part II G states "Shareholder materially participates: Yes," and the 8582 excerpt says an S-corp
loss of a materially participating shareholder reduces MAGI. **Rejected alternative:** treating the
S-corp interest as passive too — the loss would then stack with the rental inside Form 8582, MAGI would
be $122,400, the allowance would be $13,800, and the AGI/tax/8962 results would differ materially.
Rejected because the K-1's own checkbox contradicts it.

## 3. Below-market rent: rental treated as a loss-generating rental (narrow reading)

Rent is $1,500 vs. ~$2,600 market. §280A(d)(2)(C) (via the 8582 excerpt) reclassifies days rented below
fair rental value as personal-use days → **no loss at all**. But the same excerpt carves out: "Renting
below market to an unrelated tenant because rent was never raised does not by itself trigger this rule
if the rent is a fair rental for that lease." That sentence fits these facts almost verbatim — long
tenant, rent never raised — so I kept the $21,018 loss alive (then cut to $19,550 by the allowance).
**Rejected alternative:** personal-use treatment → rental loss $0, MAGI unaffected, allowance irrelevant;
AGI rises by 19,550 to $110,900, taxable income to $79,400 (outside the library's tax-table excerpt —
tax could not be computed from the library at all), and 8962 household income would jump to $110,900
(355.45% FPL → applicable figure 7.39% in the 300–400% band → monthly contribution 682.61 →
PTC ≈ 6 × (1,690 − 682.61) = 6,044; excess 3,856, repaid up to the $3,150 cap for 300–<400% FPL).
This is the single largest swing item in the return. The
carve-out's own proviso ("fair rental for *that* lease") is the weak point: $1,500 under a stale lease
is exactly what the facts describe, but I could not verify the lease itself — it is referenced as
"attached in prior-year file" and is not in the documents. **Response update:** Elena confirms the two
facts the carve-out turns on — the tenant is unrelated ("a stranger, a nurse we found through a broker
in 2019") and the rent was simply never raised. Carve-out applied; the $19,550 allowed loss stands. The
lease paper remains unavailable (client has no further information), so the "fair rental for that lease"
proviso is accepted on the client's word, not on documents — residual open item R2, and the only
scenario that would still move the big numbers is if the lease turned out not to be a bona fide
arm's-length lease.

## 4. Leo: not claimed; Sofia claimed

Custody decree ¶9 alternates: father claims odd-numbered years; 2025 is odd. The 1040 instructions
excerpt says an alternating agreement controls, and a child claimed by the other parent via Form 8332
is not your qualifying child for the CTC. **Rejected alternative:** claiming Leo anyway because he is
"primarily with the Mother" (custody ≠ claim rights here) — would add $2,200 CTC and change nothing
else, but contradicts the agreement that the library says controls. **Closed by client response:** Elena
signed the Form 8332 for Leo's dad for 2025 ("it's his year") — the release is executed, the father claims
Leo, and the rejected alternative is foreclosed.

## 5. NY residency end date: June 30 (181 days), not July 3 (184 days)

Domicile abandoned when the Brooklyn lease ended and the apartment was surrendered (6/30), with
undisputed intent to relocate (closed Tampa 7/3). The Queens nights (6/28–30) are temporary lodging at
a relative's — **rejected alternative:** treating the family as NY residents through 7/3 (184 days)
because no fixed home existed outside NY until then. Because the convenience-of-the-employer rule
sources *all* wages to NY anyway, the 3-day difference is immaterial to wages; it would only slightly
shift the day-ratio denominators and would pull July 1–3 wages into the "all income during resident
period" bucket. Also rejected the intuitively tempting "prorate wages 181/365" (Marcus $33,699, Elena
$26,762): the convenience rule forecloses it where remote work is employee convenience, and the file
says exactly that ("he just works remotely now"; "Halvorsen let me go fully remote"). The necessity
exception is **resolved by client response**: Marcus asked to move and "the company said remote was fine…
they didn't need me out of state"; Halvorsen closed its Tampa office years ago and approved Elena's request
— employee convenience at both firms, so the rule applies and full-year wage sourcing stands. Exact
residency end date: the client supplied no further facts, so 6/30 (181 days) is retained and the 7/3
alternative stays a documented residual (R3) — immaterial under the convenience rule.

## 6. Standard deduction, not itemizing

Itemized total (FL mortgage interest $6,480 for half a year + property taxes/withholding, both far short)
cannot approach $31,500 even ignoring the SALT cap. Schedule A rules are **not in the library**, so I
use the library's stated MFJ standard deduction outright. Rejected: attempting an itemized computation —
would rely on authority outside the library (SALT cap, interest allocation rules) and still lose. Client
answer (Q7): no Tampa property-tax figure exists — itemizing still loses on every available number
(residual R5).

## 7. Rental depreciation: $9,818 accepted as stated

$270,000 ÷ 27.5 = $9,818.18 → $9,818 per Elena's summary; consistent with 27.5-year residential
recovery. Rejected alternatives (mid-month convention → ~$9,694; verifying building/land split of the
$270,000 "building basis") — the library contains no depreciation authority, and the difference would
be ≤ $125; stated as accepted-from-file, not verified.

## 8. Form 8962 family size: 4 (per 1095-A), despite Leo not being a 2025 dependent

The library gives only the family-of-4 FPL row ($31,200), and all four were covered Jan–Jun.
**Rejected alternative:** family of 3 (taxpayer, spouse, Sofia) once Leo is claimed by his father —
the library contains no family-of-3 FPL row, so the alternative is not computable from authority here;
flagged as an assumption rather than forced. The 250–300% band would very likely not change (a lower
FPL pushes the percentage *up*, and at family-of-3 FPL ≈ $24,000-ish, income would be >350% FPL with a
larger applicable figure — but that FPL figure is not in the library, so it stays open).

## 9. Applicable figure: literal linear interpolation inside the 250–300% band

The library says "Table 2, linear within band," so 4% + (292.79−250)/50 × 2% = 5.7115%. **Rejected
alternative:** snapping to 25% grid rows of the real Table 2 (5.75% at 292.50–294.75% or similar) —
the real published table's row granularity is not in the library, and the library's own instruction is
linearity; the difference is pennies of monthly contribution either way.

## 10. Distributions 2023–2024 assumed tax-free returns of capital

Both years' distributions stayed under basis (24,100 vs. 12,000; 16,700 vs. 5,200), so no gain — and
the library gives no S-corp distribution (AAA/E&P) authority, so I ordered basis per the 7203 excerpt
(increase first, then distributions, then losses). Rejected: any ordering where distributions precede
income increases — would still not create gain here (14,300 vs 12,000), so the result is stable.

## 11. NY tax not computed

The library's IT-203 excerpt gives sourcing/allocation rules but no rate schedule, deductions, or

asks for, and state that any excess NY withholding is refunded per the excerpt. Flagged: the actual

## 12. Household income for 8962 = AGI as computed (no 469(i) addbacks)

The 8962 excerpt defines modified AGI as AGI + tax-exempt interest + nontaxable Social Security +
excluded foreign income. Nothing in the file adds back; notably it does **not** add back the passive
loss (unlike the 8582 MAGI). Rejected alternative: reusing the $110,900 §469(i) MAGI — wrong definition;
the two MAGIs differ by the allowed rental loss, and the return carries both (110,900 on 8582; 91,350
on 8962/1040).
