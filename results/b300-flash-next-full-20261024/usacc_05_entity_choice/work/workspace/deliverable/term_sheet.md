# Term sheet — Operating Agreement, Natarajan Herrera Advisory LLC (CA)

Framework from the practitioner checklist in `library/llc_buy_sell_checklist.md` (triggers, price, payment terms, vesting/repurchase, deadlock for 50/50, drag/tag, transfer restrictions, spousal consent). Tax anchors: 26 U.S.C. §83(b); Rev. Proc. 93-27 / 2001-43 (`library/irc_721_and_revproc_93-27.md`, `library/irc_83b.md`).

## 1. Units, capital, economics

- **Class A (Priya):** capital account $120,000 (PriceLab at appraised FMV; $31,000 tax basis carries in — §721). Fully vested. Built-in-gain tracking required (built-in-gain allocation rules outside library — flag for final drafting).
- **Class B (Tomás):** capital account $20,000 (cash). **Profits interest:** 50% of profits, **no share of existing capital beyond return of his $20,000** (preserves the Rev. Proc. 93-27 §4.01 "nothing on immediate liquidation at FMV" safe harbor — draft this liquidation waterfall literally).
- **Profits/losses: 50/50.** Capital on liquidation: return of capital accounts first, then profits split 50/50 per then-vested interests.
- No salaries/guaranteed payments (keeps distributive shares QBI-eligible — `library/irc_199a_sstb.md`); owner draws per a funding/distribution schedule amended by unanimous consent (**standing assumption**: flexible draws — client gave no draw preference; distributions above a member's tax basis need basis tracking, and Priya's outside basis starts at only ≈ $31,000 **[U]**).

## 2. Vesting (Tomás)

- 3-year schedule, **1-year cliff**: 1/3 vests at 12 months; remainder monthly over years 2–3.
- **Unvested profits interest repurchased by the LLC at cost** (cost = $0 for the profits interest; his $20,000 capital account is always his and is returned on exit/liquidation, offset only by agreed claims). Checklist: vesting of founder units with repurchase at cost for unvested units.
- Tomás treated as a partner from the grant date; **protective §83(b) within 30 days of grant, no extensions** (26 U.S.C. §83(b); Treas. Reg. §1.83-2).
- Optional (ask client — QUESTIONS.md): single-trigger acceleration on a company sale.

## 3. Buy-sell triggers and pricing

**Trigger events (all per checklist):** death; disability (>90 days); **divorce (spousal interest)**; bankruptcy/insolvency; voluntary departure; for-cause removal (fraud, felony, material breach).

- **Price for vested interests:** independent **appraisal** at trigger date (a Coastline-style FMV engagement is the working precedent); no formula discount on no-fault events (death/divorce/departure).
- **For-cause removal:** repurchase at **cost / formula discount** (discount for at-fault exits is contemplated by the checklist; percentage to be negotiated — not invented here).
- **Payment terms:** promissory note over ≤3 years with market interest + security interest in the units; life insurance on each founder sized to fund death triggers (insurance recommendation is practitioner practice [U]).
- Divorce trigger is exercised **against the entity/founder, not the marriage**: the company gets the units; the ex-spouse gets the price. Spouse never becomes a member.

## 4. Community property & family events (high priority — Mexico community property)

- **Spousal consent at signing — condition precedent.** Client confirms: Tomás's wife is in Mexico and **there is no prenuptial agreement**, so no community-property carve-out exists outside this agreement. She signs consent/assignment subordinating any community interest to this agreement's transfer and buy-sell terms (checklist: spousal consent in community-property jurisdictions); **closing cannot occur without her signature** (her willingness is the founders' open action item — see `QUESTIONS.md`).
- Priya is single today: same spousal-consent mechanism applies automatically if she later marries without a premarital carve-out (premarital agreements themselves are outside this agreement's scope [U]).
- Death: heirs (which in community property may include a surviving spouse) take **economic rights only**; company/founder option to purchase at appraisal price; no voting rights for heirs absent unanimous consent.

## 5. Governance & deadlock (50/50 is a machine for deadlock)

- Day-to-day: each founder manages assigned function (Priya: tool/models; Tomás: client relationships/sales) within a budget approved unanimously.
- **Unanimous consent** for: borrowing, new members, selling the business, changing line of business, distributions outside the schedule, hiring/firing key staff, related-party deals.
- **Deadlock ladder (checklist):** (1) principals meet within 15 days → (2) mediation (cost split) → (3) if unresolved in 45 days, **shotgun buy-sell**: either founder may serve a price notice; the other must buy the server's units or sell theirs at that same price. Draft the notice clock carefully — with 50/50 owners the shotgun is the credible threat that keeps mediation honest.
- Annual written buy-sell price agreement or appraisal-refresh option to shrink gap risk [U].

## 6. Transfer restrictions

- No transfers without **unanimous consent**; exception only for estate-planning vehicles that sign a joinder bound by this agreement [U form].
- **ROFR** on any consented sale; **tag-along** for the non-selling founder; **drag-along** once a bona fide 3rd-party offer for 100% gets unanimous approval at the governance level (checklist: drag/tag).
- Prohibited transfers void; putative transferees get economic-refund rights only, never membership.

## 7. Residency/status covenant (protects any future S election)

- Tomás covenants to report U.S.-day counts **per trip, logged monthly** (SPT flip points: 140 days in 2026, 128 in 2027 — §7701(b)(3)(A), `library/irc_7701b_substantial_presence.md`). His stated pattern — "maybe a third of the year" ≈ 122 days — projects **177.33 points for 2027 vs. the 183 threshold**: resident-status flip is ~6 days away, so the log governs, not recollections. He also covenants to deliver tax-residency determinations annually and to cooperate with a Form 2553 conversion decision each January (2-month/15-day window if ever eligible — `library/form_2553_deadline.md`).
- If an S election is ever in force, any event making Tomás a nonresident alien automatically terminates it (§1362(d)(2) note in `library/irc_1361_excerpt.md`) — the agreement should require notice within 15 days of any such risk.
- Immigration compliance for Tomás's TN is a **condition to services** pending counsel's opinion (outside library — open item).

## 8. IP & non-compete hygiene

- PriceLab contribution includes assignment of all updates/derivatives to the LLC; Tomás's client relationships developed post-formation belong to the LLC.
- Mutual in-service non-solicit/non-compete limited to pricing-strategy consulting; post-exit non-solicit of employees/active clients for 12 months (scope/duration are negotiating points [U]).
