# Advice to the CEO — Mesa Steel Supply / Redwood Fabrication (Chapter 11)

**Date: March 19, 2026.** Redwood filed Chapter 11 on March 10, 2026 (Bankruptcy Court,
District of Colorado, Case No. 26-11842-TBM). Redwood owes us **$125,000** on five open
invoices, all delivered before the filing. You asked to "file something and get paid
first." Here is the plain-English answer: filing a proof of claim does **not** get us
paid first — it only keeps us in the game. The only legitimate routes to early payment
are the 20-day administrative claim (which we already have for $78,100 of the debt) and
Redwood's pending critical-vendor motion. And before anything else: **we are currently
breaking the law every day the AR team keeps collecting.**

---

## 1. What to stop TODAY, and why

**Stop all collection activity against Redwood — immediately, in writing, today.**
Three things already happened after the filing that the bankruptcy automatic stay
(11 U.S.C. §362(a)(6)) prohibits — the stay stops "any act to collect, assess, or
recover a claim" that arose before the filing, and it applies to everyone whether or
not we received formal notice:

| Date | What we did | Why it is a stay violation |
|---|---|---|
| Mar 12 | "FINAL NOTICE — pay $210,000 within 5 days" letter, mailed and emailed | A post-petition dunning letter demanding payment is an act to collect |
| Mar 15 | Collection call to Redwood AP; our clerk said "that's not our problem, you owe us" | A collection call is an act to collect — and it proves we knew about the case from this date |
| Mar 18 | Email to Redwood's CFO threatening to hold shipments and **report to credit bureaus** | Threatening credit-bureau reporting to extract payment is an act to collect, done knowingly after we were told about the filing |

The consequence: under §362(k), a party injured by a **willful** violation of the stay
"shall recover **actual damages, including costs and attorneys' fees, and, in
appropriate circumstances, punitive damages**"; the library notes courts also sanction
creditors under §105. A judge looking at a demand letter two days after the filing, a call where our
clerk dismissed the Chapter 11, and a credit-bureau threat the day after we were told
about the case, will see a pattern. That exposure runs the other way from our
$125,000 claim — we could end up paying Redwood.

Also stop, today:
- **Any further letters, calls, emails, or credit-bureau reporting** about pre-filing
  invoices. Route every Redwood contact through counsel.
- **Do not send a "cleanup" letter on your own.** Even a retraction is a contact; let
  counsel decide whether a withdrawal, sent through proper channels, helps.
- **Do not enforce the "oldest invoice paid first" terms or condition new shipments on
  paying the old invoices.** Making Redwood pay pre-filing debts as the price of new
  steel is collecting those debts. (Whether refusing to ship at all also violates the
  stay is not addressed in our verified reference set — flagged as unverified; the
  safe course is that counsel, not the AR desk, makes every shipping decision.)
- **Keep shipping decisions separate from old debt.** Post-petition deliveries to
  Redwood should be on administrative (current-pay) terms arranged through counsel or
  court order — otherwise we are giving new steel to a bankrupt customer for free.

One more problem to fix in the numbers: the March 12 letter demands **$210,000**, but
that is the **gross** total of all six January–March invoices without crediting the
$85,000 Redwood actually paid us (48,200 + 36,800). The true balance is **$125,000**.
AR demanded a number inflated by $85,000 — before we sign a claim under penalty of
perjury, AR must confirm there is no other Redwood account and correct the demand
(AR responded 2026-03-19 that it has no additional information, so this stays open as a
confirmation item — `QUESTIONS.md` item 5). We will file at $125,000, the only figure
our documents support.

  administrative expense for the value of goods received in that window; freight and
  services do not count). Administrative claims normally outrank ordinary unsecured
  debts and are typically paid in full in a plan *(that priority-and-plan treatment is
  standard practice but is not restated in our reference library — flagged unverified)*.
  **This is our real "get paid
  first" asset — not the CEO's filing idea, but the statute.**
- **$46,900 is general unsecured** (the MS-7740 balance, freight, and cutting
  services) — this is the layer that usually takes the biggest haircut.
- **Deadline: June 11, 2026, 5:00 p.m. Mountain** — the bar date for proofs of claim.
  Miss it and we cannot vote or be paid, absent a showing of excusable neglect. The
  §503(b)(9) piece is asserted through the procedure the court's bar-date order sets
  (usually a separate motion) — we are obtaining that order. Note: the notice we hold
  is internally inconsistent (it says "Notice dated: April 2, 2026" — a future date);
  counsel should confirm the operative bar date from the actual order.
- **Reclamation (getting our steel back):** §546(c) lets a seller demand return of
  goods received in the 45 days before filing (Jan 24 – Mar 10), but only by **written
  demand no later than March 30, 2026** (20 days after the filing) — our "pay us"
  letter does not count because it demanded money, not goods. Frankly, its practical
  value is low: the reference material states the DIP lender holds a blanket lien ahead
  of reclamation, and the steel may already be cut or consumed. Decision needed by
  **March 27** so a conforming demand can be served by March 30.

## 3. What Mesa may owe REDWOOD

Two headaches we must plan for:

**(a) Preference exposure — realistically $0, theoretically up to $85,000.**
Every dollar Redwood paid us in the 90 days before its filing (Dec 10, 2025 – Mar 10,
2026) can be clawed back under §547 if it let us collect more than a liquidation
payout — insolvency in that window is presumed. We received **$180,000** in that
window (Dec 18: 51,000; Jan 22: 44,000; Feb 10: 48,200; Feb 27: 36,800 — wire).
Two defenses, and the burden is on us (§547(g)):

- *Ordinary course:* the Dec 18 and Jan 22 ACH payments (45 and 43 days after
  invoice) exactly match our 12-month history of 43–47-day ACH payments on NET-45
  terms — about **$95,000 protected**. The Feb 10 payment (29 days, after we cut
  terms to NET 15) and the Feb 27 **wire we demanded** (25 days) look like pressure
  and unusual methods — **$85,000 exposed**. Lesson for the credit manager: demanding
  a wire from a struggling customer *creates* clawback exposure.
- *New value:* we kept shipping without pay after those transfers — goods and
  services worth **$99,400 remain wholly unpaid** (all of it is in our $125,000
  claim). Unpaid new value given after a transfer offsets preference liability; it
  covers the entire $85,000 exposure on the arithmetic. **Net exposure estimate: $0**
  (worst case $85,000 if both defenses fail; an unsettled point not in our verified
  reference set is whether a court's payment of our §503(b)(9) claim later destroys
  part of that new-value shield — another reason to settle the preference file
  within the plan process, not fight it in a vacuum).
- *The trap — §502(d):* if we owe a preference and don't pay it, the court **disallows
  our entire $125,000 claim** — no voting, no distributions — until we pay it. Our
  preference defenses must be documented now, not later.

**(b) Stay damages.** The three violations in section 1: Redwood's estate is exactly
the kind of plaintiff that sues over this, and our $210,000 letter vs. $125,000 of real
ledger support makes us look worse. Stop-the-clock today limits the damage.

## 4. The critical-vendor path (the only honest "paid first" route)

Redwood filed a **critical-vendor motion (Dkt. 44), hearing April 2, 2026**, and its
notice says creditors wanting consideration must contact the debtor's counsel. Under
the *Kmart* practice note, a debtor may seek authority to pay pre-filing claims of
vendors whose **continued supply is essential and who cannot be replaced**, and the
vendor **must typically agree to customary trade terms for the case**. It is the
debtor's business judgment and the court's
call — not a right. Action: if we want to keep supplying Redwood, tell counsel today
to contact Redwood's lawyers before the April 2 hearing, and be ready to commit to
normal trade terms going forward. Being listed as a critical vendor could pay part or
all of our $125,000 early; refusing to ship and just waiting is how unsecured creditors
take haircuts.

## 7. "But they owe *us*!" — answering the sales team's question

Frank asked, reasonably: *how can we possibly pay back money we were given for steel we
actually delivered, when Redwood still owes us $210,000?* Four answers:

1. **The balance is $125,000, not $210,000.** We invoiced $210,000 total; Redwood already
   paid $85,000 of it. Anyone quoting $210,000 is counting money we've received twice —
   the same mistake that produced the March 12 letter.
2. **Nobody owes Redwood anything today.** This is not a bill; it is the *risk* that
   Redwood's estate could sue to take back payments it made in the 90 days before it
   filed. Those are "preference" claims, and they aim at every vendor Redwood paid in
   that window. The rule is about fairness among creditors, not an accusation about Mesa.
3. **A court really can unwind payments for steel we delivered — that's how §547 works**
   — but the same law gives two offsets, and on our facts they cover every dollar:
   *ordinary course* keeps safe the payments that matched our twelve-month history
   ($95,000 of the $180,000: 45-day and 43-day ACHs like always); and the **new-value
   defense** says every dollar of goods and services we shipped *after* they paid us,
   for which we were never paid, cancels the clawback dollar-for-dollar. We shipped
   $99,400 unpaid after the two vulnerable February payments — more than the $85,000
   they could target. That is why our estimated payback exposure is **$0**, with
   $85,000 only the worst case if the defense collapsed entirely.
4. **This connects to Frank's own call.** Redwood's counsel asked whether Mesa would keep
   supplying — we are an essential supplier — and the same lawyer may float a preference
   suit as negotiating pressure. The answer to that pressure is the arithmetic above,
   plus reversing "pay first" through counsel before April 2.

The one real trap to remember: if a court ever did order a payback and we refused to
pay it, §502(d) would bar Mesa from receiving **any distribution on the whole $125,000
claim** until it was paid. That is why the payment history, the January 20 terms email
and the wire-demand records get *preserved and organized* — they are the evidence that
proves both defenses — and why nobody deletes anything.
