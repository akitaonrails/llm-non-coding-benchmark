# Professional fees — what may be paid, to whom, and when

**Updated to reflect Dkt. 77 as actually entered.** The earlier version of this memo assumed, from a
one-line note in the March invoice file, an "80% interim / 100% holdback" procedure and a $112,000.00
ceiling. The order in the client file (`documents/session2/interim_compensation_order_excerpt.md`)
supersedes that assumption: **80% of fees *and 100% of expenses*** are payable absent objection, and the
holdback is **20% of fees only**. Figures below are corrected ($116,200.00, not $112,000.00).

Authorities: 11 U.S.C. §§ 330(a), 331 (library/bc_330_331.md) — professionals employed under § 327 are
paid **only upon application and court award**, or under **interim compensation procedures ordered by
the court**; payments outside those procedures are **subject to disgorgement**. DIP loan proceeds may
fund only "administrative expenses allowed by the Court" (Dkt. 61 ¶ 2, documents/dip_financing_order_excerpt.pdf).
Reported on MOR Part 5 (library/ust_mor_instructions.md). The procedures order itself is **Dkt. 77,
entered 4/14/2026**.

## The Dkt. 77 mechanics (all payments below run through this)

1. Retained professional serves a **monthly fee statement**.
2. **Absent objection within 14 days**, the Debtor **may pay 80% of fees and 100% of expenses**.
3. The **20% holdback (of fees) is paid only upon Court approval** of an interim or final fee
   application (§ 330 award; § 331 procedure).
4. **No payment for any period before the professional's retention date.** Retentions per Dkt. 77:
   Holland & Pierce **nunc pro tunc to 3/10/2026**; Corbin Advisors **effective 3/20/2026**.

Payments outside these mechanics risk § 330/§ 331 disgorgement. A payment ripened under Dkt. 77 is a
Court-authorized administrative expense, so it also clears the Dkt. 61 ¶ 2 restriction on DIP proceeds;
any amount beyond the ripened interim entitlement requires a § 330 application and award first.
