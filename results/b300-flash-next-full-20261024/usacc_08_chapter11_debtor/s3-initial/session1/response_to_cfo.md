## 1. Paying Mesa's February invoice ($62,400) out of the DIP draw — **No.**

Three independent blocks:

1. **The DIP order forbids it.** Final DIP Order, Dkt. 61 ¶ 2 (documents/dip_financing_order_excerpt.pdf): DIP proceeds "shall be used solely for post-petition operating expenses, administrative expenses allowed by the Court, and the Carve-Out. **Nothing herein authorizes payment of any pre-petition claim except as separately ordered by this Court.**" Mesa's February claim is pre-petition (invoice dated 2/2/2026, before the 3/10/2026 petition) and no order separately authorizes it.
2. **Mesa is not a critical vendor.** Critical Vendor Order, Dkt. 48, Schedule 1 (documents/critical_vendor_order.pdf) lists **only Apex Coatings, Inc. ($31,200)** within a $35,000 aggregate cap, and states: "**No other vendor is included.** Any motion to add a vendor requires notice and a hearing." Paying Mesa anyway is outside the order.
3. **It would be an avoidable post-petition transfer.** 11 U.S.C. § 549(a) (library/bc_362_363_503_549.md): payment of a pre-petition unsecured claim outside the ordinary course and without a court order is an unauthorized post-petition transfer, avoidable by the trustee and **recoverable from the recipient** — i.e., a later fiduciary could claw the $62,400 back from Mesa and surcharge us for the mess. Your setoff idea has the same defect dressed differently; see item 2.

Bonus problem: Dkt. 61 ¶ 1 allows only 10% per-line-item variance from the Approved Budget on a cumulative four-week basis. A $62,400 unbudgeted payment is a DIP default risk on top of everything else.

## 2. Netting the February invoice against what Mesa owes us for returned coils — **No.**

Dkt. 61 ¶ 3 is explicit: "**No setoff, recoupment or netting of DIP proceeds against pre-petition obligations is permitted.**" Reducing the payment to Mesa by the coil credit is still applying value to a pre-petition obligation, and the funds we'd pay from are DIP proceeds. (Whether the estate independently holds a setoff right under § 553 is **not covered by the library I am permitted to rely on — unverified, so I do not rely on it**; it would not matter anyway, because ¶ 3 governs our side of the transaction regardless.)

**Supplement — the coils are about $11,000 (per client, returned January).** That makes it worse, not better. The January return predates the petition, so both sides of your "net" are pre-petition: Mesa's $62,400 claim and the estate's ~$11,000 receivable for the coils. "Netting" would (i) pay $51,400 in DIP cash toward a pre-petition claim — the exact transfer Dkt. 61 ¶¶ 2–3 and § 549(a) prohibit, and (ii) additionally **give away an $11,000 estate asset** (our receivable against Mesa) without court approval. Even for financial-statement purposes, "pre-petition liabilities are not netted against post-petition assets" (ASC 852-10-45-9, library/asc852_fresh_start.md). The receivable must be billed and collected separately — it belongs to the estate, not to Mesa's ledger. If we later get an order adding Mesa, we pay the allowed amount and chase the $11,000 as a separate collection item.

## 3. "We'll tell counsel after" — that is exactly the problem.

Paying a pre-petition claim without an order (Dkt. 61 ¶ 2, Dkt. 48) and paying professionals without a court award (§ 330, below) are both violations that run to **me personally** (payments outside § 330/§ 331 procedures are "subject to disgorgement," 11 U.S.C. §§ 330(a), 331, library/bc_330_331.md) and expose the estate to § 549(a) avoidance. I will not sign that payment, before or after counsel finds out.

## 4. Holland & Pierce's $140,000 — **Not now; on a defined timetable.**

Professionals "are paid only upon application and court award (§ 330) or under interim compensation procedures ordered by the court (§ 331)" (11 U.S.C. §§ 330(a), 331, library/bc_330_331.md). As of today there is no award in favor of Holland & Pierce. Their 3/31/2026 invoice for $140,000 is a post-petition professional fee that must go through the fee-application process; in the March MOR Part 5 it is reported as **holdback** ($140,000, with Corbin Advisors' $45,000, total holdback $185,000; approved $0, paid $0).

**Month-end addendum:** the interim compensation order was entered 4/14/2026 (80%/100% holdback). Under that order the estate may pay up to 80% of the interim-awarded amount with 100% held back as security — i.e., **up to $112,000** ($140,000 × 0.80, subject to the order's terms and the covered period), and the balance only on final award of the application. April's MOR and the Session 2 fee memo will work this through. Nothing is payable in the March reporting period.
