**Yes, I'm sure — and it's not my judgment call, it's the Delaware LLC Act.** The library's §18-1101(c) says, verbatim: *"It is the policy of this chapter to give the maximum effect to the principle of freedom of contract… [a] manager has duties (**including fiduciary duties**)… **may be expanded or restricted or eliminated by provisions in the limited liability company agreement**."* So managers *do* owe loyalty — **unless the LLC agreement takes it away.** Ours does: §7.3 of the agreement eliminates the Manager's duties of care and loyalty. The articles describe what happens when nobody contracted around the default. Imani signed around it.

And while §7.3 can kill fiduciary duties, the statute itself forbids killing the implied covenant of good faith (§18-1101(c) proviso, §18-1101(e)) — and *Dieckman* (our library case) holds that a manager's "good faith" discretion — like Bram's §6.3 valuation — must be exercised honestly, *not arbitrarily*, even where duties are waived.
