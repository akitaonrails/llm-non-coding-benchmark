# Reply to buyer's counsel (J. Whitaker)

**Re: Brightline Cabinetry APA — Schedule 3.3, WARN, and §7.4(c)**

## 1. Schedule 3.3 cannot say "None."

Your draft assumes there are no consent issues. The data room (provided to you) shows there are:

- **Hensley Homes Master Supply Agreement (data room §1.1, 38% of revenue).** §18 bars assignment "whether by operation of law, merger, sale of assets, change of control or otherwise" absent prior written consent, "which may be withheld in its sole discretion," and makes any purported assignment **void**. In an asset sale that language is enforced according to its terms, so closing without consent would leave the largest customer contract void as to the buyer (`anti_assignment_change_of_control.md`: *SQL Solutions v. Oracle*, 1991 WL 626458 (N.D. Cal.); Restatement (Second) of Contracts §§317, 322). Our proposal: Schedule 3.3 lists Hensley and the shop-lease landlord as required consents, seller uses commercially reasonable efforts pre-closing, and receipt **without modification of economic terms** is a mutual closing condition. On process: the Hensley relationship is an owner-level relationship, so outreach should be sequenced by the seller — under confidentiality, with any buyer participation pre-agreed — before any formal consent request goes out; a consent letter arriving cold is how a workable conversation becomes a re-trade. If Hensley seeks modified terms, we should have a pre-agreed mechanism (corresponding price adjustment or a walk right for either party) rather than a post-signing dispute.
- **Shop lease (data room §3.1).** Landlord consent required, "not to be unreasonably withheld" — listed on Schedule 3.3; being requested immediately.
- **Cascade Builders (data room §1.2).** Assignment permitted to a successor to substantially all assets **upon notice** — no consent needed; we will deliver the written notice as a closing deliverable on a notice schedule.
- **Twenty-two residential contracts (data room §1.3).** Silent on assignment — rights are generally assignable and performance obligations need consent only where personal (Restatement §§317, 322); we will send customer notices and novate where a homeowner contracted for Brightline's personal performance.
- **Spray-booth permit (data room §4.1)** is non-transferable; your client must reapply (City says 30-60 days). That is a timing/closing-sequencing item — our redline adds a signing-time covenant to file and cooperation, since "sign in two weeks" only works if the permit runway starts now.

## 2. WARN — no notice is required, and here is the arithmetic behind it

WARN covers employers of **100+ employees** (excluding part-time) and plant closings/mass layoffs affecting **50+ employees at a single site** (29 U.S.C. §2101(a)(1); 20 C.F.R. §639.3). Brightline has **14 W-2 employees** (data room §2.1). Even if the 9 hourly installers currently paid on 1099s (data room §2.2) are counted as employees for the analysis — which, given the control facts in §2.2, we think is the conservative view — the total is **23**, far below 100. No WARN notice is required, including if your buyer does not hire everyone, and Texas has no mini-WARN statute (`warn_act.md`). So: we confirm headcount is below threshold, with the express caveat that you should not read our confirmation as an endorsement of the 1099 labels — see item 3 and your own successor-liability analysis (buyer-side FLSA successor exposure and Texas UI successor provisions: `worker_classification_tx_irs.md`; Tex. Lab. Code §201.041; 40 TAC §815.134).

## 3. §7.4(c) — our position

Characterizing §7.4(c) as "market-standard knowledge language" is not accurate: the clause preserves buyer recovery **regardless of knowledge, before or after signing** — that is a **pro-sandbagging** clause, and both pro- and anti-sandbagging positions are in practice; neither is simply "market" (ABA Private Target Deal Points Study, per `sandbagging.md`; *Akorn v. Fresenius* (Del. Ch. 2018), cited in our library only as dicta).

You know from the data room that nine installers do not look like contractors: exclusive service, Brightline trucks and tools, daily scheduling, hourly pay, multi-year tenure — that is the employee pattern under the common-law test (IRS Rev. Rul. 87-41, per `worker_classification_tx_irs.md`). We will not give a clean §4.12 representation on a known issue, and we will not accept a pro-sandbagging clause layered onto it; that combination would just be an indemnity for a disclosed fact with a $3.1M cap and a statute-of-limitations tail (your 7.4(b)/(d)), which is off-market for an operational rep (ABA Deal Points Study, per `baskets_caps.md`).

**Our counter-package**, which we think gets your client real protection rather than paper protection:

1. Schedule 4.12 discloses the 1099 arrangement in full; §4.12 is expressly qualified by it (so it cannot be "breached" as to the disclosed facts);
2. a **special indemnity** for pre-closing employment-tax and wage claims tied to those nine individuals, **no basket, negotiated cap in the $225k-$300k range, survival to the statute of limitations + 60 days**, secured by a separate small holdback — the only exposure structure that actually maps to your client's real risk, since your buyer must reclassify going forward regardless. The number is arithmetic, not posturing: ~3 years of employer-side payroll tax on $612,000/yr of payments (≈ $141.6k) plus the overtime premium on the hours-over-40 pattern your own diligence can see (≈ $75.9k-$144.9k at the disclosed rates and hours), before penalties; we will trade the underlying hours data to refine it, and if earlier-year payment history changes the math we will re-open it in *both* directions; and
3. deletion of §7.4(c) in favor of an anti-sandbagging / disclosed-matters exclusion.

If your client would rather have the classification issue priced than papered, we are open to that conversation as well.
