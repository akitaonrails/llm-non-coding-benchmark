Each entry: the question, the call made, and the alternative rejected (with effect if reversed).

## 1. S-corp loss: basis-limited, not "straight off" (Form 7203)
**Call:** Beginning 2025 stock basis = $11,500 by rollforward (contributions + income first,
then distributions, then losses, per Form 7203 instructions excerpt); allowed 11,500, suspended
16,500; debt basis $0 because Pike (not Marcus) guarantees the bank line and a guarantee never
creates debt basis.
**Rejected:** Marcus's friend's advice to deduct the full $28,000 against 1040 income, and his
follow-up objection that Pike's guarantee should supply extra basis. Rejected as contrary to the
basis limitation in the only authority in the library: the guarantee is Pike's (not Marcus's) and
a guarantee never creates debt basis regardless of who holds it — only a direct shareholder loan
does **[7203]**. Basis is per-shareholder, so the friend's full deduction implies the friend had
basis (loans/contributions/prior income), not that the rule is optional. Reversal effect: AGI
would drop another 16,500 (to 74,850) — not available.
*Sub-call resolved:* client confirmed no contributions since 2021, no shareholder loans, and
that Pike (not Marcus) guarantees the bank line — the rollforward and zero debt basis stand.

## 2. Below-market rent on the Brooklyn condo (biggest swing item)
**Call:** Treat the condo as a rental activity and allow the loss (through the §469(i) allowance).
The library states the below-market rule (IRC §280A(d)(2)(C), personal-use days, no loss) but
then carves out exactly these facts: "Renting below market to an unrelated tenant because rent
was never raised does not by itself trigger this rule if the rent is a fair rental for that
lease" **[8582]**. Tenant is unrelated, 6-year lease, rent simply never raised ($1,500 vs ~$2,600
market).
**Rejected:** treating every day as a personal-use day, which would knock the entire rental
activity off Schedule E — no 18,000 rent, no 29,200+9,818 deductions, no Form 8582 special
allowance. AGI would rise by 19,550 to 110,900 (taxable income 110,900 − 31,500 = 79,400 —
**outside the library's tax-table excerpt**, an additional problem), and Form 8962 would take a
higher income and a larger repayment.
*Client-confirmed:* the tenant is a stranger — a nurse found through a broker in 2019 — and the
rent was simply never raised, which is exactly the library's carve-out. Residual: "fair rental
for that lease" is still not defined in the library, and the Zillow figures are 2019 estimates,
unverified (discount ≈42%).

individual SLCSP or whether the father claimed any marketplace credit for Leo, and the library
cannot support a 3-person computation anyway (no 3-person FPL, no shared-policy allocation rule).

## 12. NY sourcing of the Brooklyn rental — outside-library assumption (added on pre-delivery audit)
**Finding (audit):** the IT-203 excerpt taxes nonresidents on "New York source income" but never
defines which income is NY-source; it says nowhere that real-property income is sourced to the
property's location. The workpaper's statement that the condo rental is NY-source for the entire
year states the standard rule but is **outside the library** and is now labeled an assumption
(`return.md` NY section; `return.json` ny.method).
**Impact:** none on deliverable fields — the NY tax computation is not a deliverable (no NY rate
tables in the library), and the rental loss is fully used on the federal Form 8582 regardless of NY
sourcing. If the IT-203 is later prepared under actual NY forms instructions, the sourcing rule
must be cited there from that authority.
**Rejected:** leaving the sourcing rule asserted as if library-supported (the pre-audit state), and
equally unsupported opposite treatment (rent non-NY during the nonresident period) — the library
is silent in both directions, so the assumption is retained and labeled.
