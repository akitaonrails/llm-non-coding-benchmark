# Judgment Calls

Every place a choice was required, the choice made, and the alternative rejected.

## 1. S-corp loss: basis-limited, not "straight off"

**Facts:** Marcus's K-1 shows a $28,000 ordinary loss; his stock basis at 1/1/25 is $11,500 (built up from his 2021 $10,000 contribution and 2021–2024 income, distributions); he made no direct shareholder loans; Pike personally guarantees the company's bank line. Marcus expects the loss to "come straight off" their income.

**Decision:** Deduct only **$11,500** in 2025; suspend **$16,500** as a carryforward. Losses are allowed only to the extent of adjusted stock basis plus debt basis (IRC §1366(d) via IRS Form 7203 Instructions 2025). Pike's *personal guarantee* does not create debt basis — only a direct loan from the shareholder to the corporation would — so Marcus's debt basis is $0.

**Alternatives rejected:**
- *Marcus's "straight off" position* (deduct full $28,000): rejected — basis is $11,500, and the 7203 instructions are explicit that excess losses are suspended.
- *Treating Pike's guarantee as Marcus's debt basis* (which would have allowed more of the loss): rejected — the 7203 instructions state a guarantee of corporate debt does not create debt basis. If Marcus actually *lent* the company money (no evidence he did), the analysis would change.

**Client follow-up (Marcus challenged the suspension, asked about Pike's guarantee):** Re-answered from the same authority. The suspension is a mechanical basis test, not a preparer judgment; a comparable return with the full loss deducted would have had sufficient basis (more capital, fewer distributions, or a direct shareholder loan). Pike's guarantee cannot count. The one available lever is a direct loan from Marcus to the company in 2025 (each dollar creates debt basis; up to $16,500 could be released this year, with a cascading effect of roughly +$6,000 refund per the estimate in `QUESTIONS.md` Q1). Client provided no further information, so the option was **not elected**; the return stands as prepared. If Marcus actually makes the loan before filing, the return must be recomputed (open item in `STATUS.md`).

## 2. Leo is not a Reyes dependent in 2025

**Facts:** Leo (9) is Elena's son from before; he resides primarily with Elena. The 2019 custody stipulation (NY Family Court V-04418-19) makes the parents alternate: **father claims in odd years, mother in even years**, with Form 8332 executed by the custodial parent for the other's years. 2025 is an odd year.

**Decision:** Claim **Sofia only**. For 2025 the father (Castellano) claims Leo. Where a decree/agreement alternates years, "the agreement controls which parent claims the child for that year" (IRS 2025 Form 1040 Instructions). CTC = 1 child × $2,200 = $2,200.

**Alternatives rejected:**
- *Claiming Leo as Elena's dependent* on the theory that he resides primarily with her: rejected — the decree controls for 2025; also, a child claimed by the other parent under a release/decree is not a qualifying child for the CTC (1040 Instructions).
- *Claiming Leo without an 8332 in the file:* not possible anyway; the 8332 for the father's year will be executed by Elena (the decree requires it). Reyes do not need the 8332 for their own return.

## 3. Below-market rent: loss still allowed

**Facts:** The Brooklyn condo rents at $1,500/month to the same unrelated tenant of 6 years; market is ~$2,600. Rent was never raised. Loss before limitation: ($21,018).

**Decision:** Treat the activity as a **passive rental activity** and allow the loss through the $25,000 special allowance ($19,550 allowed; $1,468 carryforward). The 8582 instructions say that while below-market rentals can be treated as personal use (IRC §280A(d)(2)(C), which would kill the loss), "renting below market to an unrelated tenant because rent was never raised does not by itself trigger this rule if the rent is a fair rental for that lease." That sentence matches this fact pattern exactly: unrelated tenant, rent never raised.

**Alternatives rejected:**
- *Treating all rental days as personal use* (no loss at all; $18,000 of income still reported): this is the aggressive-conservative fallback if the IRS viewed the rent as below fair rental value; rejected because the library's instructions explicitly carve out the "never raised / unrelated tenant" situation, and the loss was allowed under the special allowance instead.
- *Imputing market rent* (extra income, larger loss): rejected — no support in the library, and the tenant is unrelated with an existing lease.
- Note: the loss is in any event capped by the 8582 special allowance, so the below-market question did not change the 2025 deduction amount; it determined *whether* the loss exists at all.

## 4. NY residency period

**Facts:** Brooklyn lease ended 6/30/25; family slept at Elena's mother's in Queens 6/28–30, then drove down; Tampa closed 7/3/25, moved in that weekend.

**Decision:** **Resident period Jan 1 – Jun 30 (181 days); nonresident Jul 1 – Dec 31 (184 days).** The family was physically in New York through 6/30 (including the last three days at a relative's in Queens), and NY residence is generally maintained until the taxpayer's domicile/intent and physical presence move; the new FL home was not theirs until 7/3.

**Alternatives rejected:**
- *Residency ending 6/27* (when they left the apartment): rejected — they were still domiciled in and present in NY (Queens) through 6/30, the lease-termination date.
- *Residency ending 7/2* (day before the FL closing): rejected — no fact shows any NY tie after 6/30, and the closing/move-in was 7/3; the clean and defensible line is the lease end / last NY day, 6/30. The 1-day difference changes Elena's allocation by ~$148, which is immaterial to the refund but the 6/30 line is the better-supported one.

## 5. Convenience-of-the-employer split between Marcus and Elena (NY source of post-move wages)

**Facts:** Both employers are NYC-based. Marcus "just works remotely now" from Tampa after the family's voluntary move. "Halvorsen let me go fully remote." Both employers withheld NY all year because forms weren't updated.

**Decision (per NYS IT-203-I (2025) excerpt):**
- **Marcus: all $68,000 is NY source.** Post-move remote work for *his own convenience* (he chose to move the family to Tampa) triggers the convenience-of-the-employer rule → NY source. (Resident-period wages: $33,720.55; nonresident NY-source: $34,279.45.)
- **Elena: only $26,778 (181/365 of $54,000) is NY source.** Her employer *permitted* her to work fully remote; remote work at the employer's allowance is not the employee's own convenience, so post-move wages are not NY source.

**Alternatives rejected:**
- *Treat both as fully NY (mirror the withholding):* rejected for Elena — the IT-203 carve-out applies; withholding is not a source determination (excess withholding is refunded on IT-203).
- *Treat both as non-NY after 6/30:* rejected for Marcus — the convenience-of-employer rule makes his post-move wages NY source.
- The employer-permitted vs. own-convenience line is a factual call from the email language; if Halvorsen's remote policy is really a neutral policy Marcus also invoked, the split could shift. No question was raised because the email's own words ("Halvorsen let me go fully remote" vs. "he just works remotely now") are the best evidence available.

## 6. Standard deduction over itemized

**Decision:** Standard deduction **$31,500** (MFJ, IRS 2025 Form 1040 Instructions). Itemized would be at most the $6,480 Tampa mortgage interest plus any itemizable expenses — well under $31,500. (SALT/state-tax itemization is not in the library and the amount would not be close to the standard deduction; noted as unverified rather than cited.)

## 7. MAGI for the 8582 special allowance includes the S-corp loss

**Decision:** MAGI = $110,900 (wages + interest − *allowed* $11,500 S-corp loss, *excluding* the passive rental loss). The 8582 instructions expressly state that nonpassive losses allowed in the year (an S-corp loss where the shareholder materially participates, to the extent allowed by basis) **do** reduce MAGI, while passive losses do not.

**Alternative rejected:** MAGI of $122,400 (ignoring the S-corp loss) — would give a larger allowance ($25,000 − 0) but contradicts the instructions' explicit treatment; or MAGI of $91,350 (including the rental loss) — wrong because the allowance computes MAGI *without* passive losses.

## 8. 8962 household = family of 4 (Leo included for FPL)

**Decision:** FPL family of 4 = $31,200, and household income $91,350 is the couple's modified AGI (Leo is not required to file; he contributes no income). Leo is on the 1095-A and resides with Elena, so the household size is 4.

**Alternatives rejected:**
- *Family of 3* (excluding Leo because Reyes can't claim him): rejected — dependency status is not the household-size driver here; the 1095-A and living arrangements define the household, and the 4-person FPL is in the library.
- *Using the SLCSP premium as the "enrollment premium"*: rejected — the 1095-A columns are explicit: A = enrollment premium ($10,320), B = SLCSP ($10,140), C = APTC ($9,900).
