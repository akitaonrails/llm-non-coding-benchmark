Short answer, Marcus: because your basis is only $11,500, and the rule is unambiguous. Here's the whole picture in your numbers.

**The rule (Form 7203 excerpt — the library's authority):** your share of S-corp losses is deductible **only up to your stock basis plus debt basis** (that's IRC §1366(d)). Anything over that is suspended and carries forward until your basis is restored. It's a mechanical limit on *you*, not a judgment call.

**Why your basis is $11,500, and not more:**

| | |
|---|---:|
| Cash you put in (2021) | +10,000 |
| Your share of income 2021–2024, net of the 2021 loss | +18,700 |
| **Sum drawn out: 2023 $12,000 + 2024 $5,200** | **−17,200** |
| **Stock basis at 1/1/2025** | **11,500** |

Here's the part that matters: 10,000 + 18,700 = **28,700** — if you had never taken those distributions, your basis would have absorbed the **entire $28,000 loss**. You took home $17,200 since 2023, and that's exactly what ate the cushion. So the loss doesn't "come straight off" because you already effectively took $17,200 of it out as tax-free basis.

**Can we count Pike's guarantee? No — and this one's not close.** The excerpt says outright: *"A guarantee of corporate debt does not create debt basis; only a direct loan from the shareholder to the corporation does."* Pike signing the bank line puts Pike's credit at risk, not yours, and you never lent the company a dollar (your own note says so). The only way Pike's guarantee could ever help you is if you **actually paid on it** and treated that as a loan — which you didn't and haven't.

**About your buddy:** his CPA didn't find a loophole — his shareholder had basis his doesn't. Your friend either never drew money out, kept capital in, or made a real loan to his company (that's what creates debt basis). Basis is shareholder-specific; his facts just differ from yours.

**What happens to the $16,500:** it's suspended indefinitely — not lost. It becomes deductible when your basis is restored, which happens automatically with future S-corp income, or if you make new capital contributions or a genuine direct loan to the company.
