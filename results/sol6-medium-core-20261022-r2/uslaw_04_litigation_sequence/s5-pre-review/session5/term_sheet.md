# Proposed settlement term sheet — for negotiation, 22 October 2026

**Parties:** Juniper Health Staffing, LLC and Pinecrest Senior Living, Inc.  
**Matter:** All disputes arising from their January 15, 2024 Staffing Services Agreement through the settlement effective date, including listed invoices, hiring/conversion fees, interest, and any associated claim or defense.

## Business terms

1. **Settlement payment:** Pinecrest pays Juniper **$335,000** in cleared funds. This is an all-in compromise of principal, any contractual interest, and each side's potential fee or cost claims for this matter. It is a proposed counteroffer, not an agreement already reached.
2. **Timing:** Pinecrest pays **$265,200 within 10 business days** after signing and the remaining **$69,800 within 30 calendar days** after signing; $265,200 + $69,800 = $335,000. Juniper supplies wire instructions through a verified channel. A party receiving a changed wire instruction must confirm it by calling a known contact number.
3. **Payment protection:** No release by Juniper takes effect until all $335,000 is received in cleared funds. If Pinecrest misses a payment, Juniper gives written notice and five business days to cure. If the default is not cured, the unpaid settlement balance becomes immediately due; Juniper may pursue enforcement under the parties' agreement. Amounts actually paid are credited. This term sheet does not create a right to collect both the settlement sum and underlying claim.
4. **Releases:** Upon full payment, both parties release each other and their respective officers, employees, and agents from claims arising from the staffing agreement or the identified invoices and hires through the settlement effective date, whether known or unknown, including contractual interest and attorney-fee claims. Claims to enforce the settlement, obligations arising after the effective date, and any claim not lawfully releasable are excluded. The parties will mutually withdraw any pending demand, motion, or action about the released matter after payment, if any exists.
5. **Fees and costs:** Each side bears its own attorneys' fees and costs for negotiation and any proceeding concerning the underlying dispute. Neither seeks a prevailing-party award for released claims after full payment. Enforcement costs following an uncured breach are reserved for resolution under the signed settlement and applicable contract terms; no dollar amount is assumed.
6. **No admission:** Payment and releases are a compromise, with no admission of liability, waiver beyond this settlement, or concession that all invoices and conversion fees were correctly billed.
7. **Documentation:** The parties will sign a short final settlement agreement containing these terms, authorized signatures, notice addresses, payment instructions, and a provision that it may be signed in counterparts. The agreement becomes effective on the date of the last signature. Any change to these terms must be in a writing signed by both parties.

## Basis and negotiation limits

The corrected provisional contract claim is **$416,900 approximately**: four invoices without a recorded dispute **$265,200** + June J-4533 **$74,800 − about $3,100 = about $71,700** + five-hire fee estimate **5 × 25% × assumed $64,000 = $80,000**. At $335,000, Juniper would compromise **about $81,900** from that provisional face amount: $416,900 − $335,000 = $81,900. The proposed $69,800 above the four invoices is a negotiated lump sum for disputed June and conversion-fee exposure, not an allocation or admission that either category has that exact value.

Recommended settlement range: **$300,000–$350,000**, with a **$285,000 floor only for prompt, assured payment and the release terms above**. The floor is $285,000 − $265,200 = **$19,800** above the four invoices. A longer installment plan would require a higher price and payment protection; the $285,000 floor does not apply to an unsecured six-month schedule. Pinecrest's stated offer is $265,200 over six months, or $265,200 ÷ 6 = **$44,200 per month**, with $0 on June and $0 on fees. It therefore does not meet the recommended floor, even before accounting for delay and nonpayment risk.

We exclude the two expressly waived Rosa/Tay fees and the $410,000 interference/lost-profit theory. The February 11 waiver is limited on its face, but later texts may support defenses as to three other hires; the actual five-person compensation and the June audit remain unknown.
