## Claims and forum

We should **not** pursue the $410,000 interference/lost-profit theory to frighten Pinecrest. Pinecrest cannot tortiously interfere with its own Juniper contract. An at-will worker theory requires improper means, for which the current file supplies no evidence; consent and legitimate business purpose are further defenses. The agreement also excludes consequential damages, which is how the projected account-profit loss appears here. A complaint pressing this theory would risk dismissal, and using it as a threat without a non-frivolous basis would be improper. [Tortious interference](../../library/columbia_tortious_interference.md); [Rule 12(b)(6)](../../library/frcp_12b6.md); [Fee shifting and candor](../../library/fee_shifting_and_candor.md). Seven hires remain central to the **contract** analysis, but two fees were expressly waived.

The signed agreement incorporates Exhibit C. It sends disputes arising from the agreement, including arbitrability, exclusively to AAA arbitration before one arbitrator in Capital City. Neither the fact that Juniper did not read the exhibit nor its origin as a template supplies a documented ground to disregard it. The under-$25,000 small-claims exception fits none of these invoices. A Superior Court damages suit would likely be stayed or sent to arbitration, and a successful motion to compel could make Juniper liable for Pinecrest's reasonable fees and costs. The prevailing party in arbitration may also recover reasonable fees and costs; the clause works both ways. Dollar exposure cannot be reliably estimated from the present file. [FAA §§2–4](../../library/faa_2_4.md); [Fee shifting and candor](../../library/fee_shifting_and_candor.md).
