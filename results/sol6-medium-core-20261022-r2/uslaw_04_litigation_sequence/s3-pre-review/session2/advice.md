## Response to Dolapo's instruction

We are **not folding**. Seven hires occurred according to Juniper's roster, and we should press the contract claim for the five whose fees were not expressly waived, seek the corrected invoices, demand immediate payment of the $265,200 Pinecrest does not dispute, and arbitrate viable contract claims if payment and negotiation fail. But we should **not retain or threaten a tortious-interference claim merely to scare Pinecrest**. A claim needs a non-frivolous factual and legal basis, and the prevailing-party clause creates fee exposure if an unsound theory is pursued. [Fee shifting and candor](../../library/fee_shifting_and_candor.md).

Marcus's email is not the only problem with interference. Pinecrest cannot tortiously interfere with its own agreement with Juniper; a breach of that agreement is a contract claim. A different theory based on Juniper's at-will workers requires improper means, for which the current file has no evidence. The written waiver is direct consent for Rosa and Tay; the later texts could support consent or estoppel for three more; and Pinecrest cites a corporate agency-spend directive. [Tortious interference](../../library/columbia_tortious_interference.md). The seven hires remain relevant and potentially valuable under Section 3, but their number alone does not establish this separate tort. We will preserve the underlying facts and reassess only if concrete evidence of improper means emerges. The reply therefore continues to withdraw the interference threat while firmly demanding the contract amounts.
