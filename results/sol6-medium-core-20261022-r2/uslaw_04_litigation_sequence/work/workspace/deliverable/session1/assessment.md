## Contract and forum

The January 15, 2024 staffing agreement requires Pinecrest to pay Schedule 1 hourly rates within 30 days of invoice. Late amounts bear 1% monthly interest. Pinecrest must dispute an invoice within 15 days of receipt; otherwise it is deemed accepted. We have invoice due dates, but no receipt dates, time sheets, or billing backup. Thus, the four invoices with no recorded dispute have a strong acceptance argument, but receipt and performance still need proof. The June invoice, J-4533, drew a July 9 objection about allegedly padded shifts. Dolapo now confirms two double-entered shifts worth about $3,100; the balance claimed is $74,800 − $3,100 = **$71,700**. The client's description is approximate, so the exact credit remains to be verified against billing records. The merits of any additional disputed shifts and the objection's timeliness cannot be resolved without the invoice receipt date and shift records. An unobjected invoice is prima facie evidence of debt, subject to defenses. [Account stated and waiver](../../library/columbia_account_stated_waiver.md).

Section 3 bars direct or indirect hiring of Juniper-introduced personnel during the term and for 12 months afterward except on payment of 25% of each person's **first-year annualized compensation**. Juniper may waive the fee in writing. Bell's February 11 email expressly waived the fee for Rosa and Tay “as a one-off.” Bell confirms he intended to waive only those two and never agreed to waive the other five. We cannot demand a fee for Rosa or Tay. Pinecrest may nonetheless argue implied waiver or estoppel for Priya, Luis, and Keisha based on Bell's April and May texts, six-minute call, and lack of a recorded objection. Bell supplies no further details about the call. His denial of a later agreement helps Juniper, but it does not erase the ambiguity in the texts or establish what Lindqvist understood. Anne and Victor have no comparable documented acquiescence. [Account stated and waiver](../../library/columbia_account_stated_waiver.md).

The agreement auto-renews annually and permits termination on 60 days' notice. The May 2 email says Pinecrest will reduce agency use and finish current requests. It does not clearly give 60 days' termination notice or establish an obligation to place future staffing orders. Section 6 excludes consequential damages and leaves unpaid fees and conversion fees uncapped. Section 7 incorporates Exhibit C. Exhibit C.1 requires exclusive AAA Commercial Rules arbitration before one arbitrator in Capital City, Columbia, and delegates arbitrability. Section C.3 gives the prevailing party reasonable attorney fees and costs in arbitration or an action to compel it. The court exception applies only to a claim **solely** collecting undisputed invoices under $25,000; each listed invoice exceeds that amount. Court is available for provisional injunctive relief in aid of arbitration, but no current record supports seeking it. The FAA library describes enforcement, stay, and compulsion of covered arbitration provisions, including delegated arbitrability. [FAA §§2–4](../../library/faa_2_4.md). The fee clause creates real downside if Juniper forces an avoidable motion to compel or loses in arbitration. [Fee shifting and candor](../../library/fee_shifting_and_candor.md).

## Claims and proof

**Invoice collection / breach.** The invoice ledger lists five unpaid invoices totaling $340,000 before correction. The four without recorded objections total $265,200 and are the strongest immediate payment demand. J-4533 was $74,800; deducting the approximately $3,100 double entry gives a provisional $71,700 and a corrected aggregate of $336,900. We should acknowledge the error in the letter, seek the remaining audit details, and not assert that Pinecrest accepted J-4533 or that its objection was late. Late interest is contractual, but the record does not state the calculation convention or whether partial payments or credits exist. Demand it according to the agreement, with a reconciled calculation after ledger verification.

**Conversion fees / breach.** Seven names are listed as hires. Rosa and Tay were expressly waived. Bell says he made no further agreement to waive fees. For the other five, the CEO's $64,000 average is an unverified proxy, not each person's first-year annualized compensation. Illustrative math: 5 × 25% × $64,000 = **$80,000**. The actual claim is 25% of the proven first-year annualized compensation for each of Priya, Luis, Keisha, Anne, and Victor, subject to any established waiver or estoppel defense. Hire dates, compensation records, and individual introduction evidence remain unavailable from the client. The three hires discussed in texts carry greater litigation risk; Bell's express “one-off” limit and denial of a later agreement support Juniper, but his later responses are ambiguous. [Account stated and waiver](../../library/columbia_account_stated_waiver.md).

**Tortious interference / lost account profits.** The record shows Pinecrest cutting agency use under a corporate mandate. It does not show an agreement to maintain future orders, an improper act inducing a third party to breach, or support for $410,000 of net lost profit. Pinecrest cannot tortiously interfere with its *own* contract with Juniper. The employment terms of Juniper's personnel are unknown. If they were at will, a theory based on interference with those relationships would require improper means and face consent and legitimate-business-justification defenses, particularly given the written waiver and later texts. [Tortious interference](../../library/columbia_tortious_interference.md). The contract excludes consequential damages, which is how the projected account-profit item appears on this record. We should not assert this claim or include it in the demand. Pleading a claim without a non-frivolous basis and misstating the waiver would also raise candor concerns. [Fee shifting and candor](../../library/fee_shifting_and_candor.md).
