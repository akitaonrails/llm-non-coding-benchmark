# Advice to HR — Dana Whitcomb (October 6, 2026)

**Do not terminate Dana today or give her the current separation agreement.** At-will status permits a genuine, lawful performance decision, but it does not protect a termination motivated by retaliation or requested leave ([library/columbia_at_will_exceptions.md](../library/columbia_at_will_exceptions.md)). The present record strongly supports both concerns. A corrected release would not fix an unlawful termination.

## Why the risk is high

- Dana complained about Tom's repeated appearance and home/travel comments on September 12. Tom opened her first PIP on September 16: **September 16 − September 12 = four days**. The proposed October 6 termination is **24 days** after the complaint. Close timing and different treatment of non-complainants can support retaliation ([library/title_vii_704a.md](../library/title_vii_704a.md)). HR has not investigated the complaint.
- Dana requested intermittent FMLA leave on September 30 and submitted certification October 3. Tom's October 2 email said, “Let's get this done before she's out on leave. Once she's on FMLA it's a nightmare to move on her.” The email followed the request by **two days**. Terminating someone to prevent requested leave is interference even before the leave starts ([library/fmla_2615.md](../library/fmla_2615.md)). The file does not establish employer coverage or Dana's eligibility; HR must check both promptly, but should not use the pending request as a negative factor.
- The performance record offers some legitimate concerns: attainment declined from 118% in 2022 to 88% in 2026 YTD, and the 2025 review mentions pipeline hygiene. It also shows “Meets” through 2025, no prior discipline, and no prior PIP. The 60-day PIP was only 20 days old on October 6 (**September 16 to October 6 = 20 days**). Its timing and origin require independent scrutiny.

The comparator file sharpens the concern. Dana had **88%** YTD quota attainment and a PIP. Brennan had **79%** (**88 − 79 = 9 percentage points lower**), Osei **84%** (**88 − 84 = 4 lower**), and Park **86%** (**88 − 86 = 2 lower**); none had a PIP. Lind had **103%** (**103 − 88 = 15 higher**) and no PIP. Thus **3 of 4** comparators had lower attainment but no PIP. All three were men and had not complained, so the pattern does not isolate sex from retaliation. Quotas, territories, pipeline hygiene, prior coaching, and decision makers may differ; examine those records before drawing a final conclusion. Dana's age is 42, but this record contains no age-related remark; the age-discrimination theory presently looks weaker. The library supplies waiver rules, but no substantive age-discrimination test, so that legal theory is not verified here.

## Act now

1. **Pause the termination decision.** Remove Tom from deciding Dana's employment status while an independent reviewer examines the pre-complaint performance evidence, PIP timing, CRM update records, quota setting, and comparator treatment. Preserve Tom's October 2 email; do not backdate documents or manufacture a new rationale. Completing these steps does not predetermine the eventual employment decision.
2. **Investigate the harassment complaint promptly and impartially.** Assign someone outside Tom's chain, interview Dana, Tom, and relevant witnesses, collect the communications, document findings, take appropriate corrective action, and protect Dana from retaliation. The current delay undermines the employer's defenses and is evidence relevant to retaliation ([library/harassment_investigation_duty.md](../library/harassment_investigation_duty.md)).
3. **Process the FMLA request promptly.** Review the September 30 request and October 3 certification, determine coverage and eligibility, tell Dana what additional information is needed, if any, and communicate the decision. Keep the requested leave out of performance, PIP, and termination judgments. Requested leave is protected from interference even if it has not begun, assuming FMLA coverage and eligibility ([library/fmla_2615.md](../library/fmla_2615.md)).
4. **Issue a litigation hold now.** Preserve personnel and PIP files, quotas and territory assignments, CRM audit logs, complaint and leave records, emails, chats, texts, and relevant devices/accounts for Dana, Tom, HR, and decision makers; suspend routine deletion and confirm custodians comply. The contemplated termination after a discrimination complaint makes litigation reasonably foreseeable ([library/litigation_hold.md](../library/litigation_hold.md)).
5. **Reassess after the independent review.** A future action requires a documented, consistent, lawful reason independent of the complaint and leave request. If a separation is later supportable, use the revised ADEA terms in `corrected_separation_agreement_terms.md`; the present seven-day consideration and three-day revocation periods are inadequate ([library/adea_owbpa_626f.md](../library/adea_owbpa_626f.md)).

The file does not establish whether this employer and Dana meet FMLA coverage and eligibility requirements, whether the agreement is an individual or group-program offer, whether four weeks' pay is additional to amounts owed, or whether CRM and territory differences explain the comparator pattern. These are factual checks for the follow-up review; none changes today's recommendation.
