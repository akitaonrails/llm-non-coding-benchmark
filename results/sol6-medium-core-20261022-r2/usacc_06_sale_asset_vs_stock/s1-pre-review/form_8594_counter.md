This is a **negotiating proposal**, not an appraisal or a completed Form 8594. It assumes $3,100,000 is the entire consideration to allocate, no cash or other assets are transferred, and the stated amounts are supported at closing. Assumed liabilities may change the allocation base; their amounts and treatment remain open.

The residual method allocates consideration through Classes I–VII, up to fair market value in each class, and buyer and seller file Form 8594 consistently with their written agreement ([IRC §1060 / Form 8594 guidance](../library/irc_1060_form_8594.md)). A covenant is a Class VI §197 intangible to the buyer, while the seller's covenant payment is ordinary income. The written allocation must reflect economic reality and is difficult for the parties to disavow later ([IRC §197 and non-compete guidance](../library/irc_197_and_noncompete.md)). Buyer A therefore should not be asked merely to relabel $300,000: the purchase agreement, consulting terms, asset valuations, and Form 8594 must all support the revised economics.

At this proposal, equipment gain is $650,000 − $240,000 = $410,000 of §1245 recapture. Itemized at the register values, the gains are $230,000 router + $71,000 edgebander + $48,000 spray booth + $30,000 dust collection + $31,000 vehicles + $0 other equipment = $410,000. Each is within its recorded depreciation, so the full gain is ordinary ([IRC §1245](../library/irc_1245.md)). The $100,000 covenant is ordinary; the remaining $2,010,000 goodwill gain is modeled as long-term capital gain. The library does not expressly establish the seller's goodwill character, so that classification is a stated, unverified tax assumption.
