# Brightline Cabinetry: offer comparison for Ray Brightline

**Recommendation:** Do not sign Buyer A's proposed allocation or its 45-day exclusivity on the present terms. On the stated, conservative tax model, Buyer B's $2.85 million stock offer leaves **$2,364,000**, versus **$2,176,800** under Buyer A's drafted $3.10 million asset offer. A provisional, supportable reallocation improves Buyer A to **$2,272,000**, still $92,000 below B before liability, note-credit, and timing adjustments. The $250,000 headline premium does not overcome the different tax treatment. Obtain an independent valuation and negotiate with both buyers before choosing.

## Scope and assumptions behind the numbers

The figures in `comparison.json` are *nominal lifetime principal proceeds*, not present values or guaranteed collections. They assume a 2026 closing; Ray's $420,000 stock basis at December 31, 2025 is unchanged at closing; no transferred cash, other assets, additional goodwill basis, transaction expenses, sales tax, Texas franchise tax, or liability-related price adjustments; all receivables collect; and all of Buyer B's note principal is paid. They apply 37% to ordinary income and 20% to long-term capital gain and assume Ray materially participates, so no 3.8% net investment income tax applies ([2026 rates](../library/capital_gain_rates.md)). Material participation is not documented. The library does not resolve whether this stock gain qualifies for that exclusion; if 3.8% applies to all $2,430,000 of stock gain, B's tax rises by **$92,340** and nominal net falls to **$2,271,660**.

The asset-sale figures **charge the full $237,300 built-in-gains tax and give no personal tax benefit for its pass-through loss**. [IRC §1374](../library/irc_1374.md) says the tax paid is treated as an S corporation loss, but this library does not specify that loss's character or enough rules to compute its effect on Ray's individual return. If all $237,300 instead reduces income taxed at 37%, the personal tax saving would be $87,801; Buyer A's draft and counter nets would rise to $2,264,601 and $2,359,801. That is a sensitivity, **not** an established result. The asset-sale model also excludes any further tax upon distribution or liquidation of corporate cash; the closing balance sheet and governing rules needed to establish that result are unavailable in the library. Thus the modeled asset net can be overstated or understated. A tax adviser should reconcile the S corporation's inside and outside bases before Ray signs.

**Buyer A draft.** Receivables: $200,000 proceeds − $200,000 basis = $0 gain. Inventory: $150,000 − $140,000 = $10,000 ordinary gain. Equipment and vehicles: $900,000 − $240,000 = $660,000 gain. Aggregate original cost is $1,100,000, so all $660,000 is within prior depreciation and modeled as ordinary §1245 recapture; final recapture must be computed asset by asset ([IRC §1245](../library/irc_1245.md)). The $400,000 assigned to Ray's non-compete/consulting agreement is modeled as ordinary income; the covenant portion is expressly ordinary under the [non-compete authority](../library/irc_197_and_noncompete.md). Ordinary treatment of any consulting-services portion is an unverified assumption because that treatment is not addressed by this library. Goodwill: $1,450,000 − assumed $0 basis = $1,450,000 modeled capital gain. Thus ordinary income is $10,000 + $660,000 + $400,000 = $1,070,000; individual tax is $1,070,000 × 37% + $1,450,000 × 20% = $685,900. A's $900,000 equipment allocation exceeds the register's $650,000 fair-value estimate by $250,000; the residual method allocates only up to fair value in each class ([IRC §1060 / Form 8594 guidance](../library/irc_1060_form_8594.md)). The stated allocation needs support or revision.

**Buyer A counter.** The proposed Classes III, IV, V, VI, and VII are $200,000, $140,000, $650,000, $100,000, and $2,010,000, respectively; see `form_8594_counter.md`. Inventory gain becomes $0. Equipment gain is $650,000 − $240,000 = $410,000, all §1245 ordinary recapture on the register's individual estimates ([IRC §1245](../library/irc_1245.md)). Add a **provisional** $100,000 covenant allocation: ordinary income $510,000. The $2,010,000 residual goodwill amount is modeled as capital gain. Individual tax is $510,000 × 37% + $2,010,000 × 20% = $590,700. The proposed covenant amount is an opening position, not a finding of fair value. Ray's planned retirement helps explain a lower covenant value, but an agreement that calls it goodwill without economic support could be challenged; the parties' written allocation has substantial force ([non-compete authority](../library/irc_197_and_noncompete.md)).

**Buyer B stock.** Assuming unchanged stock basis, $2,850,000 − $420,000 = $2,430,000 capital gain and $486,000 tax at 20%. Stock rather than assets is sold, so this comparison does not trigger entity-level built-in-gains tax, absent a deemed asset-sale election. Do **not** agree to a §338(h)(10) or §336(e) election without repricing and rerunning the asset-sale model ([stock/asset overview](../library/stock_vs_asset_overview.md)).

**Built-in gains.** Brightline elected S status January 1, 2023, so a 2026 asset sale is within the five-year recognition period. The conversion appraisal identifies $900,000 built-in goodwill gain and $230,000 built-in equipment gain. Both proposed allocations produce more than those respective amounts of recognized gain, so the modeled tax is ($900,000 + $230,000) × 21% = **$237,300** ([IRC §1374](../library/irc_1374.md)). This assumes the same appreciated equipment survives through closing and no other adjustment reduces recognized built-in gain. The register lists some vehicles placed in service after conversion, so the CPA must match the 2023 appraisal to assets actually sold. If the $230,000 equipment amount is unavailable, the tax could fall by as much as $48,300; the $900,000 goodwill component alone implies $189,000 under the stated assumptions. No net operating losses were reported.

**Ray's estimate.** $3,100,000 × (1 − 20%) = **$2,480,000**. It misses inventory income, depreciation recapture, the covenant payment, and the corporate built-in-gains tax. The draft model's net is $303,200 lower.

## Payment timing and deal risk

Buyer B's stated $2,350,000 cash and $500,000 unsecured note total $2,850,000. The stock-sale gross-profit fraction is $2,430,000 ÷ $2,850,000 = **85.2631579%**. If all $2,350,000 is received at closing, installment gain then is $2,003,684.21 and tax is $400,736.84; the $500,000 principal later carries $426,315.79 gain and $85,263.16 tax as collected. The resulting **closing cash after modeled tax is $1,949,263.16**, subject to the unknown escrow treatment. This is separate from the nominal lifetime net of $2,364,000. The 6% note interest is additional income and cash flow, excluded from all offer-price and tax totals. Its tax character and any escrow timing are not established by this library. [IRC §453](../library/irc_453_installment.md) permits installment gain as payments arrive, while §1245 recapture on an installment asset sale would be recognized in the sale year regardless of payments. A's offer is all cash, so deferral does not help there.

The note is unsecured and Hensley's balance sheet was not supplied. Its $500,000 face value cannot be treated as risk-free cash; seek a bank letter of credit, funded escrow, strong guarantee, or larger closing payment. The stock offer includes a $100,000 escrow and a 12-month indemnity capped at 10% of price (**$285,000**); whether the escrow is taken from the $2,350,000 closing cash and when it releases are unknown. Under the stock structure the buyer takes Brightline with all known and unknown liabilities, subject to the indemnity. The known Hardaway dispute is $9,800, and warranty exposure is unquantified. Under A's asset structure the buyer assumes only scheduled trade payables; Brightline/Ray may still bear the dispute, warranty claims, and excluded liabilities. Trade payable, debt, and cash balances are unknown, as is whether assumed payables are additional consideration. These omissions prevent an exact cash-equivalent or liability-adjusted comparison ([stock/asset overview](../library/stock_vs_asset_overview.md)). The treatment of assumed liabilities in seller amount realized is not covered by this library and is unverified here.

## Negotiating position

1. **Buyer A:** Reject the $900,000 equipment and $400,000 covenant/consulting allocations as presented. Offer the attached $650,000 equipment, $140,000 inventory, and provisional $100,000 covenant allocation, with goodwill as the residual, subject to valuation. Separate any actual consulting services and pay for them on documented terms. Require the same allocation in the definitive agreement and each Form 8594 ([IRC §1060 / Form 8594 guidance](../library/irc_1060_form_8594.md)). Clarify cash, payables, excluded liabilities, warranty and Hardaway responsibility, and shorten or remove exclusivity while these points are resolved. At the modeled tax rates, and assuming any *additional* price is allocated to goodwill, A would need at least **$115,000 more** ($92,000 ÷ 80%), or **$3,215,000 total**, for the counter case to match B's nominal modeled net. That is only a parity point before note risk, liabilities, and the unresolved §1374-loss benefit; it is not a final reservation price. If A insists on its draft allocation, nominal parity would require $187,200 ÷ 80% = **$234,000 more**, or **$3,334,000 total**, on the same assumptions.
2. **Buyer B:** Keep the deal an actual stock sale with no deemed asset-sale election. Ask for more cash at closing or security for the $500,000 note, proof of creditworthiness, clear escrow release terms, and a narrow indemnity tied to specific disclosed liabilities. The $100,000 escrow and $285,000 indemnity cap are economically relevant even though no loss is assumed in the tax table.
3. **Before signing:** Have the CPA update stock and asset bases through closing, match the 2023 conversion appraisal to the assets sold, determine the character and individual tax effect of the §1374 tax loss, and reconcile the expected distribution or liquidation. Obtain current asset and covenant valuations and a closing liability schedule. No additional client facts were available to resolve these items.

### Authority limits

The closed library supports the cited §1245, §1374, §197, §1060, §453 and rate rules and gives a practitioner stock-versus-asset summary. It does **not** expressly establish the seller's goodwill gain character, consulting-income treatment, stock-gain NIIT result, S corporation liquidation/distribution taxation, interest-income treatment, assumed-liability amount-realized rule, or the character of the §1374 pass-through loss. Those points are identified above as assumptions or unverified, not presented as settled law.
