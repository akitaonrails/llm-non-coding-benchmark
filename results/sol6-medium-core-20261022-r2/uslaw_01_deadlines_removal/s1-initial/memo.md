## Removal assessment

**Do not file a diversity removal notice on the present record.** The complaint alleges Sound Cascade is incorporated in Washington and has its principal place of business in Kent, Washington, making it a Washington citizen under [28 U.S.C. § 1332(c)(1)](../library/28usc_1332_1441_1446_1447.md). Harbor Street's Delaware organization and Portland principal place of business do not determine an LLC's citizenship. Under the [LLC citizenship authorities](../library/llc_citizenship_cases.md), it has each member's citizenship. The client confirms that its only two members are Caleb Sorensen (35%, lives in Seattle's Ballard neighborhood) and Noor Haddad (65%, lives in Portland). **Working assumption:** Seattle and Portland are their respective domiciles. On that assumption, Caleb is a Washington citizen, Noor is an Oregon citizen, and Harbor Street is a citizen of both Washington and Oregon. Caleb's Washington citizenship defeats complete diversity with Sound Cascade. Because Harbor Street is also a properly joined and served Washington defendant in a Washington case, the [forum defendant rule, § 1441(b)(2)](../library/28usc_1332_1441_1446_1447.md), independently prevents diversity removal.

The **remaining unknown** is domicile: where someone lives does not conclusively establish that person's permanent home. The client has no further information. If Caleb's domicile is actually outside Washington, that fact would require proof and a fresh citizenship analysis; there is no basis now to assert it in a removal notice. There are no other members according to the client. The complaint's principal damages are $142,316.40 invoiced − $31,400 paid = $110,916.40, plus $18,240 for the butter loss = **$129,156.40**. That exceeds $75,000 by **$54,156.40**, without counting interest, fees, or costs, so amount is not the obstacle. These are allegations, not conceded liability. The client disputes some deliveries as short or spoiled and has photos. The relevant diversity and remand provisions are in [28 U.S.C. §§ 1332, 1441, and 1447(c)](../library/28usc_1332_1441_1446_1447.md); an improper removal can be remanded and can carry expense or fee exposure.
