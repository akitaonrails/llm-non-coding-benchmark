at $410,000, (iv) the statement that Juniper "never consented to any direct hire," and (v) a threat
to sue in Superior Court. **Juniper's demand asserts none of those things.** Juniper's demand:

- seeks **$430,550.50** through October 31, 2026 — unpaid invoices of $340,000.00 **less an
  approximately $3,100.00 credit** Juniper itself identified and applied (below), plus contractual
  interest of $13,650.50, plus conversion fees for **five** hires, not seven;
- **expressly acknowledged** the February 11, 2026 waiver as to Rosa M. and Tay W. and did not demand
  their conversion fees. Juniper takes no position that it "never consented to any direct hire"; its
  position is that the only written waiver covers exactly those two individuals;
- **asserts no tortious-interference claim**, and your draft Rule 12(b)(6) motion directed at a count
  we have never asserted is, accordingly, unnecessary. Juniper will not assert that theory;
- **does not threaten Superior Court litigation.** The agreement's arbitration provision (Exh. C.1)
  precludes it, and this letter is Juniper's operative demand.

To eliminate any confusion from your letter's description of a demand dated before Juniper's: the
figures and positions restated in this letter are Juniper's demand, and any other characterization of
Juniper's demands — in any correspondence, by whomever sent — is superseded. Please treat this letter
as the operative statement of Juniper's position.

## 2. Consent to hire is not waiver of the fee — your own exhibit proves the point

Section 3 of the parties' Staffing Services Agreement does not require Juniper's "consent" for
Pinecrest to hire a Juniper worker. It **permits** the hire "upon payment of the conversion fee,"
set at 25% of first-year compensation, within 12 months. Your point 1's premise — that Juniper
"consented to the hires" and therefore owes nothing — describes the ordinary operation of §3, not a
defense. The question is only whether the **fee** was waived, and §3 permits waiver solely "in
writing, in [Juniper's] discretion."

There is exactly one such writing — your Exhibit 1. Your quotation of it deletes, with an ellipsis,
the words that control its scope: "consider the fee waived **for Rosa and Tay** as a **one-off**."
The email waives fees for two named individuals and calls itself a one-off. Juniper agrees: as to
Rosa M. and Tay W., no fee is owed, and none was demanded. As to Priya S., Luis F., Keisha B.,
Anne O., and Victor H., there is no waiver — in writing or otherwise. Mr. Bell's responses to the
later hires ("ha, we'll talk"; "call me") are the opposite of a written waiver, and he has confirmed
he never agreed to waive those fees. Your own letter concedes this dispute is about "the scope of the
waiver" — a contract question, and under §3 the contract answers it: five fees remain payable.

Juniper demands the five conversion fees, **$80,000.00 as a minimum** (25% × $64,000 average
first-year compensation × 5). Pinecrest's payroll records contain each hire's actual first-year
compensation; please provide it. If actual compensation exceeds the average, the fees — and this
demand — increase accordingly.
