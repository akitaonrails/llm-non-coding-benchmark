## 2. Why "we never looked at the exhibits" changes nothing — and should never be said out loud

Marcus reportedly noticed "some arbitration thing." Fine. But be clear about what that admission is
worth: **nothing, legally.**

- The CEO signed this agreement (signature block: D. Okonkwo, CEO), and §7 makes "Schedules and
  Exhibits A–C" part of the entire agreement. FAA §2: an arbitration provision in a commerce
  contract is "valid, irrevocable, and enforceable, save upon such grounds as exist at law or in
  equity for the revocation of any contract" (`library/faa_2_4.md`). Not reading an exhibit you
  signed is not such a ground. "Our old lawyer drafted it off a template" is also not a ground —
  the clause is mutual; it binds Pinecrest exactly as much.
- C.1 covers "**any** dispute, claim or controversy arising out of or relating to this Agreement,
  **including … the arbitrability of any claim**." That last phrase is a delegation clause, and the
  library is explicit: a delegation clause is enforced if clear and unmistakable, and it is
  (*Rent-A-Center v. Jackson*, 561 U.S. 63 (2010), cited in `library/faa_2_4.md`). Even the question
  of whether a claim is arbitrable goes to the arbitrator, not a judge.
- Practical instruction: do **not** repeat "we never looked at the exhibits" in any letter, filing,
  or conversation with the other side. It is not a defense (R. 1.4 requires we tell the client the
  adverse fact — this note is that telling; it does not license volunteering it to the adversary).

## 3. Why court is a trap with a price tag

If we file in court anyway (or if a September letter's Superior Court threat gets carried out):

- Pinecrest applies to **stay** the case (FAA §3) and **compel arbitration** (FAA §4); on this text
  the motion is close to unwinnable for us.
- C.3 shifts fees to the prevailing party "**in any arbitration or in any action to compel
  arbitration**." The library states the consequence directly: "A party that files suit on
  arbitrable claims may be compelled to arbitrate and, under a fee clause, may owe the fees of the
  motion to compel" (`library/faa_2_4.md`), and *Zuniga* confirms Columbia enforces that as written.
  So the first court filing of this case has a realistic path to an immediate invoice from
  Pinecrest's lawyers — before any merits work begins.
- The only court doors C.1 leaves open: **C.4** — provisional injunctive relief *in aid of
  arbitration*. Useless to us: this is a money case, no injunction target. **C.6** — small-claims
  carve-out for "claims solely for collection of **undisputed** invoices **of less than $25,000**."
  Every invoice exceeds the cap (smallest is J-4571 at $59,450.00, `documents/open_invoices.csv`),
  and J-4533 is disputed in part. No small-claims route exists.

**Forum is not a preference question. It is AAA, and our reply letter already says exactly that.**

## 4. "Make them pay our legal fees" — the honest mechanics

- **Yes:** C.3 — "The prevailing party in any arbitration … shall be entitled to recover its
  reasonable attorneys' fees and costs." Enforced as written (*Zuniga*). Our reply letter's demand
  and any AAA demand will invoke it.
- **It is mutual, and that shapes the pleading strategy.** "Prevailing party" in a mixed outcome is
  a fight an arbitrator resolves; the cleaner our claims, the more plainly we are *the* prevailing
  party:
  - **Cleanest:** the four deemed-accepted invoices ($265,200.00, §2) plus interest — their own
    counsel promised payment in writing. This tranche is nearly fee-proof for us and fee-**dangerous**
    for them (they are holding money they've conceded they owe).
  - **Clean-ish:** J-4533 balance $71,700.00 (we credited the only defect in the record).
  - **Contestable:** the $80,000 fee tranche (waiver-scope fact question on Priya/Luis/Keisha).
  - **Fee-poisoned:** the tortious-interference count — their motion is pre-drafted, every element
    fails on our library (*Ashby*; consent; §§766/767/773), and §6 excludes the $410,000 damages
    theory as consequential. Assert it and *Juniper* is the candidate prevailing-against party on
    the threshold fight. This is the Session 2 memo point, restated in the client's own language:
    the fees question is another reason the tort count is a reverse weapon.
- **Fee exposure cuts at us on cost too:** our own arbitration costs (AAA Commercial Rules fees,
  arbitrator compensation) are real, and the library contains no AAA fee schedules — quantifying our
  all-in fee/cost exposure, win or lose, is the **Session 5** exercise before any settlement decision.
