## 3. Why "improved" is the honest word

1. **A written concession from their counsel** is worth more than any argument. Four invoices
   ($265,200.00) plus interest accrued on them are now effectively a promissory note — the only reason
   not yet paid is their own "conversion-fee issue" condition, which the contract does not support
   (§2 ties payment to nothing).
2. **Their best attack on J-4533 has evaporated.** "Padded hours" — after our credit — is an
   allegation with no documents behind it, eight-plus months after their audit notice.
3. **Their waiver theory is self-defeating.** Their Exhibit 1 is the scope evidence; a "one-off for
   Rosa and Tay" proves there is no general waiver.
4. **We now know the fee clause and intend to invoke it.** C.3 was invisible to you in Session 1
   ("we never looked at the exhibits"). It is real, mutual, and enforced as written (*Zuniga*). On a
   mostly-conceded invoice case plus a written-waiver clause, "prevailing party" is a realistic
   finding — if we keep the pleadings clean.
5. **Opposing counsel showed their hand early** — a 12(b)(6) draft aimed at a count we have never
   filed, in a forum that does not use it. They expected the old, messy $862,000 demand. They have
   not seen our clean one yet.

## 4. What has gotten worse (plainly)

1. **The $862,000 number.** It reconciles only by adding a dead tort count ($410,000), waived
   Rosa/Tay fees ($32,000), and padded double-entered shifts ($3,100) — check: $430,550.50 +
   $410,000 + $32,000 + $3,100 − $13,650.50 (interest counted twice in the CEO's build-up) =
   $862,000. Filing to that number invites Rule 3.1 and C.3 fee blowback. This is an expectations
   correction, not a loss of position — but you should feel it.
2. **Your own record creates candor traps.** Bell's Feb. 11 email exists, and "Juniper never consented
   to any direct hire" is false as to Rosa and Tay. We cannot use that sentence — in a letter now, in a
   filing later (R. 3.3; `library/fee_shifting_and_candor.md`). And because your answers left open
   whether some pre-Sept. 29 letter went out carrying exactly those words (assumption A8), every
   outbound document is drafted to be true **either way** — present tense, only documents in our file.
   Until we see any such letter, we are steering around an obstacle we cannot see; that is a real,
   if bounded, risk.
3. **Fees now cut both ways.** Same clause that gets our fees paid if we win gets theirs paid if we
   lose a claim. Keeping the tort count is the fastest way to write that check.
4. **Arbitration costs are real and not yet quantified.** The library contains no AAA fee schedules —
   any dollar estimate would be unverified. Session 5 quantifies all-in exposure before any settlement
