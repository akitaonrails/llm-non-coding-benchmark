## 1. Why the answer is "no," in plain English

You asked us to confirm you're fine because it's an at-will state. At-will doesn't answer the question. Columbia allows at-will termination **except** "for an unlawful reason (discrimination, retaliation, exercise of statutory rights)" (`library/columbia_at_will_exceptions.md`). The risk here isn't that you fired a mediocre performer. It's the order of events:

| Date | What happened | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-09-12 | Dana files a **written** harassment complaint about Regional VP Tom Hale | — |
| 2026-09-16 | **Tom Hale** — the man she accused — opens a PIP on her | **4 days** after her complaint |
| 2026-09-30 | She requests FMLA leave to care for her mother | 18 days after complaint |
| 2026-10-02 | Hale emails you: *"Let's get this done before she's out on leave. Once she's on FMLA it's a nightmare to move on her."* | 2 days after her request |
| 2026-10-03 | Medical certification arrives; HR still has not responded | — |
| 2026-10-06 | You ask to terminate her today | **6 days** after the leave request, **3 days** after the certification, **24 days** after the complaint |

That 10/02 email is the case. A jury does not have to infer that the leave request motivated the timing — the manager wrote it down, and he is the person who opened the PIP and, in your own words, "wants her out." Two independent legal problems follow from it, and both are documented by the company itself:

- **FMLA interference/retaliation.** "Terminating an employee to prevent her from taking approved or requested leave is interference," an employer may not use FMLA leave "as a negative factor in employment actions," and an employee "who requested leave need not have begun it" (`library/fmla_2615.md`). The fact that she hasn't started the leave yet is no help to you. Remedies include lost wages, **liquidated (double) damages**, attorney's fees, and reinstatement.
- **Title VII retaliation.** An internal harassment complaint is protected activity, and causation can be shown by "very close temporal proximity (days to a few weeks)," by "shifting or thin justifications, disparate treatment of comparators who did not complain," or by direct evidence (`library/title_vii_704a.md`). You currently have all three: 4 days to the PIP, 24 days to the termination; a PIP that is the first written performance document in 7 years; comparators with worse numbers who were never put on one; and the email.

At-will answers one question — *do we need a reason at all?* It does not answer the only question that matters here — *is this reason lawful?* Every claim in the assessment goes to the second question. At-will would defeat a claim that Dana had a job she could only be fired from for cause. Nobody is making that claim. What is in the file is a reason the statutes reach directly. At-will is the door these claims walk through, not the wall that keeps them out.

### "We have a PIP, so we're covered"

On this file the PIP is not the defense. It is the evidence. Four facts about it:

- It is the **first written performance document in a 7-year file** with no prior discipline — and it is dated **4 days** after her written complaint about the man who wrote it.
