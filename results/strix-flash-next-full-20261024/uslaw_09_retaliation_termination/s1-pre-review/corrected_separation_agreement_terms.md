# Corrected separation agreement terms

**Purpose:** the terms that must change in the company's form (documents/separation_agreement_draft.md) before it is used for **any** future separation. Nothing here is advice to use the form for Dana Whitcomb now — see `advice_to_hr.md`, section 5: do not present it to her in the current posture.

**Authority note:** every requirement below is drawn from `library/adea_owbpa_626f.md` (29 U.S.C. § 626(f)(1)), `library/columbia_at_will_exceptions.md`, `library/title_vii_704a.md`, `library/fmla_2615.md`, `library/harassment_investigation_duty.md`, `library/litigation_hold.md` — the items listed in `library/INDEX.md`. Items I could **not** verify in the library are labeled **UNVERIFIED** and are flagged as review items, not as legal requirements.

---

## 1. Consideration period — clause 7 (must change)

| | Current form | Required |
|---|---|---|
| Time to consider | **7 days** | **at least 21 days** |

- Shortfall: 21 − 7 = **14 days short**; the form gives 7/21 = **33.3%** of the statutory minimum (**66.7% short**).
- **Individual vs. group is now settled.** The client has confirmed this separation is individual — *"Just her. No RIF."* So **21 days** is the controlling minimum and the 45-day figure does not apply to Dana. The 45-day rule ("in connection with an exit incentive or other employment termination program offered to a group or class of employees") stays in the template for future RIFs only, and the individual/group determination must still be made **before drafting** on every future use, because that single fact changes the number.
- **Dated runway for Dana, if an agreement is ever presented (see § 10 — not now):** delivered 2026-10-06 → consideration period runs to **2026-10-27**; if she signs on the last permitted day, the 7-day revocation window closes **2026-11-03**, and the agreement cannot be effective before then. Minimum from receipt to effectiveness: **21 + 7 = 28 days**. The current 7-day/3-day form would have purported to be effective **2026-10-16** — so a compliant agreement is about **18 days later** than the form contemplates, and a "sign it this week" package cannot be made lawful by redrafting it.
- **Runway re-based to today, 2026-10-26** (the dates above were computed from the 10/06 request and are now 20 days stale): the earliest compliant agreement presented **tomorrow, 10/27** would run 21 days' consideration to **11/17** and close its 7-day revocation window **11/24** — i.e. **11/24 is the earliest possible effective date**, one day later for each day of delay. That is **nine days after** the PIP period expires on **11/15** (see `advice_to_hr.md` §13), so the PIP must be disposed of in writing first; a compliant form does not rescue the process and cannot be made effective inside the PIP window.
- Drafting fix: "Employee has twenty-one (21) days from receipt of this Agreement within which to consider it. If this Agreement is being offered in connection with an exit incentive or other termination program offered to a group or class of employees, Employee has forty-five (45) days to consider it."

Cite: `library/adea_owbpa_626f.md`, § 626(f)(1)(F).

## 2. Revocation period and effective date — clause 7 (must change)

| | Current form | Required |
|---|---|---|
| Revocation window | **3 days** | **at least 7 days** after execution |
| Effective date | not stated | **not effective until the revocation period expires** |

- Shortfall: 7 − 3 = **4 days short**.
- Drafting fix: "Employee may revoke this Agreement within seven (7) days after signing it. This Agreement is not effective, and no payment will be made, until that seven-day period has expired without revocation." Do **not** pay severance on signature; pay after the revocation window closes.

Cite: `library/adea_owbpa_626f.md`, § 626(f)(1)(G).

## 3. No waiver of future claims — clause 4 (must change)

- Current: "Release of all claims, including under Title VII, the ADEA, the FMLA, and state law." A blanket "all claims" with no temporal cut-off risks waiving claims that have not yet arisen, which § 626(f)(1)(C) forbids.
- Drafting fix: limit the release to claims "arising on or before the date Employee signs this Agreement," and state expressly that it does **not** waive claims arising after signature. Carve out, at minimum: rights that cannot be waived, workers' compensation, unemployment, indemnification, and vested benefit rights. **UNVERIFIED:** the library does not address which claims are non-waivable as a matter of Columbia law; confirm the carve-out list against a Columbia-law source before finalizing.

Cite: `library/adea_owbpa_626f.md`, § 626(f)(1)(C).

## 4. Written advice to consult an attorney — missing from the form as supplied (must add or confirm)

- § 626(f)(1)(E) requires that the individual "be advised **in writing** to consult with an attorney" before signing.
- The excerpt produced to us (clauses 3, 4, 7, 9, 11) contains no such advisory. The client has no additional information and has not produced the complete form, so this must be treated as **missing until the full form is produced**. If the form lacks it, every release on this form is defective as to ADEA claims.
- Drafting fix: "Employee is advised in writing to consult with an attorney of her choice before signing this Agreement."

Cite: `library/adea_owbpa_626f.md`, § 626(f)(1)(E).

## 5. Specific reference to ADEA rights, and plain language — clause 4 (must change)

- § 626(f)(1)(B) requires the waiver to "specifically refer to rights or claims arising under the ADEA"; § 626(f)(1)(A) requires it be "written in a manner calculated to be understood."
- Listing "the ADEA" inside a four-statute string is a compliance risk, not a compliance record. Give the ADEA its own sentence and describe the right in ordinary words, and keep the whole release readable (short sentences, no defined-term maze, no all-caps wall).
- Drafting fix: "Employee specifically waives rights and claims arising under the Age Discrimination in Employment Act (ADEA), including claims of age discrimination and age-based retaliation, that arose on or before the date she signs."

Cite: `library/adea_owbpa_626f.md`, § 626(f)(1)(A)–(B).

## 6. Consideration must be something she is not already owed — clause 3 (verify before use)

- § 626(f)(1)(D): the waiver must be "in exchange for consideration in addition to anything of value to which the individual is already entitled."
- "4 weeks' base pay" is only good consideration if no policy, plan, collective agreement, employment agreement, or state final-pay obligation already entitles her to it (or to any part of it). If part is already owed, the release is supported by nothing for that part. Also: accrued wages and accrued paid time off are typically owed regardless and must **not** be counted as severance. **UNVERIFIED:** whether Columbia requires final pay/PTO payout and on what timetable is not in the library — confirm with a Columbia source. **This element remains unconfirmed: the client has no additional information** about the plan or final-pay practice, so it is an open risk on the current record, not a resolved item.
- Drafting fix: recite the severance formula, state that it is in addition to all amounts already owed, and pay accrued wages/PTO separately.

Cite: `library/adea_owbpa_626f.md`, § 626(f)(1)(D).

## 7. Consequence of getting 1, 2, 4, 5 or 6 wrong — why the current form buys nothing

- "An ADEA release that fails these requirements is unenforceable as to ADEA claims; the employee may keep the severance and sue" (*Oubre v. Entergy*, 522 U.S. 422 (1998)). The company would pay 4 weeks and still face the ADEA claim.
- **Unverified and worth knowing:** the library establishes this result for ADEA claims only. Whether a defective OWBPA waiver also weakens the Title VII / FMLA / state-law releases is not addressed in any library item, so I am not asserting it — but the company should not assume the rest of the release is bulletproof merely because the ADEA part fails.

Cite: `library/adea_owbpa_626f.md`.

## 8. Governing law and scope — clause 11 (keep, and understand the exposure)

- Columbia law mirrors Title VII/FMLA "with a longer limitations period and uncapped compensatory damages." A release drafted to federal standards only may leave a longer-lived state claim on the table; the release must expressly cover the state statutory and common-law claims with their own names, and the 21/45-day and 7-day mechanics must be honored for the ADEA portion regardless of governing law.

Cite: `library/columbia_at_will_exceptions.md`.

## 9. Non-disparagement (clause 9) and other restrictive covenants — review item

- **UNVERIFIED — no library authority.** Nothing in `library/INDEX.md` addresses non-disparagement clauses, confidentiality clauses, or non-cooperation clauses, and specifically whether such clauses may lawfully restrict an employee from filing or discussing a discrimination complaint or communicating with an agency. Mutual non-disparagement may be fine; it may also be a problem. **Do not rely on this memo for that clause** — get it reviewed against a source that actually covers it.
- Practical, authority-neutral drafting safeguard: if the clause is kept, it should not be worded to prevent truthful statements to a government agency or in a proceeding, and it should not be the price of the severance.

## 10. The defect no wording can fix — when the agreement is presented

- The form's worst defect is not in its text. Presenting a release-and-termination package **24 days after an uninvestigated harassment complaint**, **6 days after an FMLA leave request** (3 days after certification), and **4 days after the decision-maker wrote "Let's get this done before she's out on leave"** makes the agreement itself an exhibit in the retaliation and interference case. A compliant 21/7 form handed over in that window would still be evidence of the same sequence.
- **Now aggravated by the client's own account.** HR has confirmed (a) nothing has been done on the complaint — *"Honestly, nothing. Tom's been traveling. I was going to get to it."*; (b) the accused authored the PIP and the termination decision — *"Tom wrote the PIP and Tom wants her out. I'm just processing it."*; and (c) the certified leave request is being left undecided at his direction — *"Tom said to wait."* A release signed after those facts, by the same decision-maker, is not a settlement document; it is the next exhibit. Any future agreement must follow an independent decision-maker's determination, a completed investigation, and an answered leave request — or it inherits all of this.
- Related: never condition anything on withdrawal of the internal complaint, and never make the complaint a term of the separation discussion. Internal complaints are protected opposition (`library/title_vii_704a.md`) and leave-taking may not be a negative factor (`library/fmla_2615.md`).
- And the hold comes first: issue the litigation hold before any separation conversation. `library/litigation_hold.md` expressly ties preservation to "an internal discrimination complaint followed by contemplated termination."

## 11. Blanket form-level changes to make now (housekeeping)

1. Reissue the form with 21-day consideration (45 days only where the separation is part of a group/exit program), 7-day revocation, deferred effective date, attorney advisory, ADEA-specific sentence, future-claims cut-off.
2. Retire the current version and pull it from HR templates so it cannot be reused by accident.
3. Add a one-page OWBPA compliance checklist (six § 626(f)(1) elements, individual-vs-group determination, consideration source) that HR must complete before any release goes out. For Dana this is already resolved on the first two points: **individual, so 21 days**, and consideration source **unconfirmed**.
4. Record the date the employee actually **received** the agreement — the 21/45-day clock, the 7-day revocation window, and the hold all depend on documented dates. For Dana: 10/06 receipt → 10/27 consideration close → 11/03 earliest effective date.
5. Add a standing rule: any separation involving an employee who has filed an internal complaint, requested or taken leave, or has a manager who is the subject of a complaint goes to counsel before any offer is made.
6. Add a companion rule on decision authority: no PIP, leave determination, or separation may be initiated, directed, or signed by a person who is the subject of a pending complaint by that employee. On the present facts this rule alone would have prevented most of the exposure in `risk_assessment.json`.
