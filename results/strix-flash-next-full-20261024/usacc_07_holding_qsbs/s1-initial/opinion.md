# Opinion — proposed Oakline Holdings restructuring (Nadia Oakline)

Prepared per `CONTRACT.md`. **Every citation below points to a file listed in `library/INDEX.md`** (cited by file, e.g. `[1202]` → `library/irc_1202_excerpt.md`). Anything I believe to be law but cannot verify in the library is expressly labeled **[UNVERIFIED — not in library]**. Facts come only from `documents/` (org chart, Properties balance sheet, intercompany cash-flow CSV, the advisor proposal PDF, and the Helio Grid stock-purchase-agreement excerpt). **Bottom line:** do steps 1–2 *with one critical carve-out* (never move the Helio Grid stock into Holdings), refuse step 3 as written, and fix the loan hygiene first. ## 1. Step 1 — Form Oakline Holdings LLC (Wyoming; partnership; Nadia 99% / revocable trust 1%)

**Tax consequence.**
- Formation itself is not a taxable event, but the entity classification depends on there being two distinct *tax* owners. A **revocable** trust is disregarded for income tax, in which case Nadia is the sole owner for tax purposes and Holdings would be a single-member (disregarded) entity, not a partnership. **[UNVERIFIED — not in library: grantor-trust/disregarding rules.]** Client has confirmed the 1% holder is her **revocable living trust** ("the advisor said we need two partners"). The advisor's two-partner trick almost certainly fails: grantor + his own revocable trust is one tax owner, so the "taxed as a partnership" premise is likely illusory. Consequences if so: (i) no partnership return is required (contradicting proposal step 4), (ii) the §752 liability shift to the "1% partner" is $0 (no second tax partner), so the contribution is a pure internal reshuffle with no federal tax event, and (iii) if partnership status is genuinely wanted, the 1% must go to an **irrevocable non-grantor trust or another real owner**.
- Wyoming formation does not move the income. A holding entity formed in Wyoming but managed from Colorado has **Colorado nexus (commercial domicile) and files there**; Wyoming has no income tax but requires an **annual report**; holding membership interests in LLCs doing business in Colorado does not avoid Colorado filing for income flowing through. [nexus → `library/state_nexus_summary.md`]

**Recommendation: qualified yes.** Form the holding LLC only (a) with the Helio stock excluded (see §3), and (b) either with a bona fide second tax owner for the 1% (e.g., an **irrevocable non-grantor** trust) or with the acknowledgment that it is disregarded for federal purposes and used mainly for state-law ownership/governance. Expect to keep filing in Colorado and to pay the Wyoming annual report — the proposal's "simplicity" claim is overstated. [nexus]

## 2. Step 2 — Contribute all four interests (Services, Properties, Digital, Helio) to Holdings

**Tax consequence.**
- Contribution of an LLC interest for a partnership interest: **no gain or loss** under §721(a). So far the advisor is right. [721 → `library/irc_721_and_752.md`]
- **Liabilities.** Under §752(b), a decrease in a partner's share of partnership liabilities is treated as a money distribution; §731(a) recognizes gain only to the extent money distributed exceeds outside basis. Contributing Properties (mortgage $1,900,000, negative tax capital) shifts **1% × $1,900,000 = $19,000** of the mortgage to the 1% partner — a deemed $19,000 distribution to Nadia. [721]
  - Her outside basis in Properties should be at least the inside net tax basis: buildings 3,400,000 − depreciation 1,120,000 = 2,280,000; + land 600,000 + note receivable 400,000 = assets 3,280,000; − mortgage 1,900,000 = **1,380,000** (per `documents/properties_balance_sheet.md`). $19,000 « $1,380,000 → **no §731(a) gain**. Client confirmed the 1% "partner" is her revocable trust — if disregarded [UNVERIFIED], the shift is **$0**; if she substitutes a real 1% owner, the shift is $19,000. Either way, estimated gain on the Properties contribution: **$0** — conditional on the balance sheet (see §8: it does not reconcile; client had no further data). The mortgage being **nonrecourse to Nadia personally** (client-confirmed) matters only for allocation mechanics: nonrecourse liabilities allocate per the library's 99/1 example; her personal exposure is unaffected either way.
- **The Helio stock is different, and this is the trap.** §1202(c)(1) requires the taxpayer to have acquired the stock **at original issue**. §1202(h) lists the transfers that preserve acquisition status — gift, death, and distribution from a partnership to a partner — and **"a contribution of stock to a partnership is not among the listed transfers"**; the partnership did not acquire the stock at original issue, **and the stock ceases to qualify in its hands**. [1202 → `library/irc_1202_excerpt.md`] Contributing the 30% Helio stake to Holdings therefore **destroys the §1202 exclusion going forward**. A later distribution back to Nadia does not repair it: the tacking under (h) treats the distributee as acquiring "in the same manner as the transferor," and the transferor (Holdings) did *not* acquire at original issue.
- Even if the contribution did not disqualify, §1202(g) flows partnership QSBS gain to a partner only if the partner held the partnership interest when the partnership acquired the stock and at all times thereafter — a trap that makes later-added partners (including family trusts) unable to share the exclusion. [1202]

**Recommendation: do it for the three Oakline LLCs; do NOT do it for Helio Grid.** Hold the Helio stock in Nadia's own name. See §3.

## 3. What the Helio Grid stock should and should not be done with

**Why it is precious — §1202 checklist from the client file:**
- Original issue: SPA states the shares were "issued directly by the Company (original issuance)" — Feb 18, 2020, 3,000,000 shares at $0.004 = $12,000 cash (money, per §1202(c)(1)). [SPA → `documents/helio_grid_stock_purchase_agreement_excerpt.pdf`; 1202]
- Corporation: Delaware **C corporation**, which the SPA represents was engaged in the active conduct of a **qualified trade or business** with **aggregate gross assets ≤ $1,000,000** at issuance (the QSB-at-issuance test of §1202(c)(1)). [SPA; 1202]
- Holding period: acquired Feb 2020 → held **6.68 years** as of Oct 25, 2026 (>5). Stock acquired after Sept. 27, 2010 and held more than 5 years → **100% exclusion**. [1202]
- Cap: per-issuer cap = greater of $10M (her Feb-2020 issuance; the $15M tier applies only to post-July-4-2025 issuances) or 10× basis = $120,000 → **cap $10,000,000**. Gain at today's 409A value: $9,400,000 − $12,000 = **$9,388,000** — fully under the cap, with only **$612,000 of headroom**. Arithmetic in `numbers.json`. [1202]

**DO:**
1. **Hold it directly, in Nadia's name**, outside Holdings.
2. **Sell the shares directly** when the exit comes — the §1202 exclusion *is* the tax-free exit; up to $10M of gain needs no "reinvestment" scheme. [1202]
3. **Gifts and estate transfers are allowed**: stock acquired by gift or at death is treated as acquired in the same manner as Nadia, **with tacked holding period** — so transferring Helio shares to an irrevocable trust or to family for estate planning does **not** break QSBS. [1202(h)(1)]
4. **Watch the cap going forward.** Her stake is worth ≈$9.4M today; if it grows so that gain exceeds $10M, every dollar above the cap is fully taxable (the $15M tier does not apply to her 2020 issuance). Whether the cap can be multiplied across donees after gifts turns on whether it is per-taxpayer or truly per-issuer — the library says only "per-issuer," so I **cannot verify** any stacking strategy here. [1202]

**DO NOT:**
1. **Do not contribute it to Holdings** (step 2 as proposed). Contribution is not a §1202(h) transfer; the stock ceases to qualify in the partnership's hands. The proposal's promise that "when Helio exits, the gain lands in the holding so you can reinvest without tax" is false twice over: the contribution kills the exclusion, and a partnership is not a tax-deferral vehicle — a partnership pays no entity-level tax and partners are taxed on gains in the year the partnership realizes them, whether or not the cash is reinvested. **[The current-inclusion point is general partnership law; the library does not contain a citable provision — labeled UNVERIFIED.]**
2. **Do not do "contribute now, distribute back before exit."** Beyond the (h) failure noted in §2, a series of interdependent, pre-planned steps is collapsed into one transaction and taxed by substance, not form (*Commissioner v. Court Holding Co.*, 324 U.S. 331 (1945); *Gregory v. Helvering*, 293 U.S. 465 (1935)). [step → `library/step_transaction.md`]
3. Do not sell *through* any entity (including a future partnership or LLC) for this stock; §1202(g) adds a partner-continuity trap even where QSBS is held properly by an entity. [1202]
4. Facts to confirm before relying on the exclusion: whether Helio has **remained a domestic C corporation** since issuance (client could not confirm — open item, §8).

**Exit-window analysis (client update, Oct 2026: acquirer talks; sale in 12–18 months; her stake ≈ $9–10M).** Arithmetic: gain = stake − $12,000 → **$8,988,000 to $9,988,000**; cap $10,000,000 (her Feb-2020 issuance; the $15M tier applies only to post-July-4-2025 issuances). The full expected range remains **100% excludable**, but headroom collapses: at a $10.0M stake, only **$12,000** of cap remains; the break-even stake is **$10,012,000** — every dollar of her proceeds above that is fully taxable. That makes **earn-outs, escrow releases, purchase-price adjustments, and any "rollover" of part of the consideration genuine tax risks**, not just deal terms. Planning rules for the next 12–18 months:
- **(i)** Nadia sells her shares **directly**, in her own name — a cash sale of the shares is the only structure whose §1202 treatment this library can verify. [1202]
- **(ii)** If the deal is structured as a **merger, company redemption/repurchase, or stock-for-stock acquisition**, §1202 treatment cannot be verified from the library **[UNVERIFIED — redemption/reorganization rules not in library]**. Before signing anything non-cash, get verified advice; specifically reject any advisor suggestion to route exit proceeds or shares through Holdings — a contribution is not a §1202(h) transfer, and pre-planned interdependent steps toward one end are collapsed and taxed by substance. [1202(h); step]
- **(iii)** Do not move Helio shares during the window except through the (h)-listed routes (outright gift, estate transfer — tacked holding period preserved). No entity transfers, no "cleanup" contributions, no contribute-then-distribute-back schemes. [1202(h); step]
- **(iv)** Timing is safe on holding period: sale in 12–18 months puts her at ~7–8 years held (acquired Feb 2020) — comfortably past the 5-year threshold. [1202]

## 4. Step 3 — Holdings makes interest-free loans among subsidiaries; "one bank account, one set of books"

**Refuse as written.** Two independent failures:

**(a) §7872 recharacterization.** Interest-free/below-market loans between related entities are recharacterized: the lender is treated as receiving imputed interest at the AFR and the borrower as paying it, with a corresponding deemed distribution/contribution between commonly owned entities. [7872 → `library/irc_7872_afr.md`] On the existing balances at the library's mid-term AFR of **4.12%** (the library's engagement AFR; these undocumented loans have no stated term — assumption in `numbers.json`):
- Properties → Services, $400,000 × 4.12% = **$16,480/yr** (alt. short-term AFR 3.96%: $15,840)
- Services → Digital, $150,000 × 4.12% = **$6,180/yr** (alt. 3.96%: $5,940)
- Total **$22,660/yr** of imputed interest (alt. $21,780).
Because every entity flows to the same partners, the income and expense largely mirror each other *on paper*, but the mismatch risks (character limits, Digital's loss-trading position, state conformity) are not verifiable from the library **[UNVERIFIED]**, and the phantom income is taxable to the lender's owner as it accrues. Note: while Services/Properties/Digital remain Nadia's *disregarded* single-member LLCs, transfers between them arguably are not "loans" for federal tax at all (one taxpayer) — **[UNVERIFIED — the library does not address disregarded-entity loans]** — so the §7872 exposure crystallizes precisely when the proposal is adopted and the entities become separate taxpayers under a partnership. The proposal manufactures a new problem.

**(b) Veil piercing / substance.** The library is explicit: loans without notes, interest, or repayment terms are **evidence of commingling that undermines separate-entity liability protection**. [7872] "One bank account, one set of books" would strip the very liability separation the proposal claims as a benefit — and worse, concentrating Services (HVAC, 42 employees, real tort exposure), Properties, and Digital under one umbrella means a Services judgment reaches the Holdings interest itself, i.e., the Properties equity and — if the advisor's step 2 were followed — the Helio stock. Do not put Helio under that umbrella (see §3).

**Client has now confirmed the fact pattern: "No paperwork. I just move money where it's needed."** That is precisely the commingling evidence the library flags [7872], and it implies the Properties→Services building leases are likewise undocumented. This practice must stop **before** any restructuring — and it matters *more*, not less, once a $9–10M Helio exit makes the operating companies attractive deep-pocket targets. (The litigation-target observation is practical judgment, not a legal proposition; the library offers no authority on it.)

## 7. Intercompany-loan remediation (the existing three advances)

Current facts (`documents/intercompany_cash_flows.csv`; org chart): Properties→Services $250,000 (2024-03-01) + $150,000 (2025-06-15) = $400,000, no documentation; Services→Digital $150,000 (2025-09-01), "email only". All zero-interest, no notes, no repayment terms.

Options per loan — pick one per advance and paper it:
1. **Repay** the advance (cleanest; the cash is all Nadia's today, so repayment is pocket-to-pocket while the entities are still disregarded); or
2. **Recharacterize as a capital contribution** (fixes the negative-capital optics at Properties without creating debt); or
3. **Paper it as a real loan**: signed promissory note, fixed maturity, interest at or above the applicable AFR (library: short 3.96% / mid 4.12% / long 4.58%), scheduled payments actually executed, and no cross-entity offsetting through a shared account. [7872]

Whichever route is chosen, do it **before** the Holdings contribution, so Holdings' books start clean; otherwise the balances become intercompany loans within the new structure and §7872 and veil-piercing exposure attach to them going forward. [7872; step] The Properties "Due from Services $400,000" asset on the balance sheet should be extinguished or converted before contribution — otherwise it is a receivable from one disregarded subsidiary to another, worth documenting but worth $0 of incremental §752 basis clarity.
