## Line 1 — Gross receipts or sales: $50,750.00

Per the contract, line 1 is what Dana actually earned in 2025, not a mechanical sum of
the information returns (which would be $18,500 + $12,200 + $9,400 + $41,350 = $81,450
and would double-count). Two components, confirmed by the client's answer (Q1):
(a) **$41,350.00** — PayHub Form 1099-K box 1a gross payment amount. The client
confirms Beacon Fintech and Orchard Health paid her **through PayHub**, so their Forms
1099-NEC ($18,500.00 and $12,200.00) are inside this gross and are *not* added again;
the PayHub payouts that actually hit the account total $40,150.85 = $41,350.00 × 97.1%
(the 2.9% processor fee is on line 10), and the Schedule C instructions excerpt in the
library keeps gross receipts on line 1 with fees deducted separately, consistent with
IRC 61(a) including all income unless excluded. (b) **$9,400.00** — Tessaro Studios
paid by direct bank transfer outside PayHub (client answer, Q1); the four ACH credits
in the account (2025-03-14 $2,400.00, 2025-06-02 $3,100.00, 2025-08-19 $2,150.00,
2025-11-04 $1,750.00 = $9,400.00) match the Tessaro 1099-NEC box 1 of $9,400.00
exactly, so there is no gross-up or mismatch question. Total: 41,350.00 + 9,400.00 =
**$50,750.00**. The 1099-K's boxes 5a/5b ($0.00 / $1,866.68) are monthly amounts, not
separate line items — February's 1,866.68 × 97.1% = 1,812.55, which ties exactly to the
2025-02-06 PayHub payout. The 2024-12-27 Tessaro credit of $3,000.00 (invoice 1041) is
2024 income under the cash method and is excluded (see *Prior-year item*).
