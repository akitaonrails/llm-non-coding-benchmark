## 1. FGTS (140.000) — "é meu salário" is precisely the argument the STJ already faced and rejected

- The library (`library/stj_fgts_quotas_previdencia.md`) cites **REsp 1.399.199/RS (2ª Seção, 2017)**: the amounts **deposited in the FGTS account during the marriage are common property and are divided**, even though withdrawal happens later. The 2ª Seção is the STJ's uniforming body — it is not a whimsical single-judge decision.
- The "it's my salary" argument relies on CC art. 1.659, VI (personal labor earnings are excluded) — but according to the library, **that article does not exclude what has already been incorporated into the assets**. The salary spent is gone; the part the law forced him to set aside during the marriage (the 140.000) is savings of the marriage.
- **Practical detail:** Fernanda's proposal **renounced** the FGTS ("porque é dele"). That is a concession favorable to Marcelo — but it cannot be turned into the argument that it is his: if the deal is closed on the basis that "the FGTS is not divided," she can question it later using the same REsp. The correct treatment (what was done): the 70.000 enters the calculation of the meação and **her waiver is captured in writing in the agreement**.

## 2. Company (1.5 mi) — here the cousin is right, but only up to the comma

Fernanda **will not enter the company and will not receive quotas** — REsp 1.595.775: the settlement is by **apuração de haveres** in cash. Her demand for half the full value (750.000) is unfounded.
- **But "ponto final" ends there.** What communicates is the **appreciation during the marriage**: CC 1.660, V communicates the fruits of well particular, and the library's formula is exactly the difference between the wedding and the separation of fact: 1.500.000 − 400.000 = **1.100.000 → her half is 550.000**.

He says he **never distributed profit, he reinvested everything** — that retained profit that made the company grow from 400 mil to 1,5 mi is exactly what art. 1.660, V communicates. If he had paid himself 100% as pró-labore and the company were still worth 400 mil, there would be nothing to divide. The decision to reinvest created the common credit.
