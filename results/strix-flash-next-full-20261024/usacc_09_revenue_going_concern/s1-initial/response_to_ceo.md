# Response to CEO — Q4 numbers for the bridge

**From:** Controller · **To:** Rafael Castellanos (CEO) · **Date:** 2025-12-31
**Re:** Your email of 2025-12-30, "Q4 numbers for the bridge — need $1.5M+"

Rafael,

I ran the three items and the cash flow against the authoritative accounting guidance in our reference file. I understand what's riding on this — but I cannot deliver the P&L you asked for. Here is my analysis, item by item, and what I will and will not sign.

## 1. Halvorsen LOI — $480,000: cannot be booked

You wrote that they're "committed." The letter itself says the opposite: it is "non-binding," it requires a definitive Master Subscription Agreement, Halvorsen procurement-committee approval, and a security review, it "creates no legal obligation on either party," and either side "may discontinue discussions at any time." The standard is explicit that a letter of intent that is expressly non-binding and subject to a definitive agreement **does not create enforceable rights and obligations** and is not a contract for revenue purposes (ASC 606-10-25-1). There is no contract, so there is no revenue. **$0.**

## 2. Saltline kits — $210,000: cannot be booked

A "bill-and-hold" only moves revenue to us when control has actually transferred, which requires **all** of: a substantive reason for the hold (yes — Saltline asked for it); the kits **identified separately as belonging to Saltline** (no — ops was told to pull from general stock and "don't bother tagging anything"); the goods ready for transfer; and our **unable to use the kits or ship them to another customer** (no — untagged general stock is exactly what we can ship to someone else). The guidance says pulling units from general, untagged stock fails the identification and no-substitution tests outright (ASC 606-10-55-83).

Separately, the side letter you had countersigned gives Saltline a **full refund on any unused kits for 180 days** if Tampa is cancelled or reduced. That makes the consideration "highly uncertain" variable consideration, recognizable only to the extent not expected to be refunded (ASC 606-10-55-22) — and we have no basis to estimate that.

On "the auditors were fine with a bill-and-hold last year": they accepted the **Trestle** deal because the customer requested it in writing, the units were **physically segregated and labeled**, and **no right of return existed** — and they wrote in the management letter that they would **not** reach that conclusion absent those facts. Our facts are different on every one of those points. **$0.**

## 3. Ironvale hosting: it is not a service — but this doesn't help revenue either

Calling it a service is a label, not an analysis. The contract identifies specific servers **by serial number**, gives us **exclusive** use of Cage 14, bars Ironvale from substituting equipment without our consent, and lets us direct the workloads, configuration, and operating systems. That is the textbook embedded lease in the standard — dedicated servers identified by serial number, used exclusively by us, whose use we direct (ASC 842-10-15-3 to 15-20). It must be capitalized: roughly a **$648,000** ROU asset and lease liability at inception (36 × $18,000), with about **$594,000** of liability remaining at 12/31 — before discounting, which will lower it once we get our borrowing rate from Treasury. Power, cooling, and network are non-lease components to be split out. Note: this reclassification does **not** change revenue or (pending the discount rate) Q4 net income — but the $594,000 obligation now appears on the balance sheet, which Crestline will see.

## What the corrected Q4 shows

**Revenue: $850,000** (subscription only), not $1,540,000:

- Net income moves from +$456,000 to a **−$234,000 loss** (456,000 − 690,000).

## Going concern: the disclosure stays

You asked me not to add a going-concern paragraph. I can't, because it isn't optional. Your own 13-week forecast shows cash going **negative the week of February 20, 2026** and reaching **−$509,000 by March 27** without new money. The standard requires management to assess substantial doubt over the year after issuance, and it says plans count only if they're **probable to be implemented and probable to fix the problem** — and that "a **non-binding** term sheet with conditions precedent is **not a probable plan**" (ASC 205-40-50). The Crestline note is expressly non-binding, and its condition — audited/reviewed Q4 revenue ≥ $1,500,000 — fails on the correct numbers. So substantial doubt exists, it is not alleviated, and the financial statements must say so.
