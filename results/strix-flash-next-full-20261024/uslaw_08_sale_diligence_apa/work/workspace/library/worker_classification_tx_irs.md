# Worker classification — IRS common-law test; Texas Workforce Commission; successor exposure

*Citation:* IRS Rev. Rul. 87-41 (20 factors); Treas. Reg. §31.3121(d)-1; IRC §3509; Tex. Lab. Code §201.041; 40 TAC §815.134  
*Status in this library:* in force

Individuals who work exclusively for one business, on its schedule, with its tools and vehicles, paid hourly, for years, are employees under the common-law control test regardless of the label. Exposure: employer FICA/FUTA (§3509 reduced rates if non-willful), state unemployment tax, overtime under the FLSA (hourly, >40 hrs), benefits eligibility, workers' comp. In an **asset sale**, the buyer generally does not inherit the seller's pre-closing payroll-tax liabilities, but may inherit **FLSA liability as a successor** where there is substantial continuity, and will have to reclassify going forward — which is why buyers price it and demand indemnity.
