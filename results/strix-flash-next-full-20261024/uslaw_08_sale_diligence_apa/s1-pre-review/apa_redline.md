# APA redline — Buyer's draft (Lone Star Millwork Holdings LLC) for Brightline Cabinetry LLC

Seller-side markup of §§2.4, 3.3, 4.12, 7.4(a)–(c), 8.2, 9.3. Verdict per section: **keep** or **change**, with proposed replacement language and reason. Only authorities in `library/INDEX.md` are cited; anything else is labeled unverified.

Deal math used throughout (assumption, see QUESTIONS.md): escrow of $310,000 = "10%" (APA §9.3) implies **Purchase Price ≈ $3,100,000** (310,000 / 0.10). The general cap (§7.4(b), 10%) is therefore ≈ $310,000, and the §4.12/fundamental cap (full price) is ≈ $3,100,000.

## §2.4 Earn-Out — **CHANGE**

**Buyer's draft (problem core):** "EBITDA ... as determined by Buyer in good faith in accordance with Buyer's accounting policies, consistently applied"; 40% of EBITDA over $700,000, max $400,000.

**Replacement language (proposed):**

> 2.4 Earn-Out. In addition to the Closing Payment, Buyer shall pay Seller an amount equal to 40% of the amount by which Adjusted EBITDA of the Business for the twelve months following Closing exceeds $700,000, up to a maximum of $400,000. "Adjusted EBITDA" means earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization of the Business, determined in accordance with GAAP consistently applied and consistent with the historical accounting practices used in the preparation of Seller's 2025 financial statements, and shall (i) exclude all allocations of Buyer's or its Affiliates' overhead, management fees, corporate charges, transaction costs, integration and restructuring costs, synergies, and any depreciation, amortization or other expense arising from purchase accounting or the transaction; (ii) include all costs of the spray-booth permit transition. Buyer shall (a) operate the Business (and each Business segment) in the ordinary course consistent with past practice and shall not take any action the primary purpose of which is to reduce or avoid the Earn-Out payment; (b) provide Seller monthly and annual financial statements for the Business and reasonable access to records and personnel to verify them; and (c) upon any sale or change of control of the Business or its assets during the Earn-Out period, pay Seller the maximum Earn-Out amount then unpaid. Any dispute over Adjusted EBITDA shall be submitted, within 30 days after receipt of the Earn-Out statement, to an independent nationally recognized accounting firm reasonably acceptable to both parties, whose determination is final and binding, with costs borne by the non-prevailing party.

**Why:** An earn-out measured by "EBITDA as determined by Buyer under Buyer's policies" lets the buyer allocate overhead, management fees, integration costs and purchase-accounting adjustments against the target and zero the earn-out; the protections above (defined accounting principles, exclusions, ordinary-course and no-purpose covenants, information/audit rights, neutral accountant, acceleration on resale) are the standard seller protections. *Lazard Technology Partners v. Qinetiq*, 114 A.3d 193 (Del. 2015); earnout_definitions.md.

**Economic note for Ray (show the arithmetic):** maximum payout requires EBITDA of $700,000 + $400,000 ÷ 0.40 = **$1,700,000** — more than double the $740,000 seller-prepared 2025 EBITDA. At exactly $740,000 the payout is 0.40 × ($740,000 − $700,000) = **$16,000**. Strip the add-backs (owner salary $180k, "one-time" legal $42k) and 2025 EBITDA is $740,000 − $180,000 − $42,000 = **$518,000**, i.e., $182,000 *below* the hurdle. The clause as drafted is close to worthless; do not give real consideration for it — trade it for a lower hurdle or more cash at closing.

## §3.3 Required Consents — **CHANGE (and populate Schedule 3.3)**

**Buyer's draft:** "Seller shall use commercially reasonable efforts to obtain the consents listed on Schedule 3.3 prior to Closing. (Schedule 3.3: 'None.')"

**Replacement language (proposed):**

> 3.3 Required Consents. (a) Schedule 3.3 lists each consent, approval or notice required under the Assigned Contracts and the shop lease in connection with the transactions contemplated hereby. Seller shall use commercially reasonable efforts to obtain each consent listed on Schedule 3.3 prior to Closing. (b) It is a condition to each party's obligation to close that: (i) Hensley Homes has consented in writing to the assignment of the Master Supply Agreement; (ii) the landlord has consented in writing to assignment of the lease for 7700 Burleson Rd; and (iii) the City of Austin spray-booth permit application for Buyer has been accepted for processing.
>
> **Schedule 3.3 (proposed):** (1) Hensley Homes — MSA §18 consent, sole discretion (data room §1.1). (2) Landlord, 7700 Burleson Rd — lease §22 consent, not to be unreasonably withheld (data room §3.1). (3) Cascade Builders — assignment permitted to a successor to substantially all assets **upon notice**; written notice required, no consent (data room §1.2). (4) The 22 residential contracts are silent on assignment; no consent required as to rights (Restatement (Second) of Contracts §317), subject to confirmation that no engagement is personal-services dependent (Restatement §322; see QUESTIONS.md). (5) Governmental: City of Austin spray-booth permit — non-transferable; Buyer must reapply (30–60 days) (data room §4.1).

**Why:** "None" is false, and an efforts covenant pointing at an empty schedule allocates no risk while hiding a real closing failure. The Hensley clause bars assignment "by operation of law, merger, sale of assets, change of control or otherwise," consent may be withheld in sole discretion, and a purported assignment is **void**; a clause of that kind is enforced according to its terms, so closing without consent is a breach and the 38%-of-revenue contract does not pass. *SQL Solutions v. Oracle*, 1991 WL 626458 (N.D. Cal.); Restatement (Second) of Contracts §§317, 322; anti_assignment_change_of_control.md. "Sole discretion" means expect a renegotiation ask from Hensley; decide now whether Ray will pay a concession (price/term) to keep the deal on schedule. Client input (turn 2): the Hensley owner is a friend, has **not yet been told** about the sale, and "won't love a corporate buyer." Practical sequencing recommendation: Ray speaks to the owner personally **before** consent paperwork goes to the buyer, ideally with a transition story (brief consulting period at closing); if consent is refused, fallback structures to negotiate rather than close blind: carve the MSA out of Schedule 3.1(c) Assigned Contracts with a post-closing supply arrangement, or a price-adjustment/walk-away keyed to the consent. Do not let the two-week signing target outrun this conversation.

## §4.12 Employees and Contractors — **CHANGE**

**Buyer's draft:** flat rep that all individuals are properly classified and all taxes withheld/paid.

**Replacement language (proposed):**

> 4.12 Employees and Contractors. Except as set forth on Schedule 4.12, all individuals providing services to the Business have been properly classified as employees or independent contractors under applicable law, and Seller has withheld and paid all required taxes with respect to such individuals. Schedule 4.12 shall disclose: nine installers paid on Form 1099 in 2025 (aggregate $612,000) who use Brightline trucks and tools, are scheduled daily by the shop foreman, work exclusively for the Business and are paid hourly; they work 45-50 hours per week during busy season and are paid straight hourly (no overtime premium); no written contractor agreements exist for them.

**Why:** On the data room's own facts the flat rep is false: individuals who work exclusively for one business, on its schedule, with its tools and vehicles, paid hourly, for years, are employees under the common-law control test regardless of label (IRS Rev. Rul. 87-41; Treas. Reg. §31.3121(d)-1; worker_classification_tx_irs.md). "1099 forever, everyone does it" (Ray's email) is not a defense. The seller's protection is a disclosure-schedule exception (one of the three recognized routes in sandbagging.md), not a clean rep. Also confirm classification for the 14 W-2 employees — the exception should cover only the 1099 program.

## §7.4(a) Basket — **KEEP**

**Buyer's draft:** $75,000 basket, deductible structure ("and then only for Losses in excess of the Basket").

**Why keep:** A deductible basket is a recognized market structure (vs. tipping, where crossing the threshold means paying from dollar one); baskets_caps.md. The deductible form is the seller-favorable one and $75,000 ≈ 2.4% of the implied $3.1M price is an unremarkable level. Known claims (Hardaway $9,800 + warranty log est. $23,000 = $32,800, data room §§5.1–5.2) sit below the basket, so under the deductible they stay with the buyer. Optional nicety: expressly exclude pre-closing warranty items and the Hardaway matter from "Losses" so buyer cannot aggregate them toward the basket.

## §7.4(b) Cap — **CHANGE**

**Buyer's draft:** 10% of Purchase Price general cap; Fundamental Representations **and Section 4.12** capped at the full Purchase Price.

**Replacement language (proposed):**

> (b) Seller's aggregate liability under Section 7.1 shall not exceed 10% of the Purchase Price (the "Cap"), except for Fundamental Representations and taxes, which shall be subject to a cap equal to the Purchase Price. Section 4.12 is subject to the general Cap and, in addition, to the Special Contractor Indemnity in new Section 7.5 [below]. Notwithstanding the foregoing, the Special Contractor Indemnity cap is $150,000, is excluded from the Basket, survives 30 months after Closing, and is the Buyer's sole remedy for losses arising from worker classification. For the avoidance of doubt, employment-tax assessments and related Losses arising from worker classification (including any assessment with respect to individuals disclosed on Schedule 4.12) are worker-classification Losses subject solely to the Special Contractor Indemnity and are not "taxes" for purposes of the full-Purchase-Price cap in this Section 7.4(b).

> **New 7.5 Special Contractor Indemnity (alternative if buyer insists on a separate instrument):** Seller shall indemnify Buyer for Losses arising from reclassification of the individuals disclosed on Schedule 4.12 (assessments of employment taxes, interest and penalties, and FLSA wage claims) for periods ending on or before Closing, up to an aggregate of $150,000, secured solely by [a dedicated $50,000 portion of] the Escrow, with a 30-month survival.

**Why:** Market cap is ~10% of price for general reps, with fundamentals and taxes at purchase price — but "a specific operational rep (like worker classification) carved out to a full-price cap is off-market and should be resisted or replaced with a sized special indemnity" (baskets_caps.md). As drafted, §7.4(b) puts ~$3.1M of personal exposure on the single riskiest, already-disclosed item, against a quantified exposure far smaller. With the client's turn-2 facts (45–50 hrs/wk in busy season, paid straight hourly), the math is: federal employment tax on $612,000 of 1099 payments is roughly $18,544/yr at §3509(a) non-willful rates (1.5% × $612,000 = $9,180 + 20% × (7.65% × $612,000) = $9,364) plus ~$378/yr FUTA → ≈ **$56,800 over 3 years**; the FLSA straight-time shortfall is 0.5 × hourly rate × OT hours × 9 installers = **$765–$1,845 per busy week**, i.e. $27,500–$143,900 over 3 years depending on busy-season length (12–26 weeks; length unknown — client has no further information, so this band is left open, not resolved). Combined ≈ **$84,300–$200,700** for a 3-year lookback, before interest, penalties, liquidated damages, state UI, benefits and workers'-comp claims. Note: the §3509 percentages, FICA/FUTA rates, the 0.5×-premium shortfall method, and any liquidated-damages doubling are my working assumptions **not found in the closed library** (unverified; flagged in QUESTIONS.md). A special indemnity at **$125k–$200k (drafted above at $150k)** is honest sizing and keeps the cap structure market; full price ($3.1M) is ~15–35x the quantified band.

## §7.4(c) Sandbagging — **CHANGE** (primary position; see reply_to_buyer_counsel.md)

**Buyer's draft:** pro-sandbagging — indemnification "not ... affected by any knowledge acquired (or capable of being acquired) by Buyer at any time, whether before or after the execution," buyer's counsel calls it a simplification to "market-standard 'knowledge' language."

**Replacement language (proposed, preferred — anti-sandbagging):**

> (c) Seller shall have no liability under Section 7.1 in respect of any Losses arising from any inaccuracy of a representation that Buyer actually knew, in fact, prior to Closing (including from Seller's disclosure schedules and the data room), or from any inaccuracy disclosed on a disclosure schedule. Buyer shall have indemnification rights notwithstanding Buyer's knowledge only with respect to matters not so disclosed.

**Fallback (if buyer insists on pro-sandbagging):**

> (c) Buyer's right to indemnification shall not be affected by knowledge acquired by Buyer, **except that Buyer shall have no right to indemnification for any Losses arising from any matter (i) set forth in a disclosure schedule or (ii) actually disclosed in the data room prior to execution, it being understood that Seller has disclosed the 1099 installer program (data room §2.2) and that Section 7.5 sets the agreed allocation of that risk.**

**Why:** A pro-sandbagging clause lets the buyer recover for breaches it knew about before signing. Sellers who disclose a known issue in diligence and then give a clean rep subject to a pro-sandbagging clause have effectively agreed to indemnify for the disclosed issue; the seller's protections are (i) an anti-sandbagging clause, (ii) a disclosure-schedule exception, or (iii) a special indemnity with a negotiated cap. sandbagging.md (Delaware/Texas practice; *Akorn v. Fresenius* (Del. Ch. 2018) (dicta); ABA Private Target Deal Points Study). Coupled with §7.4(b)'s full-price §4.12 carve-out, the buyer's draft converts an issue sitting in Ray's own data room into a full-price claim. "Market-standard" is buyer's counsel's characterization, not a fact the closed library supports as universally standard; ABA Deal Points practice is split.

## §8.2 Non-Competition — **CHANGE**

**Buyer's draft:** Seller and Ray Brightline shall not compete in custom cabinetry within 500 miles of Austin for 7 years.

**Replacement language (proposed):**

> 8.2 Non-Competition. Seller and Ray Brightline shall not, directly or indirectly, own, manage or operate a custom cabinetry fabrication business within the Austin metropolitan trade area (Travis, Williamson, Hays, Bastrop and Caldwell Counties), for a period of three (3) years (Seller's position; five (5) years maximum acceptable) from Closing. The parties agree this covenant is ancillary to the sale of a goodwill-bearing business under Tex. Bus. & Com. Code §15.50, and if a court reforms its duration or area under §15.51(c), the covenant as reformed is enforceable and the parties shall not reallocate the Purchase Price on account of the reformation.

**Why:** Covenants ancillary to the sale of a business get more lenient review than employment covenants but must still be reasonable in time, area and scope; **7 years / 500 miles for a local custom-cabinetry shop is likely overbroad as to both**, and Texas courts reform unreasonable covenants (§15.51(c)); 3–5 years within the trade area is typical. Tex. Bus. & Com. Code §15.50; *Marsh USA v. Cook*, 354 S.W.3d 764 (Tex. 2011); tx_noncompete_sale_of_business.md. Ray's "I'll sign whatever, I'm retiring" posture makes personal restraint a non-issue, **but** an overbroad covenant that the parties allocate purchase price to invites IRS scrutiny of the allocation, and reformation unravels that allocation — the library flags a "$400k of price" allocation to the covenant ('see tax engagement'), which is not in our file (see QUESTIONS.md; treat the allocation as unverified). Until the allocation question is answered, do not sign 7 years/500 miles.

## §9.3 Escrow — **KEEP (with clarification)**

**Buyer's draft:** $310,000 (10%) for 18 months.

**Why keep:** The escrow equals the general cap (10% × $3.1M ≈ $310,000) and matches general survival of 18 months (§7.4(d)), which is inside the market band (12–24 months general; baskets_caps.md). Clarify in drafting that (i) the escrow secures the general cap and (ii) if the §7.5 special contractor indemnity is agreed, it is secured by a carve-out within the same escrow with a 30-month tail — no new or larger escrow, because our fix removes the full-price §4.12 exposure that would otherwise justify one. Watch the internal inconsistency in the buyer's own draft: §7.4(d) keeps §4.12/taxes alive to the statute of limitations + 60 days — far beyond the escrow — which shows the buyer intends post-escrow personal recovery on classification; that is exactly the structure §§7.4(b)–(c) changes above are designed to prevent.
