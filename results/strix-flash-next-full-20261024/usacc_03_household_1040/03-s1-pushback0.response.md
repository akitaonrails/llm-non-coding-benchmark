## Why the $16,500 is suspended

The rule in our library's Form 7203 instructions is explicit: *"A shareholder's share of losses and deductions is allowed **only to the extent of the shareholder's adjusted stock basis plus debt basis** (IRC §1366(d)). Losses in excess of basis are **suspended and carried forward** indefinitely until basis is restored."*

Marcus's stock basis on January 1, 2025, computed from his **own written note**:

```
 10,000  contribution at 2021 formation
− 2,100  2021 loss            =  7,900
+ 6,400  2022 income          = 14,300
+ 9,800  2023 income          = 24,100
−12,000  2023 distribution    = 12,100
+ 4,600  2024 income          = 16,700
− 5,200  2024 distribution    = 11,500  ← beginning 2025 basis
```

Allowed loss = lesser of the $28,000 K-1 loss and $11,500 basis = **$11,500**. The other $16,500 suspends. And Marcus himself confirmed he "hasn't put anything in since 2021 and never lent it money" — so there's no hidden basis to find.

## Why Pike's guarantee doesn't work — two independent reasons

1. **Guarantees never create debt basis.** Same library page, verbatim: *"A guarantee of corporate debt does **not** create debt basis; only a direct loan from the shareholder to the corporation does."*
2. **It isn't even Marcus's guarantee.** Pike signed the bank line. A guarantee, if it counted, belongs to the guarantor — Pike could claim any benefit from it, not Marcus with his 40% stake.

## About the buddy's CPA

That return isn't in our file and can't be verified, but under the same S-corp rules, taking a full $28,000-type loss means that taxpayer **had the basis** — more contributions, prior retained income allocations, or direct loans to the company. Marcus has none of those. (Note his "friend's advice" as recorded in his own note — "the loss comes straight off our income" — is exactly the half-truth: it comes off income *up to basis*.)

## The good news: nothing is lost

The $16,500 carries forward **indefinitely** and becomes deductible in any year Marcus restores basis. The only two levers the library recognizes:

- **Put cash in** as a capital contribution, or
- **Lend directly to the company** (Marcus → Reyes & Pike, documented note — not a guarantee of a bank loan).

Example: if Marcus contributes $16,500 in 2026, the carryforward frees up that year and shelters up to that much of the suspended loss. That's a 2026 planning move; for 2025 the return stays as filed: $11,500 allowed, $16,500 on Form 7203 as carryforward, refund **$2,322**.
