Here's the honest answer for Imani — she's half right, and the half she's right about is exactly what we plead, just not under that label. (Recorded as an appendix to `strategy_memo.md`.)

## Why the articles don't match her case

Managers **do** owe loyalty — as a *default*. But the Delaware LLC Act, 6 Del. C. §18-1101(c), says those duties "may be expanded or restricted or **eliminated** by provisions in the limited liability company agreement," and **her own agreement eliminated them, by name**. §7.3 doesn't just say "no fiduciary duties" — it says "including the duties of care **and loyalty**," and that the Manager's duties "are solely those expressly set forth in this Agreement." Castellan (or his lawyer) wrote that sentence. The judge reads her contract, not the articles.

## Why pleading it "to scare him" backfires

The complaint must attach the very agreement that kills the count. His first motion dismisses the fiduciary count off its face — an early, cheap win **for the drafter of the waiver**, months of delay before discovery on the claims that actually threaten him, and it teaches the court our best claims were mis-labeled. A $189,100 offer's author is not scared by a count his own §7.3 defeats; he's reassured.

## What survives — and it's the same grievance

Three routes get her "he's self-dealing" story in front of a judge, in terms the waiver can't touch:

1. **§5.4 *is* the duty of loyalty, in writing.** Affiliate transactions must be arm's-length **and** disclosed in writing within 30 days. He paid his own company $1,050,000, disclosed nothing, and labeled it "management expenses" in her summaries. That's a loyalty claim — pleaded as breach of contract, which no waiver erases.
2. **The implied covenant cannot be eliminated — the statute and §7.3 both say so.** §18-1101(c)'s proviso, §7.3's own proviso ("nothing herein eliminates the implied contractual covenant"), and §18-1101(e) (no exculpation for bad-faith covenant violations). And §6.3 hands the Manager discretion — "bona fide Company purpose," fair value "determined by the Manager **in good faith**." The library's rule (*Dieckman*) is precisely that discretion must be exercised honestly, not arbitrarily — **despite exculpation**. The un-reproducible $7.2M and 6.1% live here.
3. **§7.3 waives only "the Manager's" duties.** As an 82% **Member**, voting himself the fees and the book-value buyout, Castellan's controlling-member duties are a live, medium-strength claim (claims.json #4), pending the full agreement.

## The scare factor, quantified

What actually moves him: unwinding the dilution **erases his §9.2 option** and reprices the exit toward **$1,764,000** instead of $189,100; the fee claims with her now fact-supported tolling story expose **$1,050,000+**; and the demanded ledgers expose how "$7.2M" was conjured and how his $270,000 payment was booked. That's the leverage — a dismissed loyalty count buys none of it.

So the answer to her: *we are suing him for disloyalty — we're just pleading it in the words the contract can't silence.* The complaint will tell her story ("he paid himself $1.05 million in secret and used a text message to steal eleven points of her ownership") under §5.4, the implied covenant, and the controlling-member claim. If she still insists on a standalone fiduciary count after that, it's her call as client — but it's a count we'd be handing him on a motion, and I'd log the instruction and its cost.
