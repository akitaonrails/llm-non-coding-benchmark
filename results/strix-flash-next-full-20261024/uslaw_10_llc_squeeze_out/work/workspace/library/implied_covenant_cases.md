# Implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing in Delaware LLCs

*Citation:* *Nemec v. Shrader*, 991 A.2d 1120 (Del. 2010); *Gerber v. Enterprise Products Holdings*, 67 A.3d 400 (Del. 2013); *Dieckman v. Regency GP LP*, 155 A.3d 358 (Del. 2017)  
*Status in this library:* in force

The implied covenant is a **gap-filler**: it applies only where the agreement is silent on the matter and the parties would have agreed to the term had they thought of it; it cannot override express terms. It is **not** a substitute for fiduciary duties. However, where a contract vests discretion in one party (e.g., a 'good faith' fair-value determination, or a capital call 'for a bona fide Company purpose'), the implied covenant requires that the discretion be exercised honestly and not arbitrarily or in bad faith to deprive the other party of the fruits of the bargain (*Dieckman*: conflict-committee process used to mislead unitholders violated the covenant despite exculpation).
