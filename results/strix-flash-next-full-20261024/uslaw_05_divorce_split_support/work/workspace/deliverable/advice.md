## 3. Maintenance: his stated tax treatment is wrong — in your favor

For a decree signed after 12/31/2018, alimony is **neither deductible by the payor nor taxable to the recipient** (TCJA §11051; `library/irc_1041_121_71.md`). The proposal's "deductible to Husband and taxable to Wife" is a drafting error (he appears to be pricing 2018 law). Fix the decree language: the $2,000/month is tax-free to you and un-deductible by him — so $2,000/month for 36 months ($72,000) costs him more than he thinks. Child support is never deductible or includible. Do **not** let him "gross up" or shrink maintenance on the theory that he loses a deduction he never had — but note the Columbia worksheet still adds maintenance to your income and subtracts it from his when computing child support, which trims his support number slightly (Section 4).

## 5. The house — your 3–6 year goal, answered

You said you want to stay 3–6 years but would sell if you cannot refinance. Two separate questions — and the answers are "no" and "you don't need to."

**(a) Refinance verdict: assume you cannot qualify.** Refinancing $400,000 at the current 6.1% for 24 years costs **P&I ≈ $2,648/month** (400,000 × 0.0050833 / (1 − 1.0050833⁻²⁸⁸)); first-month interest alone is $2,033. Against your $6,000/month gross, P&I alone is **44.1% of gross before taxes/insurance** (property tax and insurance on this home are not in the file — unknown). Even counting support income while it lasts (6,000 + 2,000 + 2,023 = $10,023) P&I is 26.4%, and after maintenance ends at month 36 it is **33.0% of $8,023** — before the tax and insurance that a $1.1M home carries. Typical lender limits are ~31%/43% and lenders discount or exclude support that expires (**[unverified]** — underwriting norms are not in the library). You have no pre-approval. Treat refinance as **not available unless an underwriter proves otherwise**.

**(b) You can still stay: the deferred joint sale (Scenario A2) — no new loan required.** Instead of one spouse buying the other out, the decree keeps **both of you on title and the mortgage**, gives you occupancy, and fixes a **sale date/milestone** (e.g., 3 years, or upon the youngest finishing middle school) with **net proceeds split 50/50**.

- **The tax trick is in the library:** §121(b)(1) gives **each** taxpayer a $250,000 exclusion, and §121(d)(3)(A) treats the **non-occupying spouse as having "used" the home as a principal residence while you occupy it under the divorce instrument** (`library/irc_1041_121_71.md`). So at a post-divorce joint sale, **Husband keeps his $250,000 exclusion even though he moved out**, and you keep yours — the full $500,000 shelter survives the divorce. It does not require a joint return.
- **Numbers at today's price:** gain 734,000 − 500,000 shelter = **234,000 taxable, 117,000 each**. Your tax: 117,000 × 15% = **17,550** — and the 3.8% NIIT likely does **not** hit you, because your MAGI that year (72,000 + 117,000 = 189,000) stays under the $200,000 single NIIT threshold in `library/tax_rates_assumed.md` (child support is never taxable; post-TCJA maintenance is not income — `library/irc_1041_121_71.md`). Husband's share: 117,000 × 18.8% = **21,996** (his income puts him over the threshold). Total tax **39,546** vs **43,992** for selling now on a joint return — the deferred plan is **~$4,400 cheaper**, and net proceeds 594,454 (297,227 each) vs 590,008 (295,004 each). **That tax edge is conditional:** your share escapes NIIT only while your taxable share stays at or below roughly 128,000 (72,000 + share ≤ 200,000); and `library/tax_rates_assumed.md` also prescribes a blanket conservative convention — assume NIIT on the taxable portion when comparing — under which your tax is 21,996, the total is 43,992, and **A2 and B are worth exactly the same** (678,104 each). Treat the tax saving as a bonus and the stay as the main event. **[unverified]** your exact MAGI that year; the precise §1411 mechanics (NIIT on the lesser of net investment income or MAGI over threshold) are not in the library — the library gives the threshold and the conservative convention.
- **Who pays while you stay:** P&I $2,648 + tax/insurance is more than $6,000 gross can carry. The clean fix: have **Husband pay the housing costs directly as maintenance** — the mortgage note stays his as co-owner, and $2,648 of P&I alone already exceeds the $2,000 cash maintenance he offered. Post-TCJA this is tax-neutral for both of you (non-deductible, non-includible — Section 3). Whatever pays down principal (~**$24,221** over 36 months at $2,648) should be **credited to the payer** at closing.
- **Risks — price them:** (1) *Appreciation* grows gain past the $500,000 shelter — illustratively at +3%/yr for 3 years the price ≈ 1,202,000, taxable gain ≈ 164,940 each, your MAGI crosses $200,000, and total tax rises to ≈ **62,017** (no market data in file — illustrative only); (2) **Husband's creditors or liens** could attach to his retained interest; (3) **cooperation risk** — he must sign listing agreements years from now.
