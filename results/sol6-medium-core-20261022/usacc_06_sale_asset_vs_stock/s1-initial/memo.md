# Brightline Cabinetry: the two LOIs

**Recommendation.** Do not sign Lone Star's LOI with Exhibit A as written. Counter its allocation, obtain support for fair market values, remove the unsupported $400,000 covenant/consulting allocation, and seek a price increase or other protection. Keep Hensley's offer active while its unsecured note and inherited liabilities are evaluated. The base illustration puts Hensley $187,200 ahead of Lone Star's draft after modeled tax, and $76,700 ahead of the proposed Lone Star allocation; these are **not final closing economics**. The pass-through effect of the built-in gains tax, closing bases, assumed payables, escrow mechanics, and any liquidation consequences remain open. In addition, Hensley's $500,000 principal is due over time from an unsecured obligor, while Lone Star offers $3,100,000 cash at closing.

## Basis, rates, and scope of the numbers

The JSON comparison uses these explicit assumptions: closing in 2026; $3,100,000 is Buyer A's entire tax consideration with no extra value for assumed payables; receivables have $200,000 tax basis; inventory has $140,000 basis; equipment's $240,000 register basis is current; goodwill basis is zero; Ray's stock basis remains $420,000; Ray materially participates; all capital gain is long term; no transaction expenses, other assets or liabilities, Texas franchise tax, federal timing benefit, or second distribution/liquidation tax is included. The A covenant payment is treated as ordinary income to Ray. We use the library's 37% ordinary and 20% capital rates and its stated exclusion of active-business gain from the 3.8% net investment income tax ([2026 rates](../library/capital_gain_rates.md)). The S corporation's $237,300 built-in gains tax is shown separately. **The JSON's `total_tax` is a gross preliminary burden before any shareholder tax benefit from treating that entity tax as a corporate loss.** Its `net_after_tax` is nominal stated price less that preliminary tax, not assured cash Ray can spend at closing.

These assumptions matter. The client confirms **no further information is available**. Closing payables, tax-basis updates, an inventory appraisal, a recent goodwill appraisal, Hensley's balance sheet, and whether the $100,000 escrow is withheld from its stated closing cash all remain **unknown**. Ray confirms the $500,000 note is unsecured, says he will sign a five-year covenant because he is retiring, and reports a $9,800 Hardaway dispute plus ordinary warranty exposure. The modeled figures are therefore conditional illustrations, not a reconciled expected tax bill or verified cash comparison.

Compared with the draft, Ray's $2,480,000 estimate is $303,200 too high under these assumptions. This is a comparison convention, not a closing tax return.

## Built-in gains tax and unresolved shareholder effect

Brightline elected S status on January 1, 2023. The five-year recognition period therefore runs through December 31, 2027, so a 2026 asset closing is inside it. At conversion, the appraisal recorded $900,000 built-in goodwill gain and $230,000 built-in equipment gain. In both A scenarios, the modeled recognition on each asset exceeds those starting amounts. The capped net recognized built-in gain is **$900,000 + $230,000 = $1,130,000**; at 21%, entity tax is **$237,300** ([§1374](../library/irc_1374.md)). The higher present equipment gain does not raise the equipment cap above $230,000.

The §1374 reference also says the tax paid is treated as an S corporation loss. Its effect on Ray's shareholder tax is **not quantified in the JSON** because the library does not explain the loss's character, use, or stock-basis and liquidation mechanics. If the entire $237,300 produced a current benefit at 20%, it would save $47,460; at 37%, $87,801. These are *illustrative mathematical bounds under assumed full use*, not a statement of the governing tax treatment. They would move A's draft net to $2,224,260–$2,264,601 and counter net to $2,334,760–$2,375,101, before any other adjustments. The counter and stock offer can therefore change rank. A further shareholder gain could arise when sale cash is distributed or the company liquidates: the assumed $580,000 asset basis exceeds Ray's stated $420,000 stock basis by $160,000. Whether that produces a $160,000 additional gain (a $32,000 tax illustration at 20%) depends on other balance-sheet items and governing rules **not in this library**; the possible liquidation treatment is **unverified** and excluded from the JSON. A transaction tax adviser should reconcile sale proceeds, liabilities, stock basis, corporate tax loss, distributions, and any liquidation before either LOI becomes binding.

## Note timing, cash, and liabilities

Hensley pays $2,350,000 nominal cash and $500,000 unsecured principal over five years at 6% interest. On the stated $420,000 basis, the principal gain ratio is **$2,430,000 / $2,850,000 = 85.2631579%**. If the stock sale qualifies for the installment method and no escrow or other timing rule changes the figures, the $2,350,000 initial principal payment carries **$2,003,684.21** gain and approximately **$400,736.84** capital tax; each hypothetical $100,000 principal repayment carries **$85,263.16** gain and **$17,052.63** tax. The unpaid $500,000 principal carries the remaining $426,315.79 gain and $85,263.16 tax as collected. This is timing, not a reduction of total principal tax. The library states the general installment rule but does not expressly address stock-sale notes, escrow, interest tax, or a default, so eligibility and those details remain **unverified** ([§453](../library/irc_453_installment.md)). Interest is additional consideration for time and is excluded from the JSON; if principal were paid in five equal annual installments on a declining balance, simple 6% interest would total $90,000 before tax, but the note's amortization schedule has not been supplied. Neither buyer's credit strength nor the escrow's cash timing can be verified from the files.

Lone Star assumes only scheduled trade payables, leaving Ray/Brightline with excluded liabilities unless negotiated otherwise. Hensley acquires the company with all known and unknown liabilities, including the $9,800 Hardaway dispute and warranty exposure, subject to a 12-month indemnity capped at **10% × $2,850,000 = $285,000** and a $100,000 escrow. The escrow is security for indemnity, not necessarily a price reduction; the LOI does not say whether it is withheld from the $2,350,000 cash. Stock structure also shifts latent company tax and business liabilities to Hensley, with possible recourse to Ray under the indemnity ([stock/asset overview](../library/stock_vs_asset_overview.md)). The actual trade-payable amount and precise tax treatment of its assumption are absent; treating $3,100,000 as complete asset-sale consideration is an express assumption, not a reconciliation.

## Negotiating position

1. Send Lone Star the counter allocation: $200,000 receivables, $150,000 inventory subject to valuation, $650,000 equipment subject to appraisal, $0 separately priced covenant/consulting unless supported by real services or independent covenant value, and $2,100,000 residual goodwill. Ray's willingness to retire supports negotiation but does not prove a covenant has zero value. Require identical signed Form 8594 allocations and revisit the price if an appraisal supports a different class mix ([§1060/Form 8594](../library/irc_1060_form_8594.md); [§197 and noncompete](../library/irc_197_and_noncompete.md)).
2. Ask Lone Star to increase cash price or cover the measurable tax drag from its allocation. On the *gross preliminary* comparison only, the counter trails Hensley by $76,700; if any incremental Lone Star price were wholly capital gain at 20% with no additional entity tax, **$76,700 / 80% = $95,875** extra price would close that nominal gap. That shortcut excludes note risk, liability allocation, the built-in gains tax loss benefit, possible liquidation tax, and other price-dependent items. Do not put $95,875 into an LOI as a definitive break-even number.
3. Ask Hensley for audited or current financials, a guarantee or bank letter of credit for the $500,000 note, precise payment dates, escrow funding/release language, and limits on indemnity claims. Determine who bears the Hardaway dispute and warranty claims. Confirm whether the S election can remain effective through its proposed partnership acquisition; the library does not address shareholder eligibility, so this is **unverified**.
4. Before signing either LOI, obtain a closing balance sheet, current asset and stock tax bases, trade-payables schedule, valuation of equipment/inventory/goodwill/covenant, and written calculations of the §1374 pass-through loss and any asset-sale distribution tax. Lone Star's LOI includes 45 days of exclusivity even though otherwise largely nonbinding; seek a shorter period or defer exclusivity until tax economics are settled.

**Decision today:** the face-price comparison does not justify signing Buyer A as drafted. Hensley's modeled total is higher, but its note has material credit and time risk; Lone Star's all-cash proposal may be better if the counterallocation is supported and the final tax reconciliation confirms its economics. Preserve both options until those points are resolved.
