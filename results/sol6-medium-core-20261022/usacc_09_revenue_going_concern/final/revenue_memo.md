# Northwind Analytics Inc. — Q4 2025 accounting memorandum

## Revenue reconciliation

The drafted P&L reports $1,540,000 of revenue: $850,000 subscription revenue + $480,000 Halvorsen + $210,000 Saltline. On the supplied facts, Q4 revenue is **$850,000**: $1,540,000 − $480,000 − $210,000. The $850,000 subscription line is retained as drafted because this engagement supplied no underlying subscription contracts or other evidence to reassess it. The $1,500,000 bridge threshold is missed by **$650,000**. The drafted net income reconciles: $1,540,000 − $54,000 hosting − $712,000 salaries − $318,000 other operating expenses = **$456,000**. After only the two revenue corrections, the same draft expense figures would yield a **$234,000 loss** ($850,000 − $54,000 − $712,000 − $318,000); this is not final net income because Ironvale remains unmeasured.

## Halvorsen letter of intent — remove $480,000

**Facts.** The December 19 letter describes a 24-month platform license for $480,000 but expressly says it is non-binding except for confidentiality. A definitive agreement, procurement approval, and a security review remain outstanding; either party can stop negotiations. The drafted P&L includes the full $480,000 as Q4 license revenue.

**Criteria.** Revenue requires an approved contract with committed parties, identifiable rights and payment terms, commercial substance, and probable collection. An expressly non-binding letter subject to a definitive agreement creates no enforceable revenue contract. [ASC 606 contract criteria](../library/asc606_contract_and_bill_hold.md).

**Conclusion and amount.** No revenue contract exists on the supplied facts. Reverse **$480,000** of Q4 revenue. Q4 recognized amount: **$0**. A later definitive agreement would require its own recognition assessment.

## Saltline kits — remove $210,000

**Facts.** Saltline ordered 120 kits for $210,000 and requested delivery after its Tampa site is ready, around February 10. Northwind invoiced on December 28 but planned to select kits later from general, untagged stock. The countersigned side letter permits a full refund for unused kits within 180 days if the rollout is cancelled or reduced. No storage fee applies.

**Criteria.** Bill-and-hold control transfer requires *all* of: a substantive reason for the hold, separately identified goods belonging to the customer, goods ready for physical transfer, and no ability for the seller to use or redirect them. An invoice and customer request alone do not establish transfer. A return right also makes consideration variable; revenue is limited to the amount not expected to be refunded, with a refund liability. [ASC 606 bill-and-hold and returns](../library/asc606_contract_and_bill_hold.md).

**Conclusion and amount.** The customer's requested delay supports a substantive hold, but the general, untagged stock is not identified as Saltline's property and remains available for other customers. The record does not establish that specific kits are ready for transfer. Thus bill-and-hold control transfer fails regardless of the return estimate. The refund right adds uncertainty; no supportable nonrefundable estimate was supplied. Reverse **$210,000** of Q4 revenue. Q4 recognized amount: **$0**. Reassess when identified goods transfer and the return exposure can be estimated. The prior-year auditor acceptance concerned segregated, labeled goods without a return right; these facts differ.

## Ironvale hosting — embedded lease; no Q4 revenue adjustment

**Facts.** The 36-month agreement began October 1, 2025, with an $18,000 monthly fee payable in arrears. The 12 servers and two storage arrays are identified by serial number, dedicated to Northwind, and may be substituted only on failure with Northwind's consent. Northwind sets workloads, configuration, operating systems, and utilization. Ironvale supplies power, cooling, and network. The drafted P&L expenses $54,000 for October through December (**3 × $18,000**).

**Criteria.** An identified asset whose economic benefits and use the customer controls is an embedded lease. The equipment lease is distinct from hosting services. The lessee recognizes a right-of-use asset and lease liability measured from lease payments; service consideration remains a non-lease component. [ASC 842 lease identification](../library/asc842_identification.md).

**Conclusion and amount.** The dedicated equipment is an embedded lease; power, cooling, and network are services. Ironvale is a cost and balance-sheet classification issue, with **$0 direct Q4 revenue adjustment**. The $54,000 cannot simply remain wholly labeled hosting expense. The client supplied no lease/service payment allocation, discount rate, confirmation of equipment availability at commencement, or amounts for prepayments, incentives, and initial direct costs. Accordingly the Q4 lease liability and right-of-use asset are **unknown, not zero**. The service expense, lease expense, and resulting net income also cannot be finalized.

## Going concern and financing

The 13-week forecast starts with $612,000 cash. Total receipts are **$1,424,000** and disbursements are **$2,545,000**, a net outflow of **$1,121,000**, leaving **negative $509,000** by March 27 ($612,000 − $1,121,000). Cash first turns negative in the February 20 week: $8,000 + $92,000 − $205,000 = **negative $105,000**. These conditions raise substantial doubt. The required assessment period is one year after financial-statement issuance; the issuance date was not supplied, so management must refresh the assessment through that date. Management has supplied no further mitigating plan. The $3,000,000 Crestline term sheet is non-binding and conditional on at least $1,500,000 Q4 revenue and no going-concern qualification. Corrected Q4 revenue is $650,000 below that threshold, so closing cannot be treated as probable on this record. [ASC 205-40 going concern](../library/asc205_40_going_concern.md).

Management should disclose that substantial doubt exists, the forecast liquidity shortfall, and the conditional bridge proposal. A P&L-only schedule cannot settle the financial-statement disclosure.
