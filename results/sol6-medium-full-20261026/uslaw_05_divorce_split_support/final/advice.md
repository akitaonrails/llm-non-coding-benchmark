# Advice to Wife — proposed property division and support

**The proposed “equal halves” are equal at face value.** Each side receives $770,000 on the schedule, but the assets carry different future taxes and sale costs. Under the stated liquidation assumptions, Wife receives **$613,008** and Husband **$662,000**, a **$48,992** difference. The division should account for tax consequences and Wife's economic circumstances; Columbia's rule calls for an equitable division, not a mechanical 50/50 division of stated values ([equitable distribution](../library/columbia_equitable_distribution.md)). These are comparison values, **not taxes triggered merely by the divorce or a guaranteed court adjustment**: an incident-to-divorce transfer normally carries the existing basis without immediate gain or loss ([divorce tax rules](../library/irc_1041_121_71.md)).

## The house decision

**Keep the house only with a workable refinance and budget.** On the existing $400,000 debt, 6.1% rate, and 24 years remaining, estimated principal and interest is about **$2,648/month**. That alone is **44% of Wife's $6,000 monthly gross pay** and about **57% of her $4,680 monthly net figure under the support worksheet's standardized 0.78 factor**. Taxes, insurance, maintenance, utilities, and any refinancing costs would be additional. The proposed $2,000/month maintenance lasts only 36 months; the children's support and Wife's income may also change. The client does not know whether a lender will refinance into her name. There is no lender quote, payment detail, household budget, or debt schedule, so qualification and actual affordability cannot be concluded from this file. Obtain a lender decision and full monthly budget before committing to retention. The property agreement should set a realistic refinance deadline, require removal of Husband from the joint mortgage, and provide for sale and allocation of costs and proceeds if refinancing fails. Do not assume that a decree assigning the mortgage to Wife releases either borrower from the lender's loan; that lender effect is a practical assumption, not a rule established in this library.

If Wife lives in the house as her principal residence for the planned **3–6 years**, that future occupancy would satisfy the two-year use period for a later §121 sale, assuming she owns it and no disqualifying facts arise. Her ownership period can include Husband's under the incident-to-divorce rule. The current file does **not** establish past ownership or occupancy, so eligibility for an earlier sale, including a forced sale after a failed refinance, remains unverified. A joint-return $500,000 exclusion may be available if a qualifying joint sale occurs, but filing status, timing, and each spouse's qualifications are unknown; no $500,000 benefit is included in the numbers above. Future price, loan balance, and tax law may change the estimate ([divorce tax rules](../library/irc_1041_121_71.md)).

## Bonus, maintenance, and children

**Do not accept salary-only child support.** The affidavit lists $168,000 salary and says no bonus is expected, yet the employer's March 2026 letter sets a **$45,000 target**, says half is planned for September and half for February on achievement of plan, and reports 112% of plan. Wife reports a recurring bonus of about $40,000 last year. The September pay stub's $146,212 year-to-date gross exceeds approximately $119,000 prorated salary by **about $27,212**. That gap is evidence of additional earnings, but the pay stub excerpt does not identify the pay components; it must **not** be added to the $45,000 target as though it were a separate bonus. Seek full pay stubs, bonus-plan terms, prior-year earnings records, actual September payment, and the February entitlement. Require a corrected sworn affidavit and an updated worksheet. Columbia includes expected or recurring documented bonuses in gross income and requires full disclosure; failure to disclose may lead to reopening or sanctions ([support guidelines](../library/columbia_child_support_guidelines.md); [disclosure rule](../library/columbia_equitable_distribution.md)). The February payment's marital-property characterization also needs its performance period and applicable facts; the library does not supply a rule to decide it here.

Using the documented **$45,000 target** provisionally, Husband earns $213,000/year and Wife $72,000/year. During the proposed maintenance period, the support worksheet shifts $24,000/year from Husband to Wife, leaving adjusted gross of $189,000 and $96,000. The standardized net amounts are $189,000 × 0.78 ÷ 12 = **$12,285/month** and $96,000 × 0.78 ÷ 12 = **$6,240/month**; combined **$18,525/month**. The two-child basic obligation at that tier is **$3,050/month**. Husband's share is $3,050 × $12,285 ÷ $18,525 = **$2,022.63/month**. Wife's approximately 70% of overnights makes her the residential parent; Husband's approximately 30% does not reach the 35% shared-parenting threshold. Salary-only with the same maintenance produces **$1,830/month**, $192.63 less. Once maintenance ends, unchanged income produces a provisional **$2,279.47/month** Husband share; the order should address recalculation then. Other worksheet inputs and actual bonus payments may change the order ([support guidelines](../library/columbia_child_support_guidelines.md)).

The proposed **$2,000/month for 36 months** is $72,000 total. For a new divorce instrument, it is **neither deductible to Husband nor taxable to Wife**; delete the proposal's contrary tax wording. Child support also is neither deductible nor taxable. The Columbia worksheet still adjusts the parties' incomes for maintenance, as calculated above ([divorce tax rules](../library/irc_1041_121_71.md); [support guidelines](../library/columbia_child_support_guidelines.md)).

The children live primarily with Wife. Husband's proposed alternating-year claim requires Wife to release each child's claim for the specified year on **Form 8332**. Such a release can move the child tax credit, but **not** head-of-household status, EITC, or the dependent-care credit. Wife can qualify for head of household after a decree final by December 31 only if a qualifying child lives with her more than half the year **and** she pays more than half the home's costs. The decree date and cost payer are unknown, so filing status for 2026 remains conditional. If the decree is not final by December 31, the library says marital status is determined while still married. Do not promise Husband both children on alternating years without valuing the tax benefit and specifying which child and years, whether a signed Form 8332 is required, and what happens if tax rules or custody change ([filing-status and dependents rules](../library/filing_status_dependents.md)).

## Instruments and open items

The settlement and decree should state the property allocations, carryover-basis records, mortgage/refinance deadline and fallback sale, maintenance without a false tax deduction, bonus-inclusive child-support inputs and a review when maintenance ends, and precise dependency-release terms. If any of Husband's 401(k) is awarded to Wife, obtain a **qualified domestic relations order (QDRO)** accepted by the plan. A QDRO transfer can be rolled over by the alternate payee; cash distributed to her under it is taxable to her but exempt from the specified 10% early-distribution penalty. A payment out of Husband's 401(k) without a QDRO could instead tax him and expose him to the 10% penalty if under 59½. No QDRO is required for either illustrated equalization if the 401(k) remains entirely his ([QDRO rules](../library/qdro_72t.md)).

**Still unknown:** the actual 2026 bonus and basis for any extra compensation; house acquisition and past occupancy dates; refinance terms and all-in housing expenses; brokerage tax lots; decree date and 2026 household-cost payer; future house price, loan payoff, and sale-year tax facts. These unknowns make the numerical comparison and support result provisional, but they do not justify using salary alone or signing a house award without a sale fallback.
