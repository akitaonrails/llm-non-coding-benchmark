# Proposed settlement term sheet — Juniper / Pinecrest

**Draft for negotiation; no agreement exists.** This proposes a complete settlement of the staffing agreement dispute. It does not revive the interference claim.

## Business terms

1. **Parties.** Juniper Health Staffing, LLC and Pinecrest Senior Living, Inc.
2. **Settlement payment.** Pinecrest will pay Juniper **$350,000 total**: **$265,200** within five business days after both parties sign a definitive agreement, and **$84,800 = $350,000 − $265,200** within 30 calendar days after signing. Payment is by cleared wire transfer to an account designated in writing by Juniper's counsel. This total resolves principal, claimed contractual interest, conversion fees, and disputed amounts through the effective date. No separate fee payment is included.
3. **Payment protection.** Any unpaid balance becomes immediately due if an installment is more than five business days late after written notice and an opportunity to cure. Juniper's release becomes effective only when the full **$350,000** has cleared; until then, Juniper will not commence arbitration while Pinecrest performs on time. If Pinecrest defaults after the cure period, Juniper may enforce the signed agreement and pursue unpaid supported claims, crediting all settlement payments received. The definitive agreement should specify a workable enforcement process consistent with Exhibit C's arbitration provision and a prevailing-party fee clause for enforcement.
4. **Mutual releases.** On full cleared payment, each party releases the other and its employees and agents from claims arising from the January 15, 2024 staffing agreement and related staffing, invoices, and hires through the signing date, including J-4411, J-4452, J-4490, J-4533, J-4571, alleged conversion fees for Rosa, Tay, Priya, Luis, Keisha, Anne, and Victor, and asserted lost profits or interference. The releases do not cover obligations under the settlement or conduct after signing. No release is effective against either party until full payment clears.
5. **Fees and admissions.** Each side bears its own attorney fees and costs incurred through signing. Neither side admits liability, the validity of a disputed claim, or the correctness of the other side's account of the events.
6. **Dismissal / no filing.** No arbitration or court case is identified as pending in the file. If one is pending when the definitive agreement is signed, the parties will arrange a standstill while payment is timely and dismiss it with prejudice after full payment, each bearing its own costs, subject to the settlement's enforcement terms. Otherwise, neither side will file a released claim after full payment.
7. **Documentation and execution.** Counsel will prepare a short definitive agreement identifying the parties, payment instructions, release scope, cure procedure, enforcement forum, and authorized signatories. This term sheet is a proposal and is not binding until both parties sign that agreement.

## Negotiation authority and arithmetic

Open at **$350,000**. A reasonable negotiated total is **$300,000–$350,000**, paid promptly with release after cleared payment. The lower end adds **$34,800 = $300,000 − $265,200** to Pinecrest's offer; the upper end adds **$84,800 = $350,000 − $265,200**. That increment compromises the disputed **about $71,700** corrected June balance and an **illustrative $80,000** for five hires; the **$151,700 = $71,700 + $80,000** combined figure is uncertain because the June records and individual compensation are unavailable. Rosa and Tay's fees are waived; the $410,000 tort demand is not included. The contract's fee-shifting clause creates possible upside if Juniper prevails and reciprocal risk if it loses. The **$265,200 walk-away** means a last-resort minimum only for **prompt cleared payment with the release delayed until payment**. It does **not** approve Pinecrest's present offer of the same nominal sum in six months with no interest and an immediate mutual release. If Pinecrest insists on six equal month-end payments, the planning interest on the remaining principal at the agreement's 1% monthly rate is **$9,282 = 1% × ($265,200 + $221,000 + $176,800 + $132,600 + $88,400 + $44,200)**; the six-month cash total would be at least **$274,482 = $265,200 + $9,282**, with the release held until the last cleared payment and enforceable payment protections. This illustration excludes interest already accrued under the original invoices, which cannot be calculated reliably from the present file.

For a fee-risk planning scenario, **200 assumed opposing-counsel hours × $500 assumed hourly rate = $100,000** in potential adverse attorney fees if Pinecrest prevails in arbitration. This is not a market quote, forecast, or cap. Juniper's own fees, arbitration costs, and any different reasonable fee award are unknown.
