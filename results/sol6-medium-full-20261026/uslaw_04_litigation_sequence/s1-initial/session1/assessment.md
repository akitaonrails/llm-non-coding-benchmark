# Session 1 assessment — Juniper / Pinecrest

**As of October 26, 2026, updated with the client's follow-up.** This is a preliminary assessment on the client file. The demand letter is a draft for client review. The current record does not support demanding $862,000 or filing a merits lawsuit.

## What the agreement requires

The January 15, 2024 signed staffing agreement requires payment of hourly invoices within 30 days, interest on late amounts at 1% per month, and notice of an invoice dispute within 15 days of receipt. An invoice not disputed within that period is deemed accepted. A direct hire of personnel Juniper introduced triggers a conversion fee of 25% of that person's first-year annualized compensation unless Juniper waives it in writing. Either party may terminate on 60 days' notice. Consequential damages are excluded, while liability for unpaid fees and conversion fees is not capped.

Exhibit C requires **exclusive AAA binding arbitration before one arbitrator in Capital City, Columbia**, for disputes relating to the agreement, including arbitrability. Its only stated merits-court exception is a claim **solely** to collect an undisputed invoice under $25,000 in small-claims court; none of these invoices qualifies. A court may hear provisional injunctive relief in aid of arbitration. The prevailing party can recover reasonable attorney fees and costs in arbitration and in an action to compel arbitration. Filing a merits suit risks a stay, an order to arbitrate, and Pinecrest's fees for compelling arbitration. The FAA provisions in the library support enforcement of a written arbitration clause in a transaction involving commerce; that commerce predicate has not yet been documented in this file. The delegation of arbitrability is explicit in Exhibit C. [FAA §§2–4](../../library/faa_2_4.md); [fee-shifting and candor](../../library/fee_shifting_and_candor.md).

## Strengths, defenses, and risks

The signed agreement, itemized invoice list, and Bell's July 20 identification of seven hires support contract claims. Pinecrest's July 9 objection is specific to J-4533; the other four are recorded without dispute notices. Unobjected invoices are prima facie evidence of debt under the library's account-stated rule, subject to proof of receipt and the actual course of dealing. [Account stated and waiver](../../library/columbia_account_stated_waiver.md).

Bell's February 11 email says the fee is waived for Rosa and Tay **“as a one-off.”** Bell now confirms he waived only those two and did not agree to waive fees for the other five. That supports Juniper's position but does not resolve Pinecrest's possible reliance argument. The April 22 “same deal as before?” text and Bell's “ha, we'll talk,” plus the May 14 text, “call me,” and six-minute call concerning Luis and Keisha, create a factual risk of further implied waiver or estoppel; they do not show an express written waiver for the other five. The call's exact contents and Pinecrest's understanding remain unknown. [Account stated and waiver](../../library/columbia_account_stated_waiver.md). Alleging all seven conversion fees, concealing the February waiver, or ignoring the June overcharge would be unsound; counsel must communicate adverse facts and avoid unsupported claims. [Fee-shifting and candor](../../library/fee_shifting_and_candor.md).

The May 2 staffing email says Pinecrest would reduce agency use and finish current requests. It does not establish a contract breach or an effective 60-day termination notice by itself. No minimum purchase obligation appears in the agreement. Lost future orders and lost-profit causation are therefore especially weak. We should pursue invoices and supportable conversion fees in negotiation, then arbitration if necessary, while recognizing the prevailing-party fee exposure on unsuccessful claims.
