# Session 3 advice — arbitration and legal fees

**To Dolapo Okonkwo, CEO**  
**Re: October 6 call note; advice based on the signed agreement and current file**

**Yes, Exhibit C matters.** The signed agreement says it and Exhibits A–C are the entire agreement. Exhibit C requires **exclusive binding arbitration administered by the American Arbitration Association under its Commercial Arbitration Rules, before one arbitrator seated in Capital City, Columbia**. It covers any dispute arising out of or relating to the agreement, including breach, termination, and the **arbitrability of any claim**. It survives termination. Our invoice and conversion-fee claims fall squarely within that language. The fact that Juniper did not read the exhibit or that its former lawyer used a template does not, on the present record, identify a contractual defense to it. No evidence of invalid formation or another basis to revoke the arbitration promise has been supplied. The FAA in the closed library enforces written arbitration clauses in transactions involving commerce, subject to ordinary contract-revocation grounds; whether this transaction meets that commerce requirement has not been documented. [FAA §§2–4](../../library/faa_2_4.md).

**Do not file a Superior Court merits lawsuit to pressure Pinecrest.** If Juniper does, Pinecrest can seek a stay and an order compelling arbitration under the FAA if its predicates are met. Exhibit C separately says the prevailing party in an action to compel arbitration receives reasonable attorney fees and costs. Juniper could pay Pinecrest's motion fees before the underlying claim is decided. The clause delegates arbitrability to the arbitrator, making a court fight over whether related claims belong in arbitration an especially poor bet. [FAA §§2–4](../../library/faa_2_4.md); [fee-shifting and candor](../../library/fee_shifting_and_candor.md).

The agreement has narrow court exceptions. It permits provisional injunctive relief **in aid of arbitration**, but the file identifies no present basis for that relief. It also permits small-claims court for a claim **solely** to collect an **undisputed invoice under $25,000**. Every listed invoice exceeds $25,000; the exception does not fit this dispute. Whether a tort label could avoid arbitration is itself within Exhibit C's broad language and delegation; labeling the same hiring dispute “interference” does not provide a sound court route. The tort theory also lacks support for the separate reasons explained in Session 2. [Tortious interference](../../library/columbia_tortious_interference.md); [FAA §§2–4](../../library/faa_2_4.md).

**Can Pinecrest be made to pay our legal fees? Possibly, but only if Juniper is the prevailing party under the fee clause.** Exhibit C.3 gives the prevailing party in arbitration reasonable attorney fees and costs, and also applies in an action to compel arbitration. It is reciprocal: if Pinecrest prevails, Juniper may owe its reasonable fees and costs. The library says prevailing-party provisions are enforced as written. The file does not establish who would prevail on a mixed result, how fees would be allocated among partially successful claims, or any fee amount; no recovery estimate is justified. Filing weak tort claims or forcing an avoidable motion to compel could increase that exposure. [Fee-shifting and candor](../../library/fee_shifting_and_candor.md).

**Recommended path.** Press Pinecrest for the **$265,200** in four invoices without a recorded objection; the agreement does not condition their payment on conversion-fee resolution. Correct the admitted **approximately $3,100** duplicate billing on J-4533, leaving **about $71,700 = $74,800 − $3,100** to audit. Seek proof of the five remaining hires' first-year annualized compensation and the grounds for Pinecrest's broader-waiver position. Bell's February email waived fees for Rosa and Tay only on its face, but later ambiguous messages create a factual risk. If negotiation fails, bring only supported invoice and conversion-fee claims in the contractually selected arbitration forum. The illustrative total of **about $416,900 = ($340,000 − about $3,100) + (5 × 25% × assumed $64,000)** is **not** an established recovery or a fixed demand; individual pay, billing support, and possible later waiver remain unresolved. Do not revive the $410,000 interference/lost-profit claim for leverage. [Account stated and waiver](../../library/columbia_account_stated_waiver.md); [tortious interference](../../library/columbia_tortious_interference.md); [fee-shifting and candor](../../library/fee_shifting_and_candor.md).

**Open facts.** No copy of the earlier demand described by Pinecrest, exact June credit or shift records, invoice receipt dates, five hires' compensation and introduction proof, or exact May 14 call contents are available. The client has stated it has no more information. These gaps affect the merits and any fee-risk estimate, but they do not change the clear arbitration text in the signed agreement.
