# Privileged legal memorandum — Meridian Robotics / Wei-Lin Zhao

**Date:** October 26, 2026  
**Scope:** The closed client file and reference library only. The additional cases mentioned in prior firm memos are absent from the indexed library; their holdings and current status cannot be verified here, so this analysis does not rely on them. Probability estimates below are judgment calls, not measured frequencies.

## Question Presented

Should Meridian sue Zhao in Travis County to enforce §8 of the March 4, 2024 employment agreement, which bars work for any U.S. warehouse autonomous mobile robot business for 24 months after his September 30, 2026 departure?

## Brief Answer

**No.** I estimate a **5–15% chance of obtaining any effective order against Zhao's competing employment**, and **under 5% for enforcing §8 as written**. These ranges reflect the strong California contacts and policies, Zhao's statutory option to void the Texas clauses, and the breadth of §8 even under Texas law. They are not a prediction about a particular judge. Texas law offers Meridian its best argument, but success there could still leave an order difficult to enforce in California. Filing or demanding compliance with §8 also creates affirmative California exposure and potential employee fee liability. No evidence presently supports a claim that Zhao took or used confidential information.

## Facts and limits

Meridian's Austin HR team sent Zhao an offer while he lived in Oakland; the offer specified fully remote work from California. He signed Meridian's standard agreement on March 4, 2024, about two hours after receipt, without individual negotiation or counsel. The agreement itself identifies Oakland as his work location. He worked there through September 30, 2026, visiting Austin twice. Section 8 bars employment or services anywhere in the United States for any business designing or selling warehouse autonomous mobile robots for 24 months following termination. On the stated termination date, its nominal end is **September 30, 2028**: September 30, 2026 plus 24 calendar months. Kestrel Autonomy, in Fremont, is in that field; Zhao began or was scheduled to begin there October 13, 2026. His precise new duties and any customer overlap are unknown. Section 9 separately restricts solicitation for 12 months; §10 addresses confidentiality and inventions. Section 14 chooses Texas law and exclusive Travis County courts. Section 15 says he had an opportunity to consult counsel, but the record says none was involved in negotiating these clauses. Zhao has said he will honor confidentiality duties. The client reports **no evidence he took information** and objects to the competing employment itself. The record does not establish the specific confidential information or goodwill entrusted to Zhao or any business sale or ownership interest that could support a statutory exception.

Legal ops reports that **no California non-compete voidness notices were sent**; its notice project was closed as “won't do.” The number and identity of employees who qualified for the February 14, 2024 notice are unknown. Zhao signed 19 days *after* that deadline (February 14 to March 4 in leap-year 2024: 15 remaining days in February + 4 days in March = 19), so the deadline provision did not itself require a February 14 notice to him.

## Analysis

### Which law and forum?

The Texas choice has a genuine basis because Meridian is headquartered in Austin. A contractual choice still gives way under the Restatement §187 test when another state has a materially greater interest, would supply the law absent the choice, and has a fundamental contrary policy. Texas recognizes that limit: *DeSantis* refused a chosen state's law for a non-compete when the employee's working state had the stronger interest and contrary policy. California uses the same framework under *Nedlloyd*. Here California is Zhao's residence, agreed work location, nearly exclusive place of performance, and the site of his new job. Texas has Meridian's headquarters, contracting activity, and two brief visits. California's fundamental §16600 policy and these contacts make California law substantially more likely to govern enforceability. *Application Group* refused a foreign choice for a worker moving to a California employer, which supports that conclusion, although Zhao already worked in California and so presents an even more direct California connection. [Restatement (Second) of Conflict of Laws §187(2)]; [*DeSantis v. Wackenhut Corp.*, 793 S.W.2d 670 (Tex. 1990)]; [*Nedlloyd Lines B.V. v. Superior Court*, 3 Cal.4th 459 (1992)]; [*Application Group, Inc. v. Hunter Group, Inc.*, 61 Cal.App.4th 881 (1998)].

California Labor Code §925 independently protects an employee who primarily resides and works in California from being required, as a condition of employment, to litigate a California claim elsewhere or lose California substantive protection. This standard agreement and the offer's California work term strongly support that the Texas provisions were required as a condition of employment. Because Zhao signed in 2024 without counsel **actually representing him in negotiating** these terms, the §925 counsel exception is unavailable on these facts; the acknowledgment of an opportunity to consult counsel does not establish actual representation. His later consultation before resignation does not change the negotiation facts. The venue and choice provisions are **voidable at Zhao's election**, rather than automatically void; if he requests that they be voided, §925 directs a California forum and California law. He has indicated that he *may* void them, but the record does not show a formal election. [Cal. Lab. Code §925].

Before an election, the forum clause generally supplies consent to personal jurisdiction in Travis County. If Zhao voids it, Meridian must establish independent Texas minimum contacts. A Texas employer, agreement, and two short Austin trips make that issue uncertain; the record does not resolve it. A Texas non-compete injunction also faces a serious California nonrecognition risk. Parallel proceedings can become costly: *Advanced Bionics* cautions that California courts generally should not enjoin another state's litigation, which does not make the Texas covenant enforceable in California. [Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code §17.042; *Michiana Easy Livin' Country v. Holten*, 168 S.W.3d 777 (Tex. 2005)]; [*Advanced Bionics Corp. v. Medtronic, Inc.*, 29 Cal.4th 697 (2002)].

### Enforceability under California law

California §16600 voids employment non-competes unless a statutory exception applies. *Edwards* rejected a narrow-restraint exception, and the current statute expressly calls for broad application even to narrowly tailored employment restraints. Section 8 directly bars Zhao's competing employment and thus is void under California law on the known facts. The record contains no sale or other exception facts. *Ixchel*'s rule of reason concerns business-to-business restraints, not this employment covenant. [Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §16600]; [*Edwards v. Arthur Andersen LLP*, 44 Cal.4th 937 (2008)]; [*Ixchel Pharma, LLC v. Biogen, Inc.*, 9 Cal.5th 1130 (2020)].

Section 16600.5 expressly makes a void contract unenforceable regardless of signing place or time and prohibits an employer from attempting to enforce it; it also forbids entering a void contract with an employee. This is a straightforward California-centered application because Zhao lived and worked there throughout. Meridian's March 2024 inclusion of §8 creates possible exposure under the entry prohibition, and an enforcement demand or lawsuit would create a further, distinct risk under the attempted-enforcement prohibition. The library supplies no authority fixing the outer territorial reach or a limitation period; any application to other workers outside California would be **unverified** here. [Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §16600.5].

### Enforceability if Texas law controls

Texas allows a covenant ancillary to an otherwise enforceable agreement only insofar as time, geographic area, and activity limits are reasonable and no greater than needed to protect goodwill or another business interest. Meridian bears the burden in an employment-services agreement. A promise to provide confidential information, if actually performed, can support an ancillary covenant even when performance occurs after signing. *Marsh* confirms that qualifying consideration need not have an unduly narrow connection to goodwill, although its stock-option facts are not shown here. The client cannot currently identify what information or goodwill Zhao received; his job title alone does not prove the needed interest. [Tex. Bus. & Com. Code §15.50(a), §15.51(b)-(c), §15.52]; [*Alex Sheshunoff Management Services, L.P. v. Johnson*, 209 S.W.3d 644 (Tex. 2006)]; [*Marsh USA Inc. v. Cook*, 354 S.W.3d 764 (Tex. 2011)].

Even assuming an ancillary agreement, **24 months, the entire United States, and every job or service at any company in the product category** reach well beyond the presently demonstrated need. Texas law mandates judicial reformation of unreasonable limits and enforcement only as reformed; it bars damages for breach before reformation. A narrower restraint might therefore be available if Meridian proves protectable interests and specific competitive overlap. That possibility does not establish a right to stop Zhao's Kestrel employment as such, especially without evidence of misuse or customer diversion. [Tex. Bus. & Com. Code §15.50(a), §15.51(b)-(c), §15.52].

The vacated FTC Non-Compete Clause Rule supplies no independent ban or remedy. *Ryan* set it aside nationwide; the reference library records dismissal of the FTC appeal in September 2025. The rule's vacatur does not displace California or Texas non-compete law. [FTC Non-Compete Clause Rule, 89 Fed. Reg. 38342 (May 7, 2024)]; [*Ryan LLC v. FTC*, 739 F. Supp. 3d 496 (N.D. Tex. 2024)].

### California notice, remedies, and fees

AB 1076's §16600.1 required individualized written notices by **February 14, 2024** to then-current employees and qualifying former employees employed after January 1, 2022 whose non-competes lacked an exception, delivered to their last known address and email address. Meridian's decision to send none creates unfair-competition exposure if it had workers who met that eligibility test. We cannot count affected people or quantify relief from this record. Zhao was not hired until March 4, so that *deadline-based notice duty* did not apply to him. Section 16600.1(a)'s separate ban on including an invalid non-compete in an employment contract, however, applies to his 2024 agreement. Sending notices now may address ongoing practical harm but cannot establish timely compliance with the 2024 deadline; the library does not specify the effect of late notice. [Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §16600.1].

For a §16600.5 violation, Zhao may seek an injunction, actual damages if proved, and **mandatory reasonable fees and costs if he prevails**. Section 925 permits a court to award him reasonable fees for enforcing its forum and choice protections. The library does not establish a corresponding statutory fee entitlement for Meridian. Under Texas law, any covenant damages for pre-reformation breach are barred if reformation is required. The record shows no actual Zhao damages, but attorney-fee exposure may still be material. Do not assume an amount without evidence. [Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §16600.5]; [Cal. Lab. Code §925]; [Tex. Bus. & Com. Code §15.50(a), §15.51(b)-(c), §15.52].

### Strongest positions

**Meridian:** Texas has a substantial connection; Zhao accepted an exclusive Texas forum; the contract protects confidentiality, and Texas permits later-performed promises to support a covenant. A Texas court could reform overbroad terms and might find a focused restraint reasonable on proof of specific competitive misuse or relationships. Zhao has not yet formally elected to void §14. [Restatement (Second) of Conflict of Laws §187(2)]; [Cal. Lab. Code §925]; [*Alex Sheshunoff Management Services, L.P. v. Johnson*, 209 S.W.3d 644 (Tex. 2006)]; [Tex. Bus. & Com. Code §15.50(a), §15.51(b)-(c), §15.52].

**Zhao:** His residence, contractual work location, performance, and new job are all in California; §925 lets him void both Texas clauses; California treats this employment non-compete as void regardless of tailoring; and §16600.5 makes an enforcement attempt itself actionable. Texas law would also require Meridian to prove a legitimate interest and defend a nationwide ban on all work in the sector. [Cal. Lab. Code §925]; [Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §16600]; [Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §16600.5]; [*Edwards v. Arthur Andersen LLP*, 44 Cal.4th 937 (2008)]; [Tex. Bus. & Com. Code §15.50(a), §15.51(b)-(c), §15.52].

## Recommendation

Do **not** file in Travis County, seek an injunction against Zhao's Kestrel employment, or send a demand to comply with §8. If Meridian has asserted that §8 binds him, withdraw that assertion. Preserve evidence and pursue §10 only upon specific evidence of a breach, with counsel checking the precise contractual language and governing law before action. Review §9 separately before relying on it; this file does not establish a solicitation breach or its enforceability. Identify every worker eligible for the missed §16600.1 notice, preserve the closed ticket and related records, and promptly plan individualized corrective notices and remediation with California counsel. Quantify the affected population and any actual harm before estimating financial exposure. Rework Meridian's California employment forms to remove invalid non-competes. Do not imply the closed notice project was completed.
