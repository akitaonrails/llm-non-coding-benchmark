# Recommendation — Natarajan Herrera Advisory

## Decision

Form a **California LLC taxed as a partnership**, with Priya and Tomás as its two members. The availability and mechanics of LLC partnership classification are **not specified by the library** and must be confirmed before filing. Use separate capital accounts for what each actually contributes and a 50/50 split of *future* profits. Tomás's service-earned profits interest should vest over three years with a one-year cliff. Execute an IP transfer or license that precisely defines the PriceLab rights the LLC receives. The appraisal's $120,000 conclusion is for the codebase, models, and documentation **on a going-concern license basis**; it does not by itself establish the value of an outright assignment. Obtain a valuation for the actual rights conveyed before finalizing capital accounts.

Priya's proposed $120,000 property contribution (reported $31,000 remaining tax basis) and Tomás's $20,000 cash contribution would create initial **economic capital** of $120,000 and $20,000, respectively, if the property rights conveyed are worth $120,000. Their initial capital percentages would be 120,000 / 140,000 = **85.714%** and 20,000 / 140,000 = **14.286%**. Each may nevertheless receive 50% of future profits. Tomás's services should earn only an interest in value created *after* grant: on a hypothetical liquidation immediately after grant, his service-earned interest receives $0. A partnership property contribution generally has no immediate gain under §721, and a qualifying services profits interest generally is not taxed on receipt; an existing capital interest for services is taxable. [§721 and profits-interest guidance](../library/irc_721_and_revproc_93-27.md)

The $89,000 difference between the appraised value and Priya's reported tax basis (120,000 − 31,000) is an embedded gain, **not** an amount assumed realized on contribution. The library does not specify subsequent tax-basis allocation or built-in-gain rules; the operating agreement and tax preparer must address those before filing. A protective §83(b) election for Tomás's unvested profits interest is customary if he is treated as a partner from grant, and its statutory filing deadline is 30 days after transfer. [§721 and profits-interest guidance](../library/irc_721_and_revproc_93-27.md); [§83(b)](../library/irc_83b.md)

## Why the proposed S election fails

Tomás has no green card. The calendar's 2026 weighted U.S. presence is **118 + 96/3 + 66/6 = 161 days**, below 183. His statement that he is in the U.S. about one third of the year is consistent with the calendar but is not a 2027 day count. On the supplied facts, he is a nonresident alien for 2026 under the library's test, assuming no residency election. An S corporation cannot have a nonresident-alien shareholder; becoming ineligible terminates an existing S election. [Substantial presence test](../library/irc_7701b_substantial_presence.md); [S shareholder eligibility](../library/irc_1361_excerpt.md)

Even if future residency made an S election possible, two full-time founders in a consulting business cannot safely treat $40,000 each as reasonable wages without market evidence. Distributions after underpaid wages can be recharacterized, bringing payroll tax, penalties, and interest. The stated plan is therefore neither currently eligible nor a reliable payroll-tax saving. [Reasonable compensation](../library/reasonable_compensation.md)

An S corporation permits only one class of stock. A service grant that shifts existing capital value to Tomás also creates compensation exposure. **If** PriceLab rights worth $120,000 are contributed and Tomás contributes only $20,000 of transferable property, current contributed value totals $140,000; an immediate 50/50 current-value split gives each $70,000, leaving **$50,000 of potential compensatory value** for Tomás beyond his cash contribution. Stock for services is not property for §351's 80% control test and is taxable when substantially vested. A corporation structure would require a separate, reviewed stock issuance and valuation. [S one-class rule](../library/irc_1361_excerpt.md); [§351](../library/irc_351_excerpt.md); [§83(b)](../library/irc_83b.md)

Tomás's marriage without a prenup raises a community-property and transfer-control issue. Obtain his spouse's written acknowledgement and consent to the transfer restrictions; local-law treatment of the spouse's interest is **unverified by the library**. If the founders ever revisit an S election, verify every direct and indirect shareholder's eligibility first. [S shareholder eligibility](../library/irc_1361_excerpt.md); [buy-sell checklist](../library/llc_buy_sell_checklist.md)

### Could Priya own the S corporation alone for now?

**Potentially, if she genuinely owns 100% of its equity and Tomás has no present direct or beneficial ownership.** Section 1361 bars a nonresident-alien *shareholder*; it does not bar an eligible shareholder from hiring a nonresident alien to work for the business. Priya could initially own all stock, with Tomás paid for his work under a documented employment or services arrangement. That would **not** implement their current 50/50 ownership bargain or give Tomás a present share of capital. A side agreement that actually grants him current equity, a nominee arrangement, or an undocumented promise of current 50% economics would need substantive legal review before claiming Priya is the only shareholder; the library does not specify beneficial-ownership attribution rules or the mechanics of an LLC electing corporate and S tax status. [S shareholder eligibility](../library/irc_1361_excerpt.md)

For 2027, using the supplied prior years, Tomás would need at least **128 actual U.S. days** to reach the library's substantial-presence arithmetic: 2027 days + 118/3 + 96/6 ≥ 183, so 2027 days ≥ 127⅔. His “about a third of the year” estimate is not a reliable day count and does not establish eligibility. Reaching that annual count alone does not establish his residency status on a proposed stock-issuance date; the library gives no residency-start-date rule. Any other residency route, exception, treaty effect, or spouse's ownership effect is outside the library and needs verification before stock is issued. [Substantial presence test](../library/irc_7701b_substantial_presence.md); [S shareholder eligibility](../library/irc_1361_excerpt.md)

Adding Tomás later is a **new equity transaction**, not an automatic conversion of his prior work into tax-free stock. Stock received for services is taxable when substantially vested, and stock issued for services does not count as transferred property for §351. A vesting grant also requires attention to the 30-day §83(b) deadline. The founders would need to value the company and decide whether he buys stock, receives compensatory stock, or receives a different arrangement; the library does not establish the full tax result of a later purchase or gift. During Priya-only ownership, her pay must be reasonable for her substantial services before S distributions. [§351](../library/irc_351_excerpt.md); [§83(b)](../library/irc_83b.md); [reasonable compensation](../library/reasonable_compensation.md)

If Tomás advances his planned $20,000 while Priya is the sole shareholder, they must separately document whether it is a loan or another non-equity payment; it cannot silently buy a present ownership interest while relying on sole-shareholder eligibility. The terms and tax treatment of that advance are **unverified by the library**.

## Alternatives rejected

1. **Delaware LLC with S election, 50/50, $40,000 wages:** S shareholder ineligibility on supplied 2026 facts and unverified 2027 status; unsubstantiated salaries; California operations still require foreign registration and California LLC tax and receipts fee. **If all $610,000 projected receipts fall in the relevant fee base**, the fee is $2,500 plus $800 annual tax = **$3,300**. The library does not define the geographic fee base. [S shareholder eligibility](../library/irc_1361_excerpt.md); [reasonable compensation](../library/reasonable_compensation.md); [California LLC costs and registration](../library/ca_llc_franchise.md)
2. **Delaware LLC taxed as a partnership:** potentially viable if LLC classification is confirmed, but Delaware formation plus California foreign registration adds an administrative layer. The modeled $3,300 California charge assumes all $610,000 receipts fall in its fee base; that fee base, Delaware costs, and Delaware dates are **not supplied by the library**. [California LLC costs and registration](../library/ca_llc_franchise.md)
3. **California partnership LLC with profit and capital interests strictly in contribution proportions:** legally simpler economics but misses the stated goal of equal future upside; the 85.714/14.286 modeled split is included as the third tax comparison.
4. **Immediate equal capital interests:** assuming the transferred PriceLab rights are worth $120,000 and Tomás transfers only $20,000 cash, he would receive a further $50,000 of existing capital value potentially attributable to services; a services capital interest is taxable at receipt or vesting. It also dilutes Priya's already-created asset before future performance is earned. [§721 and profits-interest guidance](../library/irc_721_and_revproc_93-27.md)

## Scope of the figures

The supplied projection reconciles: $610,000 revenue − $195,000 contractor/tool costs − $155,000 overhead = **$260,000** before owner compensation. The companion JSON is an **illustrative model**, not a tax-return forecast. It assumes the $260,000 excludes the modeled California charge, all $610,000 receipts fall in California's LLC fee base, and the $3,300 state charge reduces income allocated to owners; none of those points is established by the client data or library. Federal income-tax rates, self-employment/payroll rates and scope, sourcing, cross-border withholding, Mexican tax, California income tax, deductions, and exact 2027 §199A thresholds are not established by the library. The model labels its assumed rates and omits those unquantifiable items. The totals cannot establish which structure has the lowest actual tax. Consulting is an SSTB, and the §199A deduction can phase out at higher income; the library's 2026 thresholds are approximate and do not establish 2027 eligibility. [§199A](../library/irc_199a_sstb.md)

The client supplied no formation date, first-year dates, geographic income/cost allocation, comparative compensation, other founder income, or precise treatment of the existing capital value. The schedule assumes January 1, 2027; confirm those items before signing and filing. The tool's ownership chain and the value of the actual transferred rights also remain open.
