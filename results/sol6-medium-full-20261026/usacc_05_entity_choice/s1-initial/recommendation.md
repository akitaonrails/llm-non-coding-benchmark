# Recommendation — Natarajan Herrera Advisory

## Decision

Form a **California LLC taxed as a partnership**, with Priya and Tomás as its two members. Use separate capital accounts for what each actually contributes and a 50/50 split of *future* profits. Tomás's service-earned profits interest should vest over three years with a one-year cliff. Execute an IP transfer or license that precisely defines the PriceLab rights the LLC receives. The appraisal's $120,000 conclusion is for the codebase, models, and documentation **on a going-concern license basis**; it does not by itself establish the value of an outright assignment. Obtain a valuation for the actual rights conveyed before finalizing capital accounts.

Priya's proposed $120,000 property contribution (reported $31,000 remaining tax basis) and Tomás's $20,000 cash contribution would create initial **economic capital** of $120,000 and $20,000, respectively, if the property rights conveyed are worth $120,000. Their initial capital percentages would be 120,000 / 140,000 = **85.714%** and 20,000 / 140,000 = **14.286%**. Each may nevertheless receive 50% of future profits. Tomás's services should earn only an interest in value created *after* grant: on a hypothetical liquidation immediately after grant, his service-earned interest receives $0. A partnership property contribution generally has no immediate gain under §721, and a qualifying services profits interest generally is not taxed on receipt; an existing capital interest for services is taxable. [§721 and profits-interest guidance](../library/irc_721_and_revproc_93-27.md)

The $89,000 difference between the appraised value and Priya's reported tax basis (120,000 − 31,000) is an embedded gain, **not** an amount assumed realized on contribution. The library does not specify subsequent tax-basis allocation or built-in-gain rules; the operating agreement and tax preparer must address those before filing. A protective §83(b) election for Tomás's unvested profits interest is customary if he is treated as a partner from grant, and its statutory filing deadline is 30 days after transfer. [§721 and profits-interest guidance](../library/irc_721_and_revproc_93-27.md); [§83(b)](../library/irc_83b.md)

## Why the proposed S election fails

Tomás has no green card. The calendar's 2026 weighted U.S. presence is **118 + 96/3 + 66/6 = 161 days**, below 183. His statement that he is in the U.S. about one third of the year is consistent with the calendar but is not a 2027 day count. On the supplied facts, he is a nonresident alien for 2026 under the library's test, assuming no residency election. An S corporation cannot have a nonresident-alien shareholder; becoming ineligible terminates an existing S election. [Substantial presence test](../library/irc_7701b_substantial_presence.md); [S shareholder eligibility](../library/irc_1361_excerpt.md)

Even if future residency made an S election possible, two full-time founders in a consulting business cannot safely treat $40,000 each as reasonable wages without market evidence. Distributions after underpaid wages can be recharacterized, bringing payroll tax, penalties, and interest. The stated plan is therefore neither currently eligible nor a reliable payroll-tax saving. [Reasonable compensation](../library/reasonable_compensation.md)

An S corporation permits only one class of stock. A service grant that shifts existing capital value to Tomás also creates compensation exposure. The current contribution values total $140,000; an immediate 50/50 current-value split gives each $70,000, so Tomás receives **$50,000** more existing capital value than his $20,000 cash supports. Stock for services is not property for §351's 80% control test and is taxable when substantially vested. A corporation structure would require a separate, reviewed stock issuance and valuation. [S one-class rule](../library/irc_1361_excerpt.md); [§351](../library/irc_351_excerpt.md); [§83(b)](../library/irc_83b.md)

Tomás's marriage without a prenup raises a community-property and transfer-control issue. Obtain his spouse's written acknowledgement and consent to the transfer restrictions; local-law treatment of the spouse's interest is **unverified by the library**. If the founders ever revisit an S election, verify every direct and indirect shareholder's eligibility first. [S shareholder eligibility](../library/irc_1361_excerpt.md); [buy-sell checklist](../library/llc_buy_sell_checklist.md)

## Alternatives rejected

1. **Delaware LLC with S election, 50/50, $40,000 wages:** S shareholder ineligibility on supplied 2026 facts; unsubstantiated salaries; California operations still require foreign registration and the same California LLC tax and receipts fee. At projected $610,000 receipts, the fee is $2,500 plus $800 annual tax = **$3,300**. [S shareholder eligibility](../library/irc_1361_excerpt.md); [reasonable compensation](../library/reasonable_compensation.md); [California LLC costs and registration](../library/ca_llc_franchise.md)
2. **Delaware LLC taxed as a partnership:** viable federal classification, but it adds a Delaware filing layer while California registration and the $3,300 projected California charges remain. Delaware costs and dates are **not supplied by the library** and are excluded from the numerical comparison. [California LLC costs and registration](../library/ca_llc_franchise.md)
3. **California partnership LLC with profit and capital interests strictly in contribution proportions:** legally simpler economics but misses the stated goal of equal future upside; the 85.714/14.286 modeled split is included as the third tax comparison.
4. **Immediate equal capital interests:** Tomás would receive $50,000 of existing capital value for services under the stated appraisal/cash values; a services capital interest is taxable at receipt or vesting. It also dilutes Priya's already-created asset before future performance is earned. [§721 and profits-interest guidance](../library/irc_721_and_revproc_93-27.md)

## Scope of the figures

The supplied projection reconciles: $610,000 revenue − $195,000 contractor/tool costs − $155,000 overhead = **$260,000** before owner compensation. The companion JSON is an **illustrative model**, not a tax-return forecast. Federal income-tax rates, self-employment/payroll rates and scope, sourcing, cross-border withholding, Mexican tax, California income tax, deductions, and exact 2027 §199A thresholds are not established by the library. The model labels its assumed rates and omits those unquantifiable items. Consulting is an SSTB, and the §199A deduction can phase out at higher income; the library's 2026 thresholds are approximate and do not establish 2027 eligibility. [§199A](../library/irc_199a_sstb.md)

The client supplied no formation date, first-year dates, geographic income/cost allocation, comparative compensation, other founder income, or precise treatment of the existing capital value. The schedule assumes January 1, 2027; confirm those items before signing and filing. The tool's ownership chain and the value of the actual transferred rights also remain open.
