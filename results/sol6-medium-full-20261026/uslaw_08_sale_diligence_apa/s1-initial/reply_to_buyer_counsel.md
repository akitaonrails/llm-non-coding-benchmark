# Draft reply to J. Whitaker

Subject: Brightline APA — Schedule 3.3, WARN and §7.4(c)

J.,

Schedule 3.3 cannot read “None.” Hensley Homes’ MSA §18 requires its prior written consent to assignment, expressly including a sale of assets; it permits consent to be withheld in Hensley’s sole discretion and states an unauthorized assignment is void. Hensley accounts for 38% of Brightline’s reported $4.6 million 2025 revenue, or $1.748 million (0.38 × $4.6 million). The shop lease §22 also requires landlord consent, although it may not be unreasonably withheld. We propose both written consents as closing conditions. Cascade Builders’ framework agreement permits assignment to a successor to substantially all assets on notice, so it calls for notice and confirmation that Buyer qualifies, rather than consent. The City of Austin spray-booth permit is nontransferable and requires Buyer’s new application, with a stated 30–60 day processing period; it is a separate operating/closing timing issue, not a consent. We will populate Schedule 3.3 accordingly. The 22 smaller residential contracts are described as silent on assignment; we are reviewing their complete terms. The assignment position follows [the library’s assignment authority](../library/anti_assignment_change_of_control.md).

On WARN, the supplied census shows 14 W-2 employees. Even treating all nine 1099 installers as employees produces 23 people (14 + 9), below the federal 100-employee employer threshold; it is also below the 50-person site event threshold. On these supplied facts, Brightline is not a covered employer and a Brightline WARN notice is not required. The library states Texas has no mini-WARN statute. This answers Brightline’s headcount only; we have no census for Buyer or affiliated employers and cannot confirm their separate notice obligations. The underlying 1099 classification issue remains and must be disclosed and allocated in the APA. [WARN thresholds](../library/warn_act.md); [worker classification](../library/worker_classification_tx_irs.md).

We do not accept current §7.4(c). Its “acquired (or capable of being acquired) ... at any time” wording preserves an indemnity claim even where Buyer knew the particular inaccuracy before closing. We propose that actual, conscious pre-closing knowledge of the specific breach by Buyer’s transaction decision-makers bar the corresponding general-representation claim, with Seller bearing the burden of proof; constructive or merely available information would not count. A fact expressly excepted on a disclosure schedule would be outside the representation regardless of knowledge. The nine installers will be expressly excepted from §4.12, with any pre-closing exposure addressed by a separately negotiated, capped special indemnity that §7.4(c) would not cut off. The current full-purchase-price §4.12 cap is not acceptable. These are seller positions reflected in our proposed redline, rather than claims that either knowledge approach is universally “market standard.” [Sandbagging](../library/sandbagging.md); [baskets and caps](../library/baskets_caps.md).

We can work toward signing in two weeks, subject to agreement on the schedules, installer risk allocation, and the consent/permit timetable. We cannot represent that Hensley or the landlord will consent by that date, or that the new permit will issue then.

Regards,

Seller counsel
