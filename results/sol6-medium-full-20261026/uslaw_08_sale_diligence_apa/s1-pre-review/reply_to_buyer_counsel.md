# Draft reply to J. Whitaker

Subject: Brightline APA — Schedule 3.3, WARN, §§4.12 and 7.4(c)

J.,

Schedule 3.3 cannot read “None.” Hensley Homes’ MSA §18 requires its prior written consent to assignment, expressly including a sale of assets; it permits consent to be withheld in Hensley’s sole discretion and states an unauthorized assignment is void. Hensley accounts for 38% of Brightline’s reported $4.6 million 2025 revenue, or $1.748 million (0.38 × $4.6 million). The shop lease §22 also requires landlord consent, although it may not be unreasonably withheld. We propose both written consents as closing conditions. Cascade Builders’ framework agreement permits assignment to a successor to substantially all assets on notice, so it calls for notice and confirmation that Buyer qualifies, rather than consent. The City of Austin spray-booth permit is nontransferable and requires Buyer’s new application, with a stated 30–60 day processing period; it is a separate operating/closing timing issue, not a consent. Schedule 3.3 should reflect these four items. The 22 smaller residential contracts are described as silent on assignment; their complete terms still need review. The assignment position follows [the library’s assignment authority](../library/anti_assignment_change_of_control.md).

On WARN, the supplied census shows 14 W-2 employees. Even treating all nine 1099 installers as employees produces 23 people (14 + 9), below the federal 100-employee employer threshold; it is also below the 50-person site event threshold. On these supplied facts, Brightline is not a covered employer and a Brightline WARN notice is not required. The library states Texas has no mini-WARN statute. This answers Brightline’s headcount only; we have no census for Buyer or affiliated employers and cannot confirm their separate notice obligations. The underlying 1099 classification issue remains and must be disclosed and allocated in the APA. [WARN thresholds](../library/warn_act.md); [worker classification](../library/worker_classification_tx_irs.md).

On your point that §7.4(c) is in “every deal,” our closed reference recognizes both pro- and anti-sandbagging provisions. We do not accept current §7.4(c). Its “acquired (or capable of being acquired) ... at any time” wording preserves an indemnity claim even where Buyer knew the particular inaccuracy before closing. We propose that actual, conscious pre-closing knowledge of the specific breach by Buyer’s transaction decision-makers bar the corresponding general-representation claim, with Seller bearing the burden of proof; constructive or merely available information would not count. A fact expressly excepted on a disclosure schedule would be outside the representation regardless of knowledge. We can discuss the general knowledge rule, but an express §4.12 schedule exception and a capped allocation of the known installer issue are necessary regardless of that rule. [Sandbagging](../library/sandbagging.md).

The issue with §4.12 is the record, not the usual form of a compliance representation. The nine installers work exclusively for Brightline, under its daily schedule, using its trucks and tools, are paid hourly, and have done so for an average of 3.4 years. Those are the employee-indicating control facts identified in [the classification authority](../library/worker_classification_tx_irs.md). We cannot give an unqualified representation that they were correctly classified or that all related taxes were paid. Form 1099 treatment and industry custom do not establish compliance. No claims are pending in the supplied data room, but that does not establish proper classification or eliminate pre-closing tax and wage exposure. Seller proposes to disclose the facts and except these nine from §4.12; Buyer may receive a specific pre-closing indemnity, outside the $75,000 basket, capped at $310,000 and funded from the shared escrow, with an 18-month claim period. We can discuss its size after reviewing payroll and hours records. The current full-purchase-price §4.12 cap remains unacceptable; the library describes that treatment of an operational classification representation as off-market. [Baskets and caps](../library/baskets_caps.md).

We can work toward signing in two weeks if the schedules, installer risk allocation, earn-out accounting terms, and consent/permit closing conditions are agreed in the signed APA. Hensley and landlord consents can be obtained between signing and closing under those conditions. We cannot represent that either will consent by the signing date, or that the new permit will issue then.

Regards,

Seller counsel
