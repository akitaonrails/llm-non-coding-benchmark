## §2.4 Earn-Out — change

**Replace with:** “In addition to the Closing Payment, Buyer shall pay Seller 40% of the excess, if any, of Earn-Out EBITDA for the 12 months after Closing over $700,000, up to $400,000, within 30 days after final determination. ‘Earn-Out EBITDA’ means earnings of the acquired Business before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, calculated under the accounting principles and specific adjustments in **Exhibit 2.4**, consistently with Brightline’s historical practices, and without charges for Buyer or affiliate corporate overhead, management fees, purchase-accounting adjustments, transaction or integration expenses, or expenses unrelated to the Business, except items expressly listed in Exhibit 2.4. Buyer shall maintain separate books for the Business, conduct it in the ordinary course consistent with the earn-out framework, and not take an action with the purpose of avoiding or reducing the earn-out. Buyer shall deliver quarterly statements within 30 days after each quarter and a final calculation with supporting records within 60 days after the earn-out period; Seller may inspect relevant records on reasonable notice. Seller may object within 30 days after receiving the final calculation; unresolved accounting disputes go to a mutually selected independent accountant for determination within 30 days after appointment, whose decision on disputed items is final, with costs allocated in proportion to success. If Buyer sells or transfers substantially all of the Business before the period ends, the maximum unpaid earn-out becomes due at closing of that transaction.”

**Conforming item before signature:** Attach Exhibit 2.4 specifying treatment of Ray’s $180,000 salary add-back, $42,000 legal add-back, replacement management compensation, revenue and cost allocation, and consistent treatment of extraordinary items. Historical seller-prepared EBITDA of $740,000 implies only $16,000 under the raw formula: 40% × ($740,000 − $700,000). If the two stated add-backs are included in the $740,000, excluding them yields $518,000 ($740,000 − $180,000 − $42,000), which yields no earn-out under that formula; the underlying accounts are not supplied, so this is an illustration, not a reconciliation. The $400,000 maximum needs $1,700,000 EBITDA: $700,000 + $400,000 ÷ 40%. No projection is established by these calculations.

**Why:** Buyer-policy EBITDA can absorb overhead and integration cost and erase the contingent price. [Earn-out definitions](../library/earnout_definitions.md).

## §7.4(c) Knowledge — change to actual-knowledge anti-sandbagging

**Replace with:** “Buyer may not recover for breach of a representation or warranty to the extent Buyer had actual, conscious knowledge before Closing of the specific facts constituting that breach. Actual knowledge means knowledge of Buyer’s transaction decision-makers who received Seller’s written disclosure or acknowledged the facts in written diligence communications; it excludes constructive, imputed, or merely ‘capable of being acquired’ knowledge. Seller bears the burden to establish such actual knowledge. Any fact expressly excepted on a disclosure schedule is outside the corresponding representation whether or not Buyer otherwise knew it. This subsection does not limit a specific indemnity expressly agreed for a disclosed matter.”

**Why:** The buyer draft preserves claims despite knowledge acquired or capable of being acquired at any time. For the disclosed 1099 issue, that would leave a clean §4.12 rep exposed even after buyer review. A precise schedule exception plus a bounded special indemnity is the main protection; the anti-sandbagging clause covers other actually known breaches. [Sandbagging](../library/sandbagging.md).
