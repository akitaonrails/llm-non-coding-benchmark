## Removal

The complaint alleges unpaid invoices of **$142,316.40 − $31,400 = $110,916.40** and a separate butter loss of **$18,240**, totaling **$129,156.40**. That is **$54,156.40 above** the $75,000 diversity threshold, before interest, fees, or costs. The client disputes the debt and has evidence about short or spoiled deliveries, but supplied no quantified offset. The pleaded amount meets the threshold on this record. [28 U.S.C. §§1332, 1441](../library/28usc_1332_1441_1446_1447.md).

Sound Cascade is a Washington corporation with its principal place of business in Kent, so it is a **Washington citizen**. Harbor Street is an LLC: its citizenship follows **every member**, not its Delaware formation or Portland principal office. Its only members are Noor Haddad (65%, living in Portland) and Caleb Sorensen (35%, living in Seattle). **Assuming those residences are their domiciles**, Noor is an Oregon citizen and Caleb is a Washington citizen; Harbor Street is therefore a citizen of **Oregon and Washington**. Washington citizenship on both sides defeats complete diversity. The served LLC is itself a Washington citizen, so the forum-defendant rule also bars removal based solely on diversity. Caleb is a member, not an individually named defendant. [28 U.S.C. §§1332, 1441](../library/28usc_1332_1441_1446_1447.md); [LLC citizenship cases](../library/llc_citizenship_cases.md).

The notice period is **30 days after receipt of the initial pleading**. Using September 11 service, exclude that day: September 12–30 is **19 days**, October 1–11 is **11 days**, and day 30 is **Sunday, October 11**. Monday, **October 12**, is Columbus Day, a federal legal holiday and Western District of Washington closure, so the federal counting rule moves the endpoint to **Tuesday, October 13**. Washington does not observe Columbus Day, but the state calendar controls the state answer and the federal calendar controls the federal filing deadline. No three-day mail extension applies. [28 U.S.C. §1446(b)](../library/28usc_1332_1441_1446_1447.md); [Federal Rule 6(a), (d)](../library/frcp_4_5_6_12_excerpts.md); [Federal holidays](../library/federal_holidays_2026.md); [Washington holidays](../library/rcw_1_16_050_wa_holidays.md). Unsupported removal could be remanded with costs and actual expenses, including attorney fees. [28 U.S.C. §1447(c)](../library/28usc_1332_1441_1446_1447.md).

## Requested hypothetical if a notice were filed September 28

These are **planning calculations only**, because removal is unavailable on the current facts. Assume a notice is filed **September 28**, no answer was served before removal, and that filing is Harbor Street's **first federal appearance**. The library does not define whether a removal notice counts as a Rule 16 appearance; that premise is **unverified**.

Federal Rule 81(c)(2) takes the **longest** of three response periods. From September 11, the two 21-day periods both end **Friday, October 2** (September 12–30 = 19 days; October 1–2 = 2). Seven days after the assumed September 28 notice is **Monday, October 5**. The longest is **October 5**. Rule 6(d)'s mail extension does not apply to the original complaint. [Federal Rule 81(c)(2)](../library/frcp_81c_removed_actions.md); [Federal Rules 6, 12](../library/frcp_4_5_6_12_excerpts.md).

The scheduling order is due by the **earlier** of 90 days after service or 60 days after appearance, absent a judge's good-cause delay. Ninety days after September 11 is **Thursday, December 10** (September 12–30 = 19; October = 31, cumulative 50; November = 30, cumulative 80; December 1–10 = 10). Sixty days after the assumed September 28 appearance is **Friday, November 27** (September 29–30 = 2; October = 31, cumulative 33; November 1–27 = 27). November 27 is **Native American Heritage Day**, a Washington legal holiday. For a federal period measured after an event, Rule 6(a)(6)(C) includes the district court state's holidays. November 28–29 is a weekend, so the endpoint moves to **Monday, November 30**, earlier than December 10. The Rule 26(f) conference must occur at least **21 days before** November 30: counting back 21 calendar days gives **Monday, November 9**. Both rules also require action as soon as practicable. [Federal Rules 16(b)(2), 26(f)(1)](../library/frcp_16_26f.md); [Federal Rule 6(a)](../library/frcp_4_5_6_12_excerpts.md); [Washington holidays](../library/rcw_1_16_050_wa_holidays.md).

If September 28 is **not** an appearance and the first appearance is instead the October 5 response, 60 days after October 5 is **Friday, December 4**, earlier than December 10; the corresponding 21-day conference date is **Friday, November 13**. A court order, earlier appearance, or good-cause finding could change this hypothetical schedule. [Federal Rules 16(b)(2), 26(f)(1)](../library/frcp_16_26f.md); [Federal Rule 6(a)](../library/frcp_4_5_6_12_excerpts.md).
