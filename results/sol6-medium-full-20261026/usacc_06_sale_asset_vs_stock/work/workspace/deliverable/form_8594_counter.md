# Proposed Form 8594 allocation for Lone Star

**Negotiating proposal, subject to valuation and closing schedules.** The $3,100,000 model treats the stated cash price as the entire amount to allocate. The amount of trade payables assumed is unknown; if assumption adds consideration, the allocation and taxes must be recalculated. The client's acceptance of a five-year covenant does not establish its fair market value. A consulting payment needs defined services and separate pricing if retained.

The residual method allocates consideration to classes in order, no more than fair market value in each class, before assigning the remainder to goodwill. Both parties must report consistently with their written agreement ([§1060 and Form 8594](../library/irc_1060_form_8594.md)). The counter uses the register's $650,000 equipment estimate; Lone Star's $900,000 exceeds it by $250,000 and conflicts with the residual method **if that estimate is the actual fair market value**. A $100,000 covenant is **not an appraised value**. Its price must reflect economic reality, and any genuine consulting services need their own terms ([§197 and non-compete](../library/irc_197_and_noncompete.md)). The trade name was included in the 2023 appraisal but has no separate 2026 valuation. A separately valuable trade name may require a Class VI amount; its precise statutory classification is **unverified in the closed library**. If the covenant, trade name or tangible values differ at closing, revise this schedule rather than filing the placeholders as fact.

At these provisional amounts, equipment gain is $650,000 − $240,000 = $410,000. Each register item sells at or below original cost, so all $410,000 is depreciation recapture under [§1245](../library/irc_1245.md). The $100,000 covenant is ordinary income ([§197 and non-compete](../library/irc_197_and_noncompete.md)). Inventory has no modeled gain; goodwill contributes $2,010,000 of modeled capital gain, subject to the caveats in the memo.
